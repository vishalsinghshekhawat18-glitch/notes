## 2. Canonical Concepts Created (12 Concepts across 3 New Topics)

### Topic 11: Part V — The Union Executive (`the-union-executive`, Order: 11)
1. **`CON-T11-01`**: *President of India: Constitutional Status, Electoral College & Impeachment*
   - De jure head of state; Article 54 electoral college (elected MPs + elected MLAs incl. Delhi/Puducherry 70th CAA; excludes nominated members and all MLCs); Article 55 parity formula and quota; Article 61 impeachment procedure (2/3rd TOTAL membership; nominated MPs vote, MLAs do not); Article 71 Supreme Court dispute jurisdiction.
2. **`CON-T11-02`**: *President's Powers: Veto Powers (Art 111) & Ordinance-Making Power (Art 123)*
   - Absolute, Suspensive (overridden by simple majority; not for Money Bills), Pocket Veto (1986 Post Office Bill); 24th CAA mandatory assent for CAAs; Article 123 recess requirement, max 6m + 6w duration, and anti-repromulgation doctrines (*D.C. Wadhwa 1987*, *Krishna Kumar Singh 2017*).
3. **`CON-T11-03`**: *President's Judicial & Pardoning Powers (Article 72)*
   - 5 clemency forms: Pardon (erases guilt + sentence), Commutation, Remission, Respite, Reprieve; scope across Court Martial, Union laws, and Death penalties; exercised on binding Cabinet aid & advice (*Maru Ram 1980*, *Kehar Singh 1989*); limited judicial review against arbitrariness and delay (*Epuru Sudhakar 2006*, *Shatrughan Chauhan 2014*).
4. **`CON-T11-04`**: *Vice-President of India: Status, Election (Art 66), Removal (Art 67) & Role*
   - Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (salary under Art 97); Electoral college of ALL MPs (elected + nominated, no state MLAs); Removal initiated strictly in Rajya Sabha by Effective Majority + Lok Sabha Simple Majority (no formal impeachment); acting President max 6 months.
5. **`CON-T11-05`**: *Prime Minister & Central Council of Ministers: Architecture & Collective Responsibility*
   - De facto real executive; Article 74 aid & advice mechanism (42nd binding, 44th 1-time reconsideration, Art 74(2) non-justiciability of advice); Article 75(3) collective responsibility to Lok Sabha only; Article 75(2) individual pleasure; 91st CAA 15% Lok Sabha ceiling; 6-month non-member rule (Art 75(5)); Cabinet Committees (ACC, PAC, CCEA).
6. **`CON-T11-06`**: *Attorney General of India (Article 76) & Law Officers Architecture*
   - Highest law officer of GoI; SC Judge qualifications; pleasure tenure; Article 88 right to participate in Parliament and Committees without voting right; Article 105(4) privileges; right of audience in all courts; private practice rules; non-constitutional status of Solicitor General.

### Topic 12: Part VI — The State Executive (`the-state-executive`, Order: 12)
7. **`CON-T12-01`**: *Governor of the State: Constitutional Dual Role, Appointment & Removal*
   - Dual capacity (State Constitutional Head + Centre Agent); Canadian appointment model by President (Art 155); 7th CAA dual-state charge; Article 156 pleasure tenure; *B.P. Singhal (2010)* judicial restraints against arbitrary/political removal; Sarkaria/Punchhi appointment conventions.
8. **`CON-T12-02`**: *Governor's Discretionary Powers (Art 163), Bill Reservation (Arts 200–201) & Ordinance (Art 213)*
   - Explicit constitutional discretion under Article 163(1) and 163(2) finality; Situational discretion (hung assembly); *S.R. Bommai (1994)* mandatory floor test; *Nabam Rebia (2016)* narrow discretion; Article 200 bill reservation (mandatory for High Court endangerment); Article 201 presidential veto on state bills; Article 213 ordinance power; Article 161 pardoning limits (cannot pardon death).
9. **`CON-T12-03`**: *Chief Minister & State Council of Ministers: Constitutional Architecture*
   - Real state executive; Article 164 appointment; 91st CAA dual limits (max 15% of Legislative Assembly, minimum floor of 12 ministers); Article 164(2) collective responsibility to Legislative Assembly; Article 167 duties to Governor; *Shamsher Singh (1974)* binding aid & advice rule.
10. **`CON-T12-04`**: *Advocate General for the State (Article 165) & Rights in Legislature (Art 177)*
    - Highest law officer in State; High Court Judge qualifications; pleasure of Governor; Article 177 right to participate in Assembly/Council without voting power; Article 194(4) MLA privileges.

### Topic 13: Part XIV — Civil Services & Public Services (`civil-services-and-public-services`, Order: 13)
11. **`CON-T13-01`**: *All-India Services (Article 312) & Framework of Public Services (Arts 308–309)*
    - Part XIV classification (AIS vs Central Services vs State Services); Article 312 exclusive Rajya Sabha federal power (2/3rd present & voting resolution); IAS, IPS, and IFS (created 1966 under AIS Act 1951); dual control mechanism (immediate state control vs ultimate central disciplinary control).
12. **`CON-T13-02`**: *Doctrine of Pleasure (Art 310) & Article 311 Safeguards with Exceptions*
    - Article 310 pleasure doctrine; Article 311(1) subordinate authority dismissal bar; Article 311(2) mandatory inquiry and hearing; Second proviso three exceptions: (2a) Criminal conviction, (2b) Impracticability with written reasons, (2c) Security of the State on President/Governor satisfaction; *Union of India v. Tulsiram Patel (1985)* 5-judge bench doctrine.

## 5. Outdated Material Corrected & Epistemic Traps Shielded

1. **Anglo-Indian Nominated Seats**: Replaced references to 545 Lok Sabha seats with 543 elected seats, highlighting the 104th Constitutional Amendment Act 2019.
2. **Indian Forest Service vs Foreign Service Trap**: Embedded explicit misconception shields clarifying that IFS in All-India Services is the Indian *Forest* Service (created 1966), while the Indian *Foreign* Service is a Central Civil Service.
3. **Union vs State Cabinet Size Distinction**: Clarified that while the Union Council of Ministers has a 15% ceiling with NO minimum floor (Art 75(1A)), State Councils of Ministers have a 15% ceiling AND a strict minimum floor of 12 ministers (Art 164(1A)).
4. **Election vs Impeachment Asymmetry**: Shielded the classic UPSC trap where nominated MPs vote in Presidential impeachment but not in election, while State MLAs vote in election but not in impeachment.
