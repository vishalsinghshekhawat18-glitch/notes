## 1. Master Comparative Matrices

### Matrix A: Direct Taxes vs. Indirect Taxes Architecture

| Parameter | Direct Taxes | Indirect Taxes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Impact vs. Incidence** | **Impact & Incidence fall on the same person**; cannot be shifted | **Impact on seller/producer, Incidence shifted to consumer** |
| **Core Instruments** | Personal Income Tax (PIT), Corporate Income Tax (CIT), STT, Capital Gains | Goods & Services Tax (GST), Customs Duties, Central/State Excise |
| **Progressivity / Equity** | Inherently **Progressive** (promotes income redistribution) | Inherently **Regressive** (identical rate across all wealth levels) |
| **Economic Effect** | Contractionary / Anti-inflationary on aggregate demand | Cost-push inflationary impact on market prices of goods |
| **Collection Cost & Evasion** | Higher administrative compliance cost; susceptible to evasion | Embedded in retail prices; broad tax base; difficult to evade |

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)

1. **GST Council Voting Power**: The Central Government **does NOT hold a 50% majority** in the GST Council. It holds **one-third (33.33%)** of the weighted votes, while all States/UTs together hold **two-thirds (66.67%)**. Because a 75% majority is required to pass any decision, both Centre and States possess mutual veto power.
2. **Petroleum Inclusion Trap**: Five petroleum products are **NOT permanently outside GST**. Under Article 279A(5), they will be brought under GST whenever the GST Council recommends a date. Only **alcoholic liquor for human consumption** is permanently outside GST via constitutional definition (Article 366(12A)).
3. **Cross-Credit Utilization**: CGST credit can **NEVER be used to discharge SGST liability**, and SGST credit can **NEVER be used to discharge CGST liability**. Only IGST credit can cross between Central and State streams.
4. **Angel Tax Abolition**: Section 56(2)(viib) (the "Angel Tax" on unlisted startups) was **completely abolished in Union Budget 2024–25** for all investor classes.
5. **Divisible Pool Surcharge/Cess**: Surcharges for Union purposes (Article 271) and statutory Cesses levied under charging Acts do **not enter the divisible pool** shared with States under Article 270(1) (a major point of fiscal-federal contention).
