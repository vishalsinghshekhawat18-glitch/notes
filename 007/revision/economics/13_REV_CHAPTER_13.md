### Matrix B: Specialized Inflation Terminology

| Metric | Defining Feature & Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Core Inflation** | Headline inflation **excluding Food and Fuel** ($\text{Headline} - [\text{Food} + \text{Fuel}]$); sticky demand trend |
| **Disinflation** | Inflation rate is positive but **declining** (e.g., from 7% down to 4%); prices still rising, but slower |
| **Deflation** | Persistent **negative inflation** ($\pi < 0$); prices actually falling; debt-deflation downward spiral |
| **Stagflation** | Coexistence of **high unemployment/stagnant growth** and **high inflation** (breaks classical SRPC) |
| **Shrinkflation** | Product price remains constant while **package volume, weight, or size is reduced** |
| **Reflation** | Deliberate state stimulus (fiscal/monetary) to lift an economy out of deflation or recession |

## 2. Key Mathematical Identities & Ratios

$$\mathbf{\text{GDP Deflator}} = \frac{\mathbf{\text{Nominal GDP}}}{\mathbf{\text{Real GDP}}} \times 100 \quad \Big[\text{Comprehensive: covers all domestic goods \& services; excludes imports}\Big]$$

$$\mathbf{\text{Fisher Equation}}: \quad \mathbf{i \approx r + \pi} \quad \Big[\text{Nominal Interest Rate} \approx \text{Real Interest Rate} + \text{Inflation Rate}\Big]$$

$$\mathbf{\text{Cagan Hyperinflation Definition}}: \quad \mathbf{\pi > 50\% \text{ per month}} \quad \Big(\approx 12,875\% \text{ annualized}\Big)$$

$$\mathbf{\text{Long-Run Phillips Curve (LRPC)}}: \quad \text{Vertical at } \mathbf{u^*} \text{ (NAIRU / Natural Rate of Unemployment)}$$

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)

1. **Services in WPI**: WPI measures **ONLY GOODS**. It has **ZERO coverage of services** (no health, education, transport, or rent).
2. **Housing in CPI Rural**: The Housing component carries a weight of **10.07% in CPI Combined**, but has a weight of **0.00% in CPI Rural** (rural housing is overwhelmingly owner-occupied; rent is compiled only in urban areas).
3. **Disinflation vs. Deflation**: Disinflation is **NOT deflation**. Under disinflation, prices continue to rise, just at a slower rate (inflation rate decreases from 8% to 5%). Deflation means prices are actually falling (negative inflation rate).
4. **GDP Deflator vs. Imported Shock**: An international crude oil spike or edible oil price surge affects **CPI directly**, but does **NOT directly enter the GDP deflator** because the GDP deflator reflects only domestic output.
5. **Compiling Agency Split**: CPI Combined is compiled by **NSO (MoSPI)**; CPI-IW/AL/RL are compiled by the **Labour Bureau (Ministry of Labour & Employment)**; WPI is compiled by the **Office of Economic Adviser (DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry)**.
