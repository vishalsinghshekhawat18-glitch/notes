## 1. Core Distinction Matrices

### Matrix A: The Four-Quadrant Economic Classification of Goods

| Good Category | Excludability | Rivalry | Primary Market Failure | Policy / Remedial Mechanism | Real-World Exemplar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Private Goods** | Excludable | Rivalrous | Under-consumption by poor if high prices | Market mechanism / Targeted subsidies | Food, automobiles, apparel |
| **Club Goods** | Excludable | Non-Rivalrous | Natural monopoly / Artificial scarcity | Price regulation / Concession pricing | Tollways (uncongested), OTT services |
| **Common Pool Resources** | Non-Excludable | Rivalrous | **Tragedy of the Commons** | Property rights, quotas, community governance (E. Ostrom) | Ocean fisheries, open aquifers, pastures |
| **Pure Public Goods** | Non-Excludable | Non-Rivalrous | **Free-Rider Problem** | 100% Sovereign funding via compulsory taxation | National defense, lighthouses, clean air |

---

### Matrix B: Final vs. Intermediate Goods (The End-Use Criterion)

| Attribute | Final Goods | Intermediate Goods |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Production Boundary** | Has crossed the boundary line of production | Remains within the production boundary line |
| **End-Use Destination** | Final household consumption or Capital Formation | Resale or complete input absorption in the same accounting year |
| **National Income Treatment** | Directly included in GDP | Strictly excluded from GDP (prevention of double-counting) |
| **Depreciation Treatment** | Capital goods undergo gradual depreciation | Expended entirely within the single production cycle |
| **Decisive Test** | Determined exclusively by end-use, NEVER by physical appearance | Same physical object can be final or intermediate depending on purchaser |

### Matrix C: Specialized Non-Conforming Goods

| Good Type | Price-Demand Relationship | Underlying Behavioral Mechanism | Real-World Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Veblen Good** | Direct ($P \uparrow \implies Q_D \uparrow$) | Conspicuous consumption; higher price signals elite social exclusivity | Luxury timepieces, bespoke designer apparel |
| **Giffen Good** | Direct ($P \uparrow \implies Q_D \uparrow$) | Extreme inferior staple; negative income effect eclipses substitution effect | Coarse grains (bajra, ragi) for impoverished households |
| **Merit Good** | Social Benefit > Private Benefit | Positive consumption externalities; under-consumed if market-determined | Primary education, childhood immunizations |
| **Demerit Good** | Social Cost > Private Cost | Negative consumption externalities; over-consumed due to imperfect knowledge | Cigarettes, alcohol, gambling |

## 2. Universal Macroeconomic Identities
$$\text{Macroeconomic Equilibrium}: \quad \text{Leakages } (S + T + M) = \text{Injections } (I + G + X)$$
$$\text{Net Investment}: \quad I_{\text{net}} = I_{\text{gross}} - \text{CFC (Depreciation)}$$

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)
1. **Physical Fallacy**: A laptop or tractor is NOT inherently a capital good. If bought by a home user, it is a consumer durable.
2. **Disaster Fallacy**: An earthquake or war destroying a factory is NOT Depreciation. It is a Capital Loss recorded on the balance sheet.
3. **Govt Provision Fallacy**: PDS wheat and train tickets are Private Goods provided by the State, NOT Public Goods (they are both excludable and rivalrous).
