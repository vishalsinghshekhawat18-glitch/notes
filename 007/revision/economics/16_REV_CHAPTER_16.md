## 1. Master Comparative Matrices

### Matrix A: Current Account vs. Capital/Financial Account

| Dimension | Current Account | Capital & Financial Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Economic Meaning** | Flow of real goods, services, income & transfers | Flow of financial claims, liabilities, debt & equity |
| **Sovereign Impact** | Directly impacts **Current National Income ($Y$)** | Alters the **International Investment Position (IIP)** |
| **Components** | • Merchandise Goods (Trade Balance)<br>• Services (Software, Tourism)<br>• Primary Income (Profits, Interest, Dividends)<br>• Secondary Income (Remittances, Grants) | • Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)<br>• Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)<br>• External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs)<br>• External Assistance & NRI Bank Deposits |
| **India's Structural Balance** | **Chronic Deficit (CAD)** (Trade deficit outstrips services/remittances) | **Chronic Surplus** (Capital inflows finance the CAD) |

### Matrix B: FDI vs. FPI (Arvind Mayaram Committee 2014)

| Parameter | Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) | Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI / FII) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Equity Share Threshold** | **$\ge 10\%$ of paid-up equity** in listed firm (or any % in unlisted) | **$< 10\%$ of paid-up equity** in a listed Indian firm |
| **Managerial Control** | Lasting interest, board representation, technology transfer | Purely financial return seeking; **Zero management control** |
| **Mobility & Volatility** | Stable, illiquid, sticky capital; factory/capex creation | Highly mobile **"Hot Money"**; rapid capital flight risk |
| **Debt Implication** | **Non-Debt creating inflow** | Non-debt (equities) or debt-creating (corporate bonds/G-Secs) |

### Matrix C: NRI Deposit Schemes Comparison

| Scheme | Currency of Account | Repatriation Freedom | Who Bears Currency Depreciation Risk? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FCNR(B)** | Foreign Currency (USD, GBP, EUR) | Fully & freely repatriable | **The Commercial Bank** (Depositor is immune) |
| **NRE** | Indian Rupee (INR) | Fully & freely repatriable | **The NRI Depositor** (Receives fewer dollars if INR falls) |
| **NRO** | Indian Rupee (INR) | Capped at **\$1 Million / year** | **The NRI Depositor** (Local Indian income/rents) |

## 2. Key Mathematical Identities & Ratios

$$\mathbf{\text{Current Account Balance (CAB)}} = \text{Merchandise Trade Balance} + \mathbf{\text{Net Invisibles}} \; (\text{Services} + \text{Primary} + \text{Secondary})$$

$$\mathbf{\text{Overall BoP Balance}} = \mathbf{\text{CAB}} + \mathbf{\text{Net Capital Inflows}} + \mathbf{\text{Errors \& Omissions}} \quad \Big[\mathbf{\Delta \text{Forex Reserves}}\Big]$$

$$\mathbf{\text{Import Cover}} = \frac{\text{Foreign Exchange Reserves}}{\text{Monthly Merchandise Imports}} \quad \Big[\text{India maintains } \mathbf{\approx 10\text{--}12 \text{ months}}\Big]$$

$$\mathbf{\text{Forex Reserves Hierarchy}}: \quad \mathbf{\text{FCA}} \; (\approx 85\text{--}88\%) \;>\; \mathbf{\text{Gold}} \; (\approx 8\text{--}10\%) \;>\; \mathbf{\text{SDR}} \; (\approx 2\text{--}3\%) \;>\; \mathbf{\text{RTP}} \; (<1\%)$$

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)

1. **Remittances are Current Account**: Private remittances from the Indian diaspora are **Secondary Income in the Current Account**, NOT Capital Account (they are unrequited transfers with no debt or repayment claim).
2. **Arvind Mayaram 10% Cut-Off**: If an offshore investor acquires **10% or more** of an Indian listed company's shares, it is statutorily classified as **FDI**, not FPI.
3. **BoP Accounting Identity**: The Balance of Payments **ALWAYS balances to zero in accounting terms** ($CA + FA + EO \equiv 0$). A "BoP Deficit" refers strictly to an excess of autonomous debits over credits prior to accommodating reserve drawdowns by the central bank.
4. **Autonomous vs. Accommodating**: Autonomous transactions are conducted for **independent profit/trade motives** ("above the line"). Accommodating transactions are conducted by the **central bank** to bridge the resulting deficit/surplus ("below the line").
5. **FCNR(B) Risk Bearing**: In FCNR(B) deposits, the **bank bears the exchange rate risk**, shielding the foreign depositor from rupee depreciation.
