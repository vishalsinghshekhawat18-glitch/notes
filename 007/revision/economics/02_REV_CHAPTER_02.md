## 1. Master Transition Matrices

### Matrix A: The 2015 SNA 2008 Pricing Ladder

| Pricing Stage | Exact Mathematical Identity | Taxes / Subsidies Adjusted | Economic Intuition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Factor Cost (FC)** | $\text{Compensation of Employees} + \text{Operating Surplus} + \text{Mixed Income} + \text{CFC}$ | None (Pure factor payments) | Direct cost to the producer inside factory walls |
| **Basic Price (BP)** | $\mathbf{\text{Factor Cost} + \text{Production Taxes} - \text{Production Subsidies}}$ | **Production Taxes & Subsidies** (independent of volume) | Price the producer actually retains at the factory gate |
| **Market Price (MP)** | $\mathbf{\text{Basic Price} + \text{Product Taxes} - \text{Product Subsidies}}$ | **Product Taxes & Subsidies** (per-unit volume based) | Final price the consumer pays in the commercial market |

$$\mathbf{\text{Headline GDP}} = \sum \text{GVA at Basic Price} + (\text{Product Taxes} - \text{Product Subsidies})$$

### Matrix B: Production Taxes vs. Product Taxes

| Parameter | Production Taxes / Subsidies | Product Taxes / Subsidies |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Base / Criterion** | Levied or granted **independent of actual volume** produced | Levied or granted **per unit of output** produced or sold |
| **Tax Examples** | Land revenues, stamp registration, factory municipal tax | GST, Central Excise Duty, Customs Import Duty, Sales Tax |
| **Subsidy Examples** | SSI subsidies, capital investment subsidies, farmer connection power | PDS food subsidy (per quintal), urea/fertilizer subsidy, LPG subsidy |
| **Ladder Position** | Bridge between **Factor Cost** and **Basic Price** | Bridge between **Basic Price** and **Market Price** |

## 2. Universal Capital Productivity & Deflator Identities

$$\text{GDP Deflator} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100 \quad (\text{Covers ALL domestic output; dynamically changing weights})$$

$$\text{ICOR} = \frac{I}{\Delta Y} \quad \Big(\frac{\text{Investment}}{\Delta \text{Output}}\Big) \implies \text{Harrod-Domar}: \quad g = \frac{s}{\text{ICOR}}$$
* **Low ICOR** = High capital efficiency & fast execution.
* **High ICOR** = Capital waste, project delays & technological obsolescence.

$$\text{Output Gap} = \text{Actual GDP } (Y) - \text{Potential GDP } (Y^*)$$
* $Y > Y^* \implies$ Positive (Inflationary Gap) $\implies$ Tighten monetary policy.
* $Y < Y^* \implies$ Negative (Recessionary Gap) $\implies$ Stimulate via fiscal/monetary policy.

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)
1. **The Remittance Trap**: Inward NRI remittances (>\$100B) are **Unrequited Transfer Payments**, NOT factor earnings. They enter BoP Current Account (Secondary Income), but are strictly **EXCLUDED from GDP, GNP, and NFIA**.
2. **The Embassy Trap**: US Embassy in New Delhi is US domestic territory; an Indian working inside generates **US GDP**, but contributes to **India's GNP**.
3. **Secondhand Asset Trap**: Selling an existing car or land plot is NOT in GDP (no new production). But the broker's commission **IS** in GDP.
4. **Headline Growth Divergence**: If government cuts fuel subsidies and raises GST, headline GDP growth will spike above GVA growth even if factory production is flat.
