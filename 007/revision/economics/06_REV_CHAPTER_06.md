## 1. Master Comparative Matrices

### Matrix A: Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) vs. Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)

| Parameter | Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) | Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Statute** | **Section 42(1) of RBI Act, 1934** | **Section 24 of Banking Regulation Act, 1949** |
| **Custodian** | Deposited **exclusively with the RBI** | Maintained by the **bank with itself** |
| **Asset Form** | **Cash only** | **Approved Liquid Assets**: Cash, Gold, G-Secs (T-Bills, SDLs) |
| **Interest Yield** | **0% Interest** (RBI pays nothing) | Banks earn market interest/yield on G-Secs |
| **Statutory Limits** | **No minimum floor or ceiling** (amended 2006) | Floor removed (2007); **Ceiling capped at 40.0%** (Currently ~18%) |
| **Pledge in Repo?** | Strictly cannot be pledged | **Cannot be pledged in regular Repo**; can dip only under **MSF** |

### Matrix B: The 50 bps Operational Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) Corridor

| Window | Operational Rate | Collateral Requirement | Primary Role / Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)** | $\mathbf{\text{Repo} + 0.25\%}$ (Ceiling) | Eligible G-Secs; **permitted to dip into SLR** (up to ~2% of NDTL) | Emergency overnight penal borrowing window |
| **Policy Repo Rate** | **Master Policy Anchor** (Set by MPC) | **Unencumbered G-Secs only** (cannot touch SLR) | Routine liquidity injection via repurchase agreements |
| **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)** | $\mathbf{\text{Repo} - 0.25\%}$ (Floor) | **UNCOLLATERALIZED (Zero collateral provided by RBI)** | Absorbs excess liquidity without exhausting RBI's G-Sec stock |
| **Bank Rate** | Pegged automatically to **MSF Rate** | No collateral (rediscounting bills) | Statutory penalty benchmark for CRR/SLR defaults |

### Matrix C: Lending Rate Transmission Evolution in India

| Regime | Introduced | Computation Basis | Transmission Friction / Flaw |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prime Lending Rate (PLR)** | Pre-2003 | Informal bank assessment | Completely opaque; arbitrary corporate concessions |
| **Benchmark PLR (BPLR)** | 2003 | Cost of funds + operating cost | Banks routinely lent *below* BPLR to top corporates |
| **Base Rate** | July 2010 | Average cost of funds | Minimum floor; opaque internal cost accounting |
| **MCLR** | April 2016 | Marginal cost of funds (incremental deposit rates) | Annual reset clauses; cuts delayed by 6–12 months |
| **EBLR** | **October 2019** | **External Benchmark** (Repo / 91-Day / 182-Day T-Bills) | **Near-instant transmission**; quarterly reset mandatory |

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)
1. **The SLR in Repo Trap**: A bank **CANNOT** use its 18% SLR quota to borrow under regular Repo. It can only dip into SLR under **MSF**.
2. **The CRR Asset Trap**: CRR is **Cash only** with the RBI and earns **0% interest**.
3. **The SDF Collateral Trap**: SDF is **UNCOLLATERALIZED**. The RBI does **NOT** give government securities to banks under SDF.
4. **EBLR Reset Frequency**: EBLR rates must be reset at least once every **three months**; spreads cannot be arbitrarily changed by banks.
