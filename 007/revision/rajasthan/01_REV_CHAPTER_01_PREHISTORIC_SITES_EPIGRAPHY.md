## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Exam Traps

1. **Neolithic Classification Trap**: Baror is **pre-/Harappan** and Bairat is an **Early Historic/Mauryan** Buddhist centre. Neither should be placed in a Neolithic category.
2. **Observation vs. Inference Trap (Kalibangan)**: The excavation uncovered **intersecting grid furrows** (secure observation). The claim that gram and mustard were cultivated simultaneously is a **scholarly hypothesis**, not an inscribed fact.
3. **Skeletal Trepanning Caveat**: Kalibangan's child skull has **six trepanned circular holes** with reported signs of healing. While interpreted as prehistoric surgery or hydrocephalus treatment, it must be framed with an interpretive caveat.
4. **Drainage Material Caution**: Standard urban drainage at Kalibangan utilized terracotta pipes and baked-brick drains. Popular claims of ubiquitous scooped wooden tree-trunk drains lack primary ASI report corroboration.
5. **Barli Epigraphic Date Controversy**: Barli (Ajmer) is conventionally memorised as **443 BCE** (tied to the Mahavira era by G.H. Ojha), but modern palaeographers (e.g. Richard Salomon) date it to the **late 2nd–1st century BCE**.
6. **Coin Circulation vs. Territorial Sovereignty**: The recovery of 16 coins of Menander at Bairat confirms **trade circulation and deposition**, not direct Indo-Greek political sovereignty over eastern Rajasthan.
7. **Rairh Superlative Caution**: Rairh yielded over 3,000 punch-marked silver coins and extensive iron slag. Do not assert it as the "largest single ancient coin hoard in India" without explicit source qualification.
8. **Epigraphic Roles at Bijolia (1170 CE)**: Distinguish the agents: Composed by **Gunabhadra**, drafted/written by **Keshav**, and engraved by **Nandana**.
9. **Prashasti Hermeneutics**: Royal prashastis (e.g., Kumbhalgarh 1460 CE, Raj Prashasti 1676 CE) are official eulogistic records. Genealogical descent and divine claims represent dynastic self-presentation rather than objective scientific consensus.
10. **Prehistoric Rock Art Findspot**: **Alaniya** (on the Alaniya river near Kota, Hadoti) is a primary prehistoric rock painting locality, not a Chalcolithic copper smelting site.
