## 1. 60-Second Concept Retrieval Skeleton

```text
[INTEGRATED JUDICIAL HIERARCHY (Arts. 214–237)]:
 • Single Integrated System: Supreme Court ➔ High Court (Principal Seat: Jodhpur; Permanent Bench: Jaipur) ➔ Subordinate District Judiciary
 • High Court Bench Status: A Bench is NOT a separate High Court; Rajasthan has ONE High Court
                                         │
[CONSTITUTIONAL GROUNDING (Arts. 214–231)]:
 • Court of Record (Art. 215): Power to punish for contempt of itself; authoritative record
 • Appointment & Tenure (Art. 217): Appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal after constitutional consultation (interpreted via the Collegium system); Retirement age: 62 YEARS
 • Oath (Art. 219): Administered by the GOVERNOR of Rajasthan (or designated person)
 • Financial Allocation Rule (Art. 221):
   - Salaries & Allowances: Charged on the Consolidated Fund of the STATE
   - Pensions of High Court Judges: Charged on the Consolidated Fund of INDIA (Art. 112(3)(d)(iii))
 • Extraordinary Writ Jurisdiction (Art. 226): Substantively broader than Supreme Court's Article 32 (enforces Fundamental Rights AND "any other purpose" / non-fundamental legal rights and statutory duties)
 • Superintendence (Art. 227): Administrative and judicial supervision over all subordinate courts and tribunals within territorial limits (EXCLUDING armed forces tribunals under Art. 227(4))
 • Subordinate Courts (Arts. 233–235):
   - District Judges (Art. 233): Appointed by the Governor in consultation with the High Court (Direct bar entry requires minimum 7 years advocate practice)
   - Civil Judge / RJS (Art. 234): Recruited via rules framed by Governor after consulting RPSC and High Court; Administrative control over subordinate judiciary vested entirely in High Court (Art. 235)
                                         │
[GENESIS, BENCH ARCHITECTURE & STRENGTH]:
 • Promulgation & Inauguration: Promulgated by Rajasthan High Court Ordinance, 1949; formally inaugurated on 29 AUGUST 1949 at JODHPUR by Rajpramukh Maharaja Sawai Man Singh II
 • First Chief Justice: Justice Kamal Kant Verma (sworn in along with 11 judges)
 • Seat Evolution & Rao Committee:
   - Satyanarayan Rao Committee (1958) recommended Jodhpur as the permanent Principal Seat; Jaipur Bench was abolished in 1958
   - Jaipur Bench was restored by Presidential Order under the State Reorganisation Act, becoming operational on 31 JANUARY 1977
 • Territorial Allocation: District-level allocation between Jodhpur Principal Seat and Jaipur Bench is governed by applicable High Court administrative orders (do not use obsolete fixed-district counts from outdated maps)
 • Sanctioned Strength: 50 JUDGES (38 Permanent + 12 Additional; Department of Justice baseline); New High Court Complex at Jhalamand, Jodhpur (Inaugurated 7 Dec 2019 by President Ram Nath Kovind)
 • Current Leadership Snapshot (October 2026):
   - Chief Justice: Justice Sanjay Kumar Agrawal (appointed by President order dated 5 September 2026; DoJ register)
                                         │
[CRIMINAL APPELLATE & CONFIRMATION PROCEDURE (BNSS 2023)]:
 • Death Sentence Confirmation: Under Section 407 BNSS (formerly Sec 366 CrPC), when a Court of Session passes a death sentence, the proceedings MUST be submitted to the High Court; sentence CANNOT be executed unless confirmed by the High Court (under Section 410 BNSS, confirmed by a bench of at least two judges)
 • Criminal Appeals: Section 415 BNSS governs appeals from convictions; appeals to the High Court lie where a sentence of imprisonment EXCEEDING SEVEN YEARS is passed (distinguish from "7 years or more")
                                         │
[ALTERNATIVE DISPUTE RESOLUTION (ADR) & ACCESSIBILITY]:
 • Lok Adalats (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987):
   - Statutory body: Rajasthan State Legal Services Authority (RSLSA, Jaipur; Patron-in-Chief: Chief Justice of Rajasthan High Court)
   - Legal Status of Award: Every award is deemed a Civil Court Decree; final and binding between parties; NO ORDINARY APPEAL LIES against an award (extraordinary constitutional remedy under Arts. 226/227 remains available on limited grounds such as fraud or jurisdictional illegality)
   - Scope: Civil disputes, compoundable criminal matters; CANNOT decide non-compoundable criminal offences
 • Regular Lok Adalat vs Permanent Lok Adalat (PLA):
   - Regular Lok Adalat: Purely consensual and conciliatory; if parties do not settle, matter returns to court (Lok Adalat cannot adjudicate on merits)
   - Permanent Lok Adalat (Section 22B): Set up for specified Public Utility Services (transport, postal, power, water, hospitals); attempts conciliation first, but if parties fail to agree, PLA has statutory power to DECIDE ON MERITS (subject to statutory monetary limit, currently ₹1 crore per RSLSA notification)
 • Gram Nyayalayas (Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008):
   - Designed to bring justice to the doorsteps of rural citizens at intermediate panchayat levels
   - First Gram Nyayalaya of Rajasthan: Established at BASSI (JAIPUR district) on 27 NOVEMBER 2009
   - Presiding Officer: Nyayadhikari (possessing qualifications of a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class / Civil Judge)
```

## 3. Top 25 Corrected Prelims Traps & Disputed Points

| # | High-Risk Trap / Textbook Absolute | Verified Epistemic Correction & Exam Benchmark |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | *"Justice Manindra Mohan Shrivastava is the current Chief Justice of Rajasthan High Court."* | **Trap**: Outdated incumbent. The Department of Justice issued an order appointing **Justice Sanjay Kumar Agrawal as Chief Justice of Rajasthan High Court on 5 September 2026**. |
| **2** | *"Rajasthan High Court at Jodhpur and Jaipur Bench are two distinct, separate High Courts."* | **Trap**: Constitutional error. A Bench is an integral part of the single High Court. Rajasthan possesses **one High Court with its Principal Seat at Jodhpur and a Permanent Bench at Jaipur**. |
| **3** | *"Jodhpur Principal Seat covers exactly 21 districts and Jaipur Bench covers 29 districts as a permanent constitutional division."* | **Trap**: These 21/29 lists are tied to older 33/50-district reorganisation drafts. Jurisdiction is allocated by High Court administrative notifications, which adjust with administrative changes. |
| **4** | *"Rajasthan High Court was established on 26 January 1950."* | **Trap**: Date error. Rajasthan High Court was inaugurated on **29 August 1949 at Jodhpur** by Rajpramukh Maharaja Sawai Man Singh II under the Rajasthan High Court Ordinance, 1949. |
| **5** | *"Satyanarayan Rao Committee established the Jaipur Bench in 1958."* | **Trap**: Causation reversal. The P. Satyanarayan Rao Committee (1958) recommended that the High Court be located permanently at Jodhpur, leading to the **abolition of the Jaipur Bench in 1958**. The Bench was restored in **1977**. |
| **6** | *"Jaipur Bench was restored on 8 December 1976."* | **Trap**: The Presidential Order was issued on 8 December 1976, but the Jaipur Bench formally became **operational on 31 January 1977**. |
| **7** | *"High Court judges retire at 65 years of age, same as Supreme Court judges."* | **Trap**: High Court judges retire at **62 years** (Article 217(1)). Supreme Court judges retire at **65 years** (Article 124(2)). |
| **8** | *"Pensions of High Court judges are paid from the Consolidated Fund of the State."* | **Trap**: Financial allocation trap. **Salaries and allowances are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State**, but **pensions of High Court judges are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** (Article 112(3)(d)(iii)). |
| **9** | *"High Court's writ jurisdiction under Article 226 is narrower than Supreme Court's Article 32."* | **Trap**: Doctrinal reversal. Article 226 is **broader** than Article 32 because High Courts can issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights **AND for 'any other purpose'** (ordinary statutory rights and legal duties). |
| **10** | *"Under Article 227, the High Court exercises superintendence over military courts and armed forces tribunals."* | **Trap**: Express constitutional exception. Article 227(4) explicitly provides that the High Court's power of superintendence **does NOT extend to any court or tribunal constituted under any law relating to the Armed Forces**. |
| **11** | *"Under BNSS, any criminal conviction involving imprisonment of 7 years or more goes directly on appeal to High Court."* | **Trap**: Statutory precision. Section 415(2) BNSS specifies the threshold as a sentence of imprisonment **exceeding seven years** (i.e. more than 7 years, not 7 years exactly). |
| **12** | *"A Sessions Court's death sentence can be executed immediately if no appeal is filed."* | **Trap**: Mandatory submission. Under **Section 407 BNSS** (formerly Sec 366 CrPC), the Sessions Court **must submit the proceedings to the High Court**, and execution is stayed until the High Court formally confirms the sentence. |
| **13** | *"A single judge of the High Court can confirm a death sentence on reference."* | **Trap**: Under **Section 410 BNSS**, when a death reference is made to the High Court, the confirmation of the sentence or any new sentence must be signed by **at least two judges**. |
| **14** | *"A Lok Adalat award can be challenged through a regular statutory appeal before a District Judge."* | **Trap**: Statutory bar. Under Section 21 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, a Lok Adalat award is deemed a civil court decree, is final and binding, and **NO APPEAL lies to any court**. |
| **15** | *"A Lok Adalat award can only be challenged under Article 226 strictly on grounds of fraud."* | **Trap**: Overstated restriction. While extraordinary writ review under Articles 226/227 is limited, grounds extend beyond fraud to include violation of fundamental principles of natural justice and lack of statutory jurisdiction. |
| **16** | *"A regular Lok Adalat can decide a dispute on merits if the parties fail to reach a compromise."* | **Trap**: Procedural boundary. A **regular Lok Adalat is strictly a conciliation forum**; if parties fail to compromise, the matter must be remitted back to the court. It cannot adjudicate on merits. |
| **17** | *"Permanent Lok Adalats (PLAs) are identical to regular Lok Adalats in powers."* | **Trap**: Distinction trap. Under Section 22C/22D of the LSA Act, if conciliation fails in a **Permanent Lok Adalat** for Public Utility Services, the PLA **possesses statutory power to decide the dispute on merits**. |
| **18** | *"Permanent Lok Adalats have unlimited pecuniary jurisdiction."* | **Trap**: RSLSA notifications prescribe a monetary ceiling for disputes before PLAs, which stands at **₹1 crore** (subject to periodic state notifications). |
| **19** | *"Gram Nyayalayas are established strictly in municipal corporation zones."* | **Trap**: Jurisdiction error. Gram Nyayalayas under the 2008 Act are designed for **rural areas at the intermediate Panchayat Samiti headquarters** to provide accessible justice at the grassroots. |
| **20** | *"The first Gram Nyayalaya of Rajasthan was opened in Jodhpur."* | **Trap**: Location error. The state's first Gram Nyayalaya was established at **Bassi in Jaipur district on 27 November 2009**. |
| **21** | *"A Gram Nyayalaya is presided over by a retired village Sarpanch."* | **Trap**: Professional judicial post. Gram Nyayalayas are presided over by a **Nyayadhikari**, who is a qualified judicial officer having the rank of a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class (JMFC). |
| **22** | *"Total pending cases in Rajasthan High Court and subordinate judiciary are around 6 lakh combined."* | **Trap**: Data conflation. Official Rajasthan High Court data (2 October 2026 snapshot) records **682,946 pending cases in the High Court ALONE** (164,450 civil, 186,806 criminal, 331,690 writs). Do not conflate High Court pendency with subordinate court figures. |
| **23** | *"Sanctioned judge strength of Rajasthan High Court is 35 judges."* | **Trap**: Strength metric. Department of Justice orders specify a **sanctioned strength of 50 Judges** (38 Permanent Judges and 12 Additional Judges). |
| **24** | *"Direct recruitment to the post of District Judge is called the entry-level RJS exam."* | **Trap**: Cadre confusion. ## 4. Diagnostic Active-Recall Flashcards

### Card 1: High Court Inception & Inauguration
- **Q**: State the date of inauguration, location, and first Chief Justice of the Rajasthan High Court.
- **A**: Formally inaugurated on **29 August 1949 at Jodhpur** by Rajpramukh Maharaja Sawai Man Singh II under the Rajasthan High Court Ordinance, 1949. The first Chief Justice was **Justice Kamal Kant Verma** (sworn in with 11 companion judges).

### Card 2: Current Chief Justice (October 2026)
- **Q**: Who is the Chief Justice of the Rajasthan High Court as of October 2026?
- **A**: **Justice Sanjay Kumar Agrawal**, appointed by Presidential order on **5 September 2026** (Department of Justice notification).

### Card 3: Jaipur Bench Abolition & Restoration
- **Q**: Which committee recommended the abolition of the Jaipur Bench, and on what date did it become operational again?
- **A**: The **P. Satyanarayan Rao Committee (1958)** recommended that Jodhpur be the sole High Court seat, leading to the abolition of the Jaipur Bench in 1958. It was restored under a 1976 Presidential Order and became operational on **31 January 1977**.

### Card 4: Financial Constitutional Rule for High Court Judges
- **Q**: From which funds are the salaries and pensions of High Court judges paid?
- **A**: 
  - **Salaries and Allowances**: Charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Article 202(3)(d)).
  - **Pensions**: Charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** (Article 112(3)(d)(iii)).

### Card 5: Scope of Article 226 vs Article 32
- **Q**: Why is the writ jurisdiction of the High Court under Article 226 broader than that of the Supreme Court under Article 32?
- **A**: The Supreme Court can issue writs under Article 32 **only for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights** (Part III). The High Court under Article 226 can issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights **AND "for any other purpose"** (enforcing ordinary legal rights and statutory duties).

### Card 6: Article 227 Superintendence & Constitutional Exception
- **Q**: What does High Court superintendence encompass under Article 227, and what is expressly excluded?
- **A**: The High Court exercises administrative and judicial superintendence over **all subordinate courts and tribunals** throughout its territory (including the Board of Revenue). **Express Exclusion**: Under Article 227(4), superintendence **does not extend to any court or tribunal constituted under Armed Forces law**.

### Card 7: Death Sentence Confirmation Procedure (BNSS 2023)
- **Q**: Under what section of BNSS is a death sentence submitted to the High Court, and how many judges must confirm it?
- **A**: Under **Section 407 BNSS** (formerly Sec 366 CrPC), the Sessions Court must submit the death sentence to the High Court. Under **Section 410 BNSS**, the confirmation must be signed by **at least two High Court judges**.

### Card 8: Regular Lok Adalat vs Permanent Lok Adalat (PLA)
- **Q**: What is the key functional difference between a regular Lok Adalat and a Permanent Lok Adalat (PLA)?
- **A**: A **regular Lok Adalat is purely conciliatory**; if parties fail to settle, it cannot adjudicate on merits. A **Permanent Lok Adalat (Section 22B, LSA Act)** handles Public Utility Services; if conciliation fails, it has statutory power to **decide the dispute on merits** (up to ₹1 crore).

### Card 9: Lok Adalat Award Appeal Trap
- **Q**: Does an appeal lie against a Lok Adalat award? Can it be challenged in court?
- **A**: **No ordinary appeal lies** to any court against a Lok Adalat award (it is deemed a final civil decree). However, it can be subjected to extraordinary judicial review under **Articles 226/227** on limited grounds such as fraud, lack of jurisdiction, or violation of natural justice.

### Card 10: Gram Nyayalayas in Rajasthan
- **Q**: When and where was the first Gram Nyayalaya established in Rajasthan, and who presides over it?
- **A**: Established at **Bassi (Jaipur district) on 27 November 2009** under the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008. It is presided over by a **Nyayadhikari**, a judicial officer holding the rank and qualifications of a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class (JMFC).
