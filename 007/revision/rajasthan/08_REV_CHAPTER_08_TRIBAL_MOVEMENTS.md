## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Exam Traps

1. **Mewar Bhil Corps (1841) Funding Trap**: The MBC was **NOT funded entirely by Mewar**. It was a shared expenditure: an Imperial Gazetteer-derived baseline records an annual cost of ~Rs 1,20,000, with Mewar paying Rs 50,000 and the British Government covering the remainder.
2. **Social Origin of Govind Guru**: Born in **1858 at Bansia (Bedsa) village in Dungarpur** into a nomadic **Banjara** family, NOT a Bhil family. He organized his movement primarily among Bhils and Garasias.
3. **Founding Place of Samp Sabha (c. 1883)**: Standard Rajasthan exam literature associates the founding of Samp Sabha with **Sirohi**, though its active sphere encompassed the entire Vagad and borderland hill tract.
4. **Mangarh Dham Date & Estimates (17 Nov 1913)**:
   - Occurred on **Margashirsha Purnima (17 November 1913)**.
   - The figures of **1.5 lakh attendees and ~1,500 deaths** are widely repeated government commemorative estimates, not audited nominal rolls.
5. **Caste & Background of Motilal Tejawat**: He was NOT a tribal leader by birth; he belonged to an **Oswal Jain merchant family** from Koliyari village (Jhadol, Mewar), affectionately revered as *"Bavji"*.
6. **Genesis & Slogan of Eki Movement**: Consecrated around **1920–21 at Matrikundiya (Rashmi, Chittorgarh)**, renowned as the *"Haridwar of Rajasthan"*. Revolutionary slogan: *"न हाकिम, न हुकम"* (Neither ruler nor dictate).
7. **"Mewar Ki Pukar" Charter Nuance**: Motilal Tejawat submitted a 21-point memorandum to Maharana Fateh Singh. The widely taught exam convention is **18 accepted and 3 rejected**, though secondary lists vary on whether the rejected three were begar/forest/boars or timber/grass/boars.
8. **Pal-Chitariya / Dadhvav vs. "Neemra/Neemda"**: The major Eki movement firing of **7 March 1922** took place at **Pal-Chitariya and Dadhvav** in then-Idar State (now **Sabarkantha district, Gujarat**). Rajasthan exam textbooks frequently refer to this event as the *"Neemda / Neemra Massacre"*.
9. **Criminal Tribes Legislation vs. Jairam Pesha**:
   - Central statutes: Criminal Tribes Act of 1871 (first central act) and 1924 (consolidated act applied in Rajputana).
   - Princely state level: **Jaipur State promulgated the Jairam/Jarayam Pesha regime in 1930**, imposing compulsory registration and surveillance.
10. **Baghawas "Mukti Diwas" (1946) vs. Central Repeal (1952)**:
    - **28 October 1946 at Baghawas**: Estimated 26,000 Chaukidari Meenas surrendered their registers and resigned village watch duties (*Mukti Diwas*).
    - **31 August 1952**: Central **Criminal Tribes Laws (Repeal) Act, 1952** came into force, marking the nationwide legal denotification milestone.
