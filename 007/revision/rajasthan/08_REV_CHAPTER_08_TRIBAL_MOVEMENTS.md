## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Exam Traps

1. **Founding Place of Samp Sabha (1883 CE)**:
   - Established at **Sirohi**, NOT at Dungarpur, Banswara, or Mangarh Hill.
2. **Social Origin of Govind Guru**:
   - Born at **Bansia (Bedsa) village in Dungarpur** (1858) into a nomadic **Banjara** family, NOT a Bhil tribal family.
3. **Exact Date of Mangarh Dham Massacre**:
   - Occurred on **17 November 1913 (Margashirsha Purnima)** — a full **six years prior to the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919)**.
4. **Mangarh Massacre Casualties & Forces**:
   - Over **1,500 Bhil devotees** were killed when the British Mewar Bhil Corps (MBC), 104th Wellesley Rifles, and Baroda State troops opened combined machine-gun and artillery fire.
5. **Caste & Background of Motilal Tejawat ("Bavji")**:
   - He was NOT of tribal ancestry; he was born in an **Oswal Jain merchant family** at Koliyari village (Jhadol Thikana, Udaipur).
6. **Genesis & Slogan of the Eki Movement**:
   - Consecrated in **May 1921 at Matrikundiya (Rashmi, Chittorgarh)**, renowned as the *"Haridwar of Rajasthan"*. Slogan: *"न हाकिम, न हुकम"* (Neither Ruler, Nor Dictate).
7. **"Mewar Ki Pukar" Verdict**:
   - A charter of 21 grievances drafted by Motilal Tejawat. Maharana Fateh Singh **accepted 18 demands** and **rejected exactly 3**: (1) Abolition of Begar (forced labor), (2) Forest rights over timber, and (3) Lifting curbs on hunting wild boars.
8. **Neemra Massacre (1922)**:
   - Occurred on **7 March 1922** at Neemra village (Idar state border); British Commander Major Sutton ordered unprovoked machine-gun fire, killing **1,200 Bhils and Garasias**.
9. **Criminal Tribes Act (1871) vs. Jairam Pesha (1930)**:
   - CTA (1871) was an all-India British imperial statute; **Jairam Pesha (1930)** was a specific Jaipur Princely State enactment compelling all Meenas aged **12 and above** (both male and female) to mark daily attendance at local Thanas.
10. **Baghawas "Mukti Diwas" (1946) vs. Final Legal Repeal (1952)**:
    - **28 October 1946 at Baghawas**: 26,000 Chaukidari Meenas publicly threw down their keys and resigned en masse (*Mukti Diwas*).
    - **1952**: Final statutory repeal of Jairam Pesha enacted under Chief Minister **Tikaram Paliwal**.
