## 3. Top 25 Corrected Prelims Traps & Disputed Points

| # | High-Risk Trap / Textbook Absolute | Verified Epistemic Correction & Exam Benchmark |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | *"Rajasthan's total forest cover is 16,655 km² and tree cover is 8,733 km²."* | **Trap**: These are older ISFR data. **ISFR 2023** reports **Forest Cover = 16,548.21 km²** (4.84%) and **Tree Cover = 10,841.12 km²** (3.17%), totaling **27,389.33 km²** (8.00%). |
| **2** | *"Very Dense Forest (VDF) in Rajasthan covers only 78 sq km."* | **Trap**: ISFR 2023 records **VDF = 223.20 km²**, Moderately Dense Forest (MDF) = **4,237.41 km²**, and Open Forest (OF) = **12,087.60 km²**. |
| **3** | *"Total Recorded Forest Area (RFA) in Rajasthan is exactly 32,863 km²."* | **Trap**: GoR Forest Department's **2024–25** legal report records total RFA as **33,020.32 km²** (9.65% of state area), comprising Protected 56.55%, Reserved 36.94%, and Unclassed 6.51%. |
| **4** | *"Reserved forests legally prohibit 100% of human activities under all circumstances."* | **Trap**: Overgeneralized. While strict preservation is the rule, specific rights, concessions, and entry permissions may be granted under applicable forest acts and settlement orders. |
| **5** | *"Protected forests permit all local grazing and timber felling freely without permission."* | **Trap**: In protected forests, activities are generally permitted *unless prohibited*, but grazing and minor produce collection remain subject to state regulation, fees, and working plans. |
| **6** | *"National Forest Policy 1988 mandates 60% forest cover in mountains and 20% in plains."* | **Trap**: The 1988 policy sets a national goal of **one-third (33.3%)** forest/tree cover, with **two-thirds (66.7%) in mountainous/hilly tracts** to prevent soil erosion and land degradation. |
| **7** | *"Rajasthan has legally achieved its 20% forest cover target under the 2023 Policy."* | **Trap**: 20% forest and tree cover by 2040 is an **aspirational policy target** in the Rajasthan Forest Policy 2023, not an achieved reality. Actual current cover is ~8.00%. |
| **8** | *"Entisols are the sole soil order found throughout Western Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Both **Entisols** (young, undeveloped soils) and **Aridisols** (arid profile with calcic/gypsic horizons) are widely co-represented across Western Rajasthan. |
| **9** | *"All eastern alluvial soils are Alfisols and represent the highest fertility in Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Soil taxonomy is profile-based. Not all alluvial soils qualify as Alfisols, and fertility depends on organic carbon, available nutrients, and soil management, not just the taxonomic order. |
| **10** | *"Black soils in Rajasthan are formed exclusively from Cretaceous Deccan Trap basalt."* | **Trap**: While Hadoti black soils are related to basaltic parent material and weathering, Vertisol profiles develop through clay translocation and climate factors; black soils also occur on other local parent materials. |
| **11** | *"Gypsum universally cures all saline, sodic, and alkaline soils."* | **Trap**: **Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$) is specifically used for Sodic (Alkali) soils** to replace exchangeable $Na^+$ with $Ca^{2+}$. Ordinary saline soils require **salt leaching and drainage**, NOT gypsum application alone. |
| **12** | *"Iron pyrites and rock phosphate are standard official remedies for soil salinity."* | **Trap**: Unscientific generalisation. Salinity requires drainage and leaching; pyrites are sometimes used to acidify highly sodic/calcareous soils, but they do not universally "cure" soil salinity. |
| **13** | *"Soil Health Card measures pH, EC, and Organic Carbon as 'physical parameters'."* | **Trap**: pH and Electrical Conductivity (EC) are **chemical indicators**, while Organic Carbon (OC) is a **biological/fertility indicator**. Physical parameters refer to texture and bulk density. |
| **14** | *"Salai (Boswellia serrata) is the commercial tree that produces Guggal resin."* | **Trap**: Botanical conflation. Salai produces **Salai gum/incense resin** (*Boswellia serrata*); genuine medicinal **Guggal** is tapped from ***Commiphora wightii***. |
| **15** | *"Mahua's valid botanical scientific name is Madhuca indica."* | **Trap**: The accepted botanical name is ***Madhuca longifolia*** (with *M. indica* being a widely cited older botanical synonym). |
| **16** | *"In the 1730 Khejarli massacre, 363 Bishnois died 'in addition to' Amrita Devi and her daughters."* | **Trap**: The historical record documents a **total of 363 martyrs**, which *includes* Amrita Devi and her three daughters (Asu, Ratni, Bhagu). |
| **17** | *"Khejarli hosts the 'world's only tree fair' on Bhadrapada Shukla Dashami."* | **Trap**: While celebrated as a unique and venerable memorial fair in Rajasthan, the global uniqueness superlative is an unverified popular epithet. |
| **18** | *"Global Great Indian Bustard population is permanently fixed under 150 birds with 90% in Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: These are survey-dependent estimates from specific reporting years, not permanent constants. Note its status as **Critically Endangered (CR)** and ongoing threats from overhead power cables. |
| **19** | *"Keoladeo Ghana is currently the regular winter home of Siberian Cranes."* | **Trap**: Siberian Cranes (*Leucogeranus leucogeranus*) are a **historical winter migrant** at Keoladeo; they have not been recorded wintering regularly at the park for over two decades. |
| **20** | *"Rajasthan has only two Ramsar wetland sites: Keoladeo and Sambhar."* | **Trap**: Outdated. As of 2025, Rajasthan has **five Ramsar sites**: Keoladeo (1981), Sambhar (1990), **Menar** (Udaipur, 2025), **Khichan** (Phalodi, 2025), and **Siliserh** (Alwar, 2025). |
| **21** | *"Desert National Park is officially one of the 4 legally notified National Parks of Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: The state Forest Department lists **3 National Parks** (Ranthambhore, Keoladeo, Mukundara). DNP is categorized as **Desert National Park Sanctuary**, with its National Park final notification under process. |
| **22** | *"Dholpur–Karauli is India's 54th Tiger Reserve."* | **Trap**: Sequence error. According to NTCA official chronology, Ranipur is 53rd, Veerangana Durgavati is 54th, and **Dholpur–Karauli is the 55th Tiger Reserve of India** (and the 5th in Rajasthan). |
| **23** | *"Ramgarh Vishdhari Tiger Reserve is located exclusively in Bundi district."* | **Trap**: The notified reserve area encompasses portions of **both Bundi and Kota districts**, linking Ranthambhore and Mukundara corridors. |
| **24** | *"Sariska was the site of the world's first helicopter translocation of tigers."* | **Trap**: Conflates international translocation methods with Indian conservation history. Sariska was India's pioneering tiger reintroduction project (2008) following total local extinction, but global superlatives are unverified. |
| **25** | *"Sita Mata Sanctuary is the exclusive, only habitat of the Giant Flying Squirrel in India."* | **Trap**: The Indian Giant Flying Squirrel (*Petaurista philippensis*) occurs across several moist deciduous and hill forests in India; Sita Mata is its primary and most famous sanctuary habitat *within Rajasthan*. |

## 4. Diagnostic Active-Recall Flashcards

### Card 1: Official Forest Cover Metrics (ISFR 2023)
- **Q**: State Rajasthan's official forest cover, tree cover, and total combined cover under ISFR 2023.

  - **Forest Cover**: **16,548.21 km²** (4.84% of state geographical area).
  - **Tree Cover**: **10,841.12 km²** (3.17% of state area).
  - **Combined Forest + Tree Cover**: **27,389.33 km²** (8.00% of state area). Mapped scrub stands at 5,476.75 km².

### Card 2: Legal Forest Categories & Areas (2024–25)
- **Q**: What is Rajasthan's Recorded Forest Area (RFA), and what are the shares of Protected, Reserved, and Unclassed forests?
- **A**: Total RFA = **33,020.32 km²** (9.65% of state area).
  1. **Protected Forests (रक्षित वन)**: **18,671.49 km² (56.55%)** — LARGEST category.
  2. **Reserved Forests (आरक्षित वन)**: **12,198.71 km² (36.94%)**.
  3. **Unclassed Forests (अवर्गीकृत वन)**: **2,150.12 km² (6.51%)**.

### Card 3: Soil Reclamation Science (Saline vs Sodic)
- **Q**: How does chemical reclamation differ between Saline and Sodic (Alkali) soils?
- **A**: 
  - **Saline Soils** (high soluble salts, high EC): Managed by **leaching salts** with quality irrigation water coupled with adequate subsurface drainage.
  - **Sodic Soils** (excess exchangeable sodium, high ESP/pH): Requires **Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$)** to exchange calcium for sodium ions, followed by leaching and drainage. (Do not prescribe gypsum alone for saline soils).

### Card 4: Soil Orders of Arid Rajasthan
- **Q**: Which two USDA soil orders dominate the hot-arid plains of Western Rajasthan, and what distinguishes them?
- **A**: **Entisols** and **Aridisols**. Entisols are young soils showing little or no diagnostic profile horizon development (common in active sand dunes and shifting sands). Aridisols exhibit arid moisture regimes along with subsurface diagnostic accumulations of calcium carbonate, gypsum, or salts.

### Card 5: Vertisols Diagnostic Signature
- **Q**: What are the diagnostic physical properties of Vertisols, and where do they occur in Rajasthan?
- **A**: High content of expanding clay minerals (montmorillonite), exhibiting pronounced **shrink–swell behavior**, deep vertical cracks upon drying, and self-churning/self-swallowing (*pedoturbation*). Found prominently on the **Hadoti Plateau** (Kota, Bundi, Baran, Jhalawar).

### Card 6: Khejarli Ecological Sacrifice (1730 CE)
- **Q**: State the year, ruling maharaja, and verified martyr count of the Khejarli massacre.
- **A**: Occurred in **September 1730 CE (Bhadrapada Shukla Dashami)** at Khejarli village under **Maharaja Abhai Singh** of Jodhpur. A total of **363 Bishnois** (including Amrita Devi and her three daughters) sacrificed their lives protecting sacred Khejri trees against royal timber cutters.

### Card 7: Tiger Reserves of Rajasthan (NTCA Chronology)
- **Q**: List the 5 notified Tiger Reserves of Rajasthan, noting the official national numbering for the newest two.
- **A**: 
  1. **Ranthambhore Tiger Reserve** (Sawai Madhopur)
  2. **Sariska Tiger Reserve** (Alwar)
  3. **Mukundara Hills Tiger Reserve** (Kota, Bundi, Jhalawar, Chittorgarh)
  4. **Ramgarh Vishdhari Tiger Reserve** (Bundi & Kota) — **52nd Tiger Reserve of India**
  5. **Dholpur–Karauli Tiger Reserve** (Dholpur & Karauli) — **55th Tiger Reserve of India** (NOT 54th!)

### Card 8: Ramsar Wetland Sites of Rajasthan
- **Q**: Name all five Ramsar wetland sites in Rajasthan as of 2025/2026.
- **A**: 
  1. **Keoladeo National Park** (Bharatpur; designated 1981, on Montreux Record)
  2. **Sambhar Lake** (Jaipur/Didwana-Kuchaman/Ajmer; designated 1990)
  3. **Menar Bird Sanctuary** (Udaipur; designated 2025)
  4. **Khichan Bird Sanctuary** (Phalodi; designated 2025)
  5. **Siliserh Lake** (Alwar; designated 2025)

### Card 9: Desert National Park Legal Classification Trap
- **Q**: Why is it technically incorrect in an administrative exam to count Desert National Park as one of Rajasthan's listed National Parks?
- **A**: The state Forest Department's official protected-area register lists only **3 National Parks** (Ranthambhore, Keoladeo, Mukundara). Desert National Park is officially classified as **Desert National Park Sanctuary**; the final statutory notification upgrading it to a National Park remains under process.

### Card 10: Botanical Conflation Trap — Salai vs Guggal
- **Q**: Differentiate Salai from Guggal in terms of binomial names and economic products.
- **A**: 
  - **Salai**: ***Boswellia serrata*** (Burseraceae); produces commercial salai resin used in varnishes and incense.
  - **Guggal**: ***Commiphora wightii*** (Burseraceae); produces the renowned medicinal oleo-gum-resin (*Guggulu*) used in Ayurvedic anti-inflammatory formulations.
