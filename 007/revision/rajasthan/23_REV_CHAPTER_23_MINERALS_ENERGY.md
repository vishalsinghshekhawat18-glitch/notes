## 3. Top 25 Corrected Prelims Traps & Disputed Points

| # | High-Risk Trap / Textbook Absolute | Verified Epistemic Correction & Exam Benchmark |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | *"Rajasthan has 81 minerals identified and exactly 57 commercially exploited as a timeless constant."* | **Trap**: These are legacy geological counts. The official **Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024** reports **22 major minerals and 36 minor minerals produced** (58 combined). |
| **2** | *"Mining contributes over 15% of Rajasthan's Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP)."* | **Trap**: Gross confusion of metrics. Mining sector contribution to GSDP is **3.4%** (FY 2023–24). ₹21,922 crore is the MCDR mineral production value (~15.5% of India's total). |
| **3** | *"Mineral revenue received by the state government is over ₹20,000 crore."* | **Trap**: Mineral revenue received by the Rajasthan state exchequer was **₹7,491 crore in FY 2023–24** (with major minerals contributing ₹3,945 crore). Do not confuse state revenue with production value. |
| **4** | *"Rajasthan has a permanent 100% national monopoly in Jasper production."* | **Trap**: Jasper was historically mined as an ornamental stone, but current official state mineral policies (2024) do not list Jasper among the official sole-producer minerals. |
| **5** | *"Rajasthan produces exactly 99% of India's lead-zinc, 95% of feldspar, and 90% of rock phosphate every year."* | **Trap**: These figures conflate estimated shares of national *geological resources* with annual *production* shares. Production percentages vary year to year with dispatch records. |
| **6** | *"Rampura Agucha is conclusively proven to be the world's richest zinc mine with 15% grade."* | **Trap**: Rampura Agucha is an internationally renowned, world-class high-grade deposit, but "world's richest" is an unverified superlative unless tied to a specific international reporting standard (JORC/NI 43-101). |
| **7** | *"Bhukia–Jagpura in Banswara holds 118 million tonnes of pure gold reserves."* | **Trap**: Unit error. The official DMG figure (~105.81 to 118.88 million tonnes) represents **gold-bearing ore resources** at low grades (grams per tonne), NOT pure metallic gold. |
| **8** | *"Rohil in Sikar is India's second-largest operating uranium mine producing ore today."* | **Trap**: Status error. Official Department of Atomic Energy answers (Feb/March 2026) confirm that UCIL is the prospective lessee; the mine-and-mill project is still undergoing statutory environmental and regulatory clearance. |
| **9** | *"Selenite is an independent chemical mineral completely distinct from gypsum."* | **Trap**: Selenite is merely the clear, crystalline, well-cleaved structural variety of **gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$)**, sharing the identical chemical composition. |
| **10** | *"Degana (Nagaur) is currently the largest functioning tungsten producing mine in India."* | **Trap**: Historical importance vs operational status. Degana's Revat Hill deposit is India's most famous tungsten occurrence, but commercial mining operations have been dormant for decades. |
| **11** | *"Makrana Marble is an official UNESCO World Heritage Site."* | **Trap**: Distinction error. Makrana Marble was designated an **IUGS Global Heritage Stone Resource in 2019** by the International Union of Geological Sciences, NOT a UNESCO World Heritage property. |
| **12** | *"Kishangarh is officially measured as Asia's largest marble market."* | **Trap**: "Asia's largest" is a trade and tourism epithet; market rankings vary depending on whether volume, transaction value, or slab processing capacity is measured. |
| **13** | *"District Mineral Foundation Trust (DMFT) rules mandate 60% spending on high-priority sectors."* | **Trap**: Outdated guideline. The Ministry of Mines' **January 2024 revised PMKKKY guidelines raised the minimum high-priority allocation to at least 70%** (with up to 30% for other priority sectors). |
| **14** | *"All mining leaseholders pay 30% of royalty to DMFT."* | **Trap**: Leases granted on or after **12 January 2015 pay 10% of royalty**; only leases granted prior to that date pay **30% of royalty**. |
| **15** | *"The Rajasthan M-Sand Policy of 2020 strictly mandates 25% M-Sand in all construction."* | **Trap**: The governing framework is the **Rajasthan M-Sand Policy 2024**; mandatory percentages depend on specific PWD/government departmental circulars rather than a blanket law. |
| **16** | *"Rajasthan produces 20–22% of India's crude oil as a permanent constant."* | **Trap**: Onshore production shares fluctuate annually depending on offshore output (Bombay High) and private production curves. Quote the fiscal year explicitly from PPAC reports. |
| **17** | *"Commercial crude oil production in Barmer began immediately upon discovery in January 2004."* | **Trap**: Discovery by Cairn India occurred in January 2004 (Mangala field), but commercial oil production formally commenced on **29 August 2009**. |
| **18** | *"Pachpadra Refinery (HRRL) is owned 50:50 by HPCL and Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: The joint venture equity structure is strictly **74% HPCL : 26% Government of Rajasthan**. |
| **19** | *"Total project cost of HRRL is ₹43,129 crore."* | **Trap**: Severely outdated. The initial cost estimate was revised upwards to ₹72,937 crore, and officially revised by PIB/Cabinet to **₹79,459 crore in April 2026**. |
| **20** | *"Pachpadra Refinery is located in Barmer district."* | **Trap**: Administrative update. The refinery site at Pachpadra falls squarely within the newly created **Balotra district** (historically part of Barmer). |
| **21** | *"Rajasthan's total installed solar capacity is around 22,000 MW."* | **Trap**: Stale data. Official state energy portal figures as of **30 September 2026 record 45,121.44 MW of installed solar capacity** (total renewable capacity = 51,595.50 MW). |
| **22** | *"Rajasthan's solar policy target is 30 GW by 2025 or 90 GW by 2030."* | **Trap**: Outdated targets. The state's current official renewable policy target is **125 GW by FY 2029–30**. |
| **23** | *"Rajasthan's solar potential is officially fixed at exactly 142 GW."* | **Trap**: 142 GW is an older NISE assessment. The state government's current investor baseline estimates solar potential at up to **829 GW** under updated spatial-insolation modeling. |
| **24** | *"Wind energy potential in Rajasthan is 127 GW without qualification."* | **Trap**: Metric discipline. The National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE) assesses potential as **127.75 GW at 120 m hub-height**, rising to **284.2 GW at 150 m hub-height**. Always state hub-height. |
| **25** | *"Bhadla is the world's largest solar park and generates electricity 24 hours a day."* | **Trap**: Bhadla (~2,245 MW) is a utility-scale photovoltaic park generating power during daytime sunshine hours; global park rankings change rapidly with megawatt commissioning in China and India. |

## 4. Diagnostic Active-Recall Flashcards

### Card 1: Official Mineral Profile (Policy 2024)
- **Q**: How many major and minor minerals are officially reported as produced in Rajasthan, and what is the state's mineral revenue?
- **A**: The **Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024** reports **22 major minerals and 36 minor minerals produced** (58 combined). In FY 2023–24, the state collected **₹7,491 crore in mineral revenue** (including ₹3,945 crore from major minerals), with the sector contributing **3.4% to GSDP**.

### Card 2: Sole Producer Minerals & Epistemic Boundaries
- **Q**: Which minerals are listed as sole-producer minerals in the Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024, and what caution applies to production percentages?
- **A**: Mineral Policy 2024 lists **lead, zinc, wollastonite, selenite, calcite, and gypsum**. Crucially, in-situ *geological resource shares* (which are long-term reserve estimates) must never be conflated with annual *production shares* or commercial dispatches.

### Card 3: Rampura Agucha & Zawar Epistemic Discipline
- **Q**: State the geographic locations, key minerals, and historical/economic significance of Rampura Agucha and Zawar.
- **A**: 
  - **Rampura Agucha** (Bhilwara): World-class, high-grade lead-zinc deposit operated by Hindustan Zinc Limited (HZL); major source of zinc concentrate.
  - **Zawar** (Udaipur): Ancient lead-zinc mining centre dating back over two millennia (promoted during Maharana Lakha's reign); one of the world's oldest known zinc distillation sites.

### Card 4: Gold & Uranium Resource Nuance
- **Q**: What are the true facts regarding the gold deposits of Bhukia–Jagpura and the uranium deposits of Rohil?
- **A**: 
  - **Bhukia–Jagpura** (Banswara): Contains **~105.81 million tonnes of gold-bearing ore resources** at low grades (grams of gold per tonne of rock)—NOT refined gold metal.
  - **Rohil** (Sikar): Prospective lease granted to UCIL; official DAE updates confirm it is currently undergoing statutory environmental clearances—NOT an active producing mine.

### Card 5: Makrana Marble & Kishangarh Status
- **Q**: What international designation was awarded to Makrana Marble, and what role does Kishangarh play?
- **A**: **Makrana Marble** was recognized as an **IUGS Global Heritage Stone Resource in 2019** (an earth-science designation, not a UNESCO World Heritage site). **Kishangarh** (Ajmer) serves as a premier national and international processing and trading hub.

### Card 6: DMFT & PMKKKY Regulatory Guidelines
- **Q**: What are the revised PMKKKY expenditure rules and royalty contribution rates for the District Mineral Foundation Trust (DMFT)?
- **A**: Under the **January 2024 revised PMKKKY guidelines**, at least **70% of DMFT funds must be spent on High-Priority Sectors** (drinking water, health, education, sanitation) and up to 30% on other priority sectors. Contribution rates: **10% of royalty** for leases granted on/after 12 Jan 2015; **30% of royalty** for prior leases.

### Card 7: Four Petroliferous Basins of Rajasthan
- **Q**: Name the four petroliferous basins of Rajasthan and identify the major field in the Barmer–Sanchore Basin.
- **A**: 
  1. **Barmer–Sanchore Basin** (Major field: Mangala, commercial production began 29 August 2009; Bhagyam, Aishwarya).
  2. **Jaisalmer Basin** (Natural gas: Tanot, Dandewala).
  3. **Bikaner–Nagaur Basin** (Heavy crude: Baghewala, Poonam).
  4. **Vindhyan Basin** (Southeastern Meso-to-Neoproterozoic frontier basin).

### Card 8: Pachpadra Refinery (HRRL) Parameters
- **Q**: State the corporate equity structure, capacity, revised cost, and administrative location of HRRL.
- **A**: 
  - **Location**: Pachpadra, **Balotra district** (formerly Barmer).
  - **Capacity**: **9 MMTPA** integrated refinery and petrochemical complex (BS-VI compliant).
  - **Equity**: **74% HPCL : 26% Government of Rajasthan**.
  - **Approved Cost**: Revised to **₹79,459 crore** (April 2026 PIB release) with commercial commissioning in 2026.

### Card 9: Solar Energy Baseline (30 Sept 2026)
- **Q**: What is Rajasthan's installed solar capacity as of 30 September 2026, and what is the current policy target?
- **A**: Installed solar capacity reached **45,121.44 MW** (total installed renewable capacity = **51,595.50 MW**). The official state policy target is to achieve **125 GW of renewable capacity by FY 2029–30**.

### Card 10: Wind Potential & Amar Sagar Historical Milestone
- **Q**: What is the assessed wind energy potential of Rajasthan, and where was the state's first wind project commissioned?
- **A**: National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE) assesses potential as **127.75 GW at 120 m hub-height** and **284.2 GW at 150 m hub-height**. Rajasthan's first commercial wind project was commissioned at **Amar Sagar (Jaisalmer) on 10 April 1999**.
