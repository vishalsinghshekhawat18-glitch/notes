## 4. Diagnostic Active-Recall Flashcards

### Card 1: Official Irrigation Statistics (2023–24)
- **Q**: What are the official net and gross irrigated areas of Rajasthan, and which source commands the largest share?
- **A**: Net Irrigated Area = **95.47 lakh ha** (52.46% of Net Sown Area); Gross Irrigated Area = **129.60 lakh ha**. **Tube-wells** form the largest single source at **52.03%** of net irrigated area (open wells provide 21.95%, making total well irrigation **73.98%**; canals provide **23.19%**).

### Card 2: Groundwater Stress Dynamics (CGWB 2025)
- **Q**: State the annual extractable groundwater resource, total extraction, and stage of extraction for Rajasthan per CGWB 2025.
- **A**: Annual extractable resource = **11.62 BCM**; Annual extraction = **17.10 BCM**; Stage of groundwater extraction = **147.11%**. Out of 302 assessment units, **213 (70.53%) are classified as Over-exploited** and 23 as Critical.

### Card 3: IGNP Headworks & Feeder Architecture
- **Q**: Where does IGNP originate, what is the length of the Rajasthan Feeder, and does it permit irrigation?
- **A**: IGNP originates at **Harike Barrage** (Punjab) at the confluence of Satluj and Beas. The Rajasthan Feeder is **204 km** long (170 km in Punjab/Haryana + 34 km in Rajasthan up to Masitawali Head). It functions purely for water conveyance and permits **no direct irrigation**.

### Card 4: Modified PKC–ERCP Chronology & Status
- **Q**: What are the key agreements and official approval status of the Modified PKC–ERCP project?
- **A**: An MoU was signed on **28 January 2024**, followed by a formal Memorandum of Agreement (MoA) on **5 December 2024** between Rajasthan, MP, and the Centre. As of late 2025/2026, DPRs are under Central Water Commission appraisal; it has not yet been formally notified as an approved "National Project" with 90:10 funding.

### Card 5: Narmada Canal Project Irrigation Signature
- **Q**: What makes the Narmada Canal Project unique in Rajasthan's irrigation architecture?
- **A**: It is the first major irrigation project in Rajasthan with **mandatory sprinkler irrigation** across its entire command area of **~2.46 lakh ha** (serving 233 villages of Jalore and Barmer). Water enters from Gujarat's Sardar Sarovar Project near Silu village.

### Card 6: Gang Canal Engineering History
- **Q**: State the commissioning ruler, headworks, and inaugural date of the Gang Canal.
- **A**: Commissioned by **Maharaja Ganga Singh** of Bikaner. Originates at **Hussainiwala Headworks** on the Satluj river in Ferozepur, Punjab. Inaugurated on **26 October 1927** by Viceroy Lord Irwin. It has a total length of ~129 km (~17 km in Rajasthan).

### Card 7: Engineering Unit Trap — Bisalpur Dam
- **Q**: Why is it incorrect to state that Bisalpur Dam has a capacity of "315.50 RL"?
- **A**: **315.50 m RL (Reduced Level)** is an elevation/height coordinate denoting the dam's **Full Reservoir Level (FRL)** above mean sea level. Reservoir capacity is a volume measurement expressed in MCM (Million Cubic Metres) or TMC (Thousand Million Cubic Feet).

### Card 8: Traditional Water Harvesting — Khadin System
- **Q**: Describe the structure and agricultural mechanism of a traditional Khadin in Jaisalmer.
- **A**: A Khadin uses an earthen embankment (*Dhora*) built across a low-lying valley between rocky gravel mounds to capture monsoonal runoff. A masonry spillway (*Khadia*) releases excess water. The impounded water saturates the soil profile, allowing farmers (traditionally Paliwal Brahmins) to harvest high-yielding **Rabi crops without additional irrigation**.

### Card 9: Subterranean Moisture & The Kui / Beri
- **Q**: What is a Kui (or Beri) and which traditional water category does it extract?
- **A**: A Kui is a narrow, vertical shaft (1–1.5 m diameter, 10–15 m depth) dug by traditional well-craftsmen (*Chezwano*) down to an impermeable clay or gypsum stratum (*Khadia Patti*). It slowly collects perched subterranean moisture known as **Rejani Paani** drop by drop.

### Card 10: Three Classical Categories of Desert Water
- **Q**: Distinguish between Palar Paani, Patal Paani, and Rejani Paani.
- **A**: 
  1. **Palar Paani**: Direct rainwater harvested from rooftops, clean surfaces, or natural catchments.
  2. **Patal Paani**: Deep groundwater accessed through deep open wells and tube-wells (often brackish/saline in the desert).
  3. **Rejani Paani**: Rainwater that percolates below the sand but is trapped above an impermeable subterranean gypsum/clay layer, tapped via Kuis.
