## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Exam Traps

1. **UNESCO Hill Forts Composition**:
   - *Trap:* Selecting Mehrangarh (Jodhpur) or Junagarh (Bikaner) as UNESCO World Heritage sites.
   - *Fact:* Inscribed in June 2013 under Criteria (ii) & (iii): **Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Gagron, Jaisalmer, Ranthambore, and Amber** (*चीकू गाजर आम*). Neither Mehrangarh nor Junagarh is on the list.
2. **Vijay Stambha vs. Jain Kirti Stambha Dimensions & Deities**:
   - *Trap:* Confusing the two towers of Chittorgarh.
   - *Fact:* **Vijay Stambha** has **9 storeys (approx. 122 feet)**, built by Maharana Kumbha (1440–48), dedicated to **Lord Vishnu**, architect Jaita. **Jain Kirti Stambha** has **7 storeys (approx. 75 feet)**, built in the 12th century by Jain merchant Jija Bhagerwala, dedicated to **Lord Adinatha (Rishabhadeva)**.
3. **The "Eye of Mewar" (*Mewar ki Aankh*)**:
   - *Trap:* Equating the whole Kumbhalgarh fort with the "Eye of Mewar".
   - *Fact:* Refers specifically to the uppermost inner citadel: **Katargarh (कटारगढ़)**, where Maharana Kumbha resided and was assassinated, and where Maharana Pratap was born (Badal Mahal).
4. **Water Fort (Jal Durg) Pure Archetypes**:
   - *Trap:* Confusing Gagron with hill forts.
   - *Fact:* **Gagron Fort (Jhalawar)** is situated at the confluence of the **Ahu and Kali Sindh** rivers. The popular claim that it was built "without foundation trenches" is an unverified local engineering claim, though its rocky cliff setting is historically grounded.
5. **Lohagarh Defense & "Never Captured" Myth**:
   - *Trap:* Believing Lohagarh Fort was never captured in history.
   - *Fact:* **Lohagarh Fort (Bharatpur)** famously repulsed British General Lord Lake’s five sieges in 1805 due to its shock-absorbing mud ramparts and Sujan Ganga moat. However, it was **breached and captured by the British under Lord Combermere in January 1826**.
6. **Jaisalmer Masonry Nuance**:
   - *Trap:* Stating Jaisalmer Fort contains zero mortar anywhere.
   - *Fact:* While yellow sandstone dry interlocking masonry was extensively used in outer defensive ramparts, UNESCO site-management documentation confirms that **mortar was used in major regal and residential buildings** inside the fort.
7. **"Zameen ka Zeewar" vs. "Chidiyatunk"**:
   - *Trap:* Confusing Junagarh with Mehrangarh.
   - *Fact:* **Junagarh (Bikaner)** is called **Zameen ka Zeewar** (Jewel of the Earth) because it is built on flat ground (*Bhu-Durg*). **Mehrangarh (Jodhpur)** is built on the steep **Chidiyatunk hill** and shaped like a peacock (*Mayuradhwaja*).
8. **The Twin Taragarhs Trap**:
   - *Trap:* Assuming there is only one Taragarh in Rajasthan.
   - *Fact:* **Taragarh (Ajmer)** is "Garh Beetli" / "Gibraltar of Rajasthan", renovated by Prithviraj Sisodia and named after his wife Tara Bai. **Taragarh (Bundi)** is the star-shaped fort built in 1354 by Rao Bar Singh, housing the *Garbh Gunjan* cannon and *Chitrashala* frescoes.
9. **Chittorgarh's Sāka Historiography**:
   - *Trap:* Treating the Rani Padmini Jauhar and the 1535 Humayun Rakhi as contemporary archival facts.
   - *Fact:* Contemporary chronicler **Amir Khusrau** (*Khazain-ul-Futuh*) recorded the 1303 siege, capture, and renaming to Khizrabad without mentioning Padmini; the Queen Padmini narrative is first attested in **Malik Muhammad Jayasi’s *Padmavat* (c. 1540 CE)**. The Karnavati Rakhi episode is a beloved narrative in later chronicles (*Mirat-i-Sikandari*, khyats).
10. **Kumbhalgarh Rampart Measurement**:
    - *Trap:* Stating Kumbhalgarh is officially verified as the "second longest wall on Earth after China".
    - *Fact:* The **36 km perimeter wall** is widely cited in Rajasthan Tourism literature as the longest fortress wall in India. Absolute global rankings are qualified by comparative archaeology (e.g., the 195 km Gorgan Wall in Iran).
