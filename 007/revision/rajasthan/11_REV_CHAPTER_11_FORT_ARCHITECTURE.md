## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Exam Traps

1. **UNESCO Hill Forts Composition**:
   - *Trap:* Selecting Mehrangarh (Jodhpur) or Junagarh (Bikaner) as UNESCO World Heritage sites.
   - *Fact:* Inscribed in June 2013: **Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Gagron, Jaisalmer, Ranthambore, and Amber** (*चीकू गाजर आम*). Neither Mehrangarh nor Junagarh is on the list.
2. **Vijay Stambha vs. Jain Kirti Stambha Dimensions & Deities**:
   - *Trap:* Confusing the two towers of Chittorgarh.
   - *Fact:* **Vijay Stambha** has **9 storeys (122 feet)**, built by Maharana Kumbha (1440–48), dedicated to **Lord Vishnu**, architect Jaita. **Jain Kirti Stambha** has **7 storeys (75 feet)**, built in the 12th century by Jain merchant Jija Bhagerwala, dedicated to **Lord Adinatha (Rishabhadeva)**.
3. **The "Eye of Mewar" (*Mewar ki Aankh*)**:
   - *Trap:* Equating the whole Kumbhalgarh fort with the "Eye of Mewar".
   - *Fact:* Refers specifically to the uppermost inner citadel: **Katargarh (कटारगढ़)**, where Maharana Kumbha resided and was assassinated, and where Maharana Pratap was born (Badal Mahal).
4. **Water Fort (Jal Durg) Pure Archetypes**:
   - *Trap:* Confusing Gagron with hill forts.
   - *Fact:* **Gagron Fort (Jhalawar)** is situated at the confluence of the **Ahu and Kali Sindh** rivers and built directly on living bedrock **without foundation trenches**. The other premier Jal Durg is **Bhainsrorgarh Fort (Chittorgarh)** at the confluence of the **Chambal and Bamni** rivers.
5. **Mud Rampart Defense Mechanics**:
   - *Trap:* Assuming Lohagarh was breached by British artillery.
   - *Fact:* **Lohagarh Fort (Bharatpur)** was built with outer walls of **dense alluvial clay/mud** encasing stone. In 1805, Lord Lake’s artillery cannonballs sank into the mud without shattering stone, forcing the British to lift the siege in defeat.
6. **Mortarless Masonry Trap**:
   - *Trap:* Believing Jaisalmer Fort was built with lime mortar.
   - *Fact:* **Jaisalmer Fort** was constructed using **zero mortar, lime, or cement**. Massive yellow sandstone blocks were carved with **tongue-and-groove interlocking dry joints** held by gravity.
7. **"Zameen ka Zeewar" vs. "Chidiyatunk"**:
   - *Trap:* Confusing Junagarh with Mehrangarh.
   - *Fact:* **Junagarh (Bikaner)** is called **Zameen ka Zeewar** (Jewel of the Earth) because it is built on flat ground (*Bhu-Durg*). **Mehrangarh (Jodhpur)** is built on the steep **Chidiyatunk hill** and shaped like a peacock (*Mayuradhwaja*).
8. **The Twin Taragarhs Trap**:
   - *Trap:* Assuming there is only one Taragarh in Rajasthan.
   - *Fact:* **Taragarh (Ajmer)** is "Garh Beetli" / "Gibraltar of Rajasthan", renovated by Prithviraj Sisodia and named after his wife Tara Bai. **Taragarh (Bundi)** is the star-shaped fort built in 1354 by Rao Bar Singh, housing the *Garbh Gunjan* cannon and *Chitrashala* frescoes.
9. **The Unprecedented Joint Jauhar at Bhatner (1398 CE)**:
   - *Trap:* Thinking only Hindu women performed Jauhar.
   - *Fact:* During Sultan Timur’s siege of **Bhatner Fort (1398 CE)**, **both Hindu and Muslim women performed collective Jauhar**, as documented in Timur’s autobiography *Tuzuk-i-Timuri*.
10. **The Three Sākas of Chittorgarh Chronology**:
    - *Trap:* Mixing up the rulers and adversaries.
    - *Fact:*
      - **1st (1303):** Rawal Ratan Singh vs. Alauddin Khalji (Rani Padmini).
      - **2nd (1535):** Rawat Bagh Singh vs. Bahadur Shah of Gujarat (Rani Karnavati).
      - **3rd (1568):** Jaimal Rathore & Patta Sisodia vs. Akbar (Rani Phool Kanwar).
