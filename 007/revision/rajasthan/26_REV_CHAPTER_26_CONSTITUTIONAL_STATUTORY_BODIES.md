## 1. 60-Second Concept Retrieval Skeleton

```text
[CONSTITUTIONAL BODIES OF RAJASTHAN]:
 1. RPSC (Articles 315–323):
    • Established: Ordinance 16 August 1949; Gazette 20 August 1949; Formal inauguration 22 December 1949
    • HQ: Ghooghra Ghati, Ajmer (shifted from Jaipur in 1958 per Satyanarayan Rao Committee)
    • Appointment: GOVERNOR (Art. 316(1)); Tenure: 6 YEARS / 62 YEARS of age (41st Amendment raised from 60 to 62)
    • Composition Requirement: As nearly as may be, 50% must have 10+ years government service (Art. 316(1) proviso)
    • CRITICAL REMOVAL PROTOCOL (Art. 317):
      - Removed ONLY BY THE PRESIDENT on grounds of misbehaviour after inquiry and report by the SUPREME COURT (Art. 317(1))
      - Suspension Authority: The PRESIDENT may suspend a member pending the Supreme Court reference inquiry (Art. 317(2); NOT the Governor!)
 2. State Election Commission (SEC, Arts. 243K & 243ZA):
    • Formed: July 1994 (Single-member commission under State Election Commissioner)
    • Mandate: Conducts elections to Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) ONLY (Parliament & Assembly by ECI!)
    • Tenure: 5 YEARS / 65 YEARS of age (Rajasthan SEC Conditions of Service Rules)
    • Removal: In like manner and on like grounds as a High Court Judge (Presidential order post-Parliamentary address; NOT Governor dismissal!)
 3. State Finance Commission (SFC, Arts. 243-I & 243-Y):
    • Constituted every 5 years by the Governor to review financial position of PRIs and ULBs and recommend tax devolution
    • Sixth SFC (Chair: Pradyuman Singh): Recommended 7.25% of State net own tax revenue; Government ACCEPTED 6.75% (2020–24) and 7.0% (2024–25)
    • Seventh SFC: Interim report for 2025–26 under review
                                         │
[STATUTORY COMMISSIONS OF RAJASTHAN]:
 4. Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission (RSHRC, PHRA 1993, Sec. 21–29):
    • Notified 18 Jan 1999; fully operational 23 March 2000; HQ: Jaipur
    • Composition (Post-2019 Amendment): 1 Chairperson (Retd. Chief Justice OR Judge of High Court) + 2 Members (1 Judicial + 1 HR Expert)
    • Selection Committee (4-Member): Chief Minister (Chair), Home Minister, Assembly Speaker, Leader of Opposition
    • Appointment: Governor; Tenure: 3 YEARS / 70 YEARS of age (eligible for reappointment subject to age limit)
    • Removal: By the PRESIDENT under PHRA Section 23 (Supreme Court reference for misbehaviour/incapacity; or direct for insolvency/infirmity/conviction)
    • Limitation: Cannot inquire into events after 1 YEAR from the date of occurrence (Sec. 36(2)); purely recommendatory
 5. Rajasthan State Information Commission (RSIC, RTI Act 2005, Sec. 15–20):
    • Constituted: 18 April 2006; HQ: Jaipur
    • Composition: State Chief Information Commissioner (SCIC) + up to 10 State Information Commissioners (SICs)
    • Selection Committee (3-Member): Chief Minister (Chair), Leader of Opposition, 1 Cabinet Minister nominated by CM
    • Tenure: 3 YEARS / 65 YEARS (2019 Central Rules); AGGREGATE TENURE (if IC promoted to SCIC) CAPPED AT 5 YEARS (Sec. 16(2))
    • Penalties: Sec. 20 mandates ₹250/day up to max ₹25,000; Compensation under Sec. 19(8)(b) (NOT Sec. 20!)
    • Removal: By the GOVERNOR on reference to the Supreme Court for misbehaviour/incapacity (Sec. 17)
 6. Lokayukta of Rajasthan (Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973):
    • Act passed 1973 (Operational 28 August 1973); First Lokayukta: Justice I.D. Dua; Only Up-Lokayukta: K.P.U. Menon (1973–74)
    • Appointment: Governor after mandatory consultation with Chief Justice of High Court + Leader of Opposition
    • Tenure Sequence: Originally 5 years ➔ Raised to 8 years (2018) ➔ ROLLED BACK TO 5 YEARS / 65 YEARS (2019)
    • JURISDICTION & COMPETENT AUTHORITY:
      - Competent Authority for Ministers/Secretaries: CHIEF MINISTER (Sec. 2(c); NOT the Governor!)
      - Exclusions from Jurisdiction: Chief Minister (expressly excluded by definition), MLAs, High Court Judges, RPSC, RVC, Sarpanch/Ward Panch
    • Limitation: Cannot investigate complaints older than 5 YEARS from date of alleged action (Sec. 8(3))
 7. Rajasthan State Commission for Women (RSCW, Act of 1999):
    • Act published 28 April 1999; operational 15 May 1999; First Chair: Kanta Kathuria
    • Composition: Chairperson + up to 4 Members (including Member-Secretary); Tenure: 3 YEARS; appointed by State Government
```

## 3. Top 25 Corrected Prelims Traps & Disputed Points

| # | High-Risk Trap / Textbook Absolute | Verified Epistemic Correction & Exam Benchmark |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | *"The Governor of Rajasthan can remove an RPSC member on grounds of misbehaviour."* | **Trap**: Constitutional violation. Under **Article 317(1)**, the Chairperson or any member of a State PSC can be **removed ONLY by the President of India**, following an inquiry and report by the Supreme Court. |
| **2** | *"The Governor can suspend an RPSC member while the Supreme Court inquiry is pending."* | **Trap**: Authority trap. Under **Article 317(2)**, the **PRESIDENT** possesses the constitutional power to suspend an RPSC member in respect of whom a reference has been made to the Supreme Court. |
| **3** | *"RPSC was formally established by ordinance on 22 December 1949."* | **Trap**: Chronology conflation. The establishing Ordinance was promulgated on **16 August 1949** and published in the Gazette on **20 August 1949**. The date **22 December 1949** marks the Commission's formal inauguration/commencement. |
| **4** | *"RPSC has an inflexible statutory quota requiring exactly 50% members to be non-officials."* | **Trap**: Linguistic precision. Article 316(1) proviso specifies that **"as nearly as may be one-half"** of members must have held office for at least ten years under the Government. It is an administrative qualification guideline, not an inflexible quota. |
| **5** | *"State Election Commission of Rajasthan conducts elections for the State Legislative Assembly."* | **Trap**: Institutional jurisdiction. The **Election Commission of India (ECI)** conducts elections to Parliament, State Legislative Assemblies, and the offices of President/Vice-President. The **SEC conducts elections to Panchayats and Municipalities ONLY** (Arts. 243K & 243ZA). |
| **6** | *"The Governor can remove the State Election Commissioner at pleasure."* | **Trap**: Constitutional protection. Under Article 243K(2), the State Election Commissioner **cannot be removed from office except in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of a High Court** (requiring a 2/3rd parliamentary address; the Governor has no removal power). |
| **7** | *"The Sixth State Finance Commission's recommended 7.25% tax devolution was implemented in full by Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Recommendation vs Implementation. The 6th SFC (Pradyuman Singh) recommended 7.25% of net own tax revenue, but the state government accepted and implemented **6.75% for 2020–24 and 7.0% for 2024–25**. |
| **8** | *"The Sixth State Finance Commission is the latest commission currently operating in Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Currency update. The **Seventh State Finance Commission (7th SFC)** has been constituted, with its interim report covering 2025–26 submitted to the Finance Department. |
| **9** | *"RSHRC Chairperson must strictly be a retired Chief Justice of a High Court."* | **Trap**: Outdated requirement. Under the **Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Act, 2019**, the Chairperson can be a person who has been a **Chief Justice OR a Judge of a High Court**. |
| **10** | *"RSHRC selection committee includes the Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Council."* | **Trap**: Rajasthan legislative structure. In states with a Legislative Council, its Chairman and LoP are included. But Rajasthan is strictly **Unicameral**, so the RSHRC selection committee consists of **4 members only**: CM, Speaker, Home Minister, and LoP in the Legislative Assembly. |
| **11** | *"The Governor removes an RSHRC member on grounds of proved misbehaviour."* | **Trap**: Section 23 PHRA error. RSHRC members are **removed by the PRESIDENT**, not the Governor, following a Supreme Court inquiry on a presidential reference. |
| **12** | *"RSHRC can investigate human rights violations that took place 3 years ago."* | **Trap**: Statutory bar of limitation. Under **Section 36(2) of PHRA, 1993**, the Commission is strictly barred from inquiring into any matter after the expiry of **one year from the date on which the act was committed**. |
| **13** | *"State Information Commissioners serve for an aggregate term of 3 years only, even if promoted to SCIC."* | **Trap**: Tenure combination rule. Under Section 16(2) of the RTI Act, an Information Commissioner appointed as SCIC can hold office for an **aggregate period not exceeding 5 years** (subject to age 65), even though individual terms are set at 3 years under Central Rules. |
| **14** | *"Compensation to an RTI applicant is awarded by RSIC under Section 20 of the RTI Act."* | **Trap**: Section confusion. Section 20 deals strictly with **penalties (₹250/day up to ₹25,000)** and disciplinary recommendations. Award of **compensation to the complainant is made under Section 19(8)(b)**. |
| **15** | *"State Information Commissioners are removed by the President of India."* | **Trap**: Jurisdiction distinction. Unlike RPSC and RSHRC, the State Chief Information Commissioner and SICs are **removed by the GOVERNOR** under Section 17 of the RTI Act on a reference to and report by the Supreme Court. |
| **16** | *"The Governor is the Competent Authority for Ministers under the Rajasthan Lokayukta Act."* | **Trap**: Critical section error. Under **Section 2(c) of the Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973**, the **CHIEF MINISTER is the Competent Authority in the case of a Minister or Secretary** (the Governor is Competent Authority only for the Chief Minister, who is excluded from the Act!). |
| **17** | *"The Chief Minister of Rajasthan falls within the investigative jurisdiction of the Lokayukta."* | **Trap**: Statutory exemption. Under Section 2(g) of the Rajasthan Lokayukta Act, the definition of 'Minister' **expressly excludes the Chief Minister**. The Lokayukta has zero jurisdiction over the CM. |
| **18** | *"Members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs) can be investigated by the Lokayukta for corruption."* | **Trap**: Statutory exclusion. MLAs are **NOT included** within the positive definition of 'public servant' under the Rajasthan Lokayukta Act, 1973. |
| **19** | *"The tenure of the Rajasthan Lokayukta is permanently 8 years."* | **Trap**: Legislative history. The tenure was raised from 5 to 8 years in March 2018, but the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly amended the Act in **July 2019 to ROLL BACK the tenure to 5 years / 65 years of age**. |
| **20** | *"A citizen can lodge a complaint with the Lokayukta regarding an event that took place 7 years ago."* | **Trap**: Limitation bar. Under **Section 8(3) of the Act**, the Lokayukta shall not conduct an investigation into any complaint made after the expiry of **five years from the date on which the action complained against is alleged to have taken place**. |
| **21** | *"Lokayukta has the legal power to sentence a corrupt official to imprisonment."* | **Trap**: Investigatory boundary. The Lokayukta is an **investigative and recommendatory authority**, NOT a criminal trial court. It submits reports and recommendations to the Competent Authority, which then initiates prosecution. |
| **22** | *"Rajasthan State Commission for Women was established under a Central Government Act."* | **Trap**: State statute. It was created under a state enactment: the **Rajasthan State Commission for Women Act, 1999 (Act No. 17 of 1999)**, published on 28 April 1999. |
| **23** | *"The Chairperson of Rajasthan State Commission for Women is appointed by the Governor."* | **Trap**: Executive nomination. Under Section 3(3) of the RSCW Act, 1999, the Chairperson and members are **nominated/appointed by the STATE GOVERNMENT**, not by the Governor. |
| **24** | *"The Chairperson of the State Commission for Women has a fixed retirement age of 65 years."* | **Trap**: Silence in statute. The RSCW Act, 1999 prescribes a **term of 3 years** from the date of assumption of office; it does **not** specify a fixed age bar such as 62, 65, or 70. |
| **25** | *"State Election Commission is exempt from all judicial review under Articles 243-O and 243-ZG."* | **Trap**: Scope of judicial review. While Arts. 243-O and 243-ZG bar interference in delimitation and require election petitions for challenging results, the **constitutional High Court and Supreme Court retain powers of judicial review over arbitrary or unconstitutional orders** under Articles 226 and 136. |

## 4. Diagnostic Active-Recall Flashcards

### Card 1: RPSC Removal & Suspension Constitutional Protocol (Art. 317)
- **Q**: Who has the constitutional authority to remove and suspend an RPSC member, and what is the required procedure?
- **A**: 
  - **Removal**: ONLY by the **President of India** under Article 317(1) on grounds of misbehaviour, following an inquiry and report by the Supreme Court on a presidential reference.
  - **Suspension**: The **President** may suspend a member pending the Supreme Court reference inquiry under Article 317(2). (The Governor possesses no constitutional power to suspend an RPSC member).

### Card 2: RPSC Inception Dates & Headquarters Evolution
- **Q**: State the Ordinance date, Gazette date, formal inauguration date, and headquarters relocation of RPSC.
- **A**: 
  - Ordinance promulgated: **16 August 1949**.
  - Gazette publication: **20 August 1949**.
  - Formal inauguration: **22 December 1949**.
  - Headquarters: Shifted from **Jaipur to Ghooghra Ghati, Ajmer in 1958** pursuant to the P. Satyanarayan Rao Committee recommendations.

### Card 3: State Election Commission Mandate & Protection (Art. 243K)
- **Q**: What elections are conducted by the SEC, and how is the State Election Commissioner removed?
- **A**: The SEC conducts elections to **Panchayati Raj Institutions and Urban Local Bodies ONLY** (Assembly and Parliament are conducted by ECI). Under Article 243K(2), the Commissioner **cannot be removed except in like manner and on like grounds as a High Court Judge** (Presidential order post-parliamentary 2/3rd majority).

### Card 4: Sixth State Finance Commission Deviations
- **Q**: Who chaired the Sixth SFC, what was its recommended tax devolution percentage, and what was actually accepted by the Government?
- **A**: Chaired by **Pradyuman Singh**. The Commission recommended **7.25%** of the state's net own tax revenue. The state government accepted and implemented **6.75% for 2020–2024 and 7.0% for 2024–2025**.

### Card 5: RSHRC Post-2019 Composition & Selection Committee
- **Q**: Detail the statutory composition and the 4-member selection committee of RSHRC under the amended PHRA.
- **A**: 
  - **Composition**: **1 Chairperson** (Retd. Chief Justice OR Judge of a High Court) + **2 Members** (1 Judicial member + 1 Human Rights expert).
  - **Selection Committee (4 members)**: Chief Minister (Chairperson), Home Minister, Assembly Speaker, and Leader of Opposition in the Assembly.

### Card 6: RSHRC Removal & Bar of Limitation
- **Q**: Who removes RSHRC members, and what is the statutory bar of limitation under Section 36(2)?
- **A**: 
  - **Removal**: By the **President of India** under Section 23 of PHRA (following a Supreme Court inquiry for misbehaviour/incapacity).
  - **Limitation**: The Commission **cannot inquire into any matter after the expiry of 1 year** from the date on which the human rights violation occurred.

### Card 7: RSIC Tenure Limits & Penalty vs Compensation Sections
- **Q**: What are the individual and aggregate tenure caps for Information Commissioners, and which sections govern penalty vs compensation?
- **A**: 
  - **Tenure**: 3 years / age 65 (2019 Rules); if an IC is appointed as SCIC, the **aggregate term is capped at 5 years** (Section 16(2)).
  - **Penalty**: Imposed under **Section 20** (₹250/day up to max ₹25,000).
  - **Compensation**: Awarded to the complainant under **Section 19(8)(b)** (NOT Section 20).

### Card 8: Lokayukta Competent Authority Trap (Section 2(c))
- **Q**: Who is the Competent Authority for a Minister or Secretary under the Rajasthan Lokayukta Act, 1973?
- **A**: The **Chief Minister** is the Competent Authority for a Minister or Secretary under Section 2(c) of the Act. (The Governor is NOT the competent authority for state ministers).

### Card 9: Lokayukta Jurisdictional Exclusions & 2019 Tenure Rollback
- **Q**: Name the key public officials excluded from Lokayukta jurisdiction, and state the current tenure rule.
- **A**: 
  - **Excluded Officials**: **Chief Minister**, MLAs, High Court Judges, RPSC Chairman/Members, Rajasthan Vigilance Commission, and Sarpanch/Ward Panch.
  - **Tenure**: **5 years or 65 years of age** (amended in July 2019 to roll back the 2018 increase from 8 years to 5 years).

### Card 10: Rajasthan State Commission for Women Coordinates
- **Q**: When was the RSCW Act published, who appoints the Chairperson, and what is the tenure?
- **A**: Published in the Gazette on **28 April 1999** (operational 15 May 1999). The Chairperson is **appointed/nominated by the State Government** (not the Governor) for a **term of 3 years**.
