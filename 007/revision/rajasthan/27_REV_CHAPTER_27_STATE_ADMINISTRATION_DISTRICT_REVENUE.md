## 3. Top 25 Corrected Prelims Traps & Disputed Points

| # | High-Risk Trap / Textbook Absolute | Verified Epistemic Correction & Exam Benchmark |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | *"Rajasthan currently has 50 districts and 10 administrative divisions."* | **Trap**: Outdated administrative map. Following government review, the Rajasthan State Portal documents **41 districts**, and the RAJ MASTERS portal records **7 administrative divisions** (the 3 divisions created in 2023 were formally cancelled in Dec 2024). |
| **2** | *"Sikar, Pali, and Banswara are current administrative divisions of Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Currency error. While Sikar, Pali, and Banswara were announced as divisional headquarters in August 2023, the government **cancelled these three divisions in December 2024**, reverting to the 7-division setup. |
| **3** | *"Sudhansh Pant is the Chief Secretary of Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Outdated incumbent. Official CMO Rajasthan service records show **V. Srinivas assumed charge as Chief Secretary of Rajasthan in November 2025**. Always recheck before the exam. |
| **4** | *"The Chief Secretary holds office during the personal pleasure of the Chief Minister under the Constitution."* | **Trap**: Constitutional misnomer. The Chief Secretary is a senior civil-service posting governed by All India Services (IAS) cadre rules; there is no specific constitutional article creating the post or defining tenure. |
| **5** | *"B.S. Mehta served as Chief Secretary for more than seven full years."* | **Trap**: Arithmetical error. Bhagwat Singh Mehta served as Chief Secretary from **May 1958 to October 1964**, which equals approximately **6 years and 5 months** (not "over 7 years"). |
| **6** | *"Divisional Commissioners were abolished in 1962 and revived on 15 August 1987."* | **Trap**: Date error. Divisional Commissioners were abolished in 1962 by Mohan Lal Sukhadia and revived by the Harideo Joshi government on **26 January 1987** (Republic Day), creating Ajmer as the 6th division. |
| **7** | *"The Divisional Commissioner is the direct appellate authority over all orders of the Revenue Appellate Authority (RAA)."* | **Trap**: Statutory confusion. The RAA is a distinct statutory judicial appellate authority constituted under Section 20-A of the Land Revenue Act; appeals from RAA orders generally lie to the **Board of Revenue**, not the Divisional Commissioner. |
| **8** | *"The District Collector has the constitutional power to place police officers under suspension."* | **Trap**: Cadre boundary error. The District Magistrate coordinates district law and order, but operational police discipline and suspension lie strictly within the internal police hierarchy and Home Department under the Police Act. |
| **9** | *"The District Magistrate's order under Section 163 BNSS is legally defined as 'Curfew'."* | **Trap**: Colloquial vs Legal. Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (formerly Sec. 144 CrPC) is officially titled **"Power to issue order in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger"**; "curfew" is an informal administrative term. |
| **10** | *"BNSS Sections 126–129 govern the dispersal of unlawful assemblies."* | **Trap**: Section mapping error. **Sections 126–129 BNSS govern preventive security proceedings** (bonds for keeping the peace and good behaviour); provisions for the dispersal of assemblies begin at **Section 148 BNSS**. |
| **11** | *"Every District Collector is automatically the Returning Officer for all parliamentary and assembly seats in the district."* | **Trap**: Statutory distinction. Under Section 13AA of the RP Act 1950, the Collector is normally the **District Election Officer (DEO)**. A **Returning Officer (RO)** is a separate statutory office appointed by ECI for a specific constituency under Section 21 of the RP Act 1951. |
| **12** | *"Every Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO) is an ex-officio Sub-Registrar under the Registration Act."* | **Trap**: Delegation trap. Sub-Registrars for registering property deeds are specifically appointed/notified by the State Government (frequently Tehsildars/Naib-Tehsildars or dedicated Sub-Registrars, not inherent in the SDO post). |
| **13** | *"SDO is the trial court for all revenue matters in the state without exception."* | **Trap**: Overbroad claim. The SDO is the principal original court for **agricultural tenancy suits under the Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955**, but non-judicial revenue, demarcation, and recovery matters originate before Tehsildars and Collectors. |
| **14** | *"The Jamabandi (Record of Rights) in Rajasthan is prepared every five years."* | **Trap**: Critical rule error. Under the **Rajasthan Land Revenue (Land Records) Rules, 1957 (Rule 148/153)**, the Jamabandi is prepared **QUADRENNIALLY (EVERY FOUR YEARS)** in a sanctioned rolling programme. |
| **15** | *"Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid Girdawari strictly commence on 1 Oct, 1 Feb, and 1 May statewide."* | **Trap**: Rigid date trap. While seasonal inspection rules define harvest periods, operational field dates are governed by departmental programmes and seasonal notifications issued by the Board of Revenue. |
| **16** | *"A Jamabandi entry confers absolute, indefeasible legal title to land in Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Evidentiary nature. A Jamabandi entry carries a **rebuttable statutory presumption of truth** under Section 125 of the Land Revenue Act; it is a fiscal record of rights, NOT a guaranteed conclusive title deed. |
| **17** | *"Sanctioning a Mutation (Namantaran) legally transfers property ownership title."* | **Trap**: Core land law trap. A mutation is purely an **administrative update of fiscal revenue records** to track possession and fiscal liability; the Supreme Court has repeatedly held that mutation **neither creates nor extinguishes legal title**. |
| **18** | *"The Board of Revenue for Rajasthan has a fixed total membership of exactly 20 members."* | **Trap**: Statute reading. Section 4(2) of the Land Revenue Act, 1956 specifies that the Board consists of a Chairman and **not fewer than 3 and not more than 20 OTHER members**. Total statutory strength can reach up to 21 (Chairman + 20). |
| **19** | *"The Board of Revenue is situated at Jaipur."* | **Trap**: Location error. The permanent headquarters of the Board of Revenue for Rajasthan is located at **Ajmer** (established under the 1949 Ordinance). |
| **20** | *"The Board of Revenue conducts the decennial Population Census of Rajasthan."* | **Trap**: Constitutional jurisdiction. The Population Census is an exclusive Union subject (Entry 69, List I) conducted by the Registrar General of India under the Census Act, 1948, NOT the Board of Revenue. |
| **21** | *"Revenue appeals follow a single universal line: Tehsildar ➔ SDO ➔ Collector ➔ RAA ➔ Commissioner ➔ Board."* | **Trap**: Hierarchy oversimplification. Revenue appellate routes depend strictly on the statute (Land Revenue Act vs Tenancy Act) and the nature of the order; many orders jump directly from SDO/Collector to the RAA or the Board of Revenue. |
| **22** | *"The Secretariat is a line agency and the Directorate is a staff agency."* | **Trap**: Administrative science inversion. The **Secretariat is a STAFF agency** (advisory, policy-making, legislative drafting); the **Directorate is a LINE agency** (executive execution, field operations, delivery). |
| **23** | *"Every Revenue Inspector (Girdawar) supervises exactly 4 Patwar circles."* | **Trap**: Spurious precision. The number of Patwar circles per Inspector Land Revenue (ILR) circle varies according to geographic area, workload, and departmental sanction orders. |
| **24** | *"Under the Land Revenue Act, the District Magistrate can alter district boundaries on his own authority."* | **Trap**: Statutory authority. Under **Section 16 of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956**, the power to create, abolish, or alter divisions, districts, sub-divisions, and tehsils is vested **exclusively in the STATE GOVERNMENT** by Gazette notification. |
| **25** | *"A Patwari is a gazetted state civil servant."* | **Trap**: Service cadre. The Patwari is a **subordinate non-gazetted grassroots revenue official**, recruited under the Rajasthan Subordinate Accounts/Land Revenue Service rules. |

## 4. Diagnostic Active-Recall Flashcards

### Card 1: Official Administrative Divisions of Rajasthan (Current 2026)
- **Q**: How many districts and administrative divisions currently exist in Rajasthan, and what happened to the 2023 divisions?
- **A**: Rajasthan currently has **41 districts and 7 divisions** (Ajmer, Bharatpur, Bikaner, Jaipur, Jodhpur, Kota, Udaipur). The three divisions established in 2023 (Sikar, Pali, Banswara) were **formally cancelled in December 2024**, restoring the seven-division framework.

### Card 2: Current Chief Secretary (October 2026 Snapshot)
- **Q**: Who is the Chief Secretary of Rajasthan as of October 2026?
- **A**: **V. Srinivas**, who assumed office as Chief Secretary in **November 2025** (recorded on the official CMO Rajasthan portal).

### Card 3: Secretariat vs Directorate Distinction
- **Q**: Distinguish the functional roles of the State Secretariat and the State Directorate.
- **A**: 
  - **State Secretariat**: A **staff agency** headed administratively by the Chief Secretary, responsible for policy formulation, legislative drafting, budget allocation, and inter-departmental coordination.
  - **State Directorate**: A **line agency** headed by a Director/Commissioner, responsible for field execution, operational service delivery, and departmental management.

### Card 4: Historical Evolution of Divisional Administration
- **Q**: Trace the major historical milestones of divisional administration in Rajasthan (1949, 1962, 1987, 2005).
- **A**: 
  - **1949**: 5 original divisions created (Jaipur, Jodhpur, Bikaner, Kota, Udaipur).
  - **1962**: Abolished by Mohan Lal Sukhadia.
  - **26 January 1987**: Revived by Harideo Joshi; **Ajmer** added as 6th division.
  - **4 June 2005**: **Bharatpur** created as 7th division.

### Card 5: District Collector vs District Magistrate vs DEO
- **Q**: Differentiate the roles of District Collector, District Magistrate, and District Election Officer.
- **A**: 
  - **District Collector**: Revenue head under Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956; custodian of state lands, recovery of public dues.
  - **District Magistrate (DM)**: Executive magistrate under BNSS, 2023; coordinates public order, issues urgent preventive orders (Sec. 163 BNSS).
  - **District Election Officer (DEO)**: Statutory officer under Section 13AA of the RP Act 1950, superintending elections across all constituencies in the district (distinct from an RO appointed for a single constituency).

### Card 6: BNSS Section 163 & Preventive Proceedings
- **Q**: What are the statutory titles and scopes of Section 163 and Sections 126–129 of BNSS, 2023?
- **A**: 
  - **Section 163 BNSS** (formerly Sec. 144 CrPC): Orders in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger (colloquially called "curfew").
  - **Sections 126–129 BNSS** (formerly Sec. 107–110 CrPC): Preventive security proceedings requiring bonds for keeping the peace and good behaviour.

### Card 7: Jamabandi Revision Cycle (Land Records Rules, 1957)
- **Q**: What is the Jamabandi, and how frequently is it prepared in Rajasthan?
- **A**: The Jamabandi is the official **Record of Rights (*Khatauni*)** for agricultural land, detailing land ownership, tenancy, field numbers, and revenue assessment. Under the Rajasthan Land Revenue (Land Records) Rules, 1957, it is prepared **QUADRENNIALLY (EVERY FOUR YEARS)** in a rolling programme.

### Card 8: Legal Nature of Mutation (*Namantaran*)
- **Q**: Does sanctioning a mutation (*Namantaran*) confer or extinguish legal title to land?
- **A**: **NO**. The Supreme Court has consistently held that a mutation is purely an administrative entry in revenue records for tax and possession tracking; it **does NOT create or extinguish legal ownership title**.

### Card 9: Board of Revenue for Rajasthan (Statutory Coordinates)
- **Q**: Where is the Board of Revenue located, under what section is it constituted, and what is its statutory strength?
- **A**: Located at **Ajmer**. Constituted under **Section 4 of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956**. Its statutory composition under Section 4(2) is a **Chairman plus not fewer than 3 and not more than 20 other members** appointed by the State Government.

### Card 10: SDO as Original Tenancy Court
- **Q**: What is the primary judicial role of the Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO) in agricultural land disputes?
- **A**: Under the **Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955**, the SDO functions as the **principal court of original jurisdiction** for trying substantive tenancy suits, including suits for declaration of khatedari rights, division/partition of holdings, and ejectment of trespassers.
