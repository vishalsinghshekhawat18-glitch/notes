## 3. The CGWB 14-Basin Planning Framework

The Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) groups Rajasthan's catchments into **14 broad categories**. Note that Category 14 is a grouped classification for unassigned desert catchments and outside-basin nallahs, not a single named river:

1. **Luni Basin**: Largest named basin by area (~69,302 km² per older CGWB tables); encompasses arid western slopes.
2. **Banas Basin**: Largest basin situated wholly within Rajasthan (~47,060 km²).
3. **Chambal Basin**: Major perennial system (~31,243 km² in Rajasthan).
4. **Mahi Basin**: Southern tribal belt (~16,611 km²).
5. **Banganga Basin**: Eastern alluvial plains.
6. **Sabarmati Basin**: Southern Aravalli runoff draining to Gujarat.
7. **West Banas Basin**: Sirohi uplands draining into Little Rann.
8. **Gambhir Basin**: Eastern Rajasthan terminating towards Yamuna.
9. **Parbati Basin**: Southeastern Hadoti tributary basin of Chambal.
10. **Ruparel Basin**: Alwar–Bharatpur inland/semi-inland catchment.
11. **Sabi (Sahibi) Basin**: Northeastern plain draining into Haryana.
12. **Shekhawati Basin**: Internal drainage catchment (~9,751 km²).
13. **Sukli Basin**: Small southwestern stream catchment.
14. **Other Nallahs / Desert Internal Catchments**: Grouped category for endorheic micro-watersheds across western sand dunes.

## 5. Diagnostic Active-Recall Flashcards

### Card 1: Drainage Proportions & Divide
- **Q**: What are the textbook area proportions of Rajasthan's three drainage regimes, and what is the scientific status of the Aravalli drainage divide?
- **A**: Approximately **60% Inland/Endorheic**, **22–23% Bay of Bengal**, and **17–18% Arabian Sea**. The Aravallis form a major regional divide trending SW–NE, but it is not a monolithic impervious barrier—local saddles, river captures, and closed depressions create real hydrological exceptions.

### Card 2: Luni Hydrological Invariants
- **Q**: What causes the freshwater-to-saline transition in the Luni river downstream of Balotra?
- **A**: A multifactor process: high evaporation rates in an arid climate, evaporitic/saline soils and sediments in the lower catchment, saline groundwater influx, and low river discharge offering minimal dilution. (Purge simplistic claims of "Tethys Sea capillary salts" or Kalidasa citations).

### Card 3: Mahi River & Hydropower Profile
- **Q**: What are the distinctive geographic and engineering coordinates of the Mahi river in Rajasthan?
- **A**: Rises in Amjhoru (Dhar, MP), enters Rajasthan at Khandu (Banswara), describes an inverted 'U' shape crossing the Tropic of Cancer twice. Merges with Som and Jakham at Baneshwar Dham. Features Mahi Bajaj Sagar Dam with **140 MW** installed capacity (CEA profile, Stages I & II combined). Kadana Dam is in Gujarat; Kagdi pick-up is downstream in Banswara.

### Card 4: Chambal River Dam Cascade
- **Q**: List the 4 dams of the Chambal Valley Project in upstream-to-downstream order, noting location and power capacities.
- **A**: 
  1. **Gandhi Sagar Dam**: Mandsaur/Neemuch (MP) — 115 MW.
  2. **Rana Pratap Sagar Dam**: Rawatbhata, Chittorgarh (Rajasthan) — 172 MW.
  3. **Jawahar Sagar Dam**: Kota-Bundi border (Rajasthan) — 99 MW.
  4. **Kota Barrage**: Kota city (Rajasthan) — 0 MW (purely an irrigation barrage).  
  *Aggregate capacity: ~386 MW, shared 50:50 between Rajasthan and MP per interstate agreements.*

### Card 5: Banas River Confluences (Triveni Sangams)
- **Q**: Name the three major confluences associated with the Banas river.
- **A**: 
  1. **Bigod (Bhilwara)**: Banas + Bedach + Menal.
  2. **Rajmahal (Tonk)**: Banas + Khari + Dai.
  3. **Rameshwaram Ghat (Sawai Madhopur)**: Banas + Chambal (local tradition often includes the seasonal Seep).

### Card 6: Gambhir vs Gambhiri Distinction
- **Q**: How do you differentiate Gambhir from Gambhiri in Rajasthan geography?
- **A**: 
  - **Gambhir (गम्भीर)**: Originates in Karauli hills, flows through Bharatpur toward the Yamuna; feeds Panchana Dam (confluence of Ata, Machi, Bhadrawati, Barkheda, Bhainsawat) and Keoladeo.
  - **Gambhiri (गम्भीरी)**: Rises in MP (Jawad hills) and flows into Chittorgarh, joining the Bedach river just downstream of Chittorgarh city.

### Card 7: Inland Drainage Archaeological Pairs
- **Q**: Match the four major inland rivers with their diagnostic archaeological or engineering sites.
- **A**: 
  1. **Ghaggar**: Kalibangan (Harappan), Rangmahal (Kushan/historical), Talwara Lake.
  2. **Kantli**: Ganeshwar (Copper Age) and Sunari (Iron Age smelting).
  3. **Sabi**: Jodhpura (Painted Grey Ware and iron metallurgy).
  4. **Ruparel**: Moti Jheel Dam ("Lifeline of Bharatpur") and Sujan Ganga canal.

### Card 8: Ramsar Wetland 464 (Sambhar Lake)
- **Q**: State the official Ramsar designation coordinates and inflow rivers of Sambhar Lake.
- **A**: Designated as **Ramsar Site No. 464 on 23 March 1990** (area ~24,000 ha). Sits at the junction of Jaipur, Didwana-Kuchaman, and Ajmer. Major seasonal inflows: **Mendha** (from north), **Rupangarh** (from south), **Khari**, and **Khandela**.

### Card 9: Jaisamand & Rajsamand Epigraphic Coordinates
- **Q**: Identify the district, commissioning ruler, and signature features of Jaisamand and Rajsamand lakes.
- **A**: 
  - **Jaisamand (Dhebar)**: **Salumber district**; commissioned by Maharana Jai Singh (1685–1691) across Gomti river; 7 islands (*Babaka Bhagra*, *Piari*); Shyampura and Bhatta canals.

### Card 10: Nakki & Ana Sagar Sovereign Corrections
- **Q**: What are the corrected historical and physical facts for Nakki Lake and Ana Sagar?
- **A**: 
  - **Nakki Lake**: High-altitude upland freshwater lake (~1,200 m) in Mount Abu; origin is mythological (scratched by gods' nails), not a proven volcanic caldera; surface freezes occasionally, does not freeze solid.
  - **Ana Sagar**: Constructed by Chauhan ruler **Arnoraja (c. 1135–1150 CE)** across seasonal streams in Ajmer. Mughal additions: Daulat Bagh (by Jahangir) and 5 marble Baradari pavilions (by Shah Jahan).
