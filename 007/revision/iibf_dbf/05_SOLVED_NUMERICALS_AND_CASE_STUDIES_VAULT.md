## 18. IIBF DBF Paper 1 (IE&IFS): Master Past 5-Year Solved PYQs & Case Studies

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Paper 1 (IE&IFS) Exam Pattern & Case Study Strategy**
> Paper 1 tests theoretical macroeconomic fundamentals and statutory banking frameworks through 1-mark factual questions and 2-mark numerical case studies.

## 🧮 1. Numerical Case Study 1: Basel III Capital Adequacy Calculation

**Worked Example:** 
**Solution:** Step-by-step Solution:
1. CET1 Ratio = (CET1 / RWA) * 100 = (8,000 / 1,00,000) * 100 = 8.00% (RBI minimum is 5.50% + 2.50% CCB = 8.00% -> MET).
2. Tier 1 Ratio = ((CET1 + AT1) / RWA) * 100 = (10,000 / 1,00,000) * 100 = 10.00% (RBI minimum is 7.00% -> MET).
3. Total Capital (CRAR) = ((Tier 1 + Tier 2) / RWA) * 100 = (13,000 / 1,00,000) * 100 = 13.00%.
4. Total regulatory requirement is 9.00% CRAR + 2.50% CCB = 11.50%. Since actual CRAR is 13.00%, the bank fully satisfies all Basel III capital adequacy norms.

## 🧮 2. Numerical Case Study 2: Money Multiplier & Reserve Ratios

**Worked Example:** 
**Solution:** 1. Money Multiplier Formula: m = (1 + c) / (c + r)
   m = (1 + 0.25) / (0.25 + 0.05) = 1.25 / 0.30 = 4.167
2. Broad Money (M3) = m * M0 = 4.167 * ₹40,00,000 Cr = ₹1,66,68,000 Cr (~₹166.68 Lakh Crores).

## 19. IIBF DBF Paper 2 (PPB): Master Past 5-Year Solved PYQs & Case Studies

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Paper 2 (PPB) Exam Pattern & Case Study Strategy**
> Paper 2 tests legal banking provisions, lending norms, and day-to-day branch operational rules through multi-part case study scenarios.

## 🧮 1. Numerical Case Study 1: Working Capital Assessment (Tandon Method 2)

**Worked Example:** 
**Solution:** Step-by-step Solution:
1. Working Capital Gap (WCG) = Current Assets - Current Liabilities = 800 - 200 = ₹600 Lakhs.
2. Tandon Method 1 MPBF = 0.75 * WCG = 0.75 * 600 = ₹450 Lakhs.
3. Tandon Method 2 MPBF = (0.75 * TCA) - CL = (0.75 * 800) - 200 = 600 - 200 = ₹400 Lakhs.
4. Required NWC under Method 2 = 25% of TCA = 0.25 * 800 = ₹200 Lakhs.
   Since existing NWC is only ₹150 Lakhs, there is an NWC Deficit of ₹50 Lakhs (200 - 150). The borrower must bring in ₹50 Lakhs of long-term funds to be eligible for ₹400 Lakhs bank finance.

## ⚖️ 2. Legal Case Study 2: Garnishee Order vs Subsequent Deposits

**Worked Example:** 
**Solution:** Legal Solution:
1. Total amount attached is ₹1,00,000 (the credit balance existing at the exact moment of service of the order).
2. NO. Under Order 21 Rule 46 CPC, a Garnishee Order attaches ONLY debts 'due or accruing due' at the time of service. Deposits made after service are NOT attached.
3. The bank must return the ₹50,000 cheque with the remark 'Refer to Drawer / Account Attached by Court Order', because the entire ₹1,00,000 balance was attached. To handle subsequent transactions, the bank should open a fresh, separate account for the customer.

## 20. IIBF DBF Paper 3 (AFMB): Master Past 5-Year Solved PYQs & Numerical Case Studies

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Paper 3 (AFMB) Exam Pattern & Numerical Strategy**
> Paper 3 is the most mathematical paper in DBF / JAIIB, with over 40–50% of questions requiring numerical calculations on TVM, Ratios, Depreciation, and Marginal Costing.

## 🧮 1. Numerical Case Study 1: Marginal Costing & Break-Even Point

**Worked Example:** 
**Solution:** Step-by-step Solution:
1. Contribution per unit = Selling Price - Variable Cost = 100 - 60 = ₹40 per unit.
   P/V Ratio = (Contribution / Sales) * 100 = (40 / 100) * 100 = 40%.
2. Break-Even Point (Units) = Fixed Cost / Contribution per unit = 2,00,000 / 40 = 5,000 units.
   BEP (Rupees) = Fixed Cost / P/V Ratio = 2,00,000 / 0.40 = ₹5,00,000.
3. Margin of Safety (Units) = Actual Sales (8,000) - BEP Sales (5,000) = 3,000 units.
   MOS (Rupees) = Actual Sales (₹8,00,000) - BEP Sales (₹5,00,000) = ₹3,00,000.
4. Profit = Total Contribution - Fixed Cost = (8,000 * 40) - 2,00,000 = 3,20,000 - 2,00,000 = ₹1,20,000.

## 🧮 2. Numerical Case Study 2: Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) Calculation

**Worked Example:** 
**Solution:** Formula: Loan Amount (P) = EMI * PVIFA(r, n)
- Monthly Interest rate (r) = 12% / 12 = 1% = 0.01 per month.
- Number of monthly installments (n) = 120.
- EMI = Loan Amount / PVIFA(1%, 120)
- EMI = 10,00,000 / 69.7005 = ₹14,347.10 per month (~₹14,347).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps in Paper 3:
1. **Passbook Overdraft Balance:** A Debit balance in Passbook is an **OVERDRAFT**.
2. **TDS Slabs on Bank Interest:** ₹40,000 for general citizens, **₹50,000 for Senior Citizens** under Section 194A.

## 21. IIBF DBF Paper 4 (RBWM): Master Past 5-Year Solved PYQs & Case Studies

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Paper 4 (RBWM) Exam Pattern & Case Study Strategy**
> Paper 4 tests retail lending products, credit bureau mechanisms, investment advisory limits, and real estate regulations.

## 🧮 1. Numerical Case Study 1: Housing Loan LTV & Margin Assessment

**Worked Example:** 
**Solution:** Regulatory Solution:
1. Under RBI guidelines, for housing loans exceeding ₹10 Lakhs, stamp duty, registration, and other documentation charges CANNOT be included in the cost of housing property for LTV calculation.
2. Since property cost is ₹60 Lakhs (falling in the slab of > ₹30 Lakhs to ₹75 Lakhs), the maximum permissible LTV ratio is 80%.
   Maximum Permissible Loan = 80% of ₹60,00,000 = ₹48,00,000 (₹48 Lakhs).
3. Minimum Margin Money = 20% of ₹60 Lakhs = ₹12,00,000 + Stamp duty of ₹5,00,000 = ₹17,00,000 (Total ₹17 Lakhs contribution by borrower).
