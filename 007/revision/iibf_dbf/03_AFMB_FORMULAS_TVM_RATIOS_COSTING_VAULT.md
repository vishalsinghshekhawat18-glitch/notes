## 13. IIBF AFMB Unit 6: Time Value of Money (TVM), Annuity & YTM Mathematics

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: The Time Value of Money (TVM)**
> A rupee today is worth more than a rupee in the future due to its earning potential, purchasing power erosion via inflation, and uncertainty/risk.

## 🧮 1. Master Mathematical Formulas for Time Value of Money

$\text{PV} = \frac{\text{FV}}{(1 + r)^n} = \text{FV} \times (1 + r)^{-n}$

*Explanation:* Where \(PV\) = Present Value, \(FV\) = Future Value, \(r\) = interest rate per compounding period, and \(n\) = total number of compounding periods.

$\text{PV}_{\text{Annuity}} = C \times \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r} \right]$

*Explanation:* Used to determine the maximum loan amount sanctionable for a given equated periodic installment \(C\).

$\text{PV}_{\text{Perpetuity}} = \frac{C}{r}$

*Explanation:* A perpetuity is a stream of equal cash flows \(C\) that continues indefinitely forever (e.g. British Consols, irredeemable preference shares).

$\text{PV}_{\text{Growing Perpetuity}} = \frac{C}{r - g} \quad (r > g)$

*Explanation:* Where cash flows grow at constant annual rate \(g\) indefinitely (provided discount rate \(r > g\)).

$\text{EAR} = \left( 1 + \frac{r_{\text{nominal}}}{m} \right)^m - 1$

*Explanation:* Reflects true annual yield when interest is compounded \(m\) times per year (e.g. quarterly compounding in bank deposits where \(m=4\)).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on TVM & Annuity:
1. **Annuity Due vs Ordinary Annuity:** \(\text{Value of Annuity Due} = \text{Value of Ordinary Annuity} \times (1 + r)\). Annuity Due is always higher by a factor of \((1+r)\) because payments earn interest for one extra period.
2. **Nominal Rate vs Effective Rate:** If nominal rate is 12% p.a. compounded monthly, \(\text{EAR} = (1 + 0.01)^{12} - 1 = 12.68\%\), which is higher than 12%.
3. **Perpetuity Formula:** Present value of perpetuity is simply \(\frac{C}{r}\).

## 15. IIBF AFMB Unit 8: Ratio Analysis for Credit & Financial Health Assessment

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Financial Ratios as a Diagnostic Tool**
> Ratio analysis evaluates the financial stability, operational efficiency, liquidity, and debt-servicing capacity of borrowing enterprises by establishing mathematical relationships between financial statement line items.

## 📊 1. Master Formula Matrix for Credit Appraisal Ratios

| Ratio Category | Formula | Banking Benchmark / Target | Significance for Lenders |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current Ratio (CR) | \(\frac{\text{Current Assets (CA)}}{\text{Current Liabilities (CL)}}\) | **1.33 : 1** (Tandon Method 2) | Measures short-term solvency and working capital cushion. |
| Quick / Acid-Test Ratio | \(\frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventories} - \text{Prepaid Expenses}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}\) | **1.00 : 1** | Instant liquidity test excluding slow-moving inventory. |
| Debt-Equity Ratio (DER) | \(\frac{\text{Total Long-Term Debt}}{\text{Tangible Net Worth (Net Owned Funds)}}\) | **2 : 1** (or lower; 3:1 for infra projects) | Measures capital structure leverage and financial cushion of equity holders. |
| Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR) | \(\frac{\text{EBIT (Earnings Before Interest & Tax)}}{\text{Interest Expense}}\) | **\ge 2.00\text{ times}** | Measures ability to pay annual interest obligations from operating profits. |
| Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) | \(\frac{\text{PAT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Interest}}{\text{Interest} + \text{Principal Installment}}\) | **1.50 to 2.00** (Min 1.20) | Primary benchmark for sanctioning term loans. |
| Inventory Turnover Ratio (ITR) | \(\frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}}{\text{Average Inventory}}\) | Higher indicates fast-moving stock | Detects inventory hoarding or obsolete stock build-up. |
| Debtors Collection Period | \(\frac{\text{Average Accounts Receivable}}{\text{Net Credit Sales}} \times 365\) | Within credit terms (e.g. 60–90 days) | Measures speed of realizing cash from credit customers. |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Ratio Analysis:
1. **Quick Ratio Exclusion:** When calculating Quick Assets, **Inventories and Prepaid Expenses are SUBTRACTED** from Current Assets.
2. **Tandon Committee Benchmark:** The standard minimum Current Ratio under Tandon Method 2 is **1.33:1**.
3. **Ideal Debt-Equity Ratio:** Standard acceptable commercial benchmark for Debt-Equity Ratio is **2:1**.

## 16. IIBF AFMB Unit 9: Fundamentals of Costing, Marginal Costing & Break-Even Analysis

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Marginal Costing & Contribution Principle**
> Marginal costing segregates total costs strictly into Fixed Costs (period costs that remain constant regardless of production volume) and Variable Costs (product costs that vary directly with output volume).

## 🧮 1. Master Mathematical Formulas for Marginal Costing

$\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100 = \frac{\text{Sales} - \text{Variable Cost}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$

*Explanation:* Expresses profitability per unit of sales volume. Higher P/V ratio indicates high profit-earning capacity.

$\text{BEP (Units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}}, \quad \text{BEP (Value)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{P/V Ratio}}$

*Explanation:* The sales volume at which Total Revenue equals Total Cost (Profit = 0).

$\text{Margin of Safety (MoS)} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{Break-Even Sales} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}}$

*Explanation:* The safety cushion by which sales can decline before the business begins incurring losses.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Marginal Costing:
1. **Fixed Cost per Unit Trap:** Total Fixed Cost remains constant, but **Fixed Cost per unit DECREASES as production volume increases**.
2. **Variable Cost per Unit Trap:** Variable cost per unit remains **CONSTANT**, but total variable cost increases proportionally with volume.
3. **Margin of Safety Formula:** \(\text{MOS} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}}\).
