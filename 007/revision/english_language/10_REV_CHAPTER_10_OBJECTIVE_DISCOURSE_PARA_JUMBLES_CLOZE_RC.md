## 1. Core Distinction Matrices

### Matrix A: Para-Jumble Mandatory Pairing Matrix

| Mechanism Type | Identification Rule | Operational Verification Step | Fatal Ordering Error |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Noun ➔ Pronoun** | Full noun must precede personal/demonstrative pronoun (*he, she, it, they, this, these*). | Check if the pronoun has a clearly identified antecedent in the prior sentence. | Placing *"These findings..."* before the experiment is introduced. |
| **Acronym Expansion** | Full organization/concept name must appear before shorthand acronym. | Locate full spelled-out version; verify it precedes the abbreviated letters. | Placing *"RBI mandated..."* before *"Reserve Bank of India (RBI)"*. |
| **Polarity Marker** | Contrastive adverbs (*However, Nonetheless, Yet, But*) signal a reversal of direction. | Ensure positive thesis precedes the contrastive limitation. | Starting a standalone paragraph with *"However, growth collapsed"*. |
| **Chronological Flow** | Temporal order moves from past context ➔ recent development ➔ future prognosis. | Sequence year markers (*1991 ➔ 2015 ➔ present day*). | Inverting historical sequence without flashback syntax. |

### Matrix B: The Four Reading Comprehension Distractor Traps

| Distractor Architecture | Examiner's Method | Forensic Detection Cue | Rapid Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Extreme Words** | Inserts words like *Always, Never, Solely, Exclusively, Completely*. | passage used hedged words (*often, largely, typically*). | **ELIMINATE IMMEDIATELY**. |
| **Out-of-Scope** | Selects a statement that is true in general knowledge but absent from text. | Option cannot be linked to any specific sentence in the passage. | **ELIMINATE**. |
| **Reversal Trap** | Reverses the subject and object or inverts cause and effect. | Text says *A led to B*; option states *B triggered A*. | **ELIMINATE**. |
| **Half-Truth Trap** | First half repeats text accurately; second half adds unsubstantiated claim. | Scrutinize the final 4-5 words of the option. | **ELIMINATE**. |

## 2. 60-Second Retrieval Skeleton

```text
Para-Jumbles: Scan for Independent Topic Sentence (No pronouns, no contrast adverbs) 
➔ Build Mandatory Pairs: Noun ➔ Pronoun | Full Form ➔ Acronym | Cause ➔ Effect 
➔ Trace Transition Signals: However / Yet / Nonetheless = Polarity Flip | Furthermore / Moreover = Expansion 
➔ Cloze Test: Check Prepositional Dependencies (adhere to, comply with, abstain from) 
➔ Check Lexical Collocations (wreak havoc, pay heed, lodge protest, execute agreement) 
➔ Reading Comprehension: Skim paragraph skeletons (Sentence 1 + Sentence Last) ➔ Read Questions 
➔ Distractor Traps: Absolute words (always/never) = Toxic | Out-of-scope truths = Invalid 
➔ Tone Recognition: Analytical (empirical) vs. Critical (censorious) vs. Sardonic (acerbic) vs. Didactic (pedagogical)
```

## 3. High-Yield Examination Flashcards

#### Card 1
* **Diagnostic Question**: *In a Para-Jumble with 5 sentences, Sentence C begins with "These systemic vulnerabilities culminated in a sudden currency devaluation." Can Sentence C be the opening sentence?*
* **Answer**: No. The demonstrative adjective *"These"* demands a prior sentence that explicitly details what the systemic vulnerabilities are.

#### Card 2
* **Diagnostic Question**: *In an RC passage, the author outlines the benefits of financial technology but devotes three paragraphs to algorithmic discrimination, predatory lending, and lack of grievance redressal. What is the author's primary attitude toward the technology?*
* **Answer**: **Cautiously Critical / Skeptical**. While acknowledging potential benefits, the primary discursive weight is concentrated on highlighting structural pitfalls and regulatory deficits.

#### Card 3
* **Diagnostic Question**: *In a Cloze Test, the sentence reads: "The regulatory commission refused to ______ to industry pressure." Options: (A) accept, (B) succumb, (C) agree, (D) conform.*
* **Answer**: **(B) Succumb**. The verb *succumb* takes the fixed preposition *to* in the sense of yielding or submitting under force/pressure. While *agree* also takes *to*, *agree to pressure* is idiomatic nonsense (*agree to a proposal*, but *yield/succumb to pressure*).
