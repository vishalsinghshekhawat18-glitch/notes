## 1. Core Distinction Matrices

### Matrix A: Systemic Architecture Across Six Major Powers

| Nation | Executive Type | Legislative Structure | Judicial Review Model | Federal Nature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **India** | Parliamentary (Nominal President + Real PM) | Bicameral Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) | Integrated Judiciary; Substantive Due Process synthesis; **Basic Structure**. | Quasi-Federal / Union of States; Single citizenship; Unitary tilt in emergency. |
| **USA** | Pure Presidential (Fixed 4-year tenure) | Bicameral Congress (House of Reps + Senate) | Supreme Court; **Due Process of Law** (*Marbury v. Madison 1803*). | True Dual Federalism; Dual citizenship; State constitutions & courts. |
| **UK** | Parliamentary Monarchy (Monarch + PM) | Bicameral Parliament (Commons + Lords) | **No judicial review of primary statutes**; Doctrine of Parliamentary Sovereignty. | Unitary state with statutory devolution (Scotland, Wales, NI). |
| **France** | **Semi-Presidential** (Dual Executive: Pres + PM) | Bicameral Parliament (National Assembly + Sénat) | *Conseil Constitutionnel* (**A priori review** before promulgation); *Conseil d'État* (Admin law). | Unitary decentralized state. |
| **Japan** | Parliamentary Monarchy (Symbol Emperor + PM) | Bicameral Diet (House of Reps + House of Councillors) | Supreme Court with judicial review (**Article 81**); extreme self-restraint. | Unitary state with prefectural local self-government. |
| **Russia** | **Super-Presidential** (Dominant President + PM) | Bicameral Federal Assembly (State Duma + Fed Council) | Constitutional Court (Abstract & concrete review over federal/regional laws). | Asymmetric Federalism (85+ diverse constituent subjects). |

### Matrix B: Upper Houses of the World Compared

| Upper House | Legislative & Constitutional Powers | Special Prerogatives |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **US Senate (100)** | Equal legislative power with House of Reps on all laws, including taxation and budget. | Confirms treaties (2/3 vote); confirms federal judges & cabinet appointees; impeachment trial. **Most powerful upper house in the world**. |
| **Indian Rajya Sabha (245)** | Co-equal on ordinary bills & amendments; subordinate on Money Bills (14-day delay). | Special federal initiatives: **Article 249** (legislate on State List) and **Article 312** (create All India Services). |
| **British House of Lords (~800)** | Subordinate delaying house under Parliament Acts of 1911 & 1949. | Delay ordinary public bills for maximum **1 year**; Money Bills for maximum **1 month**. Cannot kill legislation. |
| **French Sénat (348)** | Represents territorial collectivities; co-equal in constitutional revisions. | If deadlock persists, government can grant the **National Assembly the final say** on ordinary legislation. |
| **Japanese House of Councillors (248)** | Cannot be dissolved; 6-year fixed staggered term. | Lower House overrides by 2/3 vote; Lower House selection of Prime Minister and budget prevails. |

### Matrix C: Unique Constitutional Innovations

| Concept / Doctrine | Country | Operational Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cohabitation** | **France** | Occurs when President and parliamentary majority belong to opposing parties; President retains Defense/Foreign affairs, PM commands Domestic policy. |
| **Article 9 (Peace Clause)** | **Japan** | Renunciation of war as sovereign right; prohibition of maintaining land, sea, and air forces (accommodates Self-Defense Forces for defense only). |
| **Shadow Cabinet** | **UK** | Formal opposition ministers monitoring corresponding government departments, ready to assume power smoothly. |
| **Pocket Veto** | **USA** | If President does not sign a bill within 10 days and Congress adjourns in that period, the bill dies automatically without formal veto. |
| **Decree Power (*Ukase*)** | **Russia** | President can issue binding presidential decrees having full force of law without prior legislative approval from State Duma. |
| **Droit Administratif** | **France** | Specialized body of administrative jurisprudence administered exclusively by administrative courts (*Conseil d'État*) separate from civil courts. |
