## 1. Core Distinction Matrices

### Matrix A: Constitutional Evolution Across British Enactments (1773–1947)

| Enactment | Key Executive Mutation | Key Legislative Mutation | Critical Landmark Precedent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regulating Act 1773** | Gov of Bengal ➔ **Governor-General of Bengal** (Warren Hastings) | Council of 4 members (majority vote) | Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774, Sir Elijah Impey) |
| **Pitt's India Act 1784** | **Double Government**: Court of Directors (Trade) vs. Board of Control (Politics) | Council reduced from 4 to 3 members | Coined "British Possessions in India" |
| **Charter Act 1813** | EIC trade monopoly ended (except **Tea & China trade**) | Crown sovereignty formally asserted | ₹1 Lakh for education; Christian missionaries allowed |
| **Charter Act 1833** | Gov-Gen of Bengal ➔ **Governor-General of India** (William Bentinck) | Complete legislative centralization; Bombay/Madras stripped of lawmaking | EIC commercial activity liquidated; Law Member added (Macaulay); "Acts" replace "Regulations" |
| **Charter Act 1853** | Executive & Legislative separation | **Central Legislative Council (6 members)** acting as "Mini-Parliament" | Open competition for ICS (Macaulay Committee 1854); Local representation in council |
| **GoI Act 1858** | EIC abolished; GGI ➔ **Viceroy of India** (Lord Canning); SOS for India created | Council of India (15 members in London) | "Act for the Better Government of India"; Direct Crown takeover |
| **Councils Act 1861** | Viceroy's Ordinance power (6 months); Portfolio System recognized | 3 Indians nominated (Benaras, Patiala, Dinkar Rao); Decentralization to Bombay/Madras restored | Inception of representative institutions; Legislative Councils in Bengal, NWFP, Punjab |
| **Councils Act 1892** | Limited indirect nomination on recommendation | Councils allowed to **discuss Budget** and ask questions (no voting, no supplementaries) | Representation principle seeded (word "election" avoided) |
| **Councils Act 1909** | First Indian in Viceroy's ExCo: **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** (Law Member) | Central Council expanded to 60 (official majority); Provincial councils non-official majority | **Separate Electorates for Muslims**; Lord Minto = "Father of Communal Electorates" |
| **GoI Act 1919** | 3 of 6 members in Viceroy's ExCo Indian; High Commissioner in London created | **Bicameralism at Centre** (Council of State & Legislative Assembly); Direct elections | **Provincial Dyarchy** (Transferred vs. Reserved); Communal electorates expanded to Sikhs, Christians; PSC set up 1926 |
| **GoI Act 1935** | Dyarchy at Centre (Reserved vs. Transferred—never operated) | Tripartite lists (Federal, Provincial, Concurrent); Bicameralism in 6 of 11 provinces | **Provincial Autonomy**; All-India Federation proposed; Federal Court (1937); RBI established |
| **Independence Act 1947** | Office of Viceroy & SOS abolished; GGI for each dominion (Mountbatten / Rajagopalachari) | Constituent Assemblies declared sovereign legislatures; British laws repealable | End of British rule (15 Aug 1947); Partition into 2 dominions; Lapse of British paramountcy |

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)

1. **The Election Trap**: The Constituent Assembly was **NEVER directly elected** by universal adult franchise. It was **partly elected (indirectly by provincial assemblies)** and **partly nominated (by princely states)**.
2. **The First Law Member Trap**: Lord Macaulay was the first Law Member (Charter Act 1833). Satyendra Prasad Sinha was the **first Indian Law Member** (Indian Councils Act 1909).
3. **The Dyarchy Shift Trap**: Dyarchy existed at the **provincial level under the 1919 Act**; it was abolished at the provincial level and **proposed at the central level under the 1935 Act** (though central dyarchy never came into operation).
4. **The Federation Trap**: The All-India Federation proposed in the 1935 Act **never came into existence** because the princely states failed to execute the required Instruments of Accession.
5. **The Commencement Trap**: 26 November 1949 is the date of **adoption and enactment**; 26 January 1950 is the date of **commencement**. Only 15 articles (Citizenship, Elections, Provisional Parliament, Article 394) came into force on 26 November 1949.
