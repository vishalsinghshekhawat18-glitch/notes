## 1. Core Distinction Matrices

### Matrix A: Parliamentary vs. Presidential System Institutional Matrix

| Feature Dimension | Parliamentary System (India/UK) | Presidential System (USA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Executive Structure** | **Dual Executive**: Nominal (President) vs. Real (Prime Minister). | **Single Executive**: President is both Head of State & Head of Govt. |
| **Accountability** | **Continuous & Collective**: Daily scrutiny via debates, questions, no-confidence motions. | **Periodic**: Only during elections; zero daily responsibility to Congress. |
| **Tenure Stability** | **Unstable**: Government can fall any day if it loses Lok Sabha majority. | **Fixed**: 4-year tenure; removed only by onerous Impeachment for high crimes. |
| **Ministers' Status** | **MPs only**: Must belong to Parliament (or get elected within 6 months). | **Non-Members**: Outside administrative experts ("Kitchen Cabinet"). |
| **Separation of Powers** | **Fusion of Powers**: Cabinet serves as the hyphen between Executive and Legislature. | **Strict Separation of Powers**: Organs operate in independent orbits. |
| **Dissolution of House** | **Yes**: Prime Minister can advise President to dissolve the Lok Sabha. | **No**: President cannot dissolve the House of Representatives or Senate. |

### Matrix B: Indian Westminster vs. British Westminster Divergence

| Parameter | Indian Parliamentary Model | British Parliamentary Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Head of State** | **Republic**: Elected President (indirectly elected for 5 years). | **Monarchy**: Hereditary King or Queen. |
| **Parliamentary Status** | **Non-Sovereign**: Constrained by written constitution, federalism, judicial review, FRs. | **Sovereign**: Unlimited legislative supremacy; no court can invalidate Acts. |
| **PM's Membership** | Can belong to **EITHER House** (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha). | MUST belong to **Lower House** (House of Commons) by convention. |
| **Non-MP Appointment** | Non-MP can be minister for up to **6 months** (Article 75(5)). | Non-MP cannot be minister (must be MP or elevated to House of Lords). |
| **Legal Responsibility** | **Zero**: Orders do not need countersignature; Art 74(2) bars court inquiry. | **Strict**: Countersignature mandatory; "The King can do no wrong". |
| **Shadow Cabinet** | **Absent**: No institutional alternative opposition cabinet. | **Present**: Official Opposition runs formal Shadow Cabinet. |

## 3. High-Yield Examiner Traps (Quick Scan)

1. **The Collective Responsibility Target Trap**: The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha (House of the People)**, NOT to Parliament as a whole.
2. **The PM House Membership Trap**: The Prime Minister of India does **NOT have to be a member of the Lok Sabha**. He/she can be an MP from the Rajya Sabha.
3. **The Legal Responsibility Fallacy**: India has **NO system of legal responsibility of ministers**. A minister’s countersignature is not required for presidential orders, unlike in Britain.
4. **The Reconsideration Fallacy**: The President cannot repeatedly send cabinet advice back. Under the 44th Amendment, the President can return advice for reconsideration **ONLY ONCE**.
5. **The British Minister Rule Trap**: In Britain, a non-MP cannot be appointed as a minister. In India, a non-MP can be appointed as a minister for up to **6 consecutive months** (Article 75(5)).
