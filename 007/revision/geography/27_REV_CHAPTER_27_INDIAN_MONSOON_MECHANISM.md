### Matrix A: Evolution of Monsoon Explanatory Models

| Theory / Paradigm | Proponent & Year | Master Mechanism | Major Scientific Limitation / Inadequacy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classical Thermal Concept** | **Sir Edmond Halley (1686)** | Giant seasonal land-sea breeze driven by differential heating of Asia vs Indian Ocean | Fails to explain sudden "Burst", spatial variations, or "Breaks" in monsoon |
| **Dynamic Wind Shift** | **Hermann Flohn (1951)** | **Seasonal latitudinal shift of ITCZ**; SE Trades cross Equator and deflect right as SW winds | Ignores upper-tropospheric jet streams and Tibetan plateau thermodynamics |
| **Jet Stream Theory** | **M.T. Yin (1949)** | **Sudden northward jump of Subtropical Westerly Jet (STJ)** north of Himalayas triggers onset | Explains the exact timing and suddenness of the Kerala "Burst of Monsoon" |
| **Tibetan Thermal Engine** | **Dr. P. Koteswaram (1958)** | **Tibet acts as an elevated heat source**, generating the **Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)** aloft | Connects upper-tropospheric divergence with surface monsoonal convergence |

## 3. Instant Killer Traps Checklist

- [ ] **Trap 1: Why Aravallis Do Not Block Arabian Sea Monsoon**: The Aravalli Range does **NOT** block the Arabian Sea branch because its alignment is **PARALLEL to the south-westerly wind direction**, providing no transverse barrier to force orographic ascent.
- [ ] **Trap 2: Break in Monsoon Weather Distribution**: During a "Break in Monsoon", rainfall does NOT stop everywhere in India. The Monsoon Trough shifts to the **Himalayan foothills**, causing **torrential flash floods in the Himalayan rivers (Brahmaputra, Kosi)** while the **Gangetic Plains suffer severe dry spells**.
- [ ] **Trap 3: Western Disturbances Winter Steering**: Winter Western Disturbances that bring precipitation to North India are steered by the **Southern Branch of the Subtropical Westerly Jet (STJ)**, NOT the Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ exists only in summer).
- [ ] **Trap 4: Direction of the Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)**: The TEJ blows from **EAST TO WEST** (Easterly), unlike normal mid-latitude jet streams that blow from West to East. It operates at an altitude of $14\text{–}16\text{ km}$ over Peninsular India during the summer months only.
- [ ] **Trap 5: Winter Rainfall in Tamil Nadu**: Tamil Nadu receives its primary rainfall during **October to December** from the **Northeast (Retreating) Monsoon**, which picks up moisture while traversing the **Bay of Bengal** before striking the Coromandel Coast.
