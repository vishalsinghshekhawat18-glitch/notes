# REVISION CHAPTER 02: KINEMATICS, DYNAMICS & LAWS OF MOTION

## 60-Second Concept Retrieval Skeleton
* **Distance vs Displacement**: Distance is scalar ($\ge 0$); Displacement is vector ($+, -, 0$). $\frac{|\text{Displacement}|}{\text{Distance}} \le 1$. In closed loop, displacement is 0.
* **Harmonic Average Speed**: For equal distance round-trip: $v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$ (Average velocity is 0).
* **Kinematic Equations**: $v = u + at$; $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$; $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$; $s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n-1)$.
* **Vertical Motion Under Gravity**: Time of ascent $t_a = \frac{u}{g}$; $H_{\text{max}} = \frac{u^2}{2g}$; Velocity on hitting ground from height $h$: $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ (Independent of mass).
* **Projectile Motion**: $T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$; $R = \frac{u^2\sin(2\theta)}{g}$; Max range at $\mathbf{\theta = 45^\circ}$ ($R_{\text{max}} = \frac{u^2}{g}$). Same range for $\theta$ and $(90^\circ - \theta)$.
* **Circular Motion**: Centripetal acceleration $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r$ (Inward); Centrifugal force is a **pseudo-force** (Cream separator, centrifuge).
* **Banking of Roads**: $\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg} \implies v = \sqrt{rg\tan\theta}$.
* **Newton's Laws**:
  - 1st: Law of Inertia (Mass = measure of inertia; Bus start/stop jerk).
  - 2nd: $F = \frac{dp}{dt} = ma$; Impulse $J = F \Delta t = \Delta p$ (Cricketer lowers hands to increase $\Delta t$ and reduce $F$).
  - 3rd: Action = -Reaction (Acts on **two different bodies**, never cancels out; Gun recoil, rocket thrust).
* **Conservation of Momentum**: Gun recoil $V = -\frac{m}{M}v$.
* **Friction Hierarchy**: $\mathbf{f_s^{\text{max}} > f_k > f_r}$ (Rolling friction is minimum $\rightarrow$ ball bearings). Angle of friction $\theta$ equals angle of repose $\alpha$ ($\tan\theta = \mu_s$).

## 3-Minute Active Recall Flashcard Drills

| Flash Question | Target Rapid Retrieval Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Q1: Can an object have an acceleration when its velocity is zero?** | **Yes.** At the highest peak of a vertically thrown object, instantaneous velocity is $0$, but acceleration is $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ downward. |
| **Q2: Why does an athlete run a short distance before taking a long jump?** | To acquire **Inertia of Motion**, which adds forward momentum to the jump upon takeoff. |
| **Q3: What are the two complementary launch angles that produce identical horizontal range?** | Any pair satisfying $\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ$ (e.g., $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, or $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$). |
| **Q4: Why do modern vehicles use ball bearings in wheel hubs?** | To convert **sliding friction into rolling friction**, which is orders of magnitude smaller ($\mu_r \ll \mu_k$), drastically reducing energy loss and wear. |
| **Q5: In a vacuum, which hits the ground first: a 5 kg bowling ball or a 1 g feather?** | **Both strike at the exact same instant** ($t = \sqrt{2h/g}$), because acceleration due to gravity is strictly independent of the falling object's mass. |
| **Q6: Why doesn't action and reaction cancel each other to produce zero net force?** | Because action and reaction forces act simultaneously on **two completely different physical bodies**, never on the same object. |

## Diagnostic Test-Maker Trap Matrix

| Concept | The Deceptive Trap | The Ironclad Scientific Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cut String in Circular Motion** | "A stone swung on a string flies radially outwards when cut." | It flies **tangentially** along the circular perimeter at that instant due to **Inertia of Direction**. |
| **Average Speed** | Calculating arithmetic average $\frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}$ for a two-way round trip. | Must use harmonic mean: $v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$. Net velocity is $0$. |
| **Rocket Propulsion** | "Rockets push against atmospheric air to move forward." | Rockets push against their **own expelled high-speed exhaust gases** (Conservation of momentum), working efficiently in vacuum. |
| **Static Friction Magnitude** | "Static friction is always equal to $\mu_s N$." | Limiting friction is $\mu_s N$. Actual static friction is **self-adjusting** from $0$ up to $\mu_s N$ matching the external applied force. |
