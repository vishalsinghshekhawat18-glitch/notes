# REVISION CHAPTER 04: WORK, ENERGY, POWER & CONSERVATION LAWS

## 60-Second Concept Retrieval Skeleton
* **Work Formula**: $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = F s \cos\theta$ (Scalar, SI: Joule). $1\text{ Joule} = 10^7\text{ Ergs}$.
* **Zero Work Scenarios**: $s = 0$ (pushing immovable wall); $\theta = 90^\circ$ (Coolie carrying load horizontally, satellite in circular orbit under centripetal gravity).
* **Kinetic vs Momentum**: $E_k = \frac{p^2}{2m} \implies p = \sqrt{2mE_k}$.
  - If $p$ doubles $\rightarrow E_k$ quadruples ($+300\%$ increase).
  - Same momentum $\rightarrow$ Lighter mass has **greater $E_k$**.
  - Same $E_k \rightarrow$ Heavier mass has **greater momentum**.
* **Potential Energy**: Gravitational $U = mgh$; Spring $U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$.
* **Work-Energy Theorem**: Net work done = Change in kinetic energy ($W_{\text{net}} = \Delta E_k$).
* **Power**: $P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$ (Scalar, Watt). $1\text{ hp} = 746\text{ W}$.
* **Commercial Energy**: $1\text{ Unit} = 1\text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ Joules} = 3.6\text{ MJ}$.
* **Collisions**:
  - In **ALL** collisions (Elastic or Inelastic), **Linear Momentum is ALWAYS conserved**.
  - Kinetic energy is conserved **ONLY** in elastic collisions ($e = 1$).
  - Perfectly Inelastic ($e = 0$): Maximum kinetic energy loss; bodies stick together.

## 3-Minute Active Recall Flashcard Drills

| Flash Question | Target Rapid Retrieval Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Q1: Why does a coolie carrying a heavy bag horizontally on his head do zero mechanical work against gravity?** | Because the lifting force is vertical while the displacement is horizontal ($\theta = 90^\circ$); $\cos 90^\circ = 0 \implies W = 0$. |
| **Q2: If two bodies of masses 1 kg and 4 kg have identical kinetic energies, what is the ratio of their linear momenta?** | Since $p = \sqrt{2mE_k}$, $p \propto \sqrt{m}$. Thus $\frac{p_1}{p_2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}} = \mathbf{1:2}$. |
| **Q3: What happens to kinetic energy if a vehicle's speed triples?** | It increases **9-fold** (by $800\%$), because $E_k \propto v^2$ ($3^2 = 9$). |
| **Q4: How many Joules of energy are consumed by running a 100-Watt bulb for 10 hours?** | $E = P \times t = 100\text{ W} \times (10 \times 3600\text{ s}) = 3,600,000\text{ J} = \mathbf{3.6\text{ MJ}}$ (Exactly $1\text{ kWh}$ or 1 Unit). |
| **Q5: Which physical quantity is conserved in an inelastic collision?** | **Total Linear Momentum** (and total energy), but NOT kinetic energy. |
| **Q6: Why is torque not measured in Joules even though its formula is force times distance?** | Torque is a **rotational vector** ($\vec{r} \times \vec{F}$ measured in $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$); "Joule" is reserved strictly for **scalar energy and work**. |

## Diagnostic Test-Maker Trap Matrix

| Concept | The Deceptive Trap | The Ironclad Scientific Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Centripetal Work** | "Gravitational pull does work on the Moon to keep it in orbit." | Gravity acts perpendicular to orbital velocity ($\theta = 90^\circ$); hence **work done by centripetal force is strictly ZERO**. |
| **Momentum Increase** | "Increasing momentum by 50% increases kinetic energy by 50%." | Since $E_k \propto p^2$, new $E_k = (1.5p)^2 = 2.25 E_k$, which is a **$125\%$ increase**! |
| **Torque Units** | "Work and torque both have unit Joule." | Work is scalar (Joule); Torque is a vector product ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$). **Torque is never written as Joules**. |
| **Inelastic Collision Energy** | "Energy is destroyed in a car crash." | Total energy is conserved; kinetic energy is simply converted into **thermal heat, sound, and structural deformation**. |
