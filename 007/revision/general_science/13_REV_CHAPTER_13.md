# REVISION CHAPTER 13: ATOMIC STRUCTURE, QUANTUM NUMBERS & ISOTOPES

## 60-Second Concept Retrieval Skeleton
* **Subatomic Particles**:
  - Electron ($e^-$): Thomson (1897), $-1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, $9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$ ($\frac{1}{1837}\text{th}$ of proton).
  - Proton ($p^+$): Goldstein (1886) canal rays, named Rutherford (1919), $+1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, $1.6726 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$.
  - Neutron ($n^0$): Chadwick (1932), $0\text{ C}$, $1.6749 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$ (**Heaviest subatomic particle!**). Free neutron decays ($T_{1/2} \approx 14.7\text{ min}$).
* **Quantum Model**:
  - De Broglie: $\lambda = \frac{h}{mv}$. Heisenberg: $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$ (destroys Bohr circular orbits).
  - Orbital: 3D region of $\ge 90\%$ probability density ($s$=sphere, $p$=dumbbell, $d$=double dumbbell).
* **4 Quantum Numbers**:
  - Principal ($n$): Main shell ($1, 2, 3..$), max electrons $= 2n^2$, orbitals $= n^2$.
  - Azimuthal ($l$): Subshell ($0$ to $n-1$, $s,p,d,f$), orbital shape, max electrons $= 2(2l+1)$.
  - Magnetic ($m_l$): Spatial orientation ($-l$ to $+l$).
  - Spin ($m_s$): Electron spin ($+\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2}$).
* **Aufbau & Exceptions**:
  - Aufbau $(n+l)$ rule: $4s$ fills before $3d$ ($4+0 < 3+2$).
  - Pauli: Max 2 electrons per orbital with opposite spins.
  - Hund's rule: Singly occupied first before pairing.
  - Exceptions: **Chromium ($[Ar]3d^5 4s^1$)** and **Copper ($[Ar]3d^{10} 4s^1$)** for half-filled/fully-filled stability.
* **Species**:
  - Isotopes: Same $Z$, different $A$ (Protium $^1_1H$ has **ZERO neutrons**; Deuterium $^2_1H$ makes heavy water $\text{D}_2\text{O}$; Tritium $^3_1H$ is radioactive).
  - Isobars: Same $A$, different $Z$ ($^{40}Ar, ^{40}K, ^{40}Ca$).
  - Isotones: Same neutrons $A - Z$ ($^{14}_6C, ^{15}_7N, ^{16}_8O$).
  - Isoelectronic: Same total electrons ($N^{3-}, O^{2-}, F^-, Ne, Na^+, Mg^{2+}, Al^{3+}$ all have 10 electrons).
* **Radioisotope Applications**: Carbon-14 (Fossil dating up to 50k yr), Uranium-238 (Rock/Earth age $4.5\text{ Gyr}$), Cobalt-60 (Cancer radiotherapy), Iodine-131 (Thyroid tumors), Phosphorus-32 (Blood polycythemia, agriculture), Sodium-24 (Blood circulation clots), Americium-241 (Smoke alarms).

## 3-Minute Active Recall Flashcard Drills

| Flash Question | Target Rapid Retrieval Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Q1: Which naturally occurring element's most common isotope has zero neutrons?** | **Hydrogen (Protium, $^1_1H$)**; it consists of 1 proton and 1 electron with zero neutrons. |
| **Q2: Why does an ice cube of heavy water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$) sink in ordinary liquid water?** | Heavy water has higher density ($\rho = 1.11\text{ g/cm}^3$); heavy ice density ($\sim 1.02\text{ g/cm}^3$) exceeds ordinary liquid water ($1.00\text{ g/cm}^3$). |
| **Q3: What is the ground-state electronic configuration of Chromium (Z = 24)?** | **$[Ar] 3d^5 4s^1$** (an electron shifts from $4s$ to $3d$ to achieve the extraordinary stability of a half-filled $d^5$ subshell). |
| **Q4: Why does the 4s subshell fill with electrons before the 3d subshell?** | By the **Aufbau $(n + l)$ rule**, $4s$ has $n+l = 4+0 = \mathbf{4}$, while $3d$ has $n+l = 3+2 = \mathbf{5}$; lower $(n+l)$ orbitals fill first. |
| **Q5: Which radioisotope is used for cancer radiotherapy and tumor sterilization?** | **Cobalt-60 ($^{60}_{27}Co$)**, emitting penetrating high-energy gamma ($\gamma$) radiation. |
| **Q6: Arrange electron, proton, and neutron in ascending order of rest mass.** | **Electron ($9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$) $<$ Proton ($1.6726 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$) $<$ Neutron ($1.6749 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$)**. |

## Diagnostic Test-Maker Trap Matrix

| Concept | The Deceptive Trap | The Ironclad Scientific Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Heaviest Subatomic Particle** | "Protons and neutrons have identical mass." | A **neutron is slightly heavier** ($1.6749 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$) than a proton ($1.6726 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$). Free neutrons are unstable! |
| **Isotopes Chemistry** | "Carbon-12 and Carbon-14 react differently with oxygen." | Isotopes have **identical chemical properties** because chemical reactivity is governed by valence electron configurations (same $Z$). |
| **Copper Configuration** | "Copper configuration is $[Ar] 3d^9 4s^2$." | It is $\mathbf{[Ar] 3d^{10} 4s^1}$ to achieve the maximum thermodynamic stability of a fully filled $d^{10}$ subshell. |
| **Iodine-131 Target** | "Iodine-131 treats bone cancer." | Iodine concentrates in the **Thyroid gland**; $^{131}I$ is used specifically for thyroid imaging, goitre, and thyroid cancer. |
