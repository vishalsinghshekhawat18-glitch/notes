# REVISION CHAPTER 05: FLUID MECHANICS, SURFACE TENSION & VISCOSITY

## 60-Second Concept Retrieval Skeleton
* **Pressure**: $P = \frac{F}{A}$ (Scalar, Pascal $\text{N/m}^2$). $P \propto \frac{1}{A}$ (Sharp nail, broad camel hooves). Hydrostatic pressure: $P = \rho gh$ (Hydrostatic paradox: independent of container shape).
* **Pascal's Law**: Enclosed fluid transmits pressure undiminished $\frac{f}{a} = \frac{F}{A}$ (Hydraulic brakes, lift, press).
* **Atmospheric Pressure**: $1\text{ atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ Pa} = 760\text{ mm of Hg} = 10.34\text{ m of water}$.
  - Barometer trends: Sudden drop = **Storm/Cyclone**; Gradual fall = **Rain**; Steady rise = **Fair weather**.
  - Boiling point drops at high altitudes $\rightarrow$ cooking takes longer in open pots; Pressure cooker raises BP to $120\text{--}125^\circ\text{C}$ $\rightarrow$ fast cooking.
* **Archimedes & Floatation**: Buoyant force $F_b = V_{\text{sub}} \cdot \rho_{\text{fluid}} \cdot g$.
  - Steel ship floats (hollow air chambers $\rightarrow$ low average density); Iron nail floats in mercury ($d_{\text{iron}} < \rho_{\text{mercury}}$).
  - Floating ice cube melts $\rightarrow$ **Water level remains UNCHANGED**.
  - Iceberg: $\mathbf{90\%}$ submerged, $\mathbf{10\%}$ visible above sea.
* **Surface Tension**: $T = \frac{F}{L}$ ($\text{N/m}$, $[M^1 L^0 T^{-2}]$). Liquid surface acts like stretched membrane $\rightarrow$ spherical raindrops.
  - Decreases with temperature (Hot soup tastes better).
  - Detergents lower surface tension $\rightarrow$ deep cleaning.
  - Capillarity: Jurin's Law $h \propto \frac{1}{r}$ (Lantern wick, blotting paper, plant roots).
* **Viscosity & Bernoulli**: $\eta$ decreases with temp in liquids, **increases with temp in gases**!
  - Terminal velocity: $v_t \propto r^2$.
  - Bernoulli: $P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = \text{const}$ (Higher speed $\rightarrow$ lower pressure; Aircraft wing lift, roofs blown off in storms, spinning ball Magnus effect, atomizer).

## 3-Minute Active Recall Flashcard Drills

| Flash Question | Target Rapid Retrieval Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Q1: What happens to the water level in a glass when a floating ice cube completely melts?** | The water level remains **strictly UNCHANGED**, because the ice displaces a volume of water equal to its melted liquid volume. |
| **Q2: Why does hot soup taste better than cold soup?** | Heating **lowers surface tension**, allowing the soup to spread thinly and evenly across a broader surface area of the tongue's taste buds. |
| **Q3: What does a sudden, sharp drop in the barometric mercury column indicate?** | An **approaching storm or cyclone**, caused by the sudden arrival of an intense low-pressure atmospheric center. |
| **Q4: How does temperature affect the viscosity of liquids versus gases?** | In **liquids**, viscosity **decreases** with temperature; in **gases**, viscosity **increases** with temperature due to higher molecular collisions. |
| **Q5: Why can a person float without sinking in the Dead Sea?** | The Dead Sea's extreme salinity gives it a density of $\sim 1.24\text{ g/cm}^3$, which is significantly greater than human body density ($\sim 1.06\text{ g/cm}^3$). |
| **Q6: Why do airplane wings have a curved upper surface (aerofoil shape)?** | Air moves faster over the top surface ($v \uparrow \implies P \downarrow$), creating a low-pressure zone above the wing that generates **upward dynamic lift** (Bernoulli's principle). |

## Diagnostic Test-Maker Trap Matrix

| Concept | The Deceptive Trap | The Ironclad Scientific Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gas Viscosity Trend** | "Heating honey decreases its viscosity, so heating air must also decrease its viscosity." | Liquids become less viscous, but **gases become MORE viscous with temperature** due to increased molecular momentum transfer! |
| **Floating Iceberg** | "Most of an iceberg is visible above water." | Exactly **$\mathbf{90\%}$ ($\frac{9}{10}\text{th}$) of an iceberg is submerged** beneath seawater; only $10\%$ is visible above. |
| **Pressure Cooker** | "Pressure cookers cook faster because they increase the heat input." | Pressure cookers trap steam to elevate internal pressure, raising the **boiling point of water to $120\text{--}125^\circ\text{C}$**, cooking food at higher temperatures. |
| **Hydrostatic Pressure** | "A wider bucket exerts higher pressure at the bottom than a narrow tube of equal water depth." | Hydrostatic pressure depends **only on vertical depth ($h$) and density ($\rho$)** ($P = \rho gh$), completely independent of container width/shape. |
