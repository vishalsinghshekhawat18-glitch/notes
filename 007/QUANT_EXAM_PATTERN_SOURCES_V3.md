## 2. Crucial Architectural Distinctions Across Regulatory Exams

### 2.1 The RBI Grade B Phase I vs. Phase II Separation
* **Phase I (Objective Screening)**: Contains the standalone Quantitative Aptitude section (30 Questions, 30 Marks, 25 Minutes). Due to extreme time pressure and high conceptual difficulty, candidate strategy focuses on disciplined question triage: selecting 12–16 high-accuracy questions rather than attempting to solve the entire paper.
* **Phase II (Main Examination)**: Contains **ZERO Quantitative Aptitude**. Phase II consists exclusively of:
  1. Paper I: Economic and Social Issues (ESI) — 50% Objective, 50% Descriptive.
  2. Paper II: English (Writing Skills) — 100% Descriptive.
  3. Paper III: Finance and Management (FM) — 50% Objective, 50% Descriptive.
* **Codex Boundary**: All quantitative pedagogy in this book addresses **RBI Grade B Phase I**. Claims that Phase II contains quantitative problem solving are false and strictly excluded.

### 2.2 Composite Timing vs. Sectional Timers
* **Sectional Timer Regime (SBI PO Prelims/Mains, IBPS PO Prelims/Mains, RBI Grade B Phase I)**:
  * The clock is automated and non-transferable.
  * You cannot save seconds from English or Reasoning to spend on Quant.
  * Every second spent on an arithmetic problem comes directly out of the 20- or 25-minute quantitative allocation.
* **Composite Timer Regime (IBPS RRB PO Prelims/Mains, SEBI Grade A Phase I, NABARD Grade A Prelims)**:
  * The clock is shared across multiple sections.
  * In RRB Prelims, spending 25 minutes on Reasoning leaves exactly 20 minutes for 40 Quant questions.
  * Candidates must maintain strict internal pacing alarms (e.g. 22 mins Reasoning / 23 mins Quant).

## 4. Tactical Time-Management Blueprints

### 4.1 Prelims 20-Minute Tactical Allocation (30–35 Questions)
* **Phase 1: High-Speed Engine (Minutes 0–6)**:
  * Simplification & Approximation (5–6 Qs)
  * Quadratic Root Comparisons (5 Qs)
  * Target: 10–11 questions solved accurately.
* **Phase 2: Data Interpretation Core (Minutes 6–13)**:
  * 1 to 2 standard graphical DI sets (Table, Bar, or Pie) (5–10 Qs)
  * Target: 6–8 questions solved.
* **Phase 3: Number Series (Minutes 13–16)**:
  * 5 questions (Missing or Wrong Series).
  * Strict rule: Skip stubborn series after 40 seconds of trial.
* **Phase 4: Targeted Commercial Arithmetic (Minutes 16–20)**:
  * Select 4–6 arithmetic word problems in high-comfort domains (Partnership, Ages, Percentages, Work, TSD).

### 4.2 Mains 45-Minute Tactical Allocation (30–35 Questions / 50–60 Marks)
* **Phase 1: Triage & Reconnaissance (Minutes 0–3)**:
  * Scan all sets; identify 2 approachable DI sets, 1 Caselet, and standalone Quantity Comparison / Data Sufficiency questions.
* **Phase 2: High-Scoring Data Interpretation (Minutes 3–25)**:
  * Solve the two most structured DI sets (Missing Tables or Radar/Bar hybrid).
* **Phase 3: Caselet / Venn Analysis (Minutes 25–35)**:
  * Unpack variable-based Caselet or Venn framework; solve associated 3–4 questions.
* **Phase 4: Analytical Precision (Minutes 35–45)**:
  * Solve Quantity Comparisons ($Q_1, Q_2, Q_3$) and Data Sufficiency problems using degrees-of-freedom logic without excess arithmetic.
