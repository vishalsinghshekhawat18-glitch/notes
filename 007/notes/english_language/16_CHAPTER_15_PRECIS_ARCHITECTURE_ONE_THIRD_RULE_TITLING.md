## 1. Architectural Foundations: The Epistemic Discipline of a Précis

A **précis** (from the French *précis*, meaning "precise" or "condensed") is not an informal summary, an outline, or a collection of disconnected bullet points. In formal examination rubrics (RBI Grade B, NABARD, UPSC), a précis is an **exact miniature replica** of the original text—reproducing the original author's logical progression, proportion, and tone, while utilizing exactly **one-third** of the original word count.

Evaluators treat the précis as a supreme test of **cognitive compression** and **syntactic efficiency**. In a central bank or administrative secretariat, an officer must distill 100-page statutory submissions or international financial communiqués into a single page of flawless, neutral, and unembellished prose.

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE FIVE INVIOLABLE PRÉCIS LAWS             │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. The Strict 1/3rd Word Budget (±5% Tolerance Window).     │
│ 2. Complete Third-Person & Past/Neutral Tense Discipline.   │
│ 3. 100% Non-Verbatim Paraphrasing (Zero Sentence Copying).  │
│ 4. Total Prohibition of External Facts, Opinions, or Bias.  │
│ 5. Compulsory Academic Title Capitalized in Title Case.     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. The Mathematical Word-Budget Engine

Word count compliance is evaluated with mathematical precision. On digital test consoles (TCS iON), the word-counter is visible to the evaluator.

### 2.1 The Mathematical Tolerance Formula
If the original passage contains $N$ words, the target précis budget $B$ is calculated as:

$$B = \left\lfloor \frac{N}{3} \right\rfloor \pm 5\%$$

| Original Passage Word Count ($N$) | Theoretical Target ($N/3$) | Inviolable Safety Band (±5%) | Penalty Zone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **300 words** | 100 words | 95 to 105 words | < 90 or > 110 words |
| **360 words** | 120 words | 114 to 126 words | < 108 or > 132 words |
| **450 words** | 150 words | 142 to 158 words | < 135 or > 165 words |

*Deduction Penalty Rule*: Exceeding or falling short of the tolerance window triggers automatic band deductions (typically 2 to 5 marks out of 30).

## 3. The Three-Pass Surgical Distillation Protocol

Never attempt to draft a précis while reading the passage for the first time. Master précis writers follow a disciplined **Three-Pass Protocol**:

```text
Pass 1: Macro-Scanning (Theme, Author's Central Thesis & Tone)
                           ▼
Pass 2: Surgical Deletion (Eliminating the Six Noise Categories)
                           ▼
Pass 3: Syntactic Reconstruction (Synthesizing Core Propositions)
```

### 3.1 The Six Noise Categories (Ruthless Red-Pen Deletions)
During Pass 2, strike out the following elements completely:
1. **Illustrative Examples & Anecdotes**: Stories, personal experiences, or hypothetical scenarios (*"For instance, when a merchant in a rural village..."* ➔ Strike out entirely).
2. **Statistical Lists & Catalogues of Data**: Dense numerical enumerations (*"In 2018 it was 4.2%, in 2019 it rose to 5.1%, and by 2021 reached 6.8%"* ➔ Condense to: *"Over recent years, the metric exhibited a steady upward trajectory"*).
3. **Rhetorical Questions**: Authors use rhetorical questions for dramatic effect (*"Can any civilized society permit such inequality?"* ➔ Convert to neutral assertion: *"Inequality threatens social cohesion"*).
4. **Direct Quotations & Dialogue**: Convert all quoted speech into indirect, matter-of-fact statements.
5. **Redundant Synonyms & Tautologies**: Authors often use pairs of words for emphasis (*"fair and equitable," "completely and utterly destroyed"* ➔ Retain only the singular, strongest term).
6. **Digressions & Parenthetical Aside**: Background tangents unrelated to the core thesis.

## 4. Voice, Tense & Structural Discipline

### 4.1 Strict Third-Person Conversion
A précis must be written from an objective, detached perspective. 
* Never use first-person pronouns (*I, me, my, we, our*) or second-person pronouns (*you, your*), even if the original author wrote entirely in the first person.
* *Original*: *"In my thirty years of clinical practice, I have observed that sleep deprivation erodes cognitive performance."*
* *Précis*: *"Clinical evidence demonstrates that sleep deprivation impairs cognitive performance."*

### 4.2 Tense Consistency & Indirect Speech
* If the original passage reports historical events, use the past tense.
* If the original passage outlines permanent scientific principles, economic laws, or current policy dilemmas, use the present tense consistently.
* All direct quotations must be transformed into indirect discourse:
  * *Original*: *The governor warned, "We must remain vigilant against inflation."*
  * *Précis*: *The governor urged sustained vigilance against inflationary pressures.*

### 4.3 The Single Continuous Paragraph Rule
Unless the original passage exceeds 500 words and contains fundamentally irreconcilable thematic halves, **a précis must always be composed as a single, unbroken paragraph**. Fragmenting a 100-word précis into multiple small paragraphs is heavily penalized.

## 5. The Titling Algorithm: Crafting the High-Yield Title

The title of a précis is not an afterthought; in most scoring rubrics, the title carries **3 to 5 marks** out of a total of 30. A vague or generic title forfeits these marks immediately.

### 5.1 The Three Inviolable Titling Rules
1. **Title Case Capitalization**: Capitalize all nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and subordinating conjunctions. Lowercase articles (*a, an, the*), coordinating conjunctions (*and, but, or*), and short prepositions (*in, on, at, of, for, to*), unless they are the first word.
   * *Incorrect*: *the challenges of digital banking in rural areas* ❌
   * *Correct*: *Challenges of Digital Banking in Rural Areas* ✅
2. **No Terminal Period**: Never place a period/full-stop at the end of a title.
3. **Conciseness**: The ideal title spans **3 to 7 words**. Never write a full grammatical sentence as a title.

### 5.2 The Two-Part Master Titling Formula

$$\text{Title} = [\text{Central Subject / Phenomenon}] + [\text{Operational Focus, Tension, or Horizon}]$$

| Original Passage Theme | Weak / Amateur Title (Score: 1/5) | Master Codex Title (Score: 5/5) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Passage on climate finance and developing nations** | *Global Warming and Money* | *Climate Finance: Imperatives and Global Inequities* |
| **Passage on central bank independence and state pressure** | *The Reserve Bank of India* | *Central Bank Autonomy in Macroeconomic Governance* |
| **Passage on artificial intelligence displacing labor** | *Robots Taking Our Jobs* | *Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Labor* |
| **Passage on groundwater depletion in Punjab agriculture** | *Water Crisis in Punjab* | *Groundwater Depletion: Agricultural Perils and Sustainable Solutions* |

## 6. The 60-Second Précis Memory Skeleton

```text
Précis Rule: Exact 1/3rd word budget (±5% tolerance window) 
➔ Single continuous paragraph (no line breaks) ➔ Strict Third Person (NO "I", "We", "You") 
➔ 100% Non-Verbatim Paraphrasing ➔ 0 personal opinions, 0 external data added 
➔ Cut 6 Noise Types: Examples, Long Statistics, Rhetorical Questions, Direct Quotes, Tautologies, Digressions 
➔ Title Algorithm: 3 to 7 words in Title Case (Capitalize Major Words, No Period at end) 
➔ Formula: [Theme] + [Tension / Solution].
```

## 7. Active Recall Diagnostic Flashcards

#### Card 1
* **Diagnostic Question**: *If an original passage has 360 words, what is the acceptable word count range for the précis on the TCS iON exam console?*
* **Answer**: The theoretical one-third target is 120 words. Applying the ±5% tolerance band, the ideal word count is between **114 and 126 words**. Any text below 108 or above 132 words risks direct mark deduction.

#### Card 2
* **Diagnostic Sentence**: *Identify the fatal flaw: In a précis of an author arguing for nuclear energy, the candidate writes in the final line: "However, in my opinion, solar energy is a safer alternative."*
* **Answer**: Severe violation of précis objectivity. A candidate must never insert personal opinions, subjective commentary, or external alternatives not present in the original text. The précis must exclusively reflect the original author's arguments.

#### Card 3
* **Diagnostic Question**: *How should numbers and specific statistics in the original text be handled in a précis?*
* **Answer**: Statistical lists, dates, and micro-figures should almost always be deleted or abstracted into conceptual trends (e.g., replacing *"exports were \$10B in 2020, \$12B in 2021, and \$15B in 2022"* with *"exports expanded consistently over the three-year period"*), unless a single central figure is the foundational anchor of the entire text.
