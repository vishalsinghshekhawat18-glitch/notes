## 6.1 The Linguistic Theory of Prepositional Dependency

Prepositions are relational function words that establish spatial, temporal, or logical vectors between nouns, verbs, and clauses. While ordinary prepositions govern physical space (*"on the desk"*, *"under the bridge"*), **Fixed Prepositions** operate through **lexicalized dependency**: the preceding verb, adjective, or noun exerts an immutable syntactic demand for one specific relational particle.

In competitive examinations (RBI Grade B Phase II, SBI/IBPS PO, UPSC CSE, RPSC RAS), fixed prepositions are tested heavily in:
1. **Error Spotting**: Inserting ungrammatical prepositions (*"cope up with"* instead of *"cope with"*).
2. **Cloze Tests & Sentence Fillers**: Blank spaces immediately following dependent verbs.
3. **Descriptive Writing Rigor**: Eliminating clumsy colloquial prepositions from formal essays and official correspondence.

```
[ Governing Lexical Head: Verb / Adjective / Noun ] ──► [ IMMUTABLE FIXED PREPOSITION ] ──► [ Object / Complement ]
Example: Accuse ─────────────────────────────────────► OF ──────────────────────────────► The Crime
Example: Comply ─────────────────────────────────────► WITH ────────────────────────────► The Statutory Rule
```

## 6.2 The Master Contextual Bifurcation Matrix (Contrasting Relational Triggers)

The most high-frequency examination traps arise when a single root verb demands **different prepositions depending on whether the object is a Person, a Proposal, a Condition, or an Abstract Thing**:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE DUAL-TRIGGER DEPENDENCY MATRIX                                                     │
├────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Verb / Adjective   │ Preposition & Target Object │ High-Yield Contextual Exemplar      │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Agree**          │ • **WITH** a person         │ *I agree WITH the Governor.*        │
│                    │ • **TO** a proposal/plan    │ *The board agreed TO the merger.*   │
│                    │ • **ON** a matter/subject   │ *They agreed ON the policy agenda.* │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Die**            │ • **OF** a disease/hunger   │ *He died OF cholera / malaria.*     │
│                    │ • **FROM** a cause/wound    │ *He died FROM overwork / loss of    │
│                    │                             │  blood / food poisoning.*           │
│                    │ • **FOR** a country/cause   │ *Soldiers died FOR the nation.*     │
│                    │ • **IN** an accident/harness│ *The officer died IN harness.*      │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Part**           │ • **WITH** a thing/property │ *He refused to part WITH his shares.*│
│                    │ • **FROM** a person         │ *She wept when parting FROM him.*   │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Blind**          │ • **IN** an eye (physical)  │ *He is blind IN the left eye.*      │
│                    │ • **TO** one's faults       │ *The management was blind TO risk.* │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Differ**         │ • **WITH** a person (views) │ *The member differed WITH the chair.*│
│                    │ • **FROM** a thing (unlike) │ *The new policy differs FROM 2015.* │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Compare**        │ • **TO** dissimilar things  │ *He compared the rupee TO a ship.*  │
│                    │ • **WITH** similar things   │ *Compare this GDP WITH last year.*  │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Correspond**     │ • **WITH** a person (write) │ *He corresponded WITH the director.*│
│                    │ • **TO** a thing (match)    │ *The figures correspond TO reality.*│
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Reconcile**      │ • **WITH** an opponent      │ *He reconciled WITH his rivals.*    │
│                    │ • **TO** a harsh destiny    │ *He reconciled himself TO defeat.*  │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤

│                    │ • **TO** an authority       │ *The CEO accounts TO the board.*    │
└────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 6.3 The Alphabetical Fixed Preposition Vault (A to Z Master Index)

Synthesized directly from *Vocab Prodigy* (Chapter 15) and *The Black Book of English Vocabulary*:

### Category A: Dependencies with "TO"

* **Accustomed TO**: *accustomed to working late* (Note: takes gerund *-ing*!).
* **Adhere TO**: *adhere to statutory compliance guidelines*.
* **Allude TO**: *allude to previous fiscal imbalances*.
* **Averse TO**: *averse to taking unhedged currency risk*.
* **Conform TO**: *conform to international environmental standards*.
* **Conducive TO**: *an environment conducive to economic investment*.
* **Detrimental TO**: *monetary volatility is detrimental to long-term growth*.
* **Eligible TO** (do something) vs. **Eligible FOR** (a position/benefit).
* **Impervious TO**: *impervious to external political lobbying*.
* **Incidental TO**: *costs incidental to sovereign debt restructuring*.
* **Indigenous TO**: *tribal crafts indigenous to southern Rajasthan*.
* **Inured TO**: *inured to market volatility and price shocks*.
* **Lacking in** vs. **Wanting in**.
* **Parallel TO**: *a development corridor parallel to the expressway*.
* **Pertain TO**: *regulations pertaining to non-banking financial entities*.
* **Preferable TO**: *long-term bonds are preferable to volatile equities* (Never *than*!).
* **Prone TO**: *fragile economies prone to balance-of-payments shocks*.
* **Reluctant TO**: *reluctant to increase statutory provisioning*.
* **Succumb TO**: *the enterprise succumbed to structural insolvency*.
* **Subservient TO**: *monetary policy must not be subservient to fiscal dominance*.
* **Tantamount TO**: *currency debasement tantamount to stealth taxation*.

### Category B: Dependencies with "FROM" (Prevention & Separation)

Verbs of prevention, abstention, and derivation command **FROM** (followed by a Gerund):

* **Abstain FROM**: *abstain from voting / alcohol*.
* **Refrain FROM**: *refrain from making speculative claims*.
* **Prohibit FROM**: *prohibit the NBFC from accepting retail deposits*.
* **Prevent FROM**: *prevent capital flight from destabilizing the currency*.
* **Deter FROM**: *stringent fines deter corporations from polluting*.
* **Debar FROM**: *debarred from appearing in sovereign civil examinations*.
* **Desist FROM**: *the court ordered the firm to desist from commercial trading*.
* **Exonerate FROM**: *exonerated from all criminal charges of bribery*.
* **Absolve FROM**: *absolved from their contractual financial liabilities*.
* **Derive FROM**: *principles derived from constitutional jurisprudence*.
* **Emanate FROM**: *policy signals emanating from the central bank*.
* **Deviate FROM**: *deviate from the glide path of fiscal deficit reduction*.

* **Abound WITH**: *forests abounding with biodiversity* (or *abound in*).

* **Fraught WITH**: *a geopolitical corridor fraught with military hazards*.

### Category D: Dependencies with "OF" (Condition, Deprivation & Cognition)

## 6.4 The 10 Deadliest Fixed Preposition Traps

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 1: The "Cope Up With" Colloquial Disaster**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The staff could not cope up with the heavy workload."*
> *Correction*: The phrase *"cope up with"* is completely non-existent in standard English. The only correct idiom is **Cope WITH**.
> *Corrected*: *"The staff could not **cope with** the heavy workload."*

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 2: The "Comprise Of" Redundancy**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The monetary council comprises of nine distinguished members."*
> *Correction*: **Comprise** takes zero preposition in the active voice! Use *comprises nine members*, or *consists of nine members*.

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 3: Prevent / Prohibit + TO + Infinitive**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The magistrate prohibited the demonstrators to march on the highway."*
> *Correction*: Verbs of prevention command **FROM + GERUND**: *"prohibited the demonstrators **from marching** on the highway."*

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 4: Accused With vs. Charged With**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The promoter was accused with financial embezzlement."*
> *Correction*: You are **Accused OF** a crime, but **Charged WITH** a crime! Correct: *"...accused **of** financial embezzlement."*

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 5: Apprise vs. Appraise Prepositions**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The manager appraised the board about the fiscal risks."*
> *Correction*: **Appraise** means to evaluate value (takes a direct object, no prep). **Apprise** means to inform and requires **OF**: *"The manager **apprised the board of** the fiscal risks."*

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 6: The "Order For" Commercial Pitfall**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The procurement officer ordered for ten desktop computers."*
> *Correction*: As a verb, **Order** is transitive and takes a direct object with zero preposition: *"The officer **ordered ten** desktop computers."* (Use *for* only when *order* is a noun: *"placed an order for ten..."*).

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 7: Discuss / Describe / Explain + ABOUT**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The committee discussed about the new labor codes."*
> *Correction*: **Discuss, describe, explain, examine, analyze** take direct objects with **ZERO "ABOUT"**: *"The committee **discussed the new labor codes**."*

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 8: Resemble + TO / WITH**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The new banking app resembles with its global competitors."*
> *Correction*: **Resemble** is transitive and takes zero preposition: *"The new banking app **resembles its global competitors**."*

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 9: Enter INTO (Physical Space)**
> *Exam Trap*: *"The Governor entered into the currency vault."*
> *Correction*: Entering physical space takes zero preposition (*"entered the currency vault"*). **Enter into** is used only for agreements, negotiations, or discussions (*"entered into a treaty"*).

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 10: Preferable THAN**
> *Exam Trap*: *"Public transport is preferable than private vehicle usage."*
> *Correction*: **Preferable** is followed by **TO**, never *than*: *"Public transport is **preferable to** private vehicle usage."*

## 6.5 The 60-Second Memory Skeleton (Rapid Recall)

* **NEVER SAY**:
  * *Cope up with* ──► Use **Cope WITH**.
  * *Comprises of* ──► Use **Comprises** (or *consists of*).
  * *Ordered for lunch* ──► Use **Ordered lunch**.
  * *Discussed about policy* ──► Use **Discussed policy**.
  * *Resembles with him* ──► Use **Resembles him**.
  * *Entered into the room* ──► Use **Entered the room** (vs. *entered into an agreement*).
* **Prevention + FROM + Gerund**: *Abstain, refrain, prevent, prohibit, debar, desist FROM doing*.
* **Dependencies with TO**: *Adhere to, accustomed to (-ing), averse to (-ing), conducive to, detrimental to, prone to (-ing), succumb to, preferable to*.
* **Dependencies with WITH**: *Comply with, coincide with, commensurate with, grapple with, dispense with, vie with*.
* **Dependencies with OF**: *Accuse of (vs. charged with), acquit of, apprise of, bereft of, cognizant of, devoid of*.
* **Contrasting Triggers**:
  * *Agree with* (person) vs. *Agree to* (plan).
  * *Die of* (disease) vs. *Die from* (wound/cause).
  * *Part with* (property) vs. *Part from* (person).
  * *Blind in* (eye) vs. *Blind to* (defects).
  * *Differ with* (person) vs. *Differ from* (dissimilar).

## 6.6 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

#### Card 1 (The "Cope With" & "Comprise" Mechanical Audit)
**Scenario**: Identify both grammatical errors in the following dispatch from a regional banking circular:  
*"The branch staff struggled to cope up with the influx of new retail customers, especially since the branch infrastructure only comprises of four customer-service counters."*
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
The sentence contains two major fixed preposition violations:<br/>
1. <strong>"Cope up with"</strong>: This is a ubiquitous colloquial Indianism that is strictly ungrammatical in formal English. The verb <em>cope</em> expresses managing difficulties successfully and requires only the preposition <strong>with</strong> (<em>to cope with the influx</em>). The intrusive particle <em>up</em> must be eliminated.<br/>
2. <strong>"Comprises of"</strong>: The transitive verb <em>comprise</em> means "to contain" or "to be composed of". In the active voice, it takes a direct object with zero preposition. Writing <em>comprises of</em> is an ungrammatical redundancy.<br/>
<strong>Corrected Sentence</strong>: <em>"The branch staff struggled to <strong>cope with</strong> the influx of new retail customers, especially since the branch infrastructure only <strong>comprises</strong> four customer-service counters."</em>
</details>

#### Card 2 (Contrasting Relational Triggers: Part With vs. Part From)
**Scenario**: In a probate case involving ancestral estate distribution, an heir testifies:  
*"It was agonizing for me to part from my ancestral agricultural land, but I gladly parted with my estranged siblings."*  
Explain why this testimony reverses the legal meaning through incorrect prepositional selection, and state the exact governing rules.
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
<strong>The Pathology</strong>: The witness has inverted the fixed prepositions governing the verb <em>part</em>:<br/>
• <strong>Part WITH</strong> applies strictly to <strong>inanimate objects, property, money, or land</strong> (meaning to relinquish possession or surrender ownership). You part <em>with</em> land.<br/>
• <strong>Part FROM</strong> applies strictly to <strong>living persons or human companions</strong> (meaning to separate from, leave, or say farewell). You part <em>from</em> siblings.<br/>
<strong>Impact of Inversion</strong>: As written, the heir claims they parted company from physical dirt as if it were a human friend, while treating their biological siblings as physical property to be surrendered!<br/>
<strong>Corrected Sentence</strong>: <em>"It was agonizing for me to <strong>part with</strong> my ancestral agricultural land, but I gladly <strong>parted from</strong> my estranged siblings."</em>
</details>

#### Card 3 (Transitive Verbs with Zero Prepositions)
**Scenario**: Explain why adding the preposition *about* in *"The parliamentary committee discussed about the semiconductor incentive scheme"* is a grammatical error under formal syntactic rules.
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
<strong>The Syntactic Mechanism</strong>: The verb <strong>Discuss</strong> derives from Latin <em>discutere</em> (to dash apart, investigate). In English syntax, it is a strictly <strong>monotransitive verb</strong>. Transitive verbs act directly upon their direct object without an intervening preposition. The semantic concept of "about" is already embedded within the core definition of the verb <em>discuss</em> (meaning "to talk about"). Introducing <em>about</em> creates an ungrammatical prepositional redundancy.<br/>
<strong>Rule</strong>: <em>Discuss, describe, analyze, explain, emphasize, order (verb), resemble</em> command direct nominal objects with <strong>ZERO prepositions</strong>.<br/>
<strong>Corrected Sentence</strong>: <em>"The parliamentary committee <strong>discussed the semiconductor incentive scheme</strong>."</em>
</details>
