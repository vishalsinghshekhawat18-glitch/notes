### Why India Measures Poverty via Consumption Expenditure (MPCE) Rather than Income:
In developed economies, poverty is assessed via reported tax returns and wage data. In India, official poverty estimation has historically utilized **Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure (MPCE)** compiled by NSSO/NSO household consumer expenditure surveys:
1. **Pervasive Informality**: Approximately 90% of India's workforce is informal, characterized by cash transactions, self-employment, and irregular wages with zero tax filing.
2. **Agricultural Seasonality**: Rural incomes fluctuate violently between bumper harvests and monsoon droughts. Consumption expenditure is smoothed over time and reflects actual living standards far more accurately.
3. **Under-Reporting & Concealment**: Households systematically under-report income due to tax avoidance or suspicion, whereas daily consumption of food, kerosene, soap, and clothing is reported with higher fidelity.

> **METHODOLOGICAL CLARIFICATION: OFFICIAL BENCHMARK VS. "STATUTORY" POVERTY LINE**
> India has never enacted a statutory poverty line by an Act of Parliament. Later administrative programs shifted to multi-dimensional poverty targeting (MPI) and the SECC 2011 exclusion criteria.

## 15.4 The Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)

Monetary poverty lines measure income/expenditure poverty, ignoring whether a family actually has clean drinking water, electricity, schooling, or sanitation. The **Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)**, developed using the **Alkire-Foster (AF) methodology**, measures simultaneous overlapping deprivations.

### The Mathematical Formula of MPI:
The MPI is the product of two distinct metrics:

$$\mathbf{MPI = H \times A}$$

1. **Headcount Ratio ($H$)**: The proportion of the population that is multidimensionally poor:
   $$H = \frac{q}{N} \quad (\text{where } q = \text{number of multidimensionally poor individuals})$$
2. **Intensity of Poverty ($A$)**: The average proportion of weighted deprivation indicators experienced by the poor:
   $$A = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{q} c_i}{q} \quad (\text{where } c_i = \text{deprivation score of poor person } i)$$

> **Empirical Milestone**: As per NITI Aayog's National MPI Review, multidimensional poverty in India plunged from **29.17% in 2013–14 to 11.28% in 2022–23**, representing an unprecedented escape from multidimensional poverty for **24.8 crore citizens** (spearheaded by rural electrification, PM Ujjwala gas, Jal Jeevan Mission, and SBM sanitation).

## 15.5 Inequality Metrics & The Mathematics of Distribution

Economic inequality evaluates the disparity with which total national income, wealth, or consumption is shared across society.

### 1. The Lorenz Curve:
Originated by Max O. Lorenz (1905), it is a graphical representation of cumulative income distribution:
* **Horizontal Axis**: Cumulative percentage of population (ranked from poorest to richest).
* **Vertical Axis**: Cumulative percentage of total national income or wealth earned.
* **Line of Perfect Equality (45° Diagonal)**: Every decile of the population receives the exact same share of national income (e.g., 20% of people earn 20% of income).
* **The Lorenz Curve**: Always bows beneath the 45° line. The further it sags away from the diagonal, the higher the inequality in the economy.

```

        ▲
   100% ┼─────────────────────────────────────────────╭───┤ B
        │                                          ╭───
```

---

### 2. The Gini Coefficient:
Formulated by Corrado Gini (1912), it quantifies the Lorenz Curve into a single mathematical index:

$$\mathbf{\text{Gini Coefficient}} = \frac{\mathbf{\text{Area } A}}{\mathbf{\text{Area } A + \text{Area } B}}$$

* **Gini = 0**: **Perfect Equality** (Lorenz curve coincides with the 45° line; every citizen has identical income).
* **Gini = 1 (or 100)**: **Perfect Inequality** (One single individual captures 100% of national income, while everyone else earns ₹0).
* **Wealth vs. Consumption Gini in India**:
  * **Consumption Expenditure Gini** in India is moderate (~**0.35 to 0.37**).
  * **Wealth Gini** in India is extreme (~**0.82 to 0.84**), demonstrating massive concentration of physical capital, real estate, and equity ownership among the top decile.

---

### 3. Alternative Distributional Indicators:

| Distribution Metric | Mathematical Formulation | Epistemic Interpretation & Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Palma Ratio** | $$\text{Palma Ratio} = \frac{\text{Income Share of Richest 10\%}}{\text{Income Share of Poorest 40\%}}$$ | Gabriel Palma observed that middle-class (deciles 5 to 9) capture ~50% of income across all societies; inequality is driven almost entirely by the tug-of-war between top 10% and bottom 40%. Palma $> 1$ indicates significant disparity. |
| **Poverty Gap Index (PGI)** | $$\text{PGI} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{q} \left(\frac{z - y_i}{z}\right)$$ | Measures the **depth of poverty** (the average cash shortfall of poor individuals below the poverty line $z$ as a share of $z$). Reflects how much resources are needed to eliminate poverty. |
| **Squared Poverty Gap (SPG)** *(Poverty Severity Index)* | Squares the poverty gap of each poor individual before averaging: $\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{q} \left(\frac{z - y_i}{z}\right)^2$ | Gives disproportionate mathematical weight to the poorest of the poor (measures **severity of deprivation**). |

## 15.7 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

```
CARD 1: Why did the Suresh Tendulkar Committee abandon calorie norms for estimating poverty in India?
ANSWER:
Tendulkar observed that calorie consumption was steadily declining across all expenditure classes
even as incomes and real living standards were rising (the "calorie consumption puzzle").
Anchoring poverty solely to calories would have absurdly shown rising poverty during rapid economic
growth. Therefore, Tendulkar decoupled poverty from calories, anchored it to urban living standards,
and incorporated explicit spending on health and education under MRP.
```

```
CARD 2: Contrast the Uniform Reference Period (URP), Mixed Reference Period (MRP), and Modified Mixed Reference Period (MMRP).
ANSWER:
1. URP: 30-day recall for ALL goods and services.
2. MRP: 365-day recall for 5 infrequent non-food items (clothing, footwear, durables, education,
institutional medical); 30-day recall for all remaining food/non-food items.
3. MMRP: 7-day recall for high-frequency perishable food items (edible oil, eggs, fish, meat,
vegetables, fruits, spices); 365-day recall for 5 infrequent non-food items; 30-day recall for rest.
```

```
CARD 3: Define the Lorenz Curve and the Gini Coefficient. How is the Gini Coefficient mathematically derived from the Lorenz Curve?
ANSWER:
1. Lorenz Curve: A graphical plot of cumulative percentage of population against cumulative
percentage of national income/wealth received, compared against the 45-degree Line of Perfect Equality.
2. Gini Coefficient: A mathematical measure of inequality derived as the ratio:
   Gini = Area A / (Area A + Area B)
   where Area A is the area between the 45-degree line and the Lorenz Curve, and Area B is the area
   underneath the Lorenz Curve. Ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality).
```
