## 19.1 Structural Realities & The Agrarian Dualism

Indian agriculture presents a classic structural macroeconomic paradox:
* **Output Contribution**: Agriculture and allied sectors contribute approximately **16% to 18% of India's Gross Value Added (GVA)**.
* **Workforce Absorption**: Agriculture employs approximately **45% to 46% of the country's total labor force**.

$$\mathbf{\text{Agricultural Productivity Mismatch}}: \quad \frac{\text{Workforce Share (46\%)}}{\text{GVA Share (17\%)}} \approx 2.7 \times \text{ Dilution of Labor Value}$$

This profound mismatch reflects widespread **disguised unemployment**, where millions of surplus laborers are trapped on sub-economic plots with near-zero marginal productivity ($MP_L \approx 0$).

1. **Abolition of Intermediaries (1950s)**:
   - Brought approximately **20 million tenants** into direct legal contact with the State and brought ~6 million hectares of uncultivated waste/forest land under public control.
   - *The Structural Flaw*: Legislation permitted ex-intermediaries to retain land under **"Personal Cultivation" (*Sir/Khudkasht*)**, without defining personal cultivation as physical labor. This triggered mass illegal evictions of tenants under the pretext of self-cultivation.
2. **Tenancy Reforms**:
   - *Rent Regulation*: Reduced customary feudal rents (which often exceeded 50%–60% of gross crop output) to statutory ceilings of **20% to 25% (1/4th to 1/5th)** across most States.
   - *Security of Tenure*: Prohibited arbitrary evictions; tenants could only be evicted for non-payment of rent or non-agricultural use of land.
   - *Ownership Conferment*: Enabled tenants to acquire permanent ownership rights upon payment of nominal compensation.
   - *The Reality*: Led to the underground proliferation of **informal, unrecorded oral tenancies (*Batai-dar / Adhiyar*)**, denying sharecroppers access to bank credit, crop insurance, or disaster relief.
3. **Land Ceilings Legislation (1960s–1970s)**:
   - Following the 1972 National Guidelines, ceilings were fixed per family of 5 members:
     * **10 to 18 acres** for land with assured perennial irrigation (capable of two crops).
     * **27 acres** for single-crop irrigated land.
     * **Up to 54 acres** for dry/unirrigated land.
   - *Implementation Deficit*: Landowners systematically circumvented ceilings through **fictitious/Benami transfers** in the names of distant relatives, domestic workers, pets, and religious trusts, coupled with protracted civil court litigation.
   - *Notable Exceptions*: **West Bengal (*Operation Barga*, 1978)** under the Left Front successfully registered 1.5 million sharecroppers (*Bargadars*), conferring permanent hereditary tenancy rights; **Kerala** enforced comprehensive land redistribution via Village Land Tribunals.
4. **Consolidation of Operational Holdings (*Chakbandi*)**:
   - Reduced operational fragmentation by consolidating a farmer's disparate parcels into a single consolidated farm block.
   - Achieved massive success in **Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh**, creating the contiguous farm plots that enabled tractorization and tubewell installation during the Green Revolution. Stalled across southern and eastern states due to intense farmer fears of receiving less fertile replacement soils.

### 3. Second-Generation (21st-Century) Land Reforms

Traditional land reforms focused on redistribution. Modern land reforms focus on **legal security, digital transparency, and lease markets**:

#### A. NITI Aayog Model Land Leasing Act, 2016:
* **The Problem**: Existing state laws either completely banned agricultural land leasing or placed such severe tenancy rights on leased land that landowners preferred keeping land fallow rather than leasing it out, fearing loss of ownership title.
* **The Solution**: The Model Act:
  1. **Legalizes Agricultural Land Leasing**: Allows voluntary lease agreements between land-owners and actual cultivators without creating tenancy rights or transferring ownership title.
  2. **Automatic Reversion**: Land automatically reverts to the owner upon expiration of the lease term.
  3. **Access to Credit & Benefits**: Enables tenant farmers to access institutional crop loans, Kisan Credit Cards (KCC), crop insurance (PMFBY), and input subsidies.

#### B. Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP):
* Administered by the **Department of Land Resources (DoLR)**, Ministry of Rural Development.
* Replaces the colonial presumptive title system with a **conclusive land titling system with state-guaranteed title** (Torrens System).
* **Key Components**:
  1. *Computerization of Record of Rights (RoR)*: 100% digitization of land mutation and ownership registries.
  2. *Digitization of Cadastral Maps*: Geo-referencing of spatial parcel boundaries using drone surveys and GIS.
  3. **ULPIN (Unique Land Parcel Identification Number) / "Bhu-Aadhaar"**: A **14-digit alphanumeric identification number** uniquely identifying every surveyed land parcel in India based on its precise longitude and latitude coordinates.
  4. *SVAMITVA Scheme (Survey of Villages and Mapping with Improvised Technology in Village Areas)*: Uses high-resolution drone mapping to issue legal **Property Cards** for rural inhabited (*Abadi*) lands.

#### C. The LARR Act, 2013 (Right to Fair Compensation & Transparency in Land Acquisition):
* Replaced the draconian colonial *Land Acquisition Act, 1894*.
* **Key Transformative Mandates**:
  1. **Mandatory Social Impact Assessment (SIA)**: Evaluates the project's impact on local livelihood, ecology, and food security before acquisition approval.
  2. **Mandatory Prior Consent Clauses**:
     * **70% Prior Consent** of affected landowners for Public-Private Partnership (PPP) projects.
     * **80% Prior Consent** for purely private commercial projects.
     * *Zero consent required* only for sovereign defense, national infrastructure, and public purpose projects.
  3. **Generous Solatium & Compensation**:
     * **Rural Areas**: Market Value multiplied by a factor of 1 to 2, PLUS **100% Solatium** (effectively **up to 4 times the registered market value**).
     * **Urban Areas**: Market Value, PLUS **100% Solatium** (effectively **2 times the market value**).
  4. **Comprehensive Rehabilitation & Resettlement (R&R)**: Guarantees housing, land-for-land, and employment to affected families (including agricultural laborers who do not own land).

### 3. Statutory & Policy Architecture for Irrigation

1. **Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)**:
   * Launched in **2015** with the overarching vision of extending irrigation coverage (*"Har Khet Ko Pani"*) and improving on-farm water use efficiency (*"Per Drop More Crop"*).
   * **The Four Integrated Pillars**:
     * *Accelerated Irrigation Benefit Programme (AIBP)*: MoJS (Department of Water Resources) — funding fast-track completion of ongoing major and medium multipurpose canal irrigation projects.
     * *PMKSY-Har Khet Ko Pani (HKKP)*: Creation of new minor surface water sources, restoration of water bodies, and command area development.
     * *PMKSY-Per Drop More Crop (PDMC)*: Managed by the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare — dedicated capital subsidies (up to 55% for small/marginal farmers, 45% for other farmers) to install **Drip and Sprinkler systems**.
     * *PMKSY-Watershed Development*: Managed by DoLR — in-situ soil and moisture conservation, check dams, and rainwater harvesting structures in rainfed districts.
2. **Atal Bhujal Yojana (Atal Jal)**:
   * A **₹6,000 Crore**, World Bank-assisted central sector scheme operational across **78 water-stressed districts** in 7 States (Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh).
   * **Core Philosophy**: Transitions from supply-side exploitation to **community-led groundwater demand management**, linking funding directly to behavioral changes, water budgeting, and water security plans prepared by Gram Panchayats.
3. **Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA)**:
   * Constituted under **Section 3(3) of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
   * Regulates commercial, industrial, and infrastructure groundwater extraction. Categorizes administrative assessment units (blocks/talukas) into:
     - **Safe** (extraction $< 70\%$ of annual recharge).
     - **Semi-Critical** (70%–90%).
     - **Critical** (90%–100%).
     - **Over-Exploited** ($> 100\%$ extraction; extraction exceeds natural annual recharge!).

## 19.4 Capital Formation: The Subsidies vs. Investment Dilemma

### Gross Capital Formation (GCF) in Agriculture:
Gross Capital Formation represents physical investment in enduring agricultural productive capacity (canals, drip irrigation, cold chains, rural warehousing, soil testing labs, high-yielding seed R&D).

$$\mathbf{\text{GCF in Agriculture}} = \mathbf{\text{Public GCF (State Capex)}} + \mathbf{\text{Private GCF (Farmer Tractors, Tube-wells, Leveling)}}$$

```
                          GOVERNMENT SPENDING IN AGRICULTURE
                                           │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                   ▼
[REVENUE SUBSIDIES (CONSUMPTION)]                                   [CAPITAL FORMATION (INVESTMENT)]
• Fertilizer Subsidies (Urea/DAP)                                   • Micro-irrigation networks
• Free / Subsidized Electricity                                     • Cold storage & supply chains
• Canal water subsidies                                             • Agronomic biotechnology R&D
• Unconditional cash transfers (PM-KISAN)                           • Climate-resilient seeds
         │                                                                   │
         ▼                                                                   ▼
**Forms > 80% of Public Outlay**                                    **Stagnates at < 20% of Outlay**
(Crowds out long-term productive capex!)                            (High long-term multiplier: 1:4!)
```

* **The Crowding-Out Pathology**: Every ₹1 spent on agricultural R&D yields ₹11.20 in agricultural growth; every ₹1 spent on irrigation capex yields ₹3.20. In contrast, ₹1 spent on fertilizer subsidy yields only ₹0.88! Yet, fiscal resources are overwhelmingly consumed by open-ended consumption subsidies, starving the sector of durable capital formation.

### 3. The Three Cost Formulations of CACP

| Cost Measure | Specific Mathematical & Accounting Inclusions | Epistemic Character |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Cost A2** | Direct paid-out cash and in-kind expenditure incurred by the farmer on seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, manure, hired labor, machinery rentals, fuel, irrigation, and leased land rent. | **Actual Paid-Out Direct Expenses** |
| **2. Cost A2 + FL** | $$\text{Cost}(A2 + FL) = A2 + \text{Imputed Value of Unpaid Family Labor}$$ | Cost A2 PLUS imputed economic value of unpaid family labor (based on prevailing casual wage). |
| **3. Cost C2** | $$\text{Cost } C2 = (A2 + FL) + \text{Imputed Owned Land Rent} + \text{Imputed Fixed Capital Asset Interest}$$ | **Comprehensive Economic Cost**: Reflects full economic opportunity cost of all owned capital and land. |

---

### 4. The M.S. Swaminathan Committee Recommendation vs. Government Formula

| Parameter | M.S. Swaminathan Committee Recommendation (2006) | Government Operational Formula (Since 2018–19) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MSP Benchmark Formula** | $$\text{MSP} = C2 + 50\% = 1.5 \times C2$$ | $$\text{MSP} = 1.5 \times (A2 + FL)$$ |
| **Cost Inclusions** | 50% return over the **COMPREHENSIVE cost ($C2$)**, including imputed land rent and fixed asset capital interest. | 50% margin over paid-out costs + family labor, but **EXCLUDING** imputed land rent and owned capital interest. |
| **Farmer Demand Status** | Core statutory demand of national farmer protests for legal MSP guarantee. | Current operational price floor enacted in Union Budget 2018–19. |

> **The Statutory Trap**: Farmers demand legal enactment of the **$C2 + 50\%$** formula. The current operational policy gives a 50% profit margin over **$(A2 + FL)$**, which is significantly lower than $C2$.

### 5. Sugarcane Pricing: FRP vs. SAP
Sugarcane pricing operates under a unique statutory regime governed by the *Sugarcane (Control) Order, 1966* under the *Essential Commodities Act, 1955*:
* **Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP)**:
  * Recommends by CACP and announced by CCEA.
  * **Statutory Guarantee**: Sugar mills are legally obligated to pay the FRP to cane growers within 14 days of supply, irrespective of sugar market prices or mill profitability.
  * Linked to a basic sugar recovery rate (e.g., 10.25%, with a premium for higher recovery).
* **State Advised Price (SAP)**:
  * Certain states (UP, Punjab, Haryana) declare their own SAP, which is typically fixed substantially higher than the Centre's FRP, frequently leading to accumulated **cane payment arrears** when sugar prices fall.

## 19.6 Food Management Architecture: FCI, Buffer Stocks & Food Subsidy

The **Food Corporation of India (FCI)**, established under the *Food Corporations Act, 1964*, serves as the sovereign executing agency for India's food security policy.

```
                                    FOOD SUBSIDY MECHANISM
                                               │
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  ▼                                                                                         ▼
[ECONOMIC COST OF FCI]                                                            [CENTRAL ISSUE PRICE (CIP)]
• Acquisition Cost (MSP paid to farmers)                                          • Nominal price at which grains
• Procurement Incidentals (Mandi tax, bagging, gunny bags)                          are issued to PDS beneficiaries.

         │                                                                          PMGKAY since January 2023!
         └─────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                               ▼
                              $$\mathbf{\text{Food Subsidy}} =
               [\mathbf{\text{Economic Cost}} - \mathbf{\text{Central Issue Price}}]
                                  \times \mathbf{\text{Quantity}}$$
```

* **The Shanta Kumar Committee Recommendations (2015)**:
  1. **Outsource Procurement**: FCI should hand over grain procurement to States that have developed robust infrastructure (Punjab, Haryana, AP, MP, Chhattisgarh) and focus procurement solely on non-traditional eastern states (UP, Bihar, WB, Assam).
  2. **Reduce NFSA Coverage**: Slash National Food Security Act coverage from **67% down to 40%** to cover genuinely vulnerable households.
  3. **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)**: Transition gradually to cash transfers in lieu of physical grain distribution in major million-plus cities.
  4. **Privatize Warehousing**: Liquidate excess buffer stocks via open market operations (OMSS) and construct automated modern metal silos.

## 19.7 Agricultural Marketing & Supply Chain Bottlenecks

### 1. The APMC System & Market Distortions:
Under the Constitution (Seventh Schedule, State List Entry 28), agricultural marketing is a **State subject**. States enacted Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) Acts:
* **The Original Intent**: Protect farmers from exploitation by village moneylenders by creating notified market yards (mandis) with transparent open auctions.
* **The Degeneration**:
  * Mandi yards became monopolistic cartels dominated by licensed **Arhtiyas (Commission Agents)**.
  * Excessive intermediary levies: Mandi entry tax, rural development cess, handling fees, and commission charges (forming up to 10%–15% of produce value).
  * Fragmentation: Produce cannot move seamlessly across district or state borders without paying double mandi duties.

---

### 2. Reform Interventions:
* **National Agriculture Market (e-NAM)**:
  * Launched in April 2016 as a pan-India electronic trading portal integrating over 1,360 wholesale mandis across 23 States/UTs.
  * Creates a unified virtual national market through single-point mandi licensing, electronic auction bidding, online payments, and quality assaying labs.
* **Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs)**:
  * Aggregation vehicles (registered as Producer Companies under Companies Act) enabling small and marginal farmers to pool acreage, achieve bulk purchasing power in inputs (seeds, fertilizer), and gain direct bargaining power with corporate food processors, bypassing middlemen.
  * Central scheme: Formation and Promotion of **10,000 new FPOs**.

## 19.8 Priority Sector Lending & Agricultural Credit Architecture

To prevent smallholders from falling into the debt traps of informal usurious moneylenders, the RBI mandates agricultural credit allocations:
* **Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Mandate**: Commercial banks must allocate **18% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)** to Agriculture.
  * **Sub-target**: Within this 18%, at least **10% of ANBC** is ring-fenced exclusively for **Small and Marginal Farmers (SMFs)**.
* **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)**:
  * Introduced in 1998 (on R.V. Gupta Committee model); provides revolving crop loans and working capital.
  * Expanded to cover animal husbandry, dairy, and fisheries.
* **Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS)**:
  * Short-term crop loans up to **₹3 Lakh** are provided at a benchmark interest rate of 7%.
  * The Central Government provides an **Interest Subvention of 1.5%** to lending institutions, and an additional **3% Prompt Repayment Incentive (PRI)** to farmers who repay on time.
  * **Effective Interest Rate**: Farmers pay an effective interest rate of just **4% per annum** ($7\% - 3\%$)!

## 19.10 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

```
CARD 1: Explain the components of Cost A2, Cost A2+FL, and Cost C2 used by the CACP. Which formula did the Swaminathan Committee recommend?
ANSWER:
1. Cost A2: Direct out-of-pocket expenses (seeds, fertilizer, hired labor, fuel, leased land rent).
2. Cost A2+FL: Cost A2 + imputed value of unpaid family labor.
3. Cost C2: Comprehensive cost = (A2+FL) + imputed rent on owned land + imputed interest on owned fixed assets.
4. Swaminathan Committee Recommendation: Recommended MSP = C2 + 50%. (The Union Government operates on 1.5 * [A2 + FL]).
```

```
CARD 2: Detail the structural composition of the Food Corporation of India's (FCI) "Economic Cost" of foodgrains.
ANSWER:
Economic Cost of FCI is composed of three sequential elements:
1. Pooled Cost of Grain: The MSP paid directly to farmers.
2. Procurement Incidentals: Statutory mandi taxes, state cesses, arhtiya commissions, gunny bags, labor handling, and internal state transport.
3. Distribution Cost: Inter-state rail freight, central warehousing charges, storage transit losses, and administrative overheads.
```

```
CARD 3: Compare the consent and compensation provisions under the RFCTLARR Act, 2013 for Private vs. PPP vs. Public projects.
ANSWER:
1. Consent: Private projects require 80% prior consent of affected families; PPP projects require 70% consent; pure Government public purpose projects require 0% consent.
2. Compensation: Rural areas = up to 4x market value (market value * multiplying factor of 1-2 + 100% solatium); Urban areas = 2x market value (market value + 100% solatium).
3. SIA: Mandatory Social Impact Assessment before acquisition (unless emergency defence/disaster clause invoked).
```

```
CARD 4: What is ULPIN / Bhu-Aadhaar, and how does PMKSY divide its four implementation pillars?
ANSWER:
1. ULPIN: Unique Land Parcel Identification Number - a 14-digit alphanumeric geo-referenced identification based on latitude/longitude coordinates of plot corners under DILRMP.
2. PMKSY Pillars:
   a. Accelerated Irrigation Benefit Programme (AIBP) - Major/medium irrigation projects.
   b. Har Khet Ko Pani - Minor irrigation, water bodies restoration, command area development.
   c. Per Drop More Crop (PDMC) - Micro-irrigation (Drip and Sprinkler systems).
   d. Watershed Development - Ridge-to-valley soil moisture conservation, check dams.
```
