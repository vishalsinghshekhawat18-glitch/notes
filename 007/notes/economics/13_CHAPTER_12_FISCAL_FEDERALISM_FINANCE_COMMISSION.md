## 12.1 The Constitutional Architecture of Indian Fiscal Federalism

The economic structure of the Indian Union is characterized by a structural **Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI)**:
* **Taxing Powers**: Asymmetrically concentrated with the **Central Government** (Income tax, Corporate tax, Customs, CGST/IGST), which collects over **60% of total sovereign revenue**.
* **Spending Responsibilities**: Asymmetrically concentrated with the **State Governments** (Public health, agriculture, law and order, police, education, rural development), which execute over **60% of total public expenditure**.

To bridge this gap, the Constitution creates an elaborate institutional mechanism for revenue transfers from Union to States:

## 12.2 The Divisible Pool & The Surcharge/Cess Friction Point

### 1. What Enters the Divisible Pool? (Article 270)
Under Article 270 (as amended by the 80th Amendment Act, 2000), the **Divisible Pool** consists of the **Net Proceeds** of all Central taxes and duties, **WITH THREE STATUTORY EXCLUSIONS**:

$$\mathbf{\text{Divisible Pool}} = \text{Gross Tax Revenue (GTR)} - \Big[\mathbf{\text{Cesses}} + \mathbf{\text{Surcharges (Art 271)}} + \mathbf{\text{Cost of Collection}}\Big]$$

```
                            GROSS TAX REVENUE (GTR) OF CENTRE
                                            │
        ┌───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                                       ▼
[THE DIVISIBLE POOL (Art 270)]                                        [EXCLUDED PORTION]
• Corporation Tax                                                     • Surcharges (Article 271)
• Personal Income Tax                                                 • All Cesses (Health, Edu, Agri)
• Central GST (CGST)                                                  • Statutory Cost of Collection
• Share of IGST                                                       • Taxes under Articles 268 & 269
• Customs Duties & Central Excise (petrol)                                      │
        │                                                                       ▼
        ▼                                                             **Retained 100% by the**
**Shared with States (41% as per 15th FC)**                           **Central Government!**
```

---

### 2. The Structural Friction: Shrinking of the Divisible Pool
* **The Problem**: In recent decades, the Central Government has increasingly mobilized revenue by levying **Cesses and Surcharges** rather than raising basic tax rates.
* **The Arithmetic Distortion**:
  * In FY 2011–12, cesses and surcharges formed ~**9.4%** of the Centre's Gross Tax Revenue (GTR).
  * By FY 2021–24, cesses and surcharges climbed to nearly **18%–20%** of Central GTR!
* **The Impact on States**:
  * While the Finance Commission mandated a headline vertical devolution of **41%**, that 41% applies *only* to the Divisible Pool.
  * Because ~20% of GTR is carved out into cesses and surcharges, the **effective transfer to States as a percentage of Centre's total Gross Tax Revenue is only ~30%–32%**!
  * This has become one of the fiercest bones of contention between State Finance Ministers and the Union Ministry of Finance.

## 12.3 The Finance Commission (Article 280)

The Finance Commission is a constitutional, quasi-judicial body constituted by the **President of India** every **5 years** (or earlier if deemed necessary):

### 1. Statutory Composition & Qualifications:
As per the *Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951*:
* **Chairman**: A person having experience in public affairs.
* **Four Other Members** selected from among persons who:
  1. Are, or have been, or are qualified to be appointed as **Judges of a High Court**;
  2. Have special knowledge of the **finances and accounts of Government**;
  3. Have had wide experience in **financial matters and administration**;
  4. Have special knowledge of **economics**.

---

### 2. Core Constitutional Mandate (Article 280(3)):
It shall be the duty of the Commission to make recommendations to the President regarding:
1. **Vertical Devolution**: The distribution between the Union and the States of the net proceeds of taxes which are to be divided between them.
2. **Horizontal Devolution**: The allocation between the States of the respective shares of such proceeds.
3. **Grants-in-Aid**: The principles which should govern the grants-in-aid of the revenues of the States out of the Consolidated Fund of India (**Article 275**).
4. **Augmenting Consolidated Fund of States**: Measures needed to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of the **Panchayats and Municipalities** based on recommendations made by the State Finance Commission (added via the 73rd and 74th Amendments).
5. **Any Other Matter**: Referred to the Commission by the President in the interest of sound public finance (e.g., disaster risk management, defense funding).

> **Article 281**: The President must lay every recommendation of the Finance Commission, together with an **Explanatory Memorandum** as to the action taken thereon, before each House of Parliament.  
> **Legal Nature**: The recommendations of the Finance Commission are **advisory in nature**, not legally binding on the Government. However, by convention, recommendations regarding vertical and horizontal tax devolution are treated as binding and accepted without alteration.

## 12.5 The Typology of Financial Transfers: Devolution vs. Grants

Central resource transfers to States flow through three distinct legal-institutional channels:

```
                                  CENTRAL TRANSFERS TO STATES
                                              │
         ┌────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                    ▼                                    ▼
[TAX DEVOLUTION]                     [ARTICLE 275 GRANTS]                 [ARTICLE 282 GRANTS]
• Untied, unconditional              • Statutory Grants                   • Discretionary / Schematic
• Mandated by FC formula             • Recommended by Finance Commission  • Centrally Sponsored Schemes
• 41% of Divisible Pool              • Charged on CFI                     • Tied to Union conditions
```

### 1. Statutory Grants-in-Aid (Article 275):
Recommended by the Finance Commission and charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India:
* **Post-Devolution Revenue Deficit Grants (PDRDG)**: Given to bridge the assessed structural gap between a State's post-devolution revenue and its committed non-plan expenditures.
* **Local Body Grants**: Untied and tied grants distributed to Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs). Tied grants require mandatory spending on drinking water, rainwater harvesting, and sanitation/open-defecation-free (ODF) maintenance.
* **Disaster Management Grants**: Financed via National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF) / State Disaster Response Fund (SDRF) with standard cost-sharing (75:25 for general states; 90:10 for Himalayan/NE states).

## 12.6 State Borrowing Dynamics & Off-Budget Scrutiny (Article 293)

### 1. Constitutional Borrowing Constraints:
* **Article 292**: Parliament may by law set limits on the borrowing of the Central Government upon the security of the Consolidated Fund of India.
* **Article 293(1)**: State Legislatures may by law set limits on the borrowing of the State Government upon the security of the Consolidated Fund of the State.
* **Article 293(3) - The Sovereign Trap**:
  > *"A State may not without the consent of the Government of India raise any loan if there is still outstanding any part of a loan which has been made to the State by the Government of India or in respect of which a guarantee has been given by the Government of India."*
* Since **every single State in India has an outstanding debt or loan from the Centre**, no State can borrow a single rupee from domestic markets without prior approval from the Union Ministry of Finance!

---

### 2. The Net Borrowing Ceiling (NBC) & Off-Budget Borrowing Crackdown
* **Normal Limit**: Under FRBM guidelines, a State's Net Borrowing Ceiling is anchored at **3.0% of its GSDP** (with an additional **0.5% of GSDP** unconditional/conditional window linked to power distribution sector performance reforms).
* **The "Off-Budget Borrowing" (OBB) Loophole**:
  * Several States attempted to circumvent their 3.0% FRBM cap by creating State Public Sector Undertakings (SPSUs) or Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) that took bank loans or issued bonds.
  * While formally on the balance sheet of the PSU/SPV, the loan interest and principal were serviced directly from the State's budget, or the debt was backed by an unconditional sovereign state guarantee.
* **The 2022 Central Mandate**:
  * In March 2022, the Ministry of Finance clarified that **all off-budget borrowings raised by State entities where debt servicing is done from state budgetary resources or where state guarantees are pledged SHALL BE ADJUSTED AGAINST THE STATE'S NET BORROWING CEILING under Article 293(3)**.
  * Result: Restored macroeconomic transparency and halted hidden state indebtedness.

## 12.8 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

```
CARD 1: Explain the mathematical composition of the Divisible Pool under Article 270 and identify the three statutory exclusions.
ANSWER:
Divisible Pool = Gross Tax Revenue (GTR) of the Centre minus:
1. Surcharges levied under Article 271 for Central purposes;
2. All Cesses levied for specific statutory objectives;
3. Statutory Cost of Tax Collection.
The remaining net proceeds are shared between Union and States (currently 41% to States).
```

```
CARD 2: Detail the horizontal devolution formula of the 15th Finance Commission with exact percentage weights.
ANSWER:
1. Income Distance: 45.0% (Equity)
2. Population (2011 Census): 15.0% (Scale/Need)
3. Area: 15.0% (Cost disability)
4. Forest and Ecology: 10.0% (Ecological preservation)
5. Demographic Performance: 12.5% (Reward for TFR control)
6. Tax Effort: 2.5% (Fiscal efficiency)
Total: 100%
```

```
CARD 3: Under what constitutional condition can the Central Government veto or restrict a State's market borrowing?
ANSWER:
Under Article 293(3), if a State has any outstanding loan from the Central Government or if the
Centre has guaranteed any loan for the State, the State cannot borrow from the market without
prior consent of the Government of India. Since all Indian States have outstanding central loans,
the Centre sets annual Net Borrowing Ceilings (typically 3% of GSDP) for every State.
```
