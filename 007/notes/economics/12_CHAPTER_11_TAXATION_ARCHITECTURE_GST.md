## 11.1 The Theoretical Foundations of Taxation

Taxation represents the sovereign extraction of private financial resources to finance public goods, redistribute income, and correct market failures.

> **Article 265 of the Constitution of India**:  
> *"No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law."*  
> (Executive notifications cannot create taxes; every tax requires an explicit legislative Act passed by Parliament or State Legislatures).

### The Four Canons of Taxation (Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations*, 1776):
1. **Canon of Equity (Ability to Pay)**: Citizens should contribute to state support in proportion to their respective revenue abilities (proportional sacrifice; progressive taxation).
2. **Canon of Certainty**: The tax liability, payment timing, manner of payment, and exact amount must be unambiguous and predictable to both taxpayer and state.
3. **Canon of Convenience**: Taxes must be levied at the time and in the manner most convenient for the contributor (e.g., Tax Deducted at Source - TDS, at the moment income is earned).
4. **Canon of Economy**: The administrative cost of collection and compliance must be minimal relative to the revenue yielded.

* **Impact of Tax**: The initial point where the legal liability to pay the tax is statutorily assessed (upon whom the tax is imposed by law).

## 11.2 Classification of Tax Regimes & Elasticity Metrics

### 1. By Rate Progression:
* **Progressive Tax**: The marginal tax rate increases as the tax base (income) rises ($\frac{dT}{dY} > 0$). Reduces wealth concentration (e.g., Indian Personal Income Tax).
* **Proportional Tax (Flat Tax)**: The tax rate remains constant across all income brackets. High earners pay more in absolute quantum, but identical percentage.
* **Regressive Tax**: The effective tax rate decreases as income rises. In absolute terms, poor individuals spend a far larger proportion of their income on consumer taxes (e.g., indirect taxes on basic necessities).
* **Degressive Tax**: Progressive up to a certain income threshold, after which a flat proportional rate is charged for all higher brackets.

```
Tax Rate (%)
  ▲
  │        / Progressive
  │       /
  │──────/─────── Proportional (Flat)
  │     /
  │    / \
  │   /   \______ Degressive
  │  /
  │ /
  └──────────────────────────► Income / Tax Base
```

### 2. Tax Buoyancy vs. Tax Elasticity

These two mathematical indicators assess whether tax revenues are growing in tandem with national output:

| Indicator | Mathematical Formulation | Epistemic Significance & Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tax Buoyancy** | $$\text{Tax Buoyancy} = \frac{\% \Delta \text{Total Tax Revenue}}{\% \Delta \text{Nominal GDP}}$$ | • Measures responsiveness of tax revenue including **ALL discretionary policy measures** (rate changes, new levies, base broadening, anti-evasion drives).<br/>• If Buoyancy $> 1$, tax collections grow faster than national income. |
| **Tax Elasticity** | $$\text{Tax Elasticity} = \frac{\% \Delta \text{Tax Revenue (Automatic)}}{\% \Delta \text{Nominal GDP}}$$ | • Measures **pure automatic response** of tax yield to GDP growth **WITHOUT any discretionary tax changes** (holding rates and exemptions constant).<br/>• Isolates organic revenue growth from legislative tinkering. |

### 3. The Laffer Curve: Tax Rates vs. Revenue Optimization

Formulated by economist Arthur Laffer (1974), the curve illustrates the non-linear relationship between tax rates and total tax revenue:

```
Total Tax Revenue
       ▲
       │                  Peak Revenue ($R_{max}$)
       │                        ╭───╮
       │                     ╭──     ──╮
       │                   ╭─           ─╮
       │                 ╭─               ─╮
       │                ╭                   ╮
       │               ╭                     ╮
       │              ╭                       ╮
       │             ╭                         ╮
       │            ╭                           ╮
       └───────────┴─────────────────────────────┴────────► Tax Rate (%)
                   0%          Optimal ($t^*$)   100%
```

* **At 0% Tax Rate**: Revenue is ₹0 because the state extracts nothing.
* **At 100% Tax Rate**: Revenue collapses to ₹0 because all incentive to work, produce, or invest is eliminated (or economic activity is forced 100% underground into the shadow economy).
* **The Normal Range ($0 < t < t^*$)**: Raising tax rates increases total revenue.
* **The Prohibitive Range ($t^* < t < 100$)**: Raising tax rates **decreases** total revenue because disincentives, tax avoidance, and illegal tax evasion outweigh the higher rate. In this range, **cutting the tax rate actually increases total tax collection**!

## 11.3 Direct Taxation Architecture in India

### 1. Current Law Framework: The Income-tax Act, 2025 (Effective 1 April 2026)
* **Legislative Overhaul**: Parliament enacted the comprehensive **Income-tax Act, 2025**, which came into full legal force on **1 April 2026**, completely repealing and replacing the six-decade-old **Income-tax Act, 1961** (and the Income-tax Rules, 1962 with the **Income-tax Rules, 2026**).
* **Plain-Language Codification**: Structures the tax code into clean, modular components, eliminating decades of conflicting proviso amendments and historical litigation friction.
* **Structural Transition vs. Policy Stability**:
  * *What Changed Structurally*: Codification of the **Faceless Assessment and Digital Appeals Architecture** into core statutory law; rationalized terminology (unifying historical dual concepts of "Previous Year" and "Assessment Year" into a single annual Tax Year); standardized reassessment limitation periods; and substantially softened penalty friction.
  * *What Remained Constant*: Substantive tax rates, personal tax slab structures, corporate concessional tax baselines (22% rate), and bilateral Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) treaty protections are preserved intact to guarantee macroeconomic continuity.
* **Statutory Transition Rule**: Tax years / assessment periods beginning prior to **1 April 2026** remain strictly governed by the substantive and procedural provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Tax years beginning on or after 1 April 2026 are governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025 and Income-tax Rules, 2026.

### 4. Corporate Income Tax (CIT): Current Concessional Architecture
Prior to September 2019, India's headline corporate tax rate was among the highest in emerging Asia (~30% base + surcharge + cess $\approx 34.94\%$). Via the *Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019*, India executed a historic supply-side corporate tax slash to revive private capex (preserved and codified under the Income-tax Act, 2025):

* **Standard Concessional Corporate Regime [1961 Act §115BAA $\longrightarrow$ Codified in Income-tax Act, 2025] (Domestic Companies)**:
  * Base tax rate slashed to **22%**.
  * Surcharge fixed at **10%** and Health & Education Cess at **4%**.
  * **Effective Tax Rate = 25.17%** ($22 \times 1.10 \times 1.04$).
  * Condition: The company must forego all specific tax incentives, holidays, and accelerated depreciation.
* **New Manufacturing Regime [1961 Act §115BAB $\longrightarrow$ Sunset 31 March 2024]**:
  * For new manufacturing companies incorporated on or after 1st October 2019 (sunset on 31st March 2024).
  * Base tax rate slashed to **15%**.
  * **Effective Tax Rate = 17.16%** ($15 \times 1.10 \times 1.04$).
  * Placed India's corporate tax on par with ASEAN hubs (Singapore, Vietnam, Thailand).

### 5. Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) & Alternate Minimum Tax (AMT)
* **The "Zero-Tax Company" Hazard**: Many profitable corporations used legal deductions, depreciation shields, and tax holidays under the Income Tax Act to reduce their taxable income to zero, paying zero tax despite declaring large accounting profits and paying fat dividends to shareholders.
* **Mechanism of MAT [1961 Act §115JB $\longrightarrow$ Codified in Income-tax Act, 2025]**:
  * Introduced to ensure every corporate entity pays a baseline minimum tax on its **Book Profit** (profits calculated as per the Companies Act, 2013).
  * Current MAT rate: **15%** of book profits (plus applicable surcharge and cess).
  * If tax under normal income tax provisions is less than 15% of book profits, the company pays MAT.
  * **MAT Credit**: The excess of MAT paid over normal tax can be carried forward and set off against future normal tax liability for up to **15 assessment years**.
  * **Critical Exam Rule**: MAT is **NOT applicable** to companies that opt for the concessional corporate tax regimes (such as 1961 Act §115BAA / §115BAB or their 2025 Act codifications)!
* **Alternate Minimum Tax [1961 Act §115JC $\longrightarrow$ Codified in Income-tax Act, 2025]**: Applies MAT principles to non-corporate entities (LLPs, firms, associations of persons) at **15%** of adjusted total income.

---

### 6. Abolition of Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT)
* **Pre-2020 System**: Companies paid DDT (~20.56% effective) directly out of corporate profits before distributing dividends. Dividends were tax-free in the hands of shareholders up to ₹10 Lakh.
* **Finance Act, 2020 Reform**:
  * **DDT was completely abolished**.
  * The classical system was restored: Dividends are now taxed directly in the hands of the **shareholder** at their respective individual slab rates (or corporate rates).
  * Companies deduct TDS at **10%** on dividend payments exceeding ₹5,000 per year.

### 8. Specialized Direct Tax Measures & Anti-Avoidance Instruments

#### A. Securities Transaction Tax (STT):
* Levied on the purchase and sale of equities, derivatives (futures & options), and equity mutual funds traded on recognized Indian stock exchanges.
* Primary purpose: Instant, automated tax collection and tracking of high-frequency speculative trades; prevents tax evasion in equity markets.

#### B. Equalisation Levy ("Google Tax"):
* Introduced in 2016 at **6%** on gross payments made to non-resident tech giants for online advertising services.
* Expanded in 2020 to **2%** on foreign e-commerce operators supplying goods or services to Indian residents.
* **Reform**: Phased out / repealed in the Finance Act, 2024 as India transitions toward the **OECD Two-Pillar Solution** (Pillar 1: reallocating taxing rights; Pillar 2: 15% Global Minimum Corporate Tax).

#### C. The Abolition of the "Angel Tax" [1961 Act §56(2)(viib) $\longrightarrow$ Fully Abolished in Budget 2024 / Excluded in 2025 Act]:
* **Section 56(2)(viib)** of the 1961 Act (introduced in 2012) treated funds raised by unlisted startups from investors in excess of "Fair Market Value" (FMV) as taxable "income from other sources".
* Stifled early-stage venture funding and caused severe tax disputes.
* **Union Budget 2024–25 completely abolished Angel Tax for all classes of investors**, unlocking risk capital for the Indian startup ecosystem.

#### D. General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) [1961 Act Chapter X-A $\longrightarrow$ Codified in Income-tax Act, 2025]:
* Introduced under Chapter X-A of the 1961 Act (operational from 1st April 2017) based on the **Parthasarathi Shome Committee** and preserved in the 2025 Act.
* Empowers tax authorities to declare any commercial arrangement as an **"Impermissible Avoidance Arrangement" (IAA)** if its primary purpose was obtaining a tax benefit and it lacks commercial substance (e.g., routing round-tripped black money through treaty-shopping tax havens like Mauritius or Cayman Islands).

#### E. Place of Effective Management (PoEM) [1961 Act §6(3) $\longrightarrow$ Codified in Income-tax Act, 2025]:
* **Statutory Determination of Corporate Residency**: A foreign company is treated as an Indian tax resident if its Place of Effective Management is situated in India during the relevant financial year.
* **Legal Definition**: PoEM means the place where key commercial and management decisions necessary for the conduct of the business of an entity as a whole are, in substance, made.
* **The "Active Business Outside India" (ABOI) Test**:
  - A company qualifies as ABOI if its passive income (dividends, interest, royalties, capital gains) is $\le 50\%$ of total income, AND less than 50% of its total assets, employees, or payroll are situated in India.
  - If ABOI is satisfied, PoEM is presumed to be outside India unless the majority of board meetings take place in India. Prevents Indian promoters from incorporating shell subsidiaries abroad (e.g. Dubai, Singapore) while executing all operational decisions from Mumbai.

#### F. Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) & OECD Inclusive Framework:
* Refers to tax planning strategies used by Multinational Enterprises (MNEs) that exploit gaps and mismatches in tax rules to artificially shift profits to low or no-tax locations where there is little or no economic activity.
* **The 15 Action Points of OECD/G20 BEPS**:
  1. *Action 1*: Addressing tax challenges of the Digital Economy (led to Equalisation Levy & Significant Economic Presence - SEP).
  2. *Action 5*: Countering Harmful Tax Practices (patent boxes, preferential tax regimes).
  3. *Action 6*: Preventing Treaty Abuse (Principle Purpose Test - PPT in Bilateral DTAAs to prevent treaty shopping).
  4. *Action 13*: Country-by-Country (CbC) Reporting and transfer pricing documentation for MNEs with global turnover $> €750 \text{ Million}$ (₹6,400 Crore).
* **The Two-Pillar Global Tax Consensus**:
  - *Pillar One*: Reallocation of taxing rights over 25% of residual profits of the largest MNEs to market jurisdictions where users/consumers are located, regardless of physical presence.
  - *Pillar Two*: **15% Global Minimum Corporate Tax (GloBE rules)**, ensuring MNEs pay a baseline effective tax rate worldwide to end the "race to the bottom" among sovereign tax havens.

## 11.4 The Goods and Services Tax (GST) Architecture

Rolled out on **1st July 2017**, GST represents the most radical fiscal-federal reform since independence, replacing a fragmented system of 17 central/state taxes and 23 cesses with a single, unified national market under the banner of **"One Nation, One Tax, One Market"**.

### The Pre-GST Indirect Tax Flaws:
1. **The Cascading Effect ("Tax on Tax")**: When excise duty was paid at the factory gate, State VAT was later calculated on the total amount *including* the excise duty. Taxes were levied on previously paid taxes.
2. **Inter-State Tax Barriers**: Central Sales Tax (CST) of 2% levied on inter-State movement was non-creditable, incentivizing businesses to set up fragmented warehouses in every state rather than operating along supply-chain efficiency.
3. **Multiplicity of Compliance**: Businesses were forced to file separate returns under Central Excise, Service Tax, State VAT, Octroi, Entry Tax, and Luxury Tax.

```
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Article 279A**                │ **The GST Council**: Creates a constitutional federal       │
│                                 │ decision-making body for all GST tax rates, exemptions,     │
│                                 │ thresholds, and dispute mechanisms.                         │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Article 366(12A)**            │ **Definition of GST**: Defines GST as *"any tax on supply   │
│                                 │ of goods, or services or both, EXCEPT taxes on the supply of│
│                                 │ the alcoholic liquor for human consumption."*               │
└─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Voting Structure in the GST Council (Article 279A)

The GST Council embodies "Cooperative Federalism" with a weighted voting structure designed so that neither the Centre nor the States can act unilaterally:

```
                                  GST COUNCIL VOTING POWER
                                             │
             ┌───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
             ▼                                                               ▼
   [CENTRAL GOVERNMENT]                                            [ALL STATE GOVERNMENTS]
   • Union Finance Minister (Chair)                                • 28 States + 3 UTs (Delhi, J&K,
   • Union Minister of State for Finance                             Puducherry) with Legislatures
   • **Weighted Voting Power = 1/3 (33.33%)**                      • **Weighted Voting Power = 2/3 (66.67%)**
                                                                     (Each State has equal voice: 2.15% each)
```

* **Statutory Quorum**: At least **50% of the total members** must be present.
* **Decision-Making Threshold**: Every decision of the Council must be passed by a majority of **not less than 3/4ths (75%) of the weighted votes** of the members present and voting.
* **The Arithmetic of Consensus**:
  * The Centre holds **33.33%**; it has an absolute **veto** (as $100\% - 33.33\% = 66.67\% < 75\%$).
  * The States together hold **66.67%**; they also have an absolute **veto** if they unite (as Centre's $33.33\% < 75\%$).
  * **Conclusion**: No tax proposal can pass without agreement between the Centre and at least ~20 States!

### The Dual-GST Mechanism & Transaction Typology

India adopted a dual GST model where both levels of government simultaneously levy tax on a shared base:

```
                                      SUPPLY TRANSACTION
                                              │
                      ┌───────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
                      ▼                                               ▼
            [INTRA-STATE SUPPLY]                            [INTER-STATE SUPPLY]
          (Within the same State)                         (Across different States
                      │                                      OR Foreign Import)
         ┌────────────┴────────────┐                                  │
         ▼                         ▼                                  ▼
    [CGST]                      [SGST / UTGST]                     [IGST]
Collected by Centre        Collected by State               Collected by Centre,
                                                            then apportioned to
                                                            the Consuming State!
```

* **Destination-Based / Consumption Principle**: GST is a destination-based consumption tax. Tax revenue ultimately belongs to the jurisdiction where the good or service is **consumed**, not where it is manufactured (eliminating the advantage previously held by producing states like Gujarat or Maharashtra over consuming states like Bihar or UP).

### Input Tax Credit (ITC) & Cross-Utilization Architecture

Input Tax Credit prevents the cascading effect by allowing businesses to deduct the tax already paid on input purchases from the tax liability on output sales.

#### Strict Cross-Utilization Order of Priority:
1. **IGST Credit**: Must be exhausted first against IGST liability → then CGST liability → then SGST/UTGST liability in any order.
2. **CGST Credit**: Used to offset CGST liability → then IGST liability. **CGST credit CAN NEVER be used to pay SGST!**
3. **SGST Credit**: Used to offset SGST liability → then IGST liability. **SGST credit CAN NEVER be used to pay CGST!**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                            CROSS-UTILIZATION WALL                                           │
│                                                                                             │
│       [CGST Input Credit]       ───────❌ CANNOT OFFSET ───────►    [SGST Liability]        │
│                                                                                             │
│       [SGST Input Credit]       ───────❌ CANNOT OFFSET ───────►    [CGST Liability]        │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. The 5-Tier GST Rate Structure
* **0% (Exempt)**: Essential unbranded foodgrains, fresh vegetables, milk, curd, life-saving drugs, educational services, healthcare.
* **5%**: Mass consumption items, packaged food items, edible oils, tea, coffee, footwear below ₹1,000, railways/economy flight tickets.
* **12%**: Processed food, state-run lotteries, computers, business class air tickets.
* **18% (Standard Rate)**: Capital goods, industrial intermediaries, IT services, financial services, telecom, restaurants in hotels.
* **28% (Luxury / De-merit Rate)**: Air conditioners, large automobiles, cement, gambling, horse racing, online gaming (taxed at 28% on full face value).
* **Special Carve-Outs**: Gold, silver, and jewelry taxed at **3%**; rough precious stones at **0.25%**.

---

### 3. GST Compensation Cess & The Post-2022 Reality
* **The Grand Bargain (GST Compensation Act, 2017)**: States surrendered their sovereign sales taxation powers in exchange for a constitutional guarantee: the Centre would compensate States for any shortfall in their GST revenues below an assumed **14% annual compounded growth rate** (base year 2015–16) for a transition period of **5 years (July 2017 – June 2022)**.
* **The Compensation Cess**: Funded via a special cess on luxury and demerit goods (aerated drinks, motor vehicles, tobacco products) over and above the 28% GST rate.
* **COVID Shock & Extension**: During COVID-19 (FY 2020–21), cess collections collapsed while state deficits surged. The Centre borrowed funds on behalf of States via a special RBI window.
* **Current Status**: The 5-year compensation guarantee **expired on 30th June 2022**. However, the **GST Compensation Cess was extended until March 2026** exclusively to service and repay the principal and interest of the back-to-back market loans taken during the pandemic.

---

### 4. Structural Challenges: Inverted Duty Structure (IDS)

An **Inverted Duty Structure** occurs when the GST rate on **inputs/raw materials** is higher than the GST rate on the **finished output**:

$$\text{GST Rate on Input Material} > \text{GST Rate on Finished Good}$$

* **Concrete Example**: Synthetic yarn (input) was taxed at 12% or 18%, while synthetic fabric (output) was taxed at 5%.
* **Economic Pathology**:
  1. Firms accumulate massive unutilized **Input Tax Credits (ITC)** on their balance sheets that cannot be absorbed by outward sales tax.
  2. Creates severe working capital lockups and forces companies to seek government cash refunds under Section 54(3) of CGST Act.
  3. Discourages domestic value addition and makes imported finished goods cheaper than domestically manufactured items.
* **Remedy**: Ongoing GST rate rationalization exercises by the Group of Ministers (GoM) to align raw material and final output tax rates (e.g., footwear, fertilizers, textiles).
* **Statutory Refund Formula (Rule 89(5) of CGST Rules)**:
  $$\text{Maximum Refund Amount} = \left( \frac{\text{Turnover of Inverted Rated Supply} \times \text{Net ITC}}{\text{Adjusted Total Turnover}} \right) - \text{Tax Payable on Inverted Rated Supply}$$

---

### 5. Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM - Sections 9(3) & 9(4))
Under the standard GST forward-charge mechanism, the supplier of goods or services collects GST from the buyer and deposits it with the government. Under RCM, the statutory liability to pay GST is **reversed onto the recipient/buyer**:
* **Section 9(3) Mandatory Categories**: Specific goods/services notified by government where compliance is difficult to enforce on suppliers:
  1. *Goods Transport Agency (GTA)* services supplied to businesses.
  2. *Legal Services* provided by individual advocates or senior advocates to business entities.
  3. *Arbitral Tribunal* services to business entities.
  4. *Sponsorship Services* provided to corporate bodies or partnership firms.
  5. *Directors' Remuneration* paid to independent/non-executive directors by companies.
* **Section 9(4) Supplies from Unregistered Persons**: Procurements of specified goods/services by registered entities from unregistered suppliers (anti-evasion control).
* **Critical Exam Rule on ITC**: Tax paid under RCM **must be paid in CASH through the Electronic Cash Ledger**; it CANNOT be paid by utilizing existing Input Tax Credit! However, once paid in cash, the recipient can claim ITC on that amount for future outward tax liabilities.

## 11.6 Institutional Oversight & Technology Backbone

1. **Goods and Services Tax Network (GSTN)**:
   * A non-profit, Section 8 company providing the nationwide IT infrastructure for GST registration, return filing, tax payment, and ITC ledger verification.
   * Originally established with 51% private equity (HDFC, ICICI, NSE Strategic Investment) and 49% government stake; in 2018, the GST Council converted GSTN into a **100% government-owned company** (50% Centre, 50% States jointly).
2. **E-Way Bill (Electronic Way Bill)**:
   * Mandatory electronic document generated on the portal for consignments of goods worth **exceeding ₹50,000** moved inter-State or intra-State.
   * Linked with RFID and FASTag to eliminate physical border checkpoints and prevent unaccounted transport of goods.
3. **E-Invoicing**:
   * Businesses above a notified turnover threshold must generate standardized electronic invoices on the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP), obtaining an **Invoice Reference Number (IRN)** and signed QR code.
   * Drastically cuts input tax credit fraud, duplicate billing, and phantom invoices.
4. **Transition from NAA to CCI**:
   * The **National Anti-Profiteering Authority (NAA)** was established under Section 171 of the CGST Act to ensure that tax reductions or ITC benefits are mandatorily passed on to end-consumers via price cuts.
   * The NAA was sunsetted on 1st December 2022; its anti-profiteering adjudicatory mandate was transferred to the **Competition Commission of India (CCI)**.

## 11.8 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

```
CARD 1: Explain why indirect taxes are inherently regressive while direct taxes are progressive.
ANSWER:
Direct taxes (such as Personal Income Tax) have marginal slab rates that rise as income increases,
extracting a greater percentage of wealth from high earners (ability to pay). Indirect taxes
(such as GST on consumer items) apply the exact same rate regardless of buyer wealth; a low-wage
laborer spending 80% of income on consumer goods bears a much higher tax-to-income burden than
a high earner who spends only 10% of income on consumption and saves the rest.
```

```
CARD 2: State the exact voting thresholds in the GST Council and explain how cooperative federalism is preserved mathematically.
ANSWER:
1. Quorum: 50% of total members.
2. Weighted Voting: Centre holds 1/3 (33.33%); all 31 States/UTs together hold 2/3 (66.67%).
3. Passing Threshold: 75% of weighted votes cast.
4. Mathematical consequence: Neither Centre (33.33% < 75%) nor States alone (66.67% < 75%) can
pass any resolution. Both must agree; each possesses mutual veto power.
```

```
CARD 3: What is an Inverted Duty Structure (IDS) and what are its two primary economic consequences?
ANSWER:
IDS occurs when the tax rate on raw materials/inputs exceeds the tax rate on final finished goods.
Consequences:
1. Balance-sheet blockage: Producers accumulate massive unutilized Input Tax Credits (ITC), locking up working capital.
2. Distortion of domestic manufacturing: Imports of finished goods face lower duties than the
cumulative tax burden on domestically manufactured goods, incentivizing importation over domestic value addition.
```
