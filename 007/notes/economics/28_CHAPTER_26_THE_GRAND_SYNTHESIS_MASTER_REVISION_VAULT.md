## 26.2 The Master Comparative Distinction Compendium

Ten vital analytical comparison matrices distinguishing frequently conflated economic concepts:

### Master Distinction 01: Revenue Expenditure vs. Capital Expenditure

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operational Definition** | Outlays that neither create physical/financial assets nor liquidate sovereign liabilities. | Asset-building or liability-reducing outlays that alter the sovereign balance sheet. |
| **Constituent Outlays** | Interest payments, defense operational salaries, explicit subsidies, administrative outlays, and **all Central grants to States (even if targeted for capex)**. | Construction of physical capital (highways, rail tracks, ports, hospitals), equity infusions in PSUs, and repayment of sovereign loan principal. |
| **Fiscal Impact** | Excessive growth widens the Revenue Deficit, signaling sovereign borrowing to fund consumption. | Expands national capital stock, lowers ICOR, crowds-in private investment, and drives long-term potential GDP ($Y^*$). |


### Master Distinction 02: Fiscal Deficit vs. Primary Deficit

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mathematical Formula** | $\mathbf{FD = \text{Total Expenditure} - \text{Non-Debt Receipts}}$ | $\mathbf{PD = \text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Interest Payments}}$ |
| **Core Representation** | Total net sovereign borrowing requirement from all domestic and external sources for the current year. | The fiscal stance and budgetary prudence of the executive during the current financial year, excluding legacy debt burdens. |
| **Zero Deficit Sign** | Fiscal Deficit = 0 indicates a balanced budget with zero net sovereign borrowing. | Primary Deficit = 0 indicates that current borrowing is conducted solely to service past historical debt. |


### Master Distinction 03: Consumer Price Index (CPI) vs. Wholesale Price Index (WPI)

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Commodity Coverage** | Covers both **GOODS AND SERVICES** across retail trade. | Covers **GOODS ONLY (Zero Services)** across wholesale transaction points. |
| **Compiling Authority** | National Statistical Office (NSO), MoSPI; Base Year: **2012**. | Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce; Base Year: **2011–12**. |
| **Basket Weighting** | Dominated by **Food & Beverages (45.86%)**. | Dominated by **Manufactured Products (64.23%)**. |
| **Policy Role** | Statutory nominal anchor for the RBI Monetary Policy Committee under Flexible Inflation Targeting. | Tracks factory-gate producer inflation, input price pressures, and corporate wholesale costs. |


### Master Distinction 04: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) vs. Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Equity Threshold** | Acquisition of **$\ge 10\%$ of paid-up equity** in a listed Indian entity (or any equity in an unlisted entity). | Holding of **$< 10\%$ of paid-up equity** in a listed Indian entity. |
| **Management Control** | Brings active management participation, board seats, technological transfer, and lasting strategic interest. | Purely passive financial investment with **zero management control** or board representation. |
| **Volatility Profile** | Non-debt, highly illiquid, sticky, and committed for the long haul. | Highly liquid, volatile **"Hot Money"** prone to rapid capital outflows during global shocks. |


### Master Distinction 05: Disguised Unemployment vs. Structural Unemployment

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Economic Nature** | Labor overcrowding where the **Marginal Productivity of Labor is ZERO ($\mathbf{MP_L = 0}$)**. | A chronic institutional mismatch between the skill sets of job seekers and the skill demands of industrial employers. |
| **Output Reaction** | Withdrawing surplus workers produces **zero decline** in total economic output. | Requires worker reskilling, technological retraining, or geographical relocation to restore employment equilibrium. |
| **Sectoral Prevalence** | Ubiquitous across unmechanized Indian family agriculture and informal rural enterprises. | Prevalent in industrial and services sectors undergoing automation, digitalization, or technological transitions. |


### Master Distinction 06: Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM) vs. BOT-Toll Highway Contracts

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financing Structure** | NHAI provides **40% project cost in cash grants** during construction across 5 milestones; private developer arranges 60%. | Private concessionaire arranges **100% of project financing** through commercial equity and bank debt. |
| **Toll Collection** | NHAI or the sovereign collects all user tolls directly from commuters. | Private concessionaire collects all user tolls directly from commuters over 15–30 years. |
| **Traffic & Revenue Risk** | **NHAI bears 100% of traffic risk**; the private developer is paid fixed biannual annuity payments. | **Private concessionaire bears 100% of traffic and revenue risk**; exposed directly to traffic shortfalls. |


### Master Distinction 07: Colonial Land Tenure Systems (Zamindari vs. Ryotwari vs. Mahalwari)

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Architects** | Lord Cornwallis & John Shore (1793). | Thomas Munro & Alexander Read (1820). |
| **Geographical Scope** | Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Northern Madras (covering ~19% of British India). | Madras, Bombay, Assam, Coorg (covering ~51% of British India). |
| **Ownership & Tax Liability** | Zamindars recognized as hereditary owners; peasants reduced to tenants-at-will. Fixed 10/11th to British, 1/11th to Zamindar. | Individual peasant (*Ryot*) recognized as direct sovereign owner and tax-payer. Periodic revision of 45%–55% of gross produce. |


### Master Distinction 08: Micro-Irrigation Architecture (Drip Irrigation vs. Sprinkler Irrigation)

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Delivery Method** | Water applied directly to plant root zone drop-by-drop through emitters and drippers under low pressure. | Water sprayed overhead through aerial pressurized nozzles, simulating natural rainfall. |
| **Water Use Efficiency** | **Water Use Efficiency of 90%–95%** (highest among all irrigation methods). | **Water Use Efficiency of 70%–80%**. |
| **Ideal Crop Suitability** | Row crops, orchards, vineyards, coconut, sugarcane, and high-value horticulture; facilitates fertigation. | Closely spaced field crops (pulses, oilseeds, wheat, millets, tea); highly effective on undulating or sloping topography. |


### Master Distinction 09: Development Strategies (Lewis Dual-Sector Model vs. Feldman-Mahalanobis Strategy)

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Engine** | Structural labor reallocation engine transferring surplus rural labor to modern urban industry. | Capital allocation engine ($\lambda_k$) prioritizing heavy machine goods to compound long-run capital stock. |
| **Wage & Profit Dynamism** | Surplus labor ($MP_L \approx 0$) supplied at constant subsistence wage; capitalist reinvests surplus profits until the **Lewis Turning Point**. | Restricts short-term consumption goods production to maximize domestic industrial self-reliance. |
| **Policy Application** | East Asian manufacturing miracle and labor-intensive export industrialization. | India's Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61) heavy industrialization strategy. |


### Master Distinction 10: Infrastructure Procurement (Swiss Challenge vs. Standard EPC / HAM)

| Comparative Dimension | Concept A | Concept B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origination** | Begins with an **unsolicited proposal** submitted directly by a private proponent to the government. | Fully initiated, designed, and packaged by the government agency following a detailed project report (DPR). |
| **Counter-Bidding** | Project details published to invite open competitive counter-bids from rival market challengers. | Open competitive reverse bidding (L1) evaluated strictly against standard tender qualification criteria. |
| **Matching Privilege** | Original proponent retains the **Right of First Refusal (ROFR)** to match the superior challenger bid. | Zero matching privilege or developer preference; awarded strictly to the lowest qualifying bidder. |

## 26.3 The Master Examination Trap Compendium (Top 35 High-Yield Traps)

The thirty-five most frequently tested conceptual traps, statistical illusions, and examiner tripwires:

| # | Common Candidate Fallacy / Trap | Sovereign Verified Economic Reality |
| :-: | :--- | :--- |
| **01** | "State Grants for capital assets are Capital Exp." | **FALSE.** All Central grants given to States are classified as **100% Revenue Expenditure** in the Union budget because the resulting capital asset is owned legally by the State Government, not the Centre. |
| **02** | "Fiscal Deficit is financed by printing currency." | **FALSE.** Automatic monetization through ad-hoc Treasury Bills was abolished in 1997. The Fiscal Deficit is financed by **market borrowings via dated G-Secs** and small savings deposits in the National Small Savings Fund (NSSF). |
| **03** | "State Governments can issue Treasury Bills." | **FALSE.** State Governments **CANNOT issue Treasury Bills (T-Bills)**. T-Bills are issued exclusively by the Central Government. States raise market borrowings via State Development Loans (SDLs). |
| **04** | "T-Bills pay regular semi-annual interest coupons." | **FALSE.** T-Bills have **ZERO coupon payments**. They are issued at a discount to face value and redeemed at par upon maturity. |
| **05** | "Masala Bonds create rupee exchange risk for issuer." | **FALSE.** In Masala Bonds, the **overseas foreign investor bears the currency exchange risk**, shielding the Indian corporate issuer. |
| **06** | "Centre holds a majority in the GST Council." | **FALSE.** The Centre holds **only 1/3 (33.33%)** voting power in the GST Council; States hold 2/3 (66.67%). Decisions require a 75% weighted majority, giving both mutual veto power. |
| **07** | "Petroleum is permanently barred from GST." | **FALSE.** Under Article 279A(5), petroleum products WILL enter GST from a date recommended by the GST Council. Only **alcohol for human consumption** is constitutionally excluded. |
| **08** | "CGST input credit can pay SGST liabilities." | **FALSE.** CGST credit can **NEVER offset SGST liability**, and SGST credit can never offset CGST. Only IGST credit crosses boundaries. |
| **09** | "Cesses & Surcharges enter the Article 270 pool." | **FALSE.** Cesses and surcharges levied under Article 271 are **excluded from the Divisible Pool** shared with States under Article 270. |
| **10** | "15th FC 41% cut was an austerity penalty on states." | **FALSE.** The 1% reduction from 42% to 41% was carved out solely to finance the newly formed Union Territories of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh. |
| **11** | "States can borrow abroad directly from World Bank." | **FALSE.** Under Article 293(2), Indian States **cannot borrow directly from foreign entities or multilaterals**. All foreign sovereign loans flow through the Central Government. |
| **12** | "WPI includes essential services like health/edu." | **FALSE.** WPI measures **GOODS ONLY**. It has **ZERO services coverage**. |
| **13** | "CPI Rural includes rural housing rent." | **FALSE.** The Housing group has a weight of **EXACTLY 0% in CPI Rural** because rural homes are overwhelmingly owner-occupied. |
| **14** | "Disinflation is negative inflation." | **FALSE.** Disinflation is a **slowing inflation rate** (e.g., inflation falling from 8% to 4%). Prices are still rising. Deflation means prices are actually falling ($\pi < 0$). |
| **15** | "Unemployment Rate is a % of total population." | **FALSE.** UR is calculated as a % of the **Labour Force** (Employed + Unemployed), NOT total population or working-age population. |
| **16** | "CWS requires working major days of the week." | **FALSE.** Under Current Weekly Status (CWS), working for **just 1 hour on any 1 single day** of the reference week classifies an individual as **employed**. |
| **17** | "Tendulkar line used 2,100 and 2,400 calorie norms." | **FALSE.** The Tendulkar Committee **completely abandoned calorie norms** and anchored the poverty line to urban consumption standards with explicit health and education expenditure. |
| **18** | "Rangarajan is the official poverty line of India." | **FALSE.** The Rangarajan Committee report was **never officially adopted by the Government**. The Tendulkar Committee remains the statutory 2011–12 baseline. |
| **19** | "India's MPI uses 10 indicators like Global MPI." | **FALSE.** The Global MPI uses 10 indicators; India's **National MPI uses 12 indicators** (adding *Antenatal Care* to Health and *Bank Accounts* to Standard of Living). |
| **20** | "NRI Remittances are in the Capital Account." | **FALSE.** Remittances are classified under **Secondary Income in the Current Account** as unrequited private transfers with zero future repayment obligation. |
| **21** | "FCNR(B) depositor bears the currency exchange risk." | **FALSE.** In FCNR(B) deposits, the **commercial bank bears the currency exchange risk**, shielding the depositor. |
| **22** | "REER > 100 means exports are booming and competitive." | **FALSE.** REER > 100 indicates **real currency overvaluation**, making domestic exports uncompetitive and imports cheaper. |
| **23** | "India has Full Capital Account Convertibility." | **FALSE.** India has **Full Current Account Convertibility (1994)**, but only **Partial Capital Account Convertibility**. |
| **24** | "India is a member of all 5 World Bank Group bodies." | **FALSE.** India is a member of 4 WBG bodies, but has **consistently refused to join ICSID** (International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes). |
| **25** | "SDR is an official trade currency of the IMF." | **FALSE.** The SDR is **neither a currency nor a direct claim on the IMF**. It is an international reserve asset and potential claim on usable currencies of member nations. |
| **26** | "Green Box subsidies have a strict 10% de minimis cap." | **FALSE.** Green Box subsidies are considered non-trade-distorting and have **NO financial cap or ceiling**! Amber Box subsidies are capped at 5% / 10%. |
| **27** | "Govt MSP formula gives 50% margin over Cost C2." | **FALSE.** The Government implemented **$1.5 \times (A2 + FL)$**, NOT $C2 + 50\%$. Imputed land rent and owned capital interest are excluded. |
| **28** | "Sugarcane receives an MSP." | **FALSE.** Sugarcane receives a **Fair & Remunerative Price (FRP)** which sugar mills are legally obligated to disburse within 14 days under the Sugarcane (Control) Order. |
| **29** | "CACP announces final MSP." | **FALSE.** CACP is an **advisory body**; the **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)** chaired by the Prime Minister approves and announces final MSP. |
| **30** | "MSME turnover includes export earnings." | **FALSE.** **Export turnover is completely excluded** when calculating the annual MSME turnover ceiling under the composite criteria. |
| **31** | "Manufacturing & Services MSMEs have distinct caps." | **FALSE.** Under the July 2020 composite definition, **Manufacturing and Services enterprises are treated 100% IDENTICALLY**. |
| **32** | "Defense manufacturing is reserved for public sector." | **FALSE.** Defense allows up to 74% FDI under automatic route and 100% with government approval. Only **Atomic Energy** and **Railway Operations** remain reserved. |
| **33** | "Steel has largest weight in Eight Core Industries." | **FALSE.** **Refinery Products (28.04%)** has the largest weight in the Index of Eight Core Industries; Fertilizers (2.63%) has the smallest. |
| **34** | "Asset Monetization (NMP) transfers asset ownership." | **FALSE.** The National Monetisation Pipeline (NMP) is **NOT privatization**. The Government retains 100% ownership; only brownfield operational rights are leased. |
| **35** | "India's Net-Zero pledge is anchored to 2050." | **FALSE.** India's Net-Zero pledge announced at COP26 Glasgow is anchored to **2070** (whereas the US and EU target 2050 and China targets 2060). |

## 26.4 The Universal Diagnostic Active Recall Bank (Comprehensive Testing Cards)

Master-level active recall cards spanning all nine curricular parts. Each card tests an indispensable statutory, institutional, mathematical, or theoretical principle:

### Part I: Foundations & Output Accounting (Cards 01–09)

> **Card 01: Pure Public Good & Market Failure**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What defines a Pure Public Good, and why does the private market fail to supply it?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: A pure public good is characterized by **Non-Excludability** (impossible or prohibitively costly to exclude non-payers) and **Non-Rivalry** (one individual's consumption does not reduce availability for others). The resulting **Free-Rider Problem** causes private markets to under-produce or fail completely to supply the good.

> **Card 02: Veblen Good vs. Giffen Good**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Differentiate between a Veblen good and a Giffen good.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Both possess upward-sloping demand curves. **Veblen goods** are high-status luxury goods where higher prices signal social prestige (conspicuous consumption). **Giffen goods** are non-luxury staple necessities lacking close substitutes where a price rise erodes real income, forcing impoverished households to consume more of the staple.

> **Card 03: The National Income Valuation Ladder**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the exact mathematical bridge connecting Factor Cost, Basic Price, and Market Price.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{\text{Basic Price} = \text{Factor Cost} + \text{Production Taxes} - \text{Production Subsidies}}$.<br>$\mathbf{\text{Market Price} = \text{Basic Price} + \text{Product Taxes} - \text{Product Subsidies}}$.

> **Card 04: Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR)**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR), and what does a rising ICOR signify?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{\text{ICOR} = \frac{\Delta K}{\Delta Y} = \frac{\text{Investment Share of GDP}}{\text{GDP Growth Rate}}}$. A rising ICOR signifies **declining capital efficiency**—more units of capital investment are required to generate one incremental unit of economic output.

> **Card 05: Human Development Index (HDI) Methodology**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What are the three dimensions and aggregation methodology of the post-2010 Human Development Index?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Dimensions: **Health** (Life expectancy at birth), **Education** (Mean years + Expected years of schooling), and **Standard of Living** (GNI per capita in PPP terms). Aggregated via the **Geometric Mean**: $\text{HDI} = \sqrt[3]{I_{\text{Health}} \cdot I_{\text{Edu}} \cdot I_{\text{Income}}}$.

> **Card 06: National MPI vs. Global MPI**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: How does India's National MPI differ from the UNDP Global MPI?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Global MPI features 10 indicators across 3 dimensions. NITI Aayog's National MPI expands this to **12 indicators**, adding **Antenatal Care** (under Health) and **Bank Account** (under Standard of Living).

> **Card 07: Green GDP Accounting**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Define Green GDP.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{\text{Green GDP} = \text{Standard GDP} - \text{Depletion of Natural Capital} - \text{Environmental Degradation Costs}}$. It measures net output growth adjusted for natural resource consumption and ecological externalities.

> **Card 08: Final Goods vs. Intermediate Goods Hazard**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Distinguish between Final Goods and Intermediate Goods to explain the double-counting hazard.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Intermediate goods** are fully used up as production inputs within the same accounting year. Including both intermediate inputs and final goods inflates output through double counting; GDP accounts only for **final products** or aggregates **Gross Value Added (GVA)** at each stage.

> **Card 09: Output Gap & Policy Implications**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the Output Gap, and what is its policy implication during an inflationary gap?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{\text{Output Gap} = Y_{\text{Actual}} - Y_{\text{Potential}}}$. An inflationary gap ($Y_{\text{Actual}} > Y_{\text{Potential}}$) indicates overheated aggregate demand straining capacity, generating demand-pull inflation that necessitates counter-cyclical monetary and fiscal tightening.

### Part II: Money & Central Banking (Cards 10–18)

> **Card 10: Section 33 Minimum Reserve System**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the statutory reserve backing mandated by the Minimum Reserve System under Section 33 of the RBI Act.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: The RBI Issue Department must maintain a statutory minimum reserve backing of **₹200 Crore**, of which at least **₹115 Crore must be in physical gold bullion** and the balance **₹85 Crore in foreign sovereign securities**.

> **Card 11: Monetary Aggregates M1 and M3**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Detail the precise constituents of M1 and M3 monetary aggregates.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{M_1 \text{ (Narrow Money)}} = \text{Currency with Public} + \text{Demand Deposits with Banking System} + \text{Other Deposits with RBI}$.<br>$\mathbf{M_3 \text{ (Broad Money)}} = M_1 + \text{Time Deposits with the Banking System}$.

> **Card 12: Money Multiplier Mechanics**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Write the Money Multiplier formula and explain how an increase in Currency Deposit Ratio (c) affects it.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{m = \frac{1+c}{c+r}}$. An increase in $c$ lowers the money multiplier because greater cash holding by the public drains reserves from commercial banks, curbing their secondary credit creation capacity.

> **Card 13: RBI Inflation Target Failure Definition**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What constitutes a failure of the RBI to achieve its inflation target under Section 45ZN of the RBI Act?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Failure occurs if average headline CPI inflation remains outside the **2% to 6% tolerance band (4% $\pm$ 2%) for THREE CONSECUTIVE QUARTERS**. The RBI must submit a statutory explanation to the Central Government detailing causes, remedial actions, and an expected recovery timeline.

> **Card 14: Monetary Policy Committee Composition**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the composition and voting mechanism of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Consists of **6 members**: 3 from RBI (Governor, Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, 1 RBI officer) and 3 external economists appointed by the Central Government. Each member has one vote; the **RBI Governor exercises a casting vote** in the event of a tie.

> **Card 15: Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) vs. Reverse Repo**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF), and how does it differ fundamentally from the Reverse Repo?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: The SDF is the uncollateralized floor of the LAF corridor (Repo - 25 bps). Unlike Reverse Repo, the RBI absorbs surplus liquidity via SDF **WITHOUT pledging government securities as collateral**, conserving the central bank's sovereign bond holdings.

> **Card 16: The 50 bps LAF Corridor Architecture**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Describe the operational structure of the 50 bps LAF Corridor.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Ceiling: **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF = Repo + 25 bps)**; Anchor: **Policy Repo Rate**; Floor: **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF = Repo - 25 bps)**. The width of the corridor is exactly 50 basis points.

> **Card 17: Evolution of Lending Rate Benchmarks**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Trace the historical evolution of bank lending rate benchmarks from PLR to EBLR.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Prime Lending Rate (PLR) → Benchmark PLR (BPLR, 2003) → Base Rate (2010) → Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate (MCLR, 2016) → **External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR, 2019)**.

> **Card 18: Bimal Jalan Economic Capital Framework**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What are the two core capital components established by the Bimal Jalan Economic Capital Framework?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Contingent Risk Buffer (CRB)**: Realized equity maintained between **5.5% and 6.5%** of balance sheet to meet monetary and financial stability shocks. **Currency and Gold Revaluation Account (CGRA)**: Unrealized valuation gains from market movements, which are strictly non-distributable.

### Part III: Banking, NPAs & Financial Markets (Cards 19–27)

> **Card 19: Payments Banks Statutory Constraints**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the two core statutory restrictions differentiating Payments Banks from Small Finance Banks.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Payments Banks **CANNOT lend loans** and **CANNOT issue credit cards**. They accept demand deposits up to ₹2 Lakh per individual. Small Finance Banks are full-service commercial banks that lend (mandated with a 75% Priority Sector Lending quota).

> **Card 20: Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR) in India**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the minimum Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) mandated for Indian Scheduled Commercial Banks.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Indian SCBs must maintain **9.0% minimum CRAR + 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) = 11.5%** total (PSBs maintain 12.0%). This is more conservative than the Basel III baseline of 10.5%.

> **Card 21: Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Thresholds**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What are the three parameters monitored under the RBI Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) framework?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Capital** (CRAR below 9% or CET-1 below 5.5%), **Asset Quality** (Net NPA ratio ≥ 6.0%), and **Leverage** (Tier 1 Leverage ratio $< 3.5\%$).

> **Card 22: NPA Definition & SMA Staging**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Define a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) and classify the Special Mention Account (SMA) stages.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: NPA = Principal or interest overdue for **more than 90 days**. Staging: **SMA-0** (1 to 30 days overdue), **SMA-1** (31 to 60 days overdue), **SMA-2** (61 to 90 days overdue).

> **Card 23: IBC 2016 Approval & Time Limits**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the voting majority required by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) to approve a resolution plan under IBC 2016.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Requires **66% of voting share** of financial creditors. The statutory outer resolution cap is **330 days** (including judicial litigation delays).

> **Card 24: NARCL & IDRCL Architecture**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the corporate structure of the National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited (NARCL)?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Dual Bad Bank structure: **NARCL** (51% owned by PSBs; aggregates bad loans, pays 15% cash + 85% Security Receipts backed by ₹30,600 Cr sovereign guarantee) + **IDRCL** (51% owned by Private Banks; operational debt turnaround manager).

> **Card 25: Bond Price and Yield Inverse Relationship**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Explain the inverse relationship between Bond Prices and Bond Yields.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Fixed coupon payments mean that when secondary market bond prices increase, the annual percentage return on the purchase price declines: $\text{Yield} \approx \frac{\text{Coupon}}{\text{Price}}$. Price and yield move in opposite directions.

> **Card 26: Masala Bonds Currency Risk**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What are Masala Bonds, and who bears the foreign exchange risk?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Rupee-denominated corporate debt issued by Indian entities in overseas financial centers. The **overseas foreign investor bears the currency depreciation risk**, protecting the Indian corporate issuer.

> **Card 27: Treasury Bills Tenors and Restrictions**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: List the three standard tenures of Indian Treasury Bills and identify who is legally prohibited from issuing them.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Tenors: **91 Days, 182 Days, and 364 Days**. **State Governments** are legally prohibited from issuing Treasury Bills; T-Bills are issued exclusively by the Central Government.

### Part IV: Public Finance, Fiscal Federalism & Tax (Cards 28–36)

> **Card 28: Consolidated Fund vs. Public Account**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Differentiate between the Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266(1)) and Public Account of India (Article 266(2)).  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Consolidated Fund**: Holds sovereign taxes, loans, and loan recoveries; withdrawals mandate prior parliamentary authorization (Appropriation Act). **Public Account**: Holds funds where government acts as banker/trustee (PPF, small savings); operated by executive action.

> **Card 29: Central Grants Classification**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Why are Central grants to States for capital asset creation accounted as Revenue Expenditure?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Because the resulting physical capital asset is legally owned by the State Government. The Central balance sheet records no capital asset creation or debt reduction.

> **Card 30: N.K. Singh Committee Debt Targets**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the N.K. Singh FRBM Committee debt targets for General Government, Centre, and States.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **General Government Debt ≤ 60% of GDP** (Central Government ≤ 40%; State Governments ≤ 20%). Recommended a Fiscal Deficit target of 3.0% of GDP with an escape clause allowing a 0.5% GDP deviation.

> **Card 31: Tax Buoyancy vs. Tax Elasticity**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Define Tax Buoyancy and contrast it with Tax Elasticity.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{\text{Tax Buoyancy} = \frac{\% \Delta \text{ Tax Revenue}}{\% \Delta \text{ Nominal GDP}}}$, reflecting both natural economic growth and discretionary policy rate changes. **Tax Elasticity** measures growth in revenue holding tax rates constant.

> **Card 32: GST Council Voting Mechanics**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the voting distribution and majority rule in the Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: The Central Government holds **1/3 (33.33%)** voting power; all 31 States/UTs jointly hold **2/3 (66.67%)**. Decisions require a **75% weighted majority**, granting both Centre and States mutual veto power.

> **Card 33: GST Scope Exclusions**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: List the commodities temporarily and permanently outside the scope of GST.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Permanently excluded**: Alcohol for human consumption (Article 366(12A)). **Temporarily outside**: Five petroleum items (crude oil, petrol, diesel, natural gas, ATF) under Article 279A(5).

> **Card 34: Article 270 Divisible Pool Exclusions**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What constitutes the Divisible Pool under Article 270, and what are its three exclusions?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Divisible Pool = Gross Tax Revenue minus: (1) Surcharges (Article 271); (2) All Cesses; and (3) Statutory Cost of Collection. Distributed vertically to States (41% under 15th FC).

> **Card 35: 15th Finance Commission Horizontal Weights**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Detail the horizontal devolution formula criteria of the 15th Finance Commission.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Income Distance (45%)**, **Population 2011 (15%)**, **Area (15%)**, **Forest & Ecology (10%)**, **Demographic Performance (12.5%)**, and **Tax Effort (2.5%)**.

> **Card 36: Article 293(3) State Borrowing Restrictions**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Under what constitutional provision can the Central Government cap State market borrowings?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Article 293(3)**: If a State has an outstanding loan from the Centre or backed by a Central guarantee, it cannot borrow from domestic markets without prior consent of the Government of India.

### Part V: Inflation, Employment & Poverty (Cards 37–45)

> **Card 37: Headline vs. Core Inflation**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Distinguish between Headline Inflation and Core Inflation.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Headline Inflation measures price changes across the entire basket. Core Inflation subtracts volatile Food and Fuel items: $\mathbf{\text{Core Inflation} = \text{Headline CPI} - [\text{Food} + \text{Fuel}]}$.

> **Card 38: CPI Combined vs. WPI Comparison**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the compiling agency, base year, and dominant component of CPI (Combined) vs. WPI.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: CPI Combined: NSO (MoSPI), Base 2012, Food & Beverages (45.86%), Goods + Services. WPI: Office of Economic Adviser (DPIIT), Base 2011–12, Manufactured Products (64.23%), Goods Only.

> **Card 39: Long-Run Phillips Curve (LRPC)**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Explain the Friedman-Phelps Long-Run Phillips Curve (LRPC).  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: In the long run, economic agents adjust inflation expectations, causing unemployment to return to its Natural Rate (NAIRU). The LRPC is a **vertical line at NAIRU**; permanent inflation increases yield no long-run employment gains.

> **Card 40: UPSS vs. CWS Activity Status**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Differentiate between Usual Principal and Subsidiary Status (UPSS) and Current Weekly Status (CWS).  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **UPSS** (365 days reference): Person is employed if they worked for at least 30 days in a subsidiary capacity. **CWS** (7 days reference): Person is employed if they worked for at least 1 hour on any single day of the week.

> **Card 41: Disguised Unemployment Definition**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Define Disguised Unemployment and identify the sector where it is pervasive in India.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Labor overcrowding where Marginal Productivity of Labor is zero ($\mathbf{MP_L = 0}$). Withdrawing surplus workers produces zero drop in total agricultural production.

> **Card 42: IR Code 2020 Retrenchment Threshold**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the retrenchment threshold under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Raised from 100 workers to **300 workers**. Establishments employing under 300 workers no longer require prior government permission for layoffs, retrenchments, or unit closures.

> **Card 43: Tendulkar Committee Calorie Departure**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Why did the Suresh Tendulkar Committee abandon calorie norms in estimating poverty?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Calorie intake had decoupled from living standards, declining even as incomes rose. Tendulkar anchored poverty lines to urban consumption baskets with explicit allowances for health and education under MRP.

> **Card 44: Tendulkar Statutory Poverty Headcount**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the official statutory poverty headcount ratio and poverty lines for India (2011–12).  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Headcount ratio: **21.9%** (Rural 25.7%, Urban 13.7%). Rural line: ₹816 per capita monthly (~₹27/day); Urban line: ₹1,000 per capita monthly (~₹33/day).

> **Card 45: Gini Coefficient Interpretation**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the Gini Coefficient, and what do index values of 0 and 1 represent?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: $\mathbf{G = \frac{A}{A+B}}$ derived from the Lorenz Curve. $0 = \text{Perfect Equality}$ (all individuals earn equal income); $1 = \text{Maximum Inequality}$ (one individual captures all national income).

### Part VI: External Sector, BoP & Global Bodies (Cards 46–54)

> **Card 46: BoP Accounting Equilibrium**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Explain why the Balance of Payments always balances in an accounting sense.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Due to double-entry bookkeeping: every transaction generates an equal debit and credit entry. Current Account + Capital Account + Errors & Omissions = 0.

> **Card 47: Autonomous vs. Accommodating Transactions**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Distinguish between Autonomous ("Above the Line") and Accommodating ("Below the Line") transactions.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Autonomous transactions** are executed for independent commercial considerations (trade, FDI). **Accommodating transactions** are official central bank reserve movements executed solely to bridge autonomous deficits or surpluses.

> **Card 48: Arvind Mayaram Threshold Rule**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the Arvind Mayaram Committee threshold differentiating FDI from FPI.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: In a listed Indian enterprise, holding **$\ge 10\%$ equity** is classified as FDI; holding **$< 10\%$ equity** is FPI. All investments in unlisted entities are FDI.

> **Card 49: Forex Reserves Hierarchy**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: List the four components of India's Foreign Exchange Reserves in descending order of size.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: (1) Foreign Currency Assets (FCA, ~85-88%), (2) Physical Gold Bullion (~8-10%), (3) Special Drawing Rights (SDRs, ~2-3%), and (4) Reserve Tranche Position (RTP, $<1\%$).

> **Card 50: REER Valuation Interpretation**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What does a Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) value of 107 indicate about trade competitiveness?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Indicates that the rupee is **overvalued by ~7%** relative to trading partner currencies, eroding export price competitiveness and subsidizing imports.

> **Card 51: The Impossible Trinity (Trilemma)**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the three trade-offs of the Mundell-Fleming Trilemma (The Impossible Trinity).  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: An economy cannot simultaneously maintain: (1) Fixed Exchange Rate; (2) Free Capital Mobility; and (3) Independent Monetary Policy. Selecting any two necessitates giving up the third.

> **Card 52: Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) Limit**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) annual limit for resident individuals?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Resident individuals may remit up to **$250,000 per financial year** for permissible current and capital account transactions.

> **Card 53: SDR Currency Basket Ranking**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the five currencies in the IMF's Special Drawing Rights (SDR) basket in descending order of weight.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **US Dollar (43.38%)** $>$ **Euro (29.31%)** $>$ **Chinese Renminbi (12.28%)** $>$ **Japanese Yen (7.59%)** $>$ **British Pound (7.44%)**.

> **Card 54: India Non-Membership in ICSID**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Which single World Bank Group body has India refused to join, and why?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **ICSID** (International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes). India objects to foreign tribunals bypassing domestic judicial courts, lack of appellate mechanisms, and perceived pro-investor bias.

### Part VII: Agriculture, Industry & Infrastructure (Cards 55–63)

> **Card 55: Small and Marginal Landholdings**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What proportion of India's operational landholdings belong to Small and Marginal farmers?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **86.2% of total operational holdings** (Marginal $<1$ ha: 68.5%; Small 1–2 ha: 17.7%), operating on 47.3% of total operated land area.

> **Card 56: CACP Pricing Coverage**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the number of mandated crops for which the CACP recommends pricing.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **23 crops** (22 crops under Minimum Support Price + Sugarcane under Fair and Remunerative Price - FRP). Final approval is granted by CCEA.

> **Card 57: MSP Formula: Government vs. Swaminathan**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Contrast the Government operational MSP formula with the Swaminathan Committee recommendation.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Government formula: $\mathbf{MSP = 1.5 \times (A2 + FL)}$. Swaminathan recommendation: $\mathbf{MSP = C2 + 50\%}$ (Comprehensive cost including imputed rent on owned land and interest on owned capital).

> **Card 58: Composite MSME Classification**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the unified composite criteria for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (July 2020).  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Micro**: Investment ≤ ₹1 Cr & Turnover ≤ ₹5 Cr; **Small**: Investment ≤ ₹10 Cr & Turnover ≤ ₹50 Cr; **Medium**: Investment ≤ ₹50 Cr & Turnover ≤ ₹250 Cr. Export turnover is completely excluded.

> **Card 59: Reserved Public Sector Industries**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Which two industries remain reserved exclusively for the Public Sector?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: (1) **Atomic Energy** (Atomic Energy Act, 1962), and (2) **Railway Operations**.

> **Card 60: Eight Core Industries Weights**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Identify the largest and smallest industries in the Eight Core Industries basket of the IIP.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Largest: **Refinery Products (28.04% of Core)**. Smallest: **Fertilizers (2.63% of Core)**. The Eight Core Industries constitute 40.27% of total IIP weight.

> **Card 61: Hybrid Annuity Model Risk Allocation**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Explain the financing and traffic risk structure of the Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM).  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: NHAI provides 40% project cost in cash across 5 construction milestones. Developer raises 60%. **NHAI collects tolls and bears 100% traffic risk**, paying fixed biannual annuities over 15 years.

> **Card 62: Panchamrit Climate Commitments**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Detail the five "Panchamrit" climate pledges announced by India at COP26.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: (1) 500 GW non-fossil energy capacity by 2030; (2) 50% power capacity from renewables by 2030; (3) 45% emissions intensity reduction by 2030; (4) 1 Billion tonne carbon emissions reduction by 2030; and (5) **Net-Zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2070**.

> **Card 63: Green Hydrogen and NLP Targets**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: State the core targets of the National Green Hydrogen Mission and the National Logistics Policy.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: **Green Hydrogen Mission**: At least 5 MMT annual production by 2030 (+125 GW renewable capacity). **National Logistics Policy**: Reduce logistics costs to single digits (~8-9% of GDP) by 2030.

### Part VIII: Planning, Labor Codes & Social Dynamics (Cards 64–72)

> **Card 64: Planning Commission vs. NITI Aayog**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: Contrast the constitutional status and financial powers of the Planning Commission with NITI Aayog.  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Both non-constitutional, extra-statutory bodies. Planning Commission possessed **executive powers to allocate development funds and approve State plans**. NITI Aayog possesses **ZERO fund allocation powers**, functioning purely as a policy think-tank.

> **Card 65: Aspirational Districts Programme Architecture**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the 3C framework and coverage of the Aspirational Districts Programme (ADP)?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Covers **112 backward districts** based on the 3Cs: **Convergence** (of Central & State schemes), **Collaboration** (of officials), and **Competition** (monthly delta ranking across 5 socio-economic pillars).

> **Card 66: Code on Wages 50% Wage-Allowance Rule**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: What is the 50% Wage-Allowance Rule under the Code on Wages, 2019?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Allowances cannot exceed **50% of total employee compensation**. If allowances exceed 50%, the surplus is legally added to "wages", preventing employers from depressing Provident Fund and gratuity liabilities.

> **Card 67: Fixed-Term Employment Gratuity Parity**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: How does the Industrial Relations Code, 2020 reform gratuity for Fixed-Term Employees?  
> - **Sovereign Answer**: Fixed-Term Employees are eligible for statutory gratuity on completing **one year of continuous service** (pro-rata), instead of the traditional five-year vesting threshold.

> **Card 68: Social Security Code Gig Worker Funding**  
> - **Diagnostic Question**: How is the Social Security Fund for Gig and Platform Workers financed under the Code on Social Security, 2020?
