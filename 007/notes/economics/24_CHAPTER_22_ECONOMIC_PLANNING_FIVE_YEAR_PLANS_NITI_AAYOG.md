## 22.1 Evolution of Economic Planning: Pre-Independence to 1950

### The Institutional Inception of the Planning Commission (1950)
On **March 15, 1950**, the Government of India established the **Planning Commission** through an **extra-constitutional, non-statutory executive resolution**, following the recommendations of the Advisory Planning Board (1946) chaired by K. C. Neogy.
- **Constitutional Anchor**: Although the Planning Commission itself was not mentioned in the Constitution, its mandate derived directly from the **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)**, particularly **Article 39(b)** (distribution of ownership and control of material resources to subserve common good) and **Article 39(c)** (operation of the economic system does not result in the concentration of wealth).
- **Executive Architecture**: The Prime Minister served as the ex-officio Chairman. A full-time Deputy Chairman (holding Cabinet Minister rank) managed day-to-day operations with full-time expert members and union ministers as ex-officio members.

### The National Development Council (NDC, 1952)
To provide a federal platform for consulting the states on Five-Year Plans, the **National Development Council (Rashtriya Vikas Parishad)** was constituted on **August 6, 1952**, also via an executive resolution.
- **Composition**: Prime Minister (Chairman), all Union Cabinet Ministers, Chief Ministers of all States, Administrators of Union Territories, and Members of the Planning Commission.
- **Mandate**: Final approving body for Five-Year Plan drafts, review of national socio-economic policies, and recommendation of measures for achieving plan targets.

### Deep Dive into Landmark Strategic Shifts

#### 1. The Harrod-Domar Formulation (First Plan: 1951–56)
The First Plan relied on the Harrod-Domar growth formulation where economic expansion ($g$) is governed by the national savings rate ($s$) divided by the Incremental Capital-Output Ratio ($ICOR$):
$$g = \frac{s}{ICOR}$$
Because capital was scarce, the primary mechanism was mobilizing domestic savings and directing them into high-multiplier agricultural capital assets (dams, canals). The monsoon co-operated, leading to a remarkable 3.6% actual growth rate against a 2.1% target.

#### 2. The Nehru-Mahalanobis Strategy (Second Plan: 1956–61)
Designed by statistician Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis, the Second Plan marked India's structural pivot toward capital-goods-heavy, import-substituting industrialization.
- **The Core Axiom**: To produce consumer goods in the future, India had to construct machine-making machines today.
- **The Four-Sector Division**:
  1. *Sector $K$*: Capital goods sector (basic steel, heavy chemicals, electricity) — fully managed by the State.
  2. *Sector $C_1$*: Factory-produced consumer goods.
  3. *Sector $C_2$*: Household/cottage-produced consumer goods (promoted to absorb rural labor).
  4. *Sector $C_3$*: Services and agricultural output.
- **Consequences**: Established India's industrial backbone (Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela steel plants), but starved the consumer and agricultural sectors of capital, leading to food shortages and inflation in the Third Plan.

#### 3. The Gadgil Formula for Central Assistance (Fourth Plan: 1969)
Prior to the Fourth Plan, Central assistance to state plans was discretionary. In 1969, Deputy Chairman D. R. Gadgil formulated an objective mathematical matrix for distributing Central Plan assistance:
- **Population (1971 Census)**: 60%
- **Per Capita Income (Deviational Formula)**: 20%
- **Tax Effort**: 10%
- **Special Problems (drought, border, flood)**: 10%
*(Later revised as the Pranab Mukherjee-modified Gadgil Formula in 1991: Population 60%, Income Distance 25%, Fiscal Performance 7.5%, Special Problems 7.5%).*

## 22.3 Pathology of the Planning Commission: Why Was It Replaced?

By the 2000s, structural pathologies in the Planning Commission's operating model made it obsolete in a liberalized, decentralized market economy:

1. **One-Size-Fits-All "Control-Command" Mentality**:
   The Commission dictated state-level capital expenditure through Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS) with rigid conditionalities. A hill state like Himachal Pradesh and a coastal state like Kerala were subjected to identical scheme norms.
2. **Usurpation of Constitutional Federal Finance**:
   The **Finance Commission** is a constitutional body (Article 280) designed to govern Centre-State fiscal devolution. However, the Planning Commission (an executive body) came to control the distribution of **discretionary capital plan grants** under Article 282, reducing the Finance Commission to a secondary role.
3. **Imposition of Bureaucratic Approvals**:
   State Chief Ministers were forced to travel annually to New Delhi to have their state annual plan budgets vetted and approved by Planning Commission members who often lacked grassroots administrative context.
4. **Irrelevance in a Post-1991 Market Economy**:
   When private corporate investment grew to constitute over 75% of Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF), an agency designed to allocate physical targets for state-run factories became fundamentally misaligned with market realities.

## 22.4 The Inception & Architectural Framework of NITI Aayog

In his Independence Day speech on August 15, 2014, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the abolition of the Planning Commission. On **January 1, 2015**, the Union Cabinet passed a resolution creating the **National Institution for Transforming India (NITI Aayog)**.

### Institutional Structure of NITI Aayog
- **Chairperson**: Prime Minister of India.
- **Vice-Chairperson**: Appointed by the PM; enjoys Cabinet Minister rank (Past: Arvind Panagariya, Rajiv Kumar; Present: Suman Bery).
- **Governing Council**: Comprises the Prime Minister, Chief Ministers of all States and Union Territories with legislatures (Delhi, Puducherry, J&K), and Lieutenant Governors of other UTs. This unifies the erstwhile NDC directly into the executive apex.
- **Regional Councils**: Convened to address specific inter-state or regional challenges (e.g., North-Eastern States, Himalayan ecology).
- **Full-Time Members**: Experts of Minister of State rank (e.g., V. K. Saraswat, Ramesh Chand, V. K. Paul).
- **Ex-Officio Members**: Maximum of 4 Union Ministers nominated by the PM (Defense, Home, Finance, Agriculture).
- **Chief Executive Officer (CEO)**: Appointed by the PM for a fixed tenure in the rank of Secretary to the Government of India.
- **Two Core Hubs**:
  1. **Team India Hub**: Serves as the permanent interface between States and the Central Government.
  2. **Knowledge and Innovation Hub**: Builds the institution's think-tank capabilities, data analytics, and research ecosystem.

## 22.6 Decentralized Governance & Flagship Initiatives

### 1. Cooperative & Competitive Federalism
NITI Aayog drives a twin-track federal philosophy:
- **Cooperative Federalism**: Active hand-holding of states through joint working groups, resolving Centre-State bottlenecks, and fostering shared national priorities.
- **Competitive Federalism**: Ranking states on evidence-based, transparent diagnostic indices to incentivize performance improvement through healthy interstate competition.

### 2. The Aspirational Districts Programme (ADP, 2018)
Launched in **January 2018**, the ADP targets rapid transformation in **112 most backward districts** across India.
- **The 3C Philosophy**:
  1. **Convergence** of Central & State Schemes.
  2. **Collaboration** among Central, State Prabhari Officers, and District Collectors.
  3. **Competition** among districts driven by monthly public delta rankings.
- **Five Core Focus Sectors & Weightages**:
  - *Health & Nutrition*: **30%**
  - *Education*: **30%**
  - *Agriculture & Water Resources*: **20%**
  - *Financial Inclusion & Skill Development*: **10%**
  - *Basic Infrastructure*: **10%**

### 3. The Aspirational Blocks Programme (ABP, 2023)
Building on ADP's success, the ABP was launched in **January 2023** to transform **500 underdeveloped blocks across 329 districts** in 27 States and 4 UTs, preventing intra-district disparities and deepening grassroots governance.
