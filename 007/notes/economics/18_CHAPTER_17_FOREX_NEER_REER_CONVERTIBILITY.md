### The Evolution of India's Exchange Rate Architecture:
1. **Pre-1991 Era (Fixed Peg)**: The Indian Rupee was pegged officially to the British Pound Sterling (and later to a discretionary basket of currencies). Devaluations required executive cabinet declarations (e.g., 1966 devaluation and the two-step July 1991 devaluation).
2. **Liberalised Exchange Rate Management System (LERMS, March 1992)**:
   * A **dual-rate transitional system**: Exporters surrendered 40% of their foreign exchange earnings to the RBI at an official, fixed rate (used by the government to import essential crude oil and fertilizer).
   * The remaining 60% was sold in the open market at market-determined floating rates.
3. **Unified Exchange Rate System (UERS, March 1993 to Present)**:
   * The dual-rate mechanism was scrapped; the Rupee was fully unified on a **market-determined managed floating exchange rate regime**.

## 17.2 Nominal vs. Real Exchange Rates (NER vs. RER)

### 1. Nominal Exchange Rate (NER):
The raw relative price of two currencies:
* In Indian convention (direct quotation): Number of Indian Rupees required to purchase one US Dollar (e.g., $\text{USD/INR} = ₹83.50$).

---

### 2. Real Exchange Rate (RER):
The nominal exchange rate adjusted for the **relative inflation differential** (price levels) between the two nations:

$$\mathbf{RER} = \mathbf{NER} \times \frac{\mathbf{P_{\text{foreign}}}}{\mathbf{P_{\text{domestic}}}}$$

* **Economic Interpretation**:
  * RER measures the purchasing power of the domestic currency relative to foreign goods.
  * If India experiences significantly higher inflation than the United States ($P_{\text{domestic}} \uparrow$) while the nominal exchange rate ($NER$) remains unchanged, the **RER declines (the Rupee appreciates in real terms)**!
  * **Consequence**: Indian manufactured goods become relatively more expensive compared to American goods, eroding export price competitiveness and encouraging imports.

---

### The RBI 40-Currency Basket (Base Year: 2015–16 = 100)
In December 2020, the Reserve Bank overhauled its official effective exchange rate series:
* Expanded and recalibrated from the earlier 36-currency basket (Base 2004–05) to a modern **40-currency basket (Base Year 2015–16 = 100)** to incorporate dynamic trade patterns (expanded weights for China, ASEAN, and Gulf economies).
* Uses 3-year moving average trade weights (covering both exports and imports).

## 17.4 Currency Convertibility: Current vs. Capital Account

Currency convertibility refers to the sovereign freedom granted to residents and non-residents to freely convert the domestic currency into foreign currencies (and vice versa) at market-clearing rates without administrative permits or quantitative quotas.

```
                                  CURRENCY CONVERTIBILITY
                                             │
             ┌───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
             ▼                                                               ▼
[CURRENT ACCOUNT CONVERTIBILITY (CAC)]                         [CAPITAL ACCOUNT CONVERTIBILITY (FCAC)]
• Converting currency for trade in goods,                     • Converting currency for buying foreign
  services, travel, education, medical,                         stocks, bonds, real estate, acquiring
  and inward/outward factor remittances.                        overseas firms, or taking foreign debt.
• **100% FULLY CONVERTIBLE IN INDIA (Since 1994)**!            • **PARTIALLY CONVERTIBLE IN INDIA**.
```

---

### 1. Full Current Account Convertibility (August 1994)
* Under **Article VIII of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) Articles of Agreement**, member states pledge not to impose restrictions on the making of payments and transfers for current international transactions.
* On **19th August 1994**, India officially accepted Article VIII obligations, granting **Full Current Account Convertibility**.
* **Statutory Framework**: Governed by the *Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999* (which replaced the draconian criminal statute *Foreign Exchange Regulation Act - FERA, 1973*).
* **The Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)**:
  * Established by the RBI in 2004.
  * Allows all resident Indian individuals (including minors) to freely remit up to **\$250,000 per financial year** for permissible current and capital account transactions (education abroad, travel, gift, medical, or purchasing foreign equities/property).

---

### 2. Capital Account Convertibility: The Indian Approach
Unlike the current account, India has followed a path of **gradual, calibrated, and partial capital account convertibility**:
* **Where India is Open**:
  * Inward FDI (100% automatic route across almost all industrial sectors).
  * FPI investments in Indian equity and debt markets (subject to aggregate portfolio caps and Fully Accessible Route - FAR in sovereign G-Secs).
* **Where India Maintains Strict Capital Controls**:
  * Outward debt investments by domestic retail citizens (capped under LRS).
  * External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) capped with strict tenure, use-case, and interest spread limitations.
  * Prohibition on foreign investors trading in domestic rupee commodity derivatives.

## 17.5 The Tarapore Committee Framework on Capital Account Convertibility

The Reserve Bank constituted two historic expert committees under the chairmanship of former RBI Deputy Governor **S.S. Tarapore**:
1. **Tarapore Committee I (1997)**: Recommended a 3-year phased roadmap towards Full Capital Account Convertibility (FCAC). The roadmap was paused following the devastating 1997 East Asian Financial Crisis.
2. **Tarapore Committee II (2006)**: Re-evaluated the framework and reiterated that Full Convertibility should be a gradual destination contingent upon fulfilling strict macroeconomic preconditions.

---

### The Tarapore Pre-conditions for Full Capital Account Convertibility:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **1. Fiscal Consolidation**     │ Gross Fiscal Deficit of the Central Government must be      │
│                                 │ reduced and anchored at **$\le 3.5\%$ (or $3.0\%$) of GDP**,│
│                                 │ accompanied by a reduction in total public debt ratios.     │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **2. Inflation Mandate**        │ A track record of low and stable domestic inflation anchored│

├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **3. Banking Sector Health**    │ • **Gross NPAs of commercial banks must be below 5%**       │
│                                 │   (Tarapore II recommended Net NPAs $\le 3\%$).             │
│                                 │ • Deregulation of interest rates and elimination of CRR/SLR │

│                                 │   short-term external debt and currency liabilities.        │

```

> **Why India Avoids Premature Full Capital Account Convertibility**:  
> The 1997 East Asian Tiger collapse (Thailand, Indonesia, South Korea) proved that opening capital accounts before strengthening domestic banks allows massive foreign speculative capital ("hot money") to flood the banking sector, inflate asset bubbles, and instantly flee during a shock. Capital flight causes catastrophic currency collapse, bank runs, and macroeconomic devastation. India's partial convertibility insulated it from the 1997 Asian crisis and the 2008 Global Financial Meltdown.

## 17.6 The Impossible Trinity (The Mundell-Fleming Trilemma)

Formulated by economists Robert Mundell and Marcus Fleming, the **Trilemma** states that an open macroeconomic economy **cannot simultaneously achieve all three desirable policy goals**:

```
                                  THE IMPOSSIBLE TRINITY
                                            ▲
                                           / \
                                          /   \
                                         /  1  \
                                        /       \
                      [FIXED EXCHANGE] ─────────── [FREE CAPITAL]
                            RATE                     MOBILITY
                               \                 /
                                \       3       /
                                 \             /
                                  \     2     /
                                   \         /
                                    ▼       ▼
                             [INDEPENDENT MONETARY]
                                     POLICY
```

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Trilemma Pair Chosen            │ Inevitable Trade-Off / Abandoned Goal                       │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Option 1: Fixed Exchange Rate │ Must **ABANDON Independent Monetary Policy**!               │
│ + Free Capital Mobility**       │ Domestic interest rates are held hostage to foreign central │
│ *(e.g., Hong Kong, Eurozone)*   │ bank policy to maintain the peg. Cannot cut rates in slump. │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Option 2: Independent Policy  │ Must **ABANDON Fixed Exchange Rate (Float Currency)**!       │
│ + Free Capital Mobility**       │ Central bank freely sets policy repo rates to manage        │
│ *(e.g., USA, UK, Japan)*        │ domestic growth/inflation, but currency fluctuates wildly.  │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Option 3: Fixed Exchange Rate │ Must **ABANDON Free Capital Mobility (Capital Controls)**!   │
│ + Independent Monetary Policy** │ Allows sovereign interest rate control and stable exchange  │
│ *(e.g., China historically)*    │ rate, but requires rigid administrative bans on capital flow│
└─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **India's Intermediate Position**: India operates an "Intermediate Trilemma" regime: a **managed floating exchange rate**, **substantial (but not full) capital mobility**, and a **sovereign independent inflation-targeting monetary policy**.

## 17.7 Foreign Trade Policy 2023 (FTP 2023) & International Rupee Settlement

Released by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry, the **Foreign Trade Policy 2023** took effect on **April 1, 2023**. Unlike previous Five-Year FTP iterations (e.g., FTP 2015–20), FTP 2023 is a **dynamic, perpetual policy document** without a fixed sunset date, designed to be nimble and continuously responsive to shifting global trade realities.

### The $2 Trillion Export Target by 2030
FTP 2023 establishes a national target of achieving **US$ 2 Trillion in aggregate exports by 2030**:
- **US$ 1 Trillion** in Merchandise Exports
- **US$ 1 Trillion** in Services Exports (leveraging India's global IT, BPM, financial, and engineering consultancy leadership).

### International Trade Settlement in Indian Rupee (INR)
Under the RBI framework dated **July 11, 2022**, and codified within FTP 2023, India operationalized the mechanism for invoicing, payment, and settlement of international trade in Indian Rupees:
- **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SVRA)**: Foreign correspondent banks open SVRAs with authorized dealer banks in India.
- **Settlement Mechanics**: Indian importers pay in INR into the foreign bank's SVRA; Indian exporters are paid in INR from the foreign bank's SVRA.
- **Surplus Balances**: Foreign entities can invest surplus INR balances in Indian Government Securities (G-Secs), Treasury Bills, or local investment projects.
- **Strategic Impact**: Bypasses US Dollar payment gateways (SWIFT sanctions), insulates bilateral trade from exchange rate volatility, preserves forex reserves, and marks the initial step toward the internationalization of the Rupee.

### Towns of Export Excellence (TEE) & Status Holders
- **Towns of Export Excellence**: FTP 2023 added 4 new towns—**Faridabad** (Apparel), **Mirzapur** (Handmade Carpets), **Moradabad** (Handicrafts), and **Varanasi** (Handlooms and Handicrafts)—taking the national total to **43 TEEs** eligible for financial support under the Market Access Initiative (MAI).
- **Status Holder Tiers**: Recalibrated export thresholds (1-Star at $3M up to 5-Star at $800M) allowing exporters priority customs clearances, exemption from compulsory negotiation of documents, and two-star status holders to self-certify manufactured goods.

## 17.8 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

```
CARD 1: Define the Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) and explain what an index value of 108 indicates about export competitiveness.
ANSWER:
1. REER is the trade-weighted geometric average of bilateral nominal exchange rates of the domestic
currency against a basket of major trading partner currencies (RBI uses 40 currencies),
adjusted for relative inflation differentials.
2. A REER reading of 108 (> 100) indicates that the domestic currency is OVERVALUED by approximately
8% relative to its trade partners. Consequently, domestic manufactured exports are less price-competitive
in international markets, while foreign imports become cheaper.
```

```
CARD 2: What is the Mundell-Fleming Trilemma (The Impossible Trinity)? State the three policy goals and explain why a country cannot achieve all three simultaneously.
ANSWER:
1. The Three Goals: (a) Fixed / Stable Exchange Rate; (b) Free Capital Mobility; (c) Independent Monetary Policy.
2. Why Impossible: If an economy has free capital mobility and fixes its exchange rate, any independent
interest rate change by the central bank will trigger massive arbitrage capital flows that break the peg.
To defend the peg, the central bank must match foreign interest rates, surrendering domestic monetary policy autonomy.
```

```
CARD 3: Detail the three key macroeconomic preconditions recommended by the Tarapore Committees before India can transition to Full Capital Account Convertibility.
ANSWER:
1. Fiscal Prudence: Gross Fiscal Deficit of the Centre anchored at <= 3.5% (or 3.0%) of GDP.
2. Banking Resilience: Gross NPAs of commercial banks reduced to below 5% (Net NPAs <= 3%),
supported by adequate capital adequacy ratios.
3. Macroeconomic Stability: Inflation track record contained within a stable 3% to 5% band,
supported by forex reserves covering at least 6 months of imports.
```
