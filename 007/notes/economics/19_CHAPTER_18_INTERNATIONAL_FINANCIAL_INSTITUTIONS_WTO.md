## 18.1 The Bretton Woods Conference & The Post-War Economic Order

In July 1944, delegates from 44 Allied nations convened at the Mount Washington Hotel in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, USA, to construct a new multilateral financial architecture designed to prevent the competitive currency devaluations, protectionist tariff walls, and macroeconomic chaos that catalyzed the Great Depression and World War II.

### The Twin Institutions:
1. **The International Monetary Fund (IMF)**: Mandated to maintain **global macroeconomic and monetary stability**, oversee the international monetary system, and provide short-to-medium-term balance of payments (BoP) emergency financing.
2. **The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD / World Bank)**: Mandated to finance the **long-term post-war reconstruction of war-torn Europe** and promote capital-intensive infrastructure and economic development in emerging nations.

## 18.2 The International Monetary Fund (IMF) Architecture

### 1. Quotas and Voting Power
Every member country is assigned an official **Quota** upon joining, which forms the bedrock of its financial and institutional relationship with the IMF:
* **Determinants of Quota**: A weighted formula based on:
  * **GDP (50%)**: Measured 60% by market exchange rates and 40% by PPP.
  * **Openness (30%)**: Annual ratio of current payments and receipts to GDP.
  * **Economic Variability (15%)**: Volatility of current receipts and net capital flows.
  * **International Reserves (5%)**.
* **Threefold Function of Quotas**:
  1. **Subscription**: Determines the financial contribution the member must deposit into the Fund.
  2. **Voting Power**: Each member receives 250 basic votes $+$ 1 additional vote per SDR 100,000 of quota.
  3. **Access to Financing**: Determines the maximum credit volume a member can borrow.
* **The 85% Supermajority & The US Veto**: Major structural decisions (amending Articles of Agreement, quota revisions) require an **85% supermajority**. The United States holds **~16.5% of total voting power**, granting Washington an exclusive, unilateral **statutory veto** over the IMF!
* **India's Position**: India holds a **Quota of ~2.75%** and a **Voting Share of ~2.63%**, ranking as the **8th largest member**.

---

### 2. Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) - "Paper Gold"
Created by the IMF in **1969** under the First Amendment to the Articles of Agreement as a supplementary international reserve asset:
* **Legal Nature**: The SDR is **NEITHER a currency NOR a direct claim on the IMF**. It represents a potential claim on the freely usable currencies of IMF members.
* **Allocation**: Allocated to member countries in proportion to their IMF quotas (e.g., historic general allocation of \$650 Billion in August 2021 post-COVID).
* **The SDR Currency Valuation Basket (5 Currencies)**:
  * Reviewed every 5 years by the IMF Executive Board:

### 3. IMF Lending Facilities & Flagship Publications
* **Stand-By Arrangement (SBA)**: Short-term balance of payments support (12–36 months).
* **Extended Fund Facility (EFF)**: Medium-term assistance for countries facing structural balance of payments crises requiring profound institutional reforms (used by **India during the July 1991 crisis** to anchor the LPG reforms).
* **Poverty Reduction and Growth Trust (PRGT)**: Concessional zero-interest emergency financing for low-income countries.
* **Flagship IMF Reports**:
  1. *World Economic Outlook (WEO)* (Biannual global growth projections).
  2. *Global Financial Stability Report (GFSR)*.
  3. *Fiscal Monitor*.

> **Why India Refuses to Sign ICSID**:  
> India is a member of 4 of the 5 World Bank Group bodies, but has steadfastly rejected ICSID membership:
> 1. ICSID rules allow a foreign investor to bypass domestic judicial courts and drag a sovereign government directly to international arbitration.
> 2. The ICSID Convention does not provide an independent appellate review of arbitration awards.
> 3. Historical tribunals have demonstrated systemic pro-corporate bias against developing host nations in sovereign regulatory disputes (public health, taxation, and natural resources).

## 18.4 The World Trade Organization (WTO)

Established on **1st January 1995** via the **Marrakesh Agreement** (signed in Morocco in April 1994), concluding the 8-year **Uruguay Round negotiations (1986–1994)**. It superseded the provisional **General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT, 1947)**.

### The Fundamental Non-Discrimination Principles of WTO:
1. **Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) Treatment (Article I of GATT)**:
   * A member must treat all other WTO members equally. If a country grants a special favor (such as a lower customs tariff on a product) to one trading partner, it **must immediately and unconditionally extend that identical favor to all other WTO members**.
   * *Exceptions*: Regional Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) under GATT Article XXIV, and Generalized System of Preferences (GSP) granted to developing nations.
2. **National Treatment Principle (Article III of GATT)**:
   * Once imported foreign goods have cleared customs and entered the domestic market, they **must be treated no less favorably than domestically produced goods** with respect to internal sales taxes, safety standards, or regulations.

### The Bali Package (2013) & The Historic "Peace Clause"
* **The Conflict**: Under WTO AoA rules, the subsidy on price support (MSP) is calculated as the difference between the current MSP and an **external reference price fixed at 1986–88 global rates**!
  * Due to nearly four decades of global inflation, India's rice procurement under the National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013 threatened to breach the 10% Amber Box de minimis ceiling.
* **The Breakthrough**: At the 9th WTO Ministerial Conference in **Bali (2013)**, India aggressively negotiated the **"Peace Clause"**:
  * WTO members agreed **NOT to challenge or drag any developing nation to the Dispute Settlement Mechanism** for breaching the 10% Amber Box ceiling on public stockholding for national food security.
  * At the General Council meeting in 2014, India successfully negotiated that the **Peace Clause remains in effect indefinitely** until a permanent, negotiated solution is concluded.

---

### The WTO Dispute Settlement & Appellate Body Crisis (2019–Present)
* **Two-Tier System**: Dispute Settlement Body (DSB) operates via **Panels** (first instance) and the **Appellate Body** (7-member apex legal review court).
* **The Paralysis**: Since **December 2019**, the Appellate Body has been completely **dysfunctional and paralyzed** because the United States blocked all member reappointments, leaving it below the mandatory 3-judge quorum.
* **"Appealing into the Void"**: Countries that lose at the Panel level can file an appeal to the non-functioning Appellate Body, freezing the dispute indefinitely without enforceable penalties.
* **The Interim Solution (MPIA)**: Several members (EU, China, Brazil) established the *Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement (MPIA)* under Article 25 of the DSU; India has stayed out, demanding a restored, permanent two-tier multilateral mechanism.

## 18.6 TRIPS, Public Health & The Indian Patent Battle

The Agreement on **Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS)** established global minimum standards for protecting patents, copyrights, trademarks, and industrial designs.

### 1. The 2005 Indian Patent Transition:
* Prior to 2005, the *Indian Patents Act, 1970* recognized **Process Patents ONLY** in pharmaceuticals and food (allowing Indian generic drug manufacturers to reverse-engineer life-saving drugs by inventing alternate manufacturing processes, turning India into the "Pharmacy of the Developing World").
* To comply with TRIPS, India amended its Patents Act in 2005 to grant **Product Patents** for **20 years**.

---

### 2. Safeguards Against Evergreening: Section 3(d)
To prevent multinational pharmaceutical conglomerates from extending expired 20-year monopolies through minor molecular modifications, Parliament enacted **Section 3(d)** of the Patents Act:
* **The Mandate**: The mere discovery of a new form of a known substance, new polymorph, or derivative that does not result in the **enhancement of known therapeutic efficacy** CANNOT be granted a new patent.
* **The Landmark Precedent**: In *Novartis AG vs. Union of India (2013)*, the Supreme Court of India upheld Section 3(d), rejecting Novartis's patent claim for its anti-cancer drug *Glivec* (imatinib mesylate), affirming the sovereign right to prioritize affordable healthcare.

---

### 3. Compulsory Licensing (CL) & The Doha Declaration (2001)
* **The Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health (2001)** affirmed that the TRIPS agreement should be interpreted in a manner that supports WTO members' right to protect public health and promote access to medicines for all.
* **Mechanism of Compulsory License (Section 84 of Indian Patents Act)**:
  * A Controller of Patents can authorize a third-party domestic manufacturer to produce a patented drug without the consent of the patent holder after 3 years from grant if:
    1. Reasonable requirements of the public have not been satisfied;
    2. The patented invention is not available at a reasonably affordable price;
    3. The invention is not worked in India.
  * **Historic Precedent**: In 2012, India issued its first Compulsory License to **Natco Pharma** to manufacture Bayer's kidney and liver cancer drug *Nexavar* (slashing the monthly cost from ₹2.8 Lakh to ₹8,800).
