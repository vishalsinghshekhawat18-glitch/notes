## 22.2 The Master Comparative Distinction Compendium

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MASTER DISTINCTION 1: REVENUE EXPENDITURE vs. CAPITAL EXPENDITURE                           │
├─────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Revenue Expenditure**         │ Operational consumption expenditure. Neither creates      │
│                                 │ physical/financial assets nor reduces sovereign liability.│
│                                 │ *Includes*: Interest payments, defense salaries, subsidies│
│                                 │ and **ALL GRANTS TO STATES (even if meant for capex!)**.  │
├─────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Capital Expenditure**         │ Asset or liability modifying outlay. Creates durable      │
│                                 │ productive assets (highways, rails) OR reduces sovereign  │
│                                 │ debt liabilities (repayment of past loans).               │
└─────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MASTER DISTINCTION 2: FISCAL DEFICIT vs. PRIMARY DEFICIT                                    │
├─────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Fiscal Deficit (FD)**         │ The total net borrowing requirement of the government for │
│                                 │ the financial year. $\mathbf{FD = Total Exp - NonDebt Rec}$│
├─────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Primary Deficit (PD)**        │ $\mathbf{PD = Fiscal Deficit - Interest Payments}$. Reflects│
│                                 │ the current fiscal prudence of the executive excluding the│
│                                 │ interest burden accrued from legacy historical borrowing. │
└─────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
```

```
├─────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Foreign Direct Investment**   │ Acquisition of **$\ge 10\%$ equity** in a listed Indian   │
│                                 │ firm (or any % in unlisted). Lasting interest, board seats│
│                                 │ and management control. Non-debt, illiquid, sticky capex. │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Foreign Portfolio Investment**│ Holding of **$< 10\%$ equity** in a listed firm. Purely   │
│                                 │ financial investment with **zero management control**.    │
│                                 │ Highly liquid, volatile **"Hot Money"**.                  │
└─────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MASTER DISTINCTION 5: DISGUISED UNEMPLOYMENT vs. STRUCTURAL UNEMPLOYMENT                     │

│                                 │ to technological automation or industrial transformation. │
```

## 22.4 The Universal Diagnostic Active Recall Bank (63 Core Testing Cards)

Test your mastery across the entire macroeconomics curriculum. Each card probes a critical conceptual, institutional, or mathematical foundation:

### Part I: Foundations & Output Accounting (Cards 1–9)
```
CARD 01: What defines a Pure Public Good, and why does the private market fail to supply it?
ANSWER: Non-excludable (cannot prevent non-payers from consuming) and Non-rival (one person's consumption
does not diminish availability for others). Free-rider problem causes private market failure.

CARD 02: Differentiate between a Veblen good and a Giffen good.
ANSWER: Both have upward-sloping demand curves. Veblen goods are luxury status symbols (conspicuous
consumption); Giffen goods are inferior staple necessities lacking close substitutes where a price rise
eats real income and forces higher consumption.

CARD 03: State the exact mathematical bridge between Factor Cost, Basic Price, and Market Price.
ANSWER: Basic Price = Factor Cost + Production Taxes - Production Subsidies.
Market Price = Basic Price + Product Taxes - Product Subsidies.

CARD 04: What is the Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR), and what does a rising ICOR signify?
ANSWER: ICOR = Capital Investment / Increase in Output. A rising ICOR signifies declining capital
efficiency (more units of capital are required to produce one unit of economic output).

CARD 05: What are the three dimensions and aggregation methodology of the post-2010 Human Development Index?
ANSWER: Health (Life expectancy at birth), Education (Mean years + Expected years of schooling),
Standard of Living (GNI per capita PPP). Aggregated using the GEOMETRIC MEAN of normalized indices.

CARD 06: How does India's National MPI differ from the UNDP Global MPI?
ANSWER: Global MPI has 10 indicators; NITI Aayog's National MPI has 12 indicators, adding Antenatal Care
(Health dimension) and Bank Account (Standard of Living dimension).

CARD 07: Define Green GDP.
ANSWER: Green GDP = Traditional GDP minus the monetary cost of natural resource depletion and environmental
degradation resulting from production.

CARD 08: Distinguish between Final Goods and Intermediate Goods to explain the double-counting hazard.
ANSWER: Intermediate goods are fully used up as inputs in the production of other goods within the
same accounting year. Counting intermediate goods alongside final goods inflates GDP artificially.

CARD 09: What is the Output Gap, and what is its policy implication during an inflationary gap?
ANSWER: Output Gap = Actual GDP - Potential GDP. An inflationary gap (Actual > Potential) indicates
overheated aggregate demand straining capacity, requiring monetary and fiscal tightening.
```

### Part II: Money & Central Banking (Cards 10–18)
```
CARD 10: State the statutory reserve backing mandated by the Minimum Reserve System under Section 33 of the RBI Act.
ANSWER: RBI must maintain a minimum reserve backing of ₹200 Crore, of which at least ₹115 Crore must be
in physical gold bullion and the remaining ₹85 Crore in foreign sovereign securities.

CARD 11: Detail the components of M1 and M3 monetary aggregates.
ANSWER: M1 (Narrow Money) = Currency with the Public + Demand Deposits with Banking System + 'Other'
Deposits with RBI. M3 (Broad Money) = M1 + Time Deposits with the Banking System.

CARD 12: Write the Money Multiplier formula and explain how an increase in Currency Deposit Ratio (c) affects it.
ANSWER: m = (1 + c) / (c + r). An increase in c reduces the money multiplier because more cash is held
by the public rather than deposited in commercial banks for credit expansion.

CARD 13: What constitutes a failure of the RBI to achieve its inflation target under Section 45ZN of the RBI Act?
ANSWER: If headline CPI inflation remains outside the 2% to 6% tolerance band (4% +/- 2%) for THREE
CONSECUTIVE QUARTERS. RBI must submit an explanatory report to the Central Government.

CARD 14: What is the composition and voting mechanism of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)?
ANSWER: 6 members: 3 from RBI (Governor, Deputy Governor, 1 officer) and 3 appointed by Central Govt.
Decisions by majority; the RBI Governor exercises a CASTING VOTE in the event of a tie.

CARD 15: What is the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF), and how does it differ fundamentally from the Reverse Repo?
ANSWER: SDF is the floor of the LAF corridor (-25 bps below Repo). Unlike Reverse Repo, the SDF absorbs
surplus liquidity from commercial banks WITHOUT the RBI pledging any G-Sec collateral.

CARD 16: What is the 50 bps LAF Corridor architecture?
ANSWER: MSF (Ceiling: Repo + 25 bps) <---> Policy Repo Rate (Anchor) <---> SDF (Floor: Repo - 25 bps).

CARD 17: Trace the historical evolution of bank lending rate benchmarks from PLR to EBLR.
ANSWER: Prime Lending Rate (PLR) -> Benchmark PLR (BPLR, 2003) -> Base Rate (2010) -> Marginal Cost of
Funds based Lending Rate (MCLR, 2016) -> External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR, 2019).

CARD 18: What are the two core capital components established by the Bimal Jalan Economic Capital Framework?
ANSWER: Contingent Risk Buffer (CRB: realized equity maintained between 5.5% and 6.5% of balance sheet)
and Currency and Gold Revaluation Account (CGRA: unrealized valuation gains, non-distributable).
```

### Part III: Banking, NPAs & Financial Markets (Cards 19–27)
```
CARD 19: State the two core statutory restrictions differentiating Payments Banks from Small Finance Banks.
ANSWER: Payments Banks CANNOT lend loans and CANNOT issue credit cards (demand deposits only, capped at ₹2L).
Small Finance Banks are full-fledged basic banks that can lend (with 75% PSL quota).

CARD 20: State the minimum Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) mandated for Indian Scheduled Commercial Banks.
ANSWER: Indian SCBs must maintain a minimum CRAR of 9.0% + 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) = 11.5%
(Public Sector Banks: 12.0% total). More stringent than Basel III's 8% + 2.5% = 10.5%.

CARD 21: What are the three parameters monitored under the RBI's Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) framework?
ANSWER: Capital (CRAR / Tier 1 Leverage), Asset Quality (Net NPA ratio >= 6.0%), and Leverage Ratio (< 3.5%).

CARD 22: Define a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) and classify the Special Mention Account (SMA) stages.
ANSWER: Loan overdue > 90 days. SMA-0: 1 to 30 days overdue; SMA-1: 31 to 60 days overdue; SMA-2: 61 to 90 days overdue.

CARD 23: State the voting majority required by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) to approve a resolution plan under IBC 2016.
ANSWER: 66% of voting share of financial creditors. Outer resolution timeframe: 330 days (including litigation).

CARD 24: What is the corporate structure of the National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited (NARCL)?
ANSWER: Dual Bad Bank model: NARCL (51% owned by PSBs, aggregates bad debts, pays 15% cash + 85% Security
Receipts backed by ₹30,600 Cr sovereign guarantee) + IDRCL (51% owned by Private Banks, turnaround manager).

CARD 25: Explain the inverse relationship between Bond Prices and Bond Yields.
ANSWER: As bond market prices rise, the fixed coupon payment yields a smaller percentage return on the
purchase price; hence Yield = Coupon / Market Price falls. Price and Yield move in opposite directions.

CARD 26: What are Masala Bonds, and who bears the foreign exchange risk?
ANSWER: Rupee-denominated corporate bonds issued in overseas offshore financial centers (e.g., London).
The OVERSEAS INVESTOR bears the currency depreciation risk.

CARD 27: List the three standard tenures of Indian Treasury Bills and identify who is legally prohibited from issuing them.
ANSWER: 91 Days, 182 Days, and 364 Days. STATE GOVERNMENTS are legally prohibited from issuing T-Bills.
```

### Part IV: Public Finance, Fiscal Federalism & Tax (Cards 28–36)
```
CARD 28: Differentiate between the Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266(1)) and Public Account of India (Article 266(2)).
ANSWER: CFI holds sovereign taxes and market loans, requiring prior parliamentary law (Appropriation Act)
for withdrawals. PAI holds money as a trustee (PPF, small savings); operated by executive action.

CARD 29: Why are Central grants to States for capital asset creation accounted as Revenue Expenditure?
ANSWER: Because the physical capital asset created belongs to the State Government, not the Central
Government. CFI's balance sheet acquires no physical asset or debt reduction.

CARD 30: State the N.K. Singh FRBM Committee debt targets for General Government, Centre, and States.
ANSWER: General Government Debt <= 60% of GDP by FY23 (Central Govt <= 40%; State Govts <= 20%).
Fiscal Deficit target: 3.0% of GDP (with 0.5% GDP escape clause §4(2)).

CARD 31: Define Tax Buoyancy and contrast it with Tax Elasticity.
ANSWER: Tax Buoyancy = (% Change in Tax Revenue) / (% Change in Nominal GDP), incorporating all policy
reforms and rate hikes. Tax Elasticity isolates pure automatic response holding tax laws constant.

CARD 32: State the voting distribution and majority rule in the Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council.
ANSWER: Centre holds 1/3 (33.33%); all 31 States/UTs hold 2/3 (66.67%). Decisions require 75% weighted
majority of members present and voting. Both Centre and States hold mutual veto power.

CARD 33: List the commodities temporarily and permanently outside the scope of GST.
ANSWER: Permanently outside: Alcohol for human consumption (Article 366(12A)). Temporarily outside:
5 petroleum products (crude oil, petrol, diesel, natural gas, ATF) under Article 279A(5).

CARD 34: What constitutes the Divisible Pool under Article 270, and what are its three exclusions?
ANSWER: Divisible Pool = Gross Tax Revenue minus: (1) Surcharges (Article 271); (2) All Cesses; and
(3) Statutory Cost of Collection. Shared with States (41% under 15th FC).

CARD 35: Detail the horizontal devolution formula criteria of the 15th Finance Commission.
ANSWER: Income Distance (45%), Population 2011 (15%), Area (15%), Forest & Ecology (10%),
Demographic Performance (12.5%), Tax Effort (2.5%). Total = 100%.

CARD 36: Under what constitutional provision can the Central Government cap State market borrowings?
ANSWER: Article 293(3): If a State has an outstanding loan from the Centre or backed by a Central guarantee,
it cannot borrow from domestic markets without prior consent of the Government of India.
```

### Part V: Inflation, Employment & Poverty (Cards 37–45)
```
CARD 37: Distinguish between Headline Inflation and Core Inflation.
ANSWER: Headline Inflation measures the entire index basket. Core Inflation = Headline Inflation minus
volatile Food and Fuel components, isolating underlying sticky demand-side trends.

CARD 38: State the compiling agency, base year, and dominant component of CPI (Combined) vs. WPI.
ANSWER: CPI Combined: NSO (MoSPI), Base 2012, Food & Beverages (45.86%), Goods + Services.
WPI: Office of Economic Adviser (DPIIT), Base 2011–12, Manufactured Products (64.23%), Goods Only.

CARD 39: Explain the Friedman-Phelps Long-Run Phillips Curve (LRPC).
ANSWER: In the long run, workers adjust inflation expectations. Unemployment returns to its Natural
Rate (NAIRU). The LRPC is a vertical line at NAIRU; monetary expansion creates inflation without employment gains.

CARD 40: Differentiate between Usual Principal and Subsidiary Status (UPSS) and Current Weekly Status (CWS).
ANSWER: UPSS (365 days reference): Person is employed if they worked even 30 days in subsidiary capacity.
CWS (7 days reference): Person is employed if they worked for at least 1 hour on any 1 day of the week.

CARD 41: Define Disguised Unemployment and identify the sector where it is pervasive in India.
ANSWER: A situation where more laborers are employed than technically needed; Marginal Productivity of
Labor is zero (MP_L = 0). Characteristic of Indian peasant agriculture.

CARD 42: What is the retrenchment threshold under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020?
ANSWER: Raised from 100 workers to 300 workers. Establishments with under 300 workers do not need prior
government permission for layoffs, retrenchments, or closure.

CARD 43: Why did the Suresh Tendulkar Committee abandon calorie norms in estimating poverty?
ANSWER: Calorie consumption was declining across all expenditure classes even as incomes and real living
standards rose. Tendulkar anchored poverty to urban consumption standards with health and education under MRP.

CARD 44: State the official statutory poverty headcount ratio and poverty lines for India (2011–12).
ANSWER: Suresh Tendulkar Committee: 21.9% of population (Rural 25.7%, Urban 13.7%). Rural line: ₹816/month
(~₹27/day); Urban line: ₹1,000/month (~₹33/day).

CARD 45: What is the Gini Coefficient, and what do index values of 0 and 1 represent?
ANSWER: Gini = Area A / (Area A + Area B) from Lorenz Curve. 0 = Perfect Equality (all receive identical income);
1 = Perfect Inequality (one individual captures 100% of national income).
```

### Part VI: External Sector, BoP & Global Bodies (Cards 46–54)
```
CARD 46: Explain why the Balance of Payments always balances in an accounting sense.
ANSWER: Double-entry bookkeeping: Every credit inflow must be balanced by a debit outflow, capital transfer,
or accommodating official reserve change. Current Account + Capital Account + Errors & Omissions = 0.

CARD 47: Distinguish between Autonomous ("Above the Line") and Accommodating ("Below the Line") transactions.
ANSWER: Autonomous transactions are undertaken for independent commercial/profit motives (trade, FDI).
Accommodating transactions are undertaken by the central bank (drawing down/accumulating forex) to bridge the gap.

CARD 48: State the Arvind Mayaram Committee threshold differentiating FDI from FPI.
ANSWER: In a listed Indian company, acquiring 10% or more of paid-up equity is FDI; less than 10% is FPI.
All investments in unlisted entities are FDI.

CARD 49: List the four components of India's Foreign Exchange Reserves in descending order of size.
ANSWER: (1) Foreign Currency Assets (FCA, ~85-88%), (2) Gold Bullion (~8-10%), (3) Special Drawing Rights
(SDRs, ~2-3%), (4) Reserve Tranche Position (RTP, <1%).

CARD 50: What does a Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) value of 107 indicate about trade competitiveness?
ANSWER: An index value of 107 (> 100) indicates that the currency is OVERVALUED by ~7% relative to its trade
partners, impairing export price competitiveness and encouraging imports.

CARD 51: State the three trade-offs of the Mundell-Fleming Trilemma (The Impossible Trinity).
ANSWER: An economy cannot simultaneously achieve: (1) Fixed Exchange Rate; (2) Free Capital Mobility;
and (3) Independent Monetary Policy. Choosing any two necessitates abandoning the third.

CARD 52: What is the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) annual limit for resident individuals?
ANSWER: Up to $250,000 per financial year for permissible current and capital account transactions.

CARD 53: State the five currencies in the IMF's Special Drawing Rights (SDR) basket in descending order of weight.
ANSWER: US Dollar (43.38%) > Euro (29.31%) > Chinese Renminbi (12.28%) > Japanese Yen (7.59%) > British Pound (7.44%).

CARD 54: Which single World Bank Group body has India refused to join, and why?
ANSWER: ICSID (International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes). India objects to foreign
corporations bypassing domestic courts, lack of an independent appellate review, and historical pro-investor bias.
```

### Part VII: Agriculture, Industry & Infrastructure (Cards 55–63)
```
CARD 55: What proportion of India's operational landholdings belong to Small and Marginal farmers?
ANSWER: 86.2% of total operational holdings (Marginal <1 ha: 68.5%; Small 1-2 ha: 17.7%), operating on 47.3% of operated land.

CARD 56: State the number of mandated crops for which the CACP recommends pricing.
ANSWER: 23 crops (22 crops under Minimum Support Price + Sugarcane under Fair and Remunerative Price - FRP).
Final approval by CCEA.

CARD 57: Contrast the Government's operational MSP formula with the Swaminathan Committee's recommendation.
ANSWER: Government formula: MSP = 1.5 * (A2 + FL). Swaminathan National Commission on Farmers formula:
MSP = C2 + 50% (Comprehensive cost including imputed owned land rent and capital interest).

CARD 58: State the unified composite criteria for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (July 2020).
ANSWER: Micro: Investment <= ₹1 Cr & Turnover <= ₹5 Cr; Small: Investment <= ₹10 Cr & Turnover <= ₹50 Cr;
Medium: Investment <= ₹50 Cr & Turnover <= ₹250 Cr. Zero manufacturing-services distinction; export turnover excluded!

CARD 59: Which two industries remain reserved exclusively for the Public Sector?
ANSWER: (1) Atomic Energy (Atomic Energy Act, 1962), and (2) Railway Operations.

CARD 60: Identify the largest and smallest industries in the Eight Core Industries basket of the IIP.
ANSWER: Largest: Refinery Products (28.04% of Core; rank 1). Smallest: Fertilizers (2.63% of Core; rank 8).
Eight Core Industries form 40.27% of total IIP.

CARD 61: Explain the financing and traffic risk structure of the Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM).
ANSWER: NHAI provides 40% project cost in cash in 5 milestones. Concessionaire raises 60%. NHAI collects
all tolls and bears 100% traffic risk, paying the developer fixed biannual annuities over 15 years.

CARD 62: Detail the five "Panchamrit" climate pledges announced by India at COP26.
ANSWER: (1) 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; (2) 50% electric power capacity from renewables by 2030;
(3) 45% reduction in emissions intensity of GDP by 2030; (4) 1 Billion tonne carbon emissions cut by 2030;
(5) Net-Zero carbon emissions by 2070!

CARD 63: State the core targets of the National Green Hydrogen Mission and the National Logistics Policy.
ANSWER: Green Hydrogen: At least 5 MMT annual production by 2030 (+125 GW RE). Logistics Policy: Reduce
national logistics costs to single digits (~8-9% of GDP) by 2030 and achieve top-25 rank in World Bank LPI.
```
