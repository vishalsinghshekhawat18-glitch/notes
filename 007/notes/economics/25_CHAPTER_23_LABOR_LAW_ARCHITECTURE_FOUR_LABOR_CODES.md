## 23.1 Constitutional & Historical Foundations of Indian Labor Policy

In the constitutional architecture of India, **Labor is a Concurrent List subject (List III, Seventh Schedule)**:
- **Entry 22**: Trade unions; industrial and labour disputes.
- **Entry 23**: Social security and social insurance; employment and unemployment.
- **Entry 24**: Welfare of labour including conditions of work, provident funds, employers' liability, workmen's compensation, invalidity and old age pensions, and maternity benefits.

### The 2nd National Commission on Labour (2002)
The genesis of the four labor codes lies in the **Second National Commission on Labour (2002)**, chaired by **Ravindra Varma**. The commission concluded that:
1. India's existing labor laws were archaic, contradictory, excessively complex, and unsuited to an open market economy.
2. The prevailing framework of **over 40 central labor acts and 200+ state laws** created overlapping jurisdictions, inspector harassment, and institutional corruption.
3. It recommended consolidating the entire legislative architecture into **four or five broad codes** covering:
   - Industrial Relations
   - Wages
   - Social Security
   - Safety, Health & Working Conditions
   - Welfare

## 23.4 Deep Dive: Code by Code Analytical Breakdown

### 1. The Code on Wages, 2019
The Code on Wages removes statutory thresholds and extends the right to minimum wages and timely payment to **all 500+ million workers** across both organized and unorganized sectors.

- **Unified Definition of Wages**:
  Consists of three components: Basic Pay, Dearness Allowance (DA), and Retaining Allowance.
  - **The 50% Rule**: Allowances (HRA, conveyance, overtime, bonus) cannot exceed **50% of total compensation**. If allowances exceed 50%, the excess amount is automatically added back to "wages", protecting workers' provident fund (EPF) and gratuity contributions from being artificially diluted by employers.
- **Universal Statutory Minimum Wage**:
  Eliminates the historical distinction between "scheduled employment" and non-scheduled employment. Minimum wages must be paid across all industries.
- **National Floor Minimum Wage**:
  - The Central Government is empowered to fix a statutory **National Floor Minimum Wage**, taking into account regional minimum living standards.
  - State Governments may fix their state minimum wage rates, but **no State can set a minimum wage below the National Floor Minimum Wage**.
  - If a State's existing minimum wage is already higher than the National Floor, the State cannot reduce it.
- **Review Frequency**: Minimum wages must be reviewed and revised at intervals not exceeding **5 years**.
- **Gender Inclusivity**: Prohibits gender discrimination in recruitment and wage payment for identical work.

---

### 2. The Industrial Relations Code, 2020
The IR Code balances workers' collective bargaining rights with employer flexibility to hire and expand.

- **Threshold for Prior Government Permission Raised**:
  - Under Chapter V-B of the legacy Industrial Disputes Act, establishments with 100 or more workers required prior government approval before retrenchment, lay-off, or closure.
  - The IR Code raises this statutory threshold from **100 workers to 300 workers**.
  - Establishments with fewer than 300 workers can lay off or retrench workers without prior government sanction, subject to paying statutory compensation (15 days' wages per completed year of service).
- **Statutory Recognition of Fixed-Term Employment (FTE)**:
  - Employers can hire workers on fixed-term contracts for specified project durations without third-party labor contractors.
  - **Equality of Treatment**: FTE workers are entitled to identical wages, working hours, healthcare allowances, and statutory benefits as permanent employees doing similar work.
  - **Gratuity Concession**: FTE workers are eligible for statutory gratuity on a pro-rata basis if they render service under the contract for a period of **one year** (contrasting with the standard 5-year requirement for permanent workers).
- **Sole Negotiating Union Framework**:
  - To prevent inter-union rivalries, if an establishment has a single trade union with **51% or more membership**, it is recognized as the sole negotiating union.
  - If no union has 51%, a **Negotiating Council** is formed consisting of representatives of trade unions that possess at least **20% membership**.
- **Mandatory 14-Day Notice for Strikes & Lockouts**:
  - Workers across **all industrial establishments** (not just public utilities) are prohibited from going on strike without giving **at least 14 days' notice** (and not more than 60 days before the strike).
  - Strikes are strictly prohibited during the pendency of conciliation proceedings and 7 days after their conclusion.
- **Worker Reskilling Fund**:
  Establishes a statutory fund financed by employers contributing an amount equal to **15 days' last drawn wages** for every retrenched worker. The fund must be utilized to credit funds directly to the worker's bank account within 45 days of retrenchment.

---

### 3. The Code on Social Security, 2020
The Code on Social Security creates an institutional framework to transition from fragmented welfare boards to universal social protection.

- **Statutory Recognition of Gig & Platform Workers**:
  - **Gig Worker**: A person who performs work or participates in a work arrangement and earns from such activities outside of traditional employer-employee relationships.
  - **Platform Worker**: A person engaged in platform work (mediated by an online platform or mobile application, e.g., delivery drivers, ride-hailing drivers).
- **Social Security Fund for Gig & Platform Workers**:
  - Establishes a dedicated National Social Security Fund financed through:
    1. Central and State budgetary grants.
    2. Mandatory contributions from online aggregators (e.g., ride-sharing, food delivery, logistics, e-marketplaces).
  - **Aggregator Contribution Contribution Rate**: Aggregators must contribute **1% to 2% of their annual turnover**, subject to a maximum cap of **5% of the aggregate amount paid or payable to gig and platform workers**.
- **National Social Security Board**:
  Chaired by the Union Minister for Labour and Employment, with representation from central ministries, state governments, aggregators, and gig worker unions.
- **Universal Registration on Central Portal**:
  Statutory mandate for all unorganized, gig, and platform workers to register via Aadhaar on the **e-Shram portal**.

---

### 4. The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020
The OSH Code consolidates disparate safety, hygiene, and working condition standards into a single regulatory code.

- **Revised Factory Threshold**:
  The threshold definition of a "factory" is raised:
  - From 10 to **20 workers** (for premises using electricity).
  - From 20 to **40 workers** (for premises operating without electricity).
- **Contract Labor Threshold**:
  The threshold for applying the Contract Labor provisions is raised from 20 to **50 contract workers**.
- **Women Permitted for Night Shifts**:
  - Women are statutorily permitted to work in **all establishments for all types of work**, including night shifts (between 7:00 PM and 6:00 AM).
  - **Mandatory Safeguards**: Permitted strictly with the woman worker's explicit consent, subject to employer guarantees regarding occupational safety, transport facility from residence, and crèche provisions.
- **Inter-State Migrant Worker Protections**:
  - A worker who moves independently from one state to another and obtains employment earning up to ₹18,000 per month is statutorily classified as an inter-state migrant worker.
  - **Entitlements**: Mandatory appointment letter, annual **journey allowance** to and from their native place paid by the employer, access to the Public Distribution System (PDS) in either the home or host state (ONORC), and free annual medical checkups for workers aged 45+.
- **Single Registration & Pan-India License**:
  Replaces 10+ separate registrations with a single electronic registration, eliminating inspector visits through computerized randomized inspections.

### Critical Bottlenecks Delaying Full Pan-India Notification:
1. **Federal Harmonization**: Labor is highly sensitive politically. Some industrialized states have hesitated to notify the 300-worker threshold and 14-day strike notice over trade union protests.
2. **The 50% Wage Rule & Take-Home Salary Concerns**: Corporate employers expressed concern that capping allowances at 50% would sharply increase provident fund and gratuity obligations, increasing payroll costs while reducing workers' immediate take-home pay.
3. **Aggregator Welfare Financing Mechanics**: Formulating the precise algorithmic calculation of aggregator contributions (1-2% of turnover vs 5% of payout) required extensive inter-ministerial coordination with the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).
