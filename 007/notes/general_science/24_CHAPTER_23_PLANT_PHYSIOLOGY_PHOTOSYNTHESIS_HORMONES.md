Plant physiology examines the chemical, thermodynamic, and bioenergetic principles underlying autotrophic terrestrial survival. ## 23.1 PLANT WATER RELATIONS & TRANSPIRATION DYNAMICS

### 23.1.1 Thermodynamic Concept of Water Potential ($\Psi_w$)
Water moves spontaneously from a region of higher chemical potential (free energy) to one of lower chemical potential. By international convention, the water potential of pure water at standard temperature and atmospheric pressure is defined as **Zero ($\Psi_w = 0\text{ MPa}$)**. Addition of solutes lowers free energy, rendering water potential negative ($\Psi_w < 0$).

$$\Psi_w = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$$

* **Solute Potential ($\Psi_s$, Osmotic Potential):** Magnitude of reduction in water potential due to the dissolution of solute molecules. Always **negative**; becomes increasingly negative as solute concentration rises.
* **Pressure Potential ($\Psi_p$, Turgor Pressure):** Hydrostatic pressure exerted by the cell sap against the rigid cellulosic cell wall. Usually **positive** in turgid cells; zero in flaccid cells; negative during extreme tension in xylem vessels.

```
       PLASMOLYSIS PROGRESSION IN PLANT CELLS
       
 [Hypertonic External Medium]    [Isotonic Medium]          [Hypotonic Medium]
       (Exosmosis)                  (Dynamic Equilibrium)      (Endosmosis)
            │                               │                        │
            ▼                               ▼                        ▼
 [Plasmolyzed Cell]              [Flaccid Cell]             [Turgid Cell]
 Protoplast shrinks completely   Protoplast in contact      Protoplast exerts high
 away from cell wall; space      with wall without turgor   TURGOR PRESSURE (Turgid;
 filled with external solution   (Turgor Pressure = 0)      highest Psi_w)
```

* **Plasmolysis:** When a plant cell is placed in a hypertonic solution, water diffuses out through the selectively permeable tonoplast and plasma membrane (**exosmosis**). The protoplast contracts away from the cell wall. The space between the shrunken protoplast and the cell wall becomes occupied by the external hypertonic solution. Plasmolysis is reversed by placing the cell in a hypotonic medium (**deplasmolysis**).
* **Imbibition:** A specialized physical adsorption of water by hydrophilic, solid colloidal matrices without forming a true solution. Driven by steep water potential gradients (e.g., swelling of dry seeds upon germination, swelling of wooden doors during humid monsoons; exerts massive imbibition pressure up to $100\text{ MPa}$).

### 23.1.2 Water Absorption Pathways in Roots
Water absorbed by root hairs crosses the cortical layers to enter the central vascular cylinder via two distinct anatomical pathways:
1. **Apoplast Pathway:** Passive movement occurring exclusively through non-living interconnected cell walls and intercellular spaces. Water moves by mass flow without crossing any selective semi-permeable membranes; offers low hydraulic resistance.
   * *The Endodermal Barrier:* The apoplastic flux is completely arrested at the **Endodermis** due to radial and transverse wall bandings impregnated with hydrophobic, waxy **Suberin**, termed the **Casparian Strips**.
2. **Symplast Pathway:** Movement through living protoplasts interconnected by cytoplasmic bridges called **Plasmodesmata**. At the endodermis, all water is forced out of the apoplast and into the symplast across the endodermal plasma membrane, establishing strict metabolic control over solute uptake.

```
   [Soil Solution] ──► [Root Hair] ──► [Cortex] ──► [Endodermis (Casparian Strip)] ──► [Pericycle] ──► [Xylem Vessels]
                                                               │
                                         (Forced into SYMPLAST by Suberin Band)
```

### 23.1.3 The Mechanism of Ascent of Sap
The vertical transport of water and dissolved inorganic ions from root tips to the uppermost canopy of tall trees (e.g., *Sequoia sempervirens*, $>110\text{ meters}$) operates via two distinct mechanisms:
1. **Root Pressure Theory (Joseph Priestley):** Active accumulation of mineral ions in root xylem sap creates an osmotic influx of water, generating a positive hydrostatic pressure (**Root Pressure**, $0.1 - 0.2\text{ MPa}$).
   * *Guttation:* Under conditions of high soil moisture and low atmospheric transpiration (e.g., humid nights/early mornings), root pressure forces droplets of liquid xylem sap through specialized marginal vein-end pores called **Hydathodes** (e.g., in grasses, strawberry, tomato). Guttation fluid contains dissolved minerals, distinguishing it from pure condensed atmospheric dew.
   * *Limitation:* Root pressure is insufficient to lift water beyond a few meters and is completely absent in tall conifers.
2. **Cohesion-Tension-Transpiration Pull Theory (Dixon & Joly, 1894):** The universally accepted physical mechanism for long-distance xylem transport.
   * Driven by **Transpiration Pull**: Evaporation of water from mesophyll cell walls into intercellular substomatal cavities generates a profound negative hydrostatic pressure (suction tension up to $-2.0\text{ to }-3.0\text{ MPa}$) transmitted down the continuous water column in xylem.
   * Maintained by intrinsic properties of water molecules:
     * **Cohesion:** High tensile strength ($>100\text{ MPa}$) due to mutual hydrogen bonding between adjacent water molecules, preventing cavitation (column breakage).
     * **Adhesion:** Electrostatic attraction between polar water molecules and the hydrophilic lignocellulosic walls of xylem tracheary elements.
     * **Surface Tension:** High surface tension at the air-water interface of mesophyll micro-capillaries.

### 23.1.4 Stomatal Mechanics: The Active Potassium ($K^+$) Pump Theory (Levitt, 1974)
Stomata are micro-pores flanked by two specialized epidermal **Guard Cells** (dumbbell-shaped in monocot grasses; kidney/bean-shaped in dicots) whose inner walls facing the pore are thick and inelastic, while outer walls are thin and elastic.
* **Daytime Opening Mechanism:**
  1. In the presence of sunlight, guard cells carry out photosynthesis or consume stored starch, synthesizing **Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)**, which combines with $\text{CO}_2$ via PEP Carboxylase to form **Malic Acid**.
  2. Malic acid dissociates into **Malate anions** and **$\text{H}^+$ protons**.
  3. An ATP-driven proton pump on the guard cell membrane actively expels $\text{H}^+$ into adjacent subsidiary cells while importing **Potassium ions ($K^+$)** into the guard cell, balanced by inward **Chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$)** influx.
  4. Hyper-accumulation of $K^+$ and malate inside the central vacuole dramatically lowers guard cell osmotic/water potential ($\Psi_w \ll 0$), inducing **endosmosis** of water from subsidiary cells.
  5. Increased turgor pressure causes the thin outer walls to bulge outward, pulling the thick inner walls apart, **opening the stomatal aperture**.
* **Night Closure Mechanism:** Cessation of light leads to abscisic acid (ABA) release or reversal of the proton pump, driving $K^+$ and malate efflux, causing guard cell flaccidity and stomatal closure.

## 23.2 MINERAL NUTRITION & BIOLOGICAL NITROGEN FIXATION

### 23.2.1 Essential Mineral Elements (Arnon & Stout Criteria, 1939)
An element is declared strictly essential if:
1. The plant cannot complete its vegetative or reproductive life cycle in its total absence.
2. The requirement is specific and non-substitutable by any other element.
3. The element is directly involved in plant nutrition and cellular metabolism (structural or enzymatic).

* **Macronutrients (Concentration $> 10\text{ mmol/kg}$ dry matter):** Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen, Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, Calcium, Magnesium, Sulfur ($\text{C, H, O, N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S}$).
* **Micronutrients / Trace Elements ($< 10\text{ mmol/kg}$ dry matter):** Iron, Manganese, Copper, Molybdenum, Zinc, Boron, Chlorine, Nickel ($\text{Fe, Mn, Cu, Mo, Zn, B, Cl, Ni}$).

| Element | Metabolic Role & Enzymatic Activations | Diagnostic Deficiency Symptoms |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nitrogen ($\text{N}$)** | Constituent of amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll, and vitamins; absorbed as $\text{NO}_3^-$ or $\text{NH}_4^+$. | Generalized **Chlorosis** (yellowing) beginning first in older mature leaves; stunted growth. |
| **Phosphorus ($\text{P}$)** | Constituent of ATP, phospholipids, nucleotides; key in phosphorylation reactions; absorbed as $\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-$. | Dark green or purple/bronze foliar pigmentation (anthocyanin accumulation); poor root development. |
| **Potassium ($\text{K}$)** | Osmoregulation; stomatal opening/closing ($K^+$ pump); maintains anion-cation cellular balance. | Marginal chlorosis and **necrosis of leaf tips and margins** ("marginal scorch"). |
| **Calcium ($\text{Ca}$)** | Forms **Calcium Pectate** in middle lamella of cell walls; stabilizes mitotic spindle fibers and membranes. | Stunted root tips; necrotic growing apical buds (**immobile element**: symptoms first in young tissues). |
| **Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$)** | **Central coordinating atom of the Chlorophyll porphyrin ring**; essential activator of RuBisCO and PEP carboxylase. | Severe **Interveinal Chlorosis** appearing first in older leaves (highly mobile element). |
| **Sulfur ($\text{S}$)** | Present in amino acids **Cysteine and Methionine**, coenzyme A, thiamine, biotin, and ferredoxin. | Chlorosis appearing primarily in young leaves first (relatively immobile). |
| **Iron ($\text{Fe}$)** | Component of ferredoxin and cytochromes; essential activator for **catalase** and chlorophyll biosynthesis. | Severe interveinal chlorosis of younger leaves. |
| **Zinc ($\text{Zn}$)** | Required for synthesis of **Auxin (IAA)**; activates carbonic anhydrase and alcohol dehydrogenase. | **"Little Leaf" disease**; rosette leaves; chlorosis. |
| **Manganese ($\text{Mn}$)** | Essential cofactor for **Photolysis of Water** in Photosystem II ($2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{O}_2 + 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^-$). | **"Marsh Spot" disease** of peas; grey specks in oats. |
| **Molybdenum ($\text{Mo}$)** | Critical structural component of **Nitrogenase** and **Nitrate Reductase** complexes. | **"Whiptail" disease** of cauliflower; defective leaf blades. |
| **Boron ($\text{B}$)** | Required for uptake and utilization of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, **Pollen grain germination**, and carbohydrate phloem translocation. | Death of root and shoot tips; "heart rot" of sugar beet. |

### 23.2.2 Biological Nitrogen Fixation (BNF)
Molecular atmospheric dinitrogen ($\text{N}\equiv\text{N}$) possesses an extraordinarily stable covalent triple bond requiring immense energy ($945\text{ kJ/mol}$) to break. Only prokaryotes possessing the enzyme complex **Nitrogenase** can reduce atmospheric nitrogen to ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$).

```
                    SYMBIOTIC NITROGEN FIXATION IN ROOT NODULES
                    
  [Rhizobium Leguminosarum] ──► Attracted by plant flavonoids / lectins to root hair
             │
             ▼ Root hair curls; Rhizobium enters via INFECTION THREAD
  [Cortical Cell Division]  ──► Forms Root Nodule housing modified bacteroids
             │
             ▼ High [O2] is LETHAL to Nitrogenase enzyme!
  [LEGHEMOGLOBIN Synthesis] ──► Pink/red oxygen-scavenging hemeprotein maintains
                                 anoxic micro-environment for active Nitrogenase
```

* **The Overall Stoichiometry of Biological Nitrogen Fixation:**
  $$\text{N}_2 + 8\text{e}^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 16\text{ATP} \xrightarrow{\text{Nitrogenase}} 2\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2 + 16\text{ADP} + 16\text{P}_i$$
  * *Bioenergetic Cost:* Fixing a single molecule of $\text{N}_2$ into two molecules of $\text{NH}_3$ consumes **$16\text{ ATP}$ molecules** ($8\text{ ATP}$ per ammonia generated), derived from host plant respiration.
  * *Composition of Nitrogenase:* A two-component metalloenzyme consisting of a smaller **Fe-protein (Dinitrogenase reductase)** and a larger **Mo-Fe protein (Dinitrogenase)**.
  * *The Oxygen Dilemma & Leghemoglobin:* Nitrogenase is irreversibly inactivated by molecular oxygen. Legume nodules solve this through the expression of **Leghemoglobin**—a symbiotic pink/red chromoprotein (globin synthesized by plant, heme by bacterium) acting as a high-affinity **Oxygen Scavenger**, delivering oxygen to bacteroid respiratory chains for ATP synthesis while maintaining an ultralow free oxygen tension in the catalytic zone.

## 23.3 PHOTOSYNTHESIS: LIGHT REACTIONS & CHEMIOSMOSIS

Photosynthesis is an anabolic, endergonic oxidation-reduction process in which light energy converts water and atmospheric carbon dioxide into carbohydrates, evolving oxygen:

$$6\text{CO}_2 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Light Energy}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + 6\text{O}_2$$

(Demonstrated by **C.B. van Niel** using purple sulfur bacteria that $\text{O}_2$ evolves strictly from the photolysis of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, not from $\text{CO}_2$; verified isotopically by Ruben, Kamen et al. using $^{18}\text{O}$).

```
                     LIGHT-DEPENDENT REACTIONS (Z-SCHEME)
                     
      Energy
        ▲
        │                    [Primary Acceptor]                [Primary Acceptor]
        │                           │                                  │
        │                           ▼ (Plastoquinone,                  ▼ (Ferredoxin)
        │                             Cyt b6f, PC)                     │
        │                     ┌─────►                                  ▼
        │                     │                                  [NADP+ Reductase]
        │             [PS II (P680)]                           NADP+ + H+ ──► NADPH
        │                     ▲
        │                     │
        │             [Photolysis of H2O]
        │             2H2O ──► O2 + 4H+ + 4e-
        │
        └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────►
```

### 23.3.1 Photosystems & Pigment Architecture
Pigments are organized into discrete **Light Harvesting Complexes (LHC)** or **Photosystems** embedded within the thylakoid membrane:
1. **Photosystem II (PS II):** Reaction center chlorophyll *a* absorbs optimally at **$680\text{ nm}$ ($P_{680}$)**. Located predominantly in the appressed (stacked) thylakoid membranes of grana.
2. **Photosystem I (PS I):** Reaction center chlorophyll *a* absorbs optimally at **$700\text{ nm}$ ($P_{700}$)**. Located in non-appressed stroma lamellae and outer granal margins.

### 23.3.2 Non-Cyclic vs. Cyclic Photophosphorylation

| Parameter | Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation (Z-Scheme) | Cyclic Photophosphorylation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Photosystems Involved** | Both **PS II and PS I** operate sequentially. | Operates strictly via **PS I only**. |
| **Electron Source & Flow** | Continuous supply from **Photolysis of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$**; unidirectional, open electron flow ($\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{PS II} \to \text{Cyt } b_6f \to \text{PS I} \to \text{NADP}^+$). | No photolysis; electrons expelled from $P_{700}$ return cyclically back to $P_{700}$ via cytochrome cascade. |
| **Photolysis of Water** | **Occurs** at Oxygen Evolving Complex (OEC) mediated by $\text{Mn}^{2+}, \text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Cl}^-$. | **Absent**; no water consumed. |
| **Oxygen Evolution** | **Yes ($\text{O}_2$ is evolved)** into atmosphere. | **No oxygen evolved**. |
| **End Products** | Synthesizes **ATP, NADPH, and $\text{O}_2$**. | Synthesizes **ATP only** (No NADPH produced). |
| **Dominant Location** | Grana thylakoid membranes. | Stroma lamellae (which lack PS II and NADP reductase). |

### 23.3.3 The Chemiosmotic Hypothesis (Peter Mitchell, 1961)
ATP synthesis is driven by an electrochemical **Proton Gradient ($\Delta \mu_{\text{H}^+}$)** established across the thylakoid membrane, characterized by high proton concentration (acidic $\text{pH} \approx 4.5$) inside the **Thylakoid Lumen** and low proton concentration (alkaline $\text{pH} \approx 8.0$) in the **Stroma**.
* **Protons Accumulate in the Lumen Due To:**
  1. Photolysis of water on the inner luminal face releases $\text{H}^+$ into the lumen.
  2. Plastoquinone ($PQ$), a mobile hydrogen carrier, picks up protons from the stroma while transporting electrons, pumping them across the membrane into the lumen.
  3. Protons in the stroma are consumed by NADP Reductase to reduce $\text{NADP}^+$ into $\text{NADPH} + \text{H}^+$.
* **ATP Synthesis:** Protons diffuse down their steep electrochemical gradient back into the stroma exclusively through the transmembrane channel of the **$\text{CF}_0$** subunit of ATP Synthase. The conformational rotation of the catalytic **$\text{CF}_1$** headpiece drives the phosphorylation of $\text{ADP} + \text{P}_i \longrightarrow \text{ATP}$.

## 23.4 CARBON FIXATION: $C_3, C_4$ & CAM PATHWAYS

Dark reactions (light-independent reactions) take place in the stroma of chloroplasts, utilizing ATP and NADPH generated in light reactions to reduce $\text{CO}_2$ into hexose sugars.

```
                           CARBON FIXATION DIVERGENCE
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                            ▼                            ▼
     [C3 PATHWAY]                 [C4 PATHWAY]                 [CAM PATHWAY]
   - Calvin Cycle               - Hatch-Slack Pathway        - Crassulacean Acid Metabolism
   - 1st Product: 3-PGA         - 1st Product: OAA (4C)      - Temporal Separation (Night/Day)
   - RuBisCO fixes CO2          - Kranz Anatomy              - Scotoactive Stomata
   - High Photorespiration      - Zero Photorespiration      - Desert Succulents
```

### 23.4.1 The Calvin Cycle ($C_3$ Pathway) (Melvin Calvin, Nobel 1961)
Operates in all photosynthetic plants (wheat, rice, barley, soybeans). Comprises three sequential enzymatic stages:
1. **Carboxylation:** Atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ combines with the 5-carbon primary acceptor molecule **Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP)**, catalyzed by **RuBisCO (RuBP Carboxylase-Oxygenase)**, forming two molecules of the 3-carbon acid **3-Phosphoglyceric Acid (3-PGA)**—the first stable intermediate.
2. **Reduction:** 3-PGA is phosphorylated and reduced into Triose Phosphate (Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, G3P). To produce one molecule of glucose, $6\text{ cycles}$ are required.
3. **Regeneration of RuBP:** Requires ATP phosphorylation to maintain cyclic operation.
* **Energetic Balance Sheet per Glucose Molecule ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$):**
  $$\text{Fixation of } 6\text{ CO}_2 \implies \mathbf{18\text{ ATP}} + \mathbf{12\text{ NADPH}} \text{ consumed}.$$
  *(Per single $\text{CO}_2$ molecule: $3\text{ ATP} + 2\text{ NADPH}$).*

### 23.4.2 Photorespiration ($C_2$ Cycle / Photosynthetic Carbon Oxidation)
RuBisCO is a bifunctional enzyme with active sites binding both $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ competitively. Under high light intensity, elevated temperatures ($>25^\circ\text{C}$), and high $\text{O}_2/\text{CO}_2$ ratios, RuBisCO functions as an **Oxygenase**:

$$\text{RuBP (5C)} + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{RuBisCO}} \text{3-PGA (3C)} + \text{2-Phosphoglycolate (2C)}$$

* **Compartmental Coordination:** Operates across three coordinated organelles: **Chloroplast $\longrightarrow$ Peroxisome $\longrightarrow$ Mitochondria**.
* **Thermodynamic Wastefulness:** Photorespiration consumes ATP and oxygen, releases previously fixed $\text{CO}_2$ in mitochondria without producing ATP, NADPH, or sugars, squandering up to $25–40\%$ of photosynthetic energy in $C_3$ crops.

### 23.4.3 The $C_4$ Pathway (Hatch-Slack Pathway)
Evolved in tropical plants (Maize, Sugarcane, Sorghum, *Amaranthus*) to completely bypass photorespiration through spatial compartmentalization:
* **Kranz Anatomy:** Leaves display two concentric rings of photosynthetic tissue around vascular bundles:
  1. *Mesophyll Cells:* Lack RuBisCO; contain high levels of **PEP Carboxylase (PEPCase)**, which has high affinity for $\text{HCO}_3^-$ and zero affinity for oxygen. Primary $\text{CO}_2$ acceptor is **Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP, 3C)**, forming the first stable product, **Oxaloacetate (OAA, 4C)**. OAA is reduced to Malate and transported to bundle sheath cells via plasmodesmata.
  2. *Bundle Sheath Cells:* Large, thick-walled, suberized cells lacking intercellular spaces, containing agranal chloroplasts rich in **RuBisCO**. Malate undergoes decarboxylation:
     $$\text{Malate (4C)} \longrightarrow \text{Pyruvate (3C)} + \text{CO}_2$$
     The released $\text{CO}_2$ elevates bundle-sheath $[\text{CO}_2]$ to extraordinarily high levels, saturating RuBisCO's carboxylase activity and **completely suppressing photorespiration**. Pyruvate returns to mesophyll cells to regenerate PEP.
* **Energetic Cost:** Fixing $1\text{ CO}_2$ requires $5\text{ ATP} + 2\text{ NADPH}$ ($30\text{ ATP}$ per glucose molecule; higher than $C_3$'s $18\text{ ATP}$, but dramatically more efficient under high tropical temperatures and light).

### 23.4.4 CAM Pathway (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism)
Adapted for extreme arid xerophytic environments (cacti, pineapple, *Agave*, *Vanilla*):
* **Temporal Separation:**
  * *Night (Scotoactive Stomata Open):* To conserve water, stomata open strictly at night. $\text{CO}_2$ is fixed by PEPCase into OAA, reduced to Malic Acid, and stored in large central vacuoles.
  * *Day (Stomata Tightly Closed):* Malic acid is released from vacuoles, decarboxylated to yield $\text{CO}_2$, which enters the Calvin cycle driven by light reactions, while transpiration water loss is reduced by $90\%$.

## 23.5 CELLULAR RESPIRATION & BIOENERGETICS

Cellular respiration is an oxidative, exergonic catabolic process degrading organic hexose fuels to produce ATP.

```
                      CELLULAR RESPIRATION FLOWCHART
                      
           [Glucose (6C)]
                 │
                 ▼  GLYCOLYSIS (Cytoplasm / Anaerobic, Net: 2 ATP + 2 NADH)
           [2 Pyruvate (3C)]
                 │
                 ▼  LINK REACTION (Mitochondrial Matrix, 2 NADH + 2 CO2)
           [2 Acetyl-CoA (2C)]
                 │
                 ▼  KREBS CYCLE (TCA Cycle, Matrix, 2 GTP + 6 NADH + 2 FADH2 + 4 CO2)
           [Reducing Equivalents (NADH, FADH2)]
                 │
                 ▼  ELECTRON TRANSPORT CHAIN (Inner Mitochondrial Membrane)
           [ATP Synthesis via Oxidative Phosphorylation]
```

### 23.5.1 Glycolysis (EMP Pathway — Embden, Meyerhof, Parnas)
Occurs in the **Cytoplasm** of all living cells; anaerobic (requires zero $\text{O}_2$); universal metabolic pathway:
* Converts 1 molecule of Glucose ($6\text{C}$) into 2 molecules of **Pyruvate ($3\text{C}$)** via 10 enzyme-catalyzed steps:
  * *Preparatory Phase (Energy Investment):* Phosphorylation consumes **$2\text{ ATP}$**. Key regulatory pacemaker step is catalyzed by **Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)**:
    $$\text{Fructose-6-Phosphate} + \text{ATP} \xrightarrow{\text{PFK-1}} \text{Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate} + \text{ADP}$$
  * *Pay-Off Phase (Energy Yield):* Generates **$4\text{ ATP}$** (via substrate-level phosphorylation) and **$2\text{ NADH} + 2\text{H}^+$**.
* **Net Glycolytic Yield:** $\mathbf{2\text{ ATP}} + \mathbf{2\text{ NADH}} + \mathbf{2\text{ Pyruvate}}$.

### 23.5.2 Fermentation (Anaerobic Fate of Pyruvate)
In the absence of oxygen, pyruvate cannot enter mitochondria; NADH must be reoxidized to $\text{NAD}^+$ to permit continued glycolysis:
1. **Alcoholic Fermentation (Yeast):** Pyruvate is decarboxylated by *Pyruvate Decarboxylase* to Acetaldehyde, which is reduced by *Alcohol Dehydrogenase* to **Ethanol**:
   $$\text{Pyruvate} \longrightarrow \text{Acetaldehyde} + \text{CO}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{NADH} \to \text{NAD}^+} \text{Ethanol } (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH})$$
   (Yeast cells poison themselves when alcohol concentration reaches $\sim 13\%$).
2. **Lactic Acid Fermentation (Lactic Acid Bacteria & Fatigued Human Skeletal Muscle):**
   $$\text{Pyruvate} + \text{NADH} + \text{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{Lactate Dehydrogenase}} \text{Lactate} + \text{NAD}^+$$
   Accumulation of lactic acid in myocytes induces muscular fatigue and cramping.

### 23.5.3 The Link Reaction (Oxidative Decarboxylation of Pyruvate)
Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix via a pyruvate translocase:

$$\text{2 Pyruvate (3C)} + 2\text{CoA-SH} + 2\text{NAD}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex}} 2\text{Acetyl-CoA (2C)} + 2\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{NADH} + 2\text{H}^+$$

### 23.5.4 The Krebs Cycle (Tricarboxylic Acid / Citric Acid Cycle) (Hans Krebs, 1937)
Occurs in the **Mitochondrial Matrix**:
* Acetyl-CoA ($2\text{C}$) condenses with Oxaloacetate (OAA, $4\text{C}$) via *Citrate Synthase* to form **Citrate ($6\text{C}$)**.
* Sequence: Citrate $\to$ Isocitrate $\to$ $\alpha$-Ketoglutarate ($5\text{C}$) $\to$ Succinyl-CoA ($4\text{C}$) $\to$ Succinate $\to$ Fumarate $\to$ Malate $\to$ Oxaloacetate.
* **Per Acetyl-CoA Turn:** Generates $3\text{ NADH} + 1\text{ FADH}_2 + 1\text{ GTP (ATP via SLP)} + 2\text{ CO}_2$.
* *(Per Glucose Molecule = 2 turns: $\mathbf{6\text{ NADH}} + \mathbf{2\text{ FADH}}_2 + \mathbf{2\text{ GTP}} + \mathbf{4\text{ CO}}_2$).*
* *Marker Enzyme:* **Succinate Dehydrogenase** (converts succinate to fumarate using FAD) is the *only* TCA enzyme physically embedded in the **inner mitochondrial membrane** (as Complex II of the ETC).

### 23.5.5 Electron Transport Chain (ETC) & ATP Balance
Embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane:
* **Complex I:** NADH Dehydrogenase (oxidizes NADH, pumps $4\text{ H}^+$).
* **Complex II:** Succinate Dehydrogenase (oxidizes $\text{FADH}_2$, pumps zero $\text{ H}^+$).
* **Complex III:** Cytochrome $bc_1$ complex (pumps $4\text{ H}^+$).
* **Complex IV:** Cytochrome $c$ Oxidase (contains cytochromes $a, a_3$ and copper centers; reduces terminal $\text{O}_2$ to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$; pumps $2\text{ H}^+$). Cyanide and carbon monoxide block Complex IV.
* **Complex V:** $F_0-F_1$ ATP Synthase.
* **Stoichiometric ATP Equivalents:**
  * Oxidation of $1\text{ NADH} \implies \approx \mathbf{2.5\text{ ATP}}$ (classically $3\text{ ATP}$).
  * Oxidation of $1\text{ FADH}_2 \implies \approx \mathbf{1.5\text{ ATP}}$ (classically $2\text{ ATP}$).
  * Complete aerobic oxidation of $1\text{ molecule of glucose}$ yields **$\mathbf{30\text{ to }32\text{ ATP}}$** (modern) or **$\mathbf{36\text{ to }38\text{ ATP}}$** (classical).

### 23.5.6 Respiratory Quotient (RQ)
The volumetric ratio of $\text{CO}_2$ evolved to $\text{O}_2$ consumed in respiration:

$$\text{RQ} = \frac{\text{Volume of CO}_2\text{ evolved}}{\text{Volume of O}_2\text{ consumed}}$$

1. **Carbohydrates (Glucose):** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \implies \mathbf{\text{RQ} = \frac{6}{6} = 1.0}$.
2. **Fats (Tripalmitin):** $2\text{C}_{51}\text{H}_{98}\text{O}_6 + 145\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 102\text{CO}_2 + 98\text{H}_2\text{O} \implies \mathbf{\text{RQ} = \frac{102}{145} \approx 0.7}$.
3. **Proteins:** $\mathbf{\text{RQ} \approx 0.8 - 0.9}$.
4. **Organic Acids (Malic Acid):** Highly oxidized fuels; $\mathbf{\text{RQ} > 1.0}$ (Malic acid $\text{RQ} = 1.33$).
5. **CAM Succulents at Night:** Incomplete oxidation without $\text{CO}_2$ release $\implies \mathbf{\text{RQ} = 0}$.

## 23.7 PHOTOPERIODISM & VERNALIZATION

### 23.7.1 Photoperiodism (Garner & Allard, 1920)
The physiological response of plants to the relative duration of day and night (photoperiod):
* **Phytochrome Pigment:** A homodimeric chromoprotein photoreceptor existing in two interconvertible forms:
  * **$P_r$ Form:** Absorbs **Red Light ($\lambda = 660\text{ nm}$)**; biologically inactive.
  * **$P_{fr}$ Form:** Absorbs **Far-Red Light ($\lambda = 730\text{ nm}$)**; biologically active form that regulates flowering.
* **Photoperiodic Categories:**
  1. *Short-Day Plants (SDPs / Long-Night Plants):* Flower only when photoperiod is *less* than a critical photoperiod (require an uninterrupted critical dark period, e.g., Soybean, Tobacco, Rice). Flash of light during night suppresses flowering.
  2. *Long-Day Plants (LDPs / Short-Night Plants):* Flower when day length *exceeds* critical photoperiod (e.g., Wheat, Radish, Spinach, Sugar beet).
  3. *Day-Neutral Plants (DNPs):* Flowering is independent of day length (e.g., Tomato, Sunflower, Maize).

### 23.7.2 Vernalization (Lysenko)
The quantitative or qualitative requirement for exposure to low temperatures ($1–6^\circ\text{C}$) to induce flowering in biennials and winter annuals (e.g., winter varieties of wheat and rye). Prevents premature reproductive development during autumn, ensuring flowering occurs during favorable post-winter spring conditions. Mediated by the hypothetical flowering hormone precursor **Vernalin**.

## 23.8 HIGH-YIELD PRACTICE QUESTIONS

### Question 1 (UPSC CSE Prelims Standard)
Consider the following statements regarding photosynthesis in higher plants:
1. In $C_4$ plants, the primary carboxylation occurs in the bundle sheath cells catalyzed by RuBisCO.
2. Photorespiration does not produce ATP or NADPH and leads to a net loss of fixed carbon.
3. Cyclic photophosphorylation involves both Photosystem I and Photosystem II, evolving molecular oxygen.
4. The oxygen released during oxygenic photosynthesis originates from carbon dioxide molecules.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A) 2 only  
B) 1 and 2 only  
C) 2 and 4 only  
D) 1, 3 and 4 only  

**Analytical Answer with Epistemic Breakdown:**
* Statement 1 is **INCORRECT**: In $C_4$ plants, primary carboxylation occurs in **mesophyll cells** catalyzed by PEP Carboxylase (forming OAA). RuBisCO operates downstream in bundle sheath cells.
* Statement 2 is **CORRECT**: Photorespiration (the $C_2$ glycolate pathway) consumes ATP and oxygen, yields no NADPH or energy reserves, and releases previously fixed $\text{CO}_2$, representing an energetic waste.
* Statement 3 is **INCORRECT**: Cyclic photophosphorylation operates exclusively through **Photosystem I (PS I)**, produces ATP only, and involves no photolysis of water or $\text{O}_2$ evolution.
* Statement 4 is **INCORRECT**: Oxygen evolved originates exclusively from the **photolysis of water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, proven experimentally by van Niel and verified using $^{18}\text{O}$.
* **Correct Option:** **A) 2 only**.

### Question 2 (Competitive State PSC Standard)
Which phytohormone is widely utilized by farmers as a selective chemical herbicide to eliminate broadleaf dicotyledonous weeds in cereal monocot crops?
A) Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA)  
B) 2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D)  
C) Abscisic Acid (ABA)  
D) Gibberellic Acid ($\text{GA}_3$)  

**Analytical Answer:**
* **2,4-D** is a synthetic auxin that selectively kills broadleaf dicot weeds while leaving mature monocot grass crops unharmed, making it a universal selective weedicide.
* **Correct Option:** **B) 2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D)**.

### Question 3 (Advanced Concept Standard)
During the complete aerobic respiration of one mole of tripalmitin (fat), what is the calculated Respiratory Quotient (RQ)?
A) $1.0$  
B) $0.7$  
C) $0.9$  
D) $1.33$  

**Analytical Answer:**
* Fats are poor in oxygen relative to carbon and hydrogen atoms, requiring substantially more oxygen molecules for combustion:
  $$2\text{C}_{51}\text{H}_{98}\text{O}_6 + 145\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 102\text{CO}_2 + 98\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  $$\text{RQ} = \frac{\text{CO}_2 \text{ evolved}}{\text{O}_2 \text{ consumed}} = \frac{102}{145} \approx \mathbf{0.7}$$
* **Correct Option:** **B) 0.7**.
