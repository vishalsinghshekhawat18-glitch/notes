### High-Yield Elemental Peculiarities
* **Alkali Metals (Sodium $Na$, Potassium $K$, Lithium $Li$)**: So soft they can be sliced like butter with an ordinary kitchen knife. They have low densities (Lithium, Sodium, and Potassium **float on water**!).
* **Why Sodium and Potassium are Stored Under Kerosene Oil**:  
  They are violently reactive alkali metals. If exposed to air, they react vigorously with atmospheric oxygen and moisture, generating explosive hydrogen gas that catches fire spontaneously ($2\text{Na} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2 + \text{Heat}$). Kerosene provides a non-reactive, water-free barrier.
* **Why White Phosphorus is Stored Under Water**:  
  White Phosphorus ($P_4$) has an exceptionally low ignition temperature ($\sim 30^\circ\text{C}$). Exposed to ambient air, it catches fire spontaneously (spontaneous combustion), producing dense white fumes of phosphorus pentoxide ($P_4O_{10}$). Because phosphorus does not react with water and is denser than water, it is stored submerged safely under water.

## 17.2 Chemical Properties & The Reactivity (Activity) Series

### The Activity Series of Metals
The arrangement of metals in the decreasing order of their chemical reactivity:

$$\mathbf{K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au}$$

```
    ┌────── Potassium (K) & Sodium (Na): React violently with COLD WATER!
    │      Magnesium (Mg): Reacts with HOT BOILING WATER (forming Mg(OH)₂)
    │      Al, Zn, Fe: React ONLY with STEAM (forming Metal Oxide + H₂)
    ▼      Lead (Pb) to Copper (Cu): Do NOT react with water or steam!
   [ H ]   Hydrogen Reference Line
    ▲      Copper (Cu), Silver (Ag), Gold (Au):
    │      Do NOT react with dilute acids (cannot displace H₂)!
    └────── Dissolve only in Aqua Regia or strong oxidizing acids (HNO₃).
```

### Reaction of Metals with Water
1. **Potassium and Sodium**: React violently and exothermically with cold water; the evolved hydrogen instantly catches fire:
   $$2\text{Na (s)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O (l)} \longrightarrow 2\text{NaOH (aq)} + \mathbf{\text{H}_2\text{ (g)}} + \text{Fire!}$$
2. **Calcium**: Reacts less violently; heat is insufficient for hydrogen to ignite. The piece of Calcium **floats on water** because bubbles of hydrogen gas stick to its surface!
3. **Aluminum, Zinc, Iron**: Form **Oxides (NOT Hydroxides!)** with steam:
   $$3\text{Fe (s)} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O (steam)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4\text{ (s, Magnetic Iron Oxide)}} + 4\text{H}_2\text{ (g)}$$

### Amphoteric Oxides: The Acid-Base Dualists
Most metallic oxides are basic in nature ($\text{MgO, CaO, } \text{Na}_2\text{O}$). However, certain metal oxides react with **BOTH acids AND bases** to produce salt and water:
* **Aluminum Oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$)**:
  - As a Base: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 6\text{HCl} \longrightarrow 2\text{AlCl}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$
  - As an Acid: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \mathbf{2\text{NaAlO}_2\text{ (Sodium Aluminate)}} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
* **Zinc Oxide ($\text{ZnO}$)**: Reacts with $NaOH$ to form **Sodium Zincate ($\text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2$)**.

---

### Aqua Regia: Dissolving the Noble Metals
Gold ($Au$) and Platinum ($Pt$) are chemically unreactive noble metals that resist attack by concentrated $HCl$ or concentrated $HNO_3$ individually. They dissolve only in **Aqua Regia (*Royal Water*)**:
* **Composition**: Freshly prepared mixture of **Concentrated Hydrochloric Acid ($HCl$) and Concentrated Nitric Acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) in a $3:1$ volume ratio**:
  $$\mathbf{3\text{ parts Conc. HCl} : 1\text{ part Conc. HNO}_3}$$
* *Mechanism*: Nitric acid oxidizes $HCl$ into free, reactive **nascent Chlorine gas ($[Cl]$)** and nitrosyl chloride ($NOCl$), which attack gold to form soluble chlorauric acid ($\text{HAuCl}_4$).

## 17.3 Metallurgy: The Isolation of Metals from Ores

* **Mineral**: A naturally occurring inorganic compound of a metal found inside the Earth's crust.
* **Ore**: A mineral from which the metal can be extracted **profitably and conveniently** (*"All ores are minerals, but all minerals are NOT ores"*).
* **Gangue / Matrix**: The useless earthy, sandy, or rocky impurities (silica, clay) associated with raw mined ore.
* **Flux & Slag**: A substance added to ore to convert non-fusible gangue into easily fusible, floating molten waste called **Slag**:
  $$\text{Gangue (Acidic } SiO_2) + \text{Flux (Basic } CaO) \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Slag (Fusible } CaSiO_3)}$$

### The Four Stages of Metallurgical Extraction

```
           [ 1. CRUSHING & GRINDING ] ──► Ball mills & pulverizers
                       │
                       ▼
           [ 2. CONCENTRATION OF ORE ] (Removing earthy gangue)
                 ├── Gravity Separation / Hydraulic Washing (Dense oxide ores)
                 ├── Magnetic Separation (Separating magnetic Fe from non-magnetic)
                 └── Froth Floatation (Exclusively for SULFIDE ORES using Pine Oil!)
                       │
                       ▼
           [ 3. EXTRACTION OF CRUDE METAL ]
                 ├── Calcination (Heating in ABSENCE of air: Carbonate/Hydrated ores)
                 ├── Roasting (Heating in EXCESS of air: Sulfide ores)
                 └── Smelting / Reduction (Coke carbon reduction or Electrolytic)
                       │
                       ▼
           [ 4. REFINING OF CRUDE METAL ]
                 ├── Electrolytic Refining (Copper, Aluminum via Hall-Héroult)
                 ├── Mond Process (Purifying Nickel via volatile Ni(CO)₄)
                 └── Zone Refining (Ultra-pure semiconductors: Silicon, Germanium)
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Calcination vs. Roasting**:
> * **Calcination**: Heating ore strongly **in the ABSENCE of air** (or limited air) below its melting point. Used for **Carbonate ores** (drives off $\text{CO}_2$) and hydrated oxides (drives off water):
>   $$\text{ZnCO}_3\text{ (Calamine)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{ZnO} + \text{CO}_2$$
> * **Roasting**: Heating ore strongly **in the EXCESS of air** below its melting point. Used exclusively for **Sulfide ores** (converts sulfides to oxides, releasing sulfur dioxide gas):
>   $$2\text{ZnS (Zinc Blende)} + 3\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{ZnO} + 2\text{SO}_2$$

## 17.4 Alloys & Amalgams: Engineered Metallurgy

An **Alloy** is a homogeneous metallic mixture of two or more metals (or a metal and a non-metal) fused together in molten state to achieve enhanced strength, hardness, and corrosion resistance:
* **Amalgam**: Any alloy of **Mercury ($Hg$)** with another metal (e.g., Sodium amalgam, Dental amalgam of silver-tin-mercury for tooth fillings).
  - *Exam Exception*: **Iron ($Fe$) does NOT form an amalgam!** For this reason, liquid mercury is transported and stored commercially in **iron containers**.

## 17.5 Master Chapter Distinction Matrix

| Parameter | Calcination | Roasting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Atmospheric Condition** | Done in **ABSENCE or strict limitation of air**. | Done in **continuous EXCESS of air**. |
| **Target Ore Type** | **Carbonates** ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) and **Hydrated Oxides**. | **Sulfide Ores** ($S^{2-}$). |

## 17.6 High-Yield Diagnostic Examination Traps

1. **The German Silver Composition Trap**:
   - *Trap*: "What percentage of silver is present in German Silver? (a) 20% (b) 50% (c) 0%".
   - *Correction*: **Exactly 0% Silver!** German Silver is composed of **Copper ($60\%$), Zinc ($20\%$), and Nickel ($20\%$)**. It is named purely for its silvery appearance.
2. **Mercury Container Storage**:
   - *Trap*: "Liquid mercury is stored in copper bottles."
   - *Correction*: Mercury dissolves copper, zinc, gold, and silver, forming amalgams. Mercury **does not form an amalgam with Iron**, which is why it is stored and transported exclusively in **Iron flasks**.
3. **Rust-Proof Stainless Steel Component**:
   - *Trap*: "Nickel makes stainless steel rust-proof."
   - *Correction*: **Chromium ($Cr, \ge 11\%$)** is the vital element that reacts with oxygen to form an invisible, self-healing **Chromium(III) oxide ($\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$)** passivation film that blocks rust. Nickel adds ductility and shine.
4. **Aluminium Reaction with Nitric Acid**:
   - *Trap*: "Aluminium dissolves violently in concentrated nitric acid."
   - *Correction*: **Aluminium becomes passive!** Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ is a powerful oxidizing agent that instantly oxidizes the surface of aluminium, forming an impenetrable, non-reactive film of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ that halts all further reaction.
