### Epistemic Stance & Learning Objectives
Biological taxonomy and systematics establish an objective, evolutionary, and phylogenetic framework for the cataloging of terrestrial biodiversity. Mastery of this domain requires an exact comprehension of the hierarchical taxonomic ranks, the criteria governing Whittaker's Five-Kingdom classification, the molecular basis of Woese's Three-Domain paradigm (based on 16S rRNA sequences), the ultrastructural biology and pathogenesis of Monerans, Protists, Fungi, and sub-cellular pathogens (viruses, viroids, prions), as well as the diagnostic morphological criteria separating major plant and animal phyla. Competitive examinations systematically test taxonomic nomenclature conventions, bacterial cell-wall biophysics (Gram-positive vs. Gram-negative), lichen symbioses, and viral replication mechanics.

## 22.1 TAXONOMY, SYSTEMATICS & NOMENCLATURE

### 22.1.1 Fundamentals of Taxonomy & Systematics
* **Taxonomy (coined by A.P. de Candolle):** The branch of biology concerned with the **Characterization, Identification, Nomenclature, and Classification** of organisms.
* **Systematics (coined by Carl Linnaeus, from Latin *systema*):** The study of the diversity of organisms and all their comparative and evolutionary (**phylogenetic**) relationships.
* **Classical vs. Modern Taxonomy:**
  * *Classical Taxonomy:* Based strictly on observable external morphological features (typological concept).
  * *Modern / Biosystematics:* Synthesizes morphology with cellular ultrastructure, cytogenetics, biochemical pathways, ecological niche dynamics, and molecular phylogenetics.

### 22.1.2 Binomial Nomenclature (Carl Linnaeus, 1753)
Standardized international system formalized in *Species Plantarum* (1753) and *Systema Naturae* (10th Edition, 1758):
1. **Two-Word Epithet:** Every scientific name comprises two Latinized words:
   * First word: **Genus (Generic name)**, starts with an uppercase letter (e.g., *Homo*, *Mangifera*).
   * Second word: **Specific epithet**, starts with a lowercase letter (e.g., *sapiens*, *indica*).
2. **Typography:** Printed in *italics*; when handwritten, both words are **underlined separately** (e.g., _Homo_ _sapiens_).
3. **Citation of Author:** The author who first published the valid description is appended in roman font, abbreviated at the end: *Mangifera indica* Linn.
4. **Law of Priority:** The first validly published scientific name from the benchmark dates (1 May 1753 for plants, 1 Jan 1758 for animals) takes absolute precedence.
5. **Tautonyms:** Binomial names where genus and species names are identical (e.g., *Naja naja* [Indian cobra], *Rattus rattus* [black rat]).
   * *Rule:* Tautonyms are valid in **zoology** (ICZN), but strictly prohibited in **botany** (ICN).

### 22.1.3 The Taxonomic Hierarchy (Obligate Categories)
Organized in ascending order of inclusivity (descending order of shared morphological specificity):

$$\text{Species} \longrightarrow \text{Genus} \longrightarrow \text{Family} \longrightarrow \text{Order} \longrightarrow \text{Class} \longrightarrow \text{Phylum (Animals) / Division (Plants)} \longrightarrow \text{Kingdom} \longrightarrow \text{Domain}$$

* **Species:** The fundamental real evolutionary unit of biological classification; a group of interbreeding natural populations sharing a common gene pool and reproductively isolated from other groups (**Biological Species Concept** by Ernst Mayr).
* **Taxonomic Suffix Standards in Botany (ICN):**
  * Division: `-phyta` (e.g., Magnoliophyta)
  * Class: `-opsida` or `-phyceae` (e.g., Liliopsida, Chlorophyceae)
  * Order: `-ales` (e.g., Rosales, Solanales)
  * Family: `-aceae` (e.g., Solanaceae, Poaceae, Fabaceae)

## 22.2 EVOLUTION OF CLASSIFICATION SYSTEMS

```
                         CHRONOLOGY OF TAXONOMIC SYSTEMS
                         
  [2 Kingdoms (Linnaeus, 1758)]   ──► Plantae & Animalia (Lacked prokaryote/eukaryote distinction)
               │
  [3 Kingdoms (Haeckel, 1866)]    ──► Added Kingdom PROTISTA (Unicellular organisms)
               │
  [4 Kingdoms (Copeland, 1956)]   ──► Added Kingdom MONERA (Prokaryotic organisms)
               │
  [5 Kingdoms (Whittaker, 1969)]  ──► Added Kingdom FUNGI (Absorptive heterotrophy)
               │
  [3 Domains / 6 Kingdoms (Woese, 1990)] ──► Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya (16S rRNA Phylogeny)
```

### 22.2.1 R.H. Whittaker's Five-Kingdom System (1969)
Established upon five explicit, non-arbitrary biological criteria:
1. **Complexity of Cell Structure:** Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic.
2. **Complexity of Body Organization:** Unicellular vs. Multicellular / Tissue-Organ level.
3. **Mode of Nutrition:** Phototrophic autotrophy vs. Absorptive heterotrophy (saprophytism) vs. Ingestive heterotrophy (holozoic).
4. **Life Style / Ecological Role:** Producers (Plantae) vs. Decomposers (Fungi) vs. Consumers (Animalia).
5. **Phylogenetic / Evolutionary Relationships:** Tracing ancestral lineages.

| Feature | Monera | Protista | Fungi | Plantae | Animalia |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Type** | **Prokaryotic** | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic |
| **Cell Wall** | Non-cellulosic (**Peptidoglycan** / Polysaccharide + amino acid) | Present in some (e.g., dinoflagellates); absent in protozoans | Present (**Chitin** and $\beta$-glucans) | Present (**Cellulose**, hemicellulose, pectin) | **Absent** |
| **Nuclear Envelope**| **Absent** | Present | Present | Present | Present |
| **Body Organization**| Cellular | Cellular | Multicellular / Loose tissue | Tissue / Organ | Tissue / Organ / Organ system |
| **Mode of Nutrition**| Autotrophic (chemosynthetic/photosynthetic) & Heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic) | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) & Heterotrophic (holozoic/parasitic) | Heterotrophic (**Absorptive / Saprophytic** or parasitic) | Autotrophic (**Photosynthetic**) | Heterotrophic (**Holozoic / Ingestive**) |

### 22.2.2 Carl Woese’s Three-Domain System (1990)
Using molecular sequencing of **16S ribosomal RNA (rRNA)** (a universal, slowly evolving molecular chronometer), Woese divided all terrestrial cellular life into **Three Evolutionary Domains** above the kingdom rank:
1. **Domain Archaea (Kingdom Archaebacteria):** Primitive prokaryotes possessing ether-linked branched isoprenoid membrane lipids and unique pseudomurein walls; thrive in extreme habitats.
2. **Domain Bacteria (Kingdom Eubacteria):** True eubacteria with ester-linked unbranched fatty acid lipids and peptidoglycan walls.
3. **Domain Eukarya:** All eukaryotic organisms, encompassing Kingdoms Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia.

## 22.3 KINGDOM MONERA: BACTERIA & ARCHAEA

Monerans are exclusively prokaryotic, single-celled, ubiquitous organisms.

```
                                 KINGDOM MONERA
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                            ▼                            ▼
   [ARCHAEBACTERIA]               [EUBACTERIA]                 [MYCOPLASMA]
   - Ether-linked lipids          - Peptidoglycan cell wall    - Lacks cell wall entirely
   - Methanogens, Halophiles,     - Gram-pos & Gram-neg        - Smallest free-living cells
     Thermoacidophiles            - Cyanobacteria (BGA)        - Penicillin-resistant
```

### 22.3.1 Archaebacteria (The Extremophiles)
Characterized by unique plasma membranes where branched-chain hydrocarbons (phytanyl chains) are bonded to glycerol via **Ether Linkages** (rather than the ester linkages found in eubacteria and eukaryotes). This confers extreme thermal and mechanical stability against hydrolytic cleavage in hostile environments.
1. **Methanogens:** Strict obligate anaerobes that reduce $\text{CO}_2$ or formate to **Methane ($\text{CH}_4$)** using $\text{H}_2$:
   $$\text{CO}_2 + 4\text{H}_2 \longrightarrow \text{CH}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
   Abundant in marshy swamps and in the **rumen of ruminant herbivores** (cattle, buffaloes), responsible for commercial biogas generation from dung. (E.g., *Methanobacterium*, *Methanococcus*).
2. **Halophiles:** Inhabit saturated hypersaline lakes (e.g., Great Salt Lake, Dead Sea). Under aerobic conditions, they utilize sunlight via membrane-bound purple pigment **Bacteriorhodopsin** to drive ATP synthesis via proton-pumping without fixating carbon dioxide. (E.g., *Halobacterium*).
3. **Thermoacidophiles:** Flourish in boiling sulfuric hot springs and deep-sea hydrothermal vents ($\text{Temp } \approx 80–100^\circ\text{C}, \text{pH } \approx 1–2$). Oxidize elemental sulfur to sulfuric acid aerobically:
   $$2\text{S} + 3\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$$
   (E.g., *Thermoplasma*, *Sulfolobus*). Source of heat-stable **Taq DNA Polymerase** (*Thermus aquaticus*, a thermophilic eubacterium) used in PCR.

### 22.3.2 Eubacteria: Structure & Gram Staining Biophysics
* **Cell Wall Architecture:** Composed of **Peptidoglycan (Murein)**—an alternating polymer of $N$-acetylglucosamine (NAG) and $N$-acetylmuramic acid (NAM) crosslinked by short tetrapeptide chains.
* **Gram Staining Protocol (Hans Christian Gram, 1884):**

```
[Bacterial Smear on Slide]
          │  1. Crystal Violet (Primary Stain, 1 min) -> All cells stain Purple
          ▼
[Iodine Treatment (Mordant, 1 min)] -> Forms insoluble Crystal Violet-Iodine (CV-I) Complex
          │  2. Decolorization with 95% Ethanol / Acetone (10 - 20 sec)
          ▼
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┐
  ▼                                                                                 ▼
[Gram-Positive Bacteria]                                          [Gram-Negative Bacteria]
- Thick peptidoglycan wall (20 - 80 nm)                            - Thin peptidoglycan (2 - 7 nm)
- Dehydrated by alcohol; pores shrink                              - Outer membrane rich in LPS dissolved
- CV-I complex trapped: RETAIN PURPLE / VIOLET                      - CV-I washes out; cells decolorized
                                                                    - Counterstained with SAFRANIN:
                                                                      STAIN PINK / RED
```

| Parameter | Gram-Positive Bacteria | Gram-Negative Bacteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peptidoglycan Layer** | Very thick ($20–80\text{ nm}$), multilayered ($>50\%$ dry weight). | Thin ($2–7\text{ nm}$), single-layered ($5–10\%$ dry weight). |
| **Outer Membrane** | Absent. | **Present**; contains **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)** acting as **Endotoxin** (Lipid A induces septic shock/fever). |
| **Teichoic Acids** | Present (provide antigenic specificity and negative charge). | Completely absent. |
| **Periplasmic Space** | Narrow or absent. | Distinct, prominent fluid space between inner and outer membranes. |
| **Susceptibility to Penicillin** | **High** (penicillin inhibits transpeptidase enzyme cross-linking peptidoglycan). | **Low** (outer membrane acts as a protective permeability barrier). |
| **Representative Examples** | *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Bacillus anthracis*, *Clostridium tetani*. | *Escherichia coli*, *Salmonella typhi*, *Vibrio cholerae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. |

### 22.3.3 Bacterial Reproduction & Genetic Recombination
True sexual reproduction involving meiosis and gametic fusion is absent. Bacteria exchange genetic material via three parasexual mechanisms:
1. **Transformation (Fred Griffith, 1928):** Uptake of naked exogenous foreign DNA from the surrounding medium by a competent recipient bacterium (proven by Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty in 1944).
2. **Conjugation (Lederberg & Tatum, 1946):** Direct unidirectional transfer of genetic material (usually a conjugative **F-plasmid**, fertility factor) from a donor cell ($F^+$) to a recipient cell ($F^-$) across a cytoplasmic bridge formed by **Sex Pili**.
3. **Transduction (Zinder & Lederberg, 1952):** Transfer of bacterial DNA fragments from one bacterium to another mediated by a **Bacteriophage** vector (e.g., Lambda phage in *Salmonella*).
* **Endospore Formation:** Under unfavorable environmental stress (desiccation, high heat, radiation), bacteria (e.g., *Bacillus*, *Clostridium*) form a highly resistant, dehydrated dormant resting structure called an **Endospore**. Impregnated with **Calcium Dipicolinate** in the spore cortex, granting extreme heat and chemical resistance (survives boiling water; requires pressurized autoclaving at $121^\circ\text{C}$ for $15–20\text{ mins}$ at $15\text{ psi}$ to kill).

### 22.3.4 Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae) & Mycoplasmas
* **Cyanobacteria (BGA):** Gram-negative photosynthetic eubacteria possessing **Chlorophyll a** (identical to higher plants) and auxiliary phycobiliproteins (**Phycocyanin** [blue] and **Phycoerythrin** [red]).
  * *Oxygenic Photosynthesis:* The first organisms to evolve oxygenic photosynthesis on primitive Earth ($\sim 2.5\text{ billion years ago}$), transforming the reducing atmosphere into an oxidizing one.
  * *Nitrogen Fixation:* Specific filamentous species (*Nostoc*, *Anabaena*) possess specialized, pale, thick-walled, non-photosynthetic cells called **Heterocysts**. Heterocysts lack Photosystem II (preventing $\text{O}_2$ evolution) and house the oxygen-sensitive enzyme **Nitrogenase**, fixing atmospheric $\text{N}_2$ into ammonium ions ($\text{NH}_4^+$). Form symbiotic associations with *Azolla* (water fern, used as green manure in paddy fields) and *Cycas* coralloid roots.
* **Mycoplasma (PPLO — Pleuropneumonia-Like Organisms):**
  * **Completely lack a cell wall**; enclosed only by a triple-layered sterol-containing plasma membrane.
  * Exhibit extreme pleomorphism (variable shape: spherical, filamentous).
  * **Smallest known free-living cells** (diameter $\approx 0.1 - 0.3\text{ }\mu\text{m}$).
  * Can survive **without oxygen** (facultative anaerobes).
  * **Insensitive to Penicillin / Cephalosporins** (which target peptidoglycan cell walls), but highly sensitive to antibiotics targeting 70S ribosomes (e.g., Tetracycline, Erythromycin).

## 22.4 KINGDOM PROTISTA: SINGLE-CELLED EUKARYOTES

Encompasses all unicellular eukaryotic microorganisms. Form the evolutionary bridge connecting prokaryotic monerans with multicellular fungi, plants, and animals.

```
                                KINGDOM PROTISTA
                                       │
     ┌──────────────────┬──────────────┴─────┬──────────────────┬─────────────────┐
     ▼                  ▼                    ▼                  ▼                 ▼
[Chrysophytes]  [Dinoflagellates]       [Euglenoids]      [Slime Moulds]    [Protozoans]
- Diatoms       - Red Tides (Gonyaulax) - Euglena         - Saprophytic     - Amoeboid,
- Silica shells - 2 Flagella            - Mixotrophic     - Plasmodium        Ciliated,
- Diatomite     - Bioluminescence       - Pellicle                            Flagellated,
                                                                              Sporozoan
```

### 22.4.1 Chrysophytes (Diatoms & Golden Algae)
* Microscopic, photosynthetic floating organisms (phytoplankton); primary producers of the oceans.
* **Frustule Wall Architecture:** Cell wall forms two thin overlapping shells fitting together like a soapbox (upper **epitheca** fitting over lower **hypotheca**). The walls are heavily impregnated with indestructible crystalline **Silica ($\text{SiO}_2$)**.
* **Diatomaceous Earth (Kieselguhr):** Accumulation of billions of years of dead diatom siliceous shells on ocean beds. Because the soil is gritty and porous, it is industrially mined for polishing, filtration of oils and syrups, and insulation.

### 22.4.2 Dinoflagellates
* Mostly marine, biflagellated photosynthetic protists with stiff cellulose plates on their outer wall. Possess two flagella: one longitudinal and one transverse in a furrow between wall plates, producing a characteristic spinning locomotion ("whirling whips").
* **Red Tides:** Explosive population blooms of toxic red dinoflagellates (e.g., **Gonyaulax**) discolour ocean waters red and release lethal neurotoxins (**Saxitoxin**), causing massive fish kills and paralytic shellfish poisoning (PSP) in humans consuming affected oysters/clams.
* **Bioluminescence:** Certain species (e.g., *Noctiluca scintillans*, "sea sparkle") possess luciferase enzymes that emit visible green-blue light when mechanically disturbed.

### 22.4.3 Euglenoids
* Freshwater organisms found in stagnant water. Lack a rigid cellulosic cell wall; instead, bounded by an elastic, flexible, protein-rich outer layer called the **Pellicle**.
* Possess two flagella (one short, one long) and a light-sensitive **Eye spot (Stigma)**.
* **Mixotrophic Nutrition:** In the presence of sunlight, they perform oxygenic photosynthesis (contain chlorophyll a and b identical to higher plants); in the absence of light, they shift to predatory heterotrophic feeding on smaller organisms. (E.g., *Euglena viridis*).

### 22.4.4 Slime Moulds (Myxomycetes)
* Saprophytic protists moving along decaying twigs and leaves engulfing organic debris.
* Under favorable conditions, they form a multinucleate, amoeboid aggregate lacking cell walls called a **Plasmodium** (can grow several feet wide).
* Under unfavorable conditions, the plasmodium differentiates to form fruiting bodies bearing **spores at their tips**. These spores possess extremely durable **true cellulosic walls** that survive desiccation for years and are dispersed by air currents.

### 22.4.5 Protozoans (Primitive Heterotrophs)
Unicellular heterotrophs categorized by their locomotory apparatus:
1. **Amoeboid Protozoans:** Move and capture prey using temporary cytoplasmic extensions called **Pseudopodia** ("false feet"). E.g., *Amoeba proteus*; **Entamoeba histolytica** (parasite causing amoebic dysentery; characterized by mononucleated cysts with chromatoid bars). Marine forms possess silica shells on their surface.
2. **Flagellated Protozoans:** Possess whip-like flagella. E.g., **Trypanosoma brucei** (transmitted by the bite of the **Tsetse Fly** [*Glossina palpalis*], causing fatal **African Sleeping Sickness**); *Leishmania donovani* (transmitted by the **Sandfly** [*Phlebotomus*], causing visceral leishmaniasis / **Kala-azar**).
3. **Ciliated Protozoans:** Actively moving organisms possessing thousands of coordinated beating cilia and a permanent oral opening called a **Gullet**. Exhibit **Nuclear Dualism**:
   * *Macronucleus:* Polyploid vegetative nucleus controlling day-to-day cellular metabolism.
   * *Micronucleus:* Diploid generative nucleus controlling sexual conjugation. E.g., **Paramecium caudatum** ("slipper animalcule").
4. **Sporozoans:** Non-motile, exclusively endoparasitic protozoans having an infectious spore-like stage in their life cycle. E.g., **Plasmodium vivax / falciparum** (etiological agent of human malaria, vectored by female *Anopheles* mosquito; *P. falciparum* causes lethal malignant tertian malaria).

## 22.5 KINGDOM FUNGI: THE ABSORPTIVE HETEROTROPHS

Achlorophyllous, heterotrophic eukaryotic organisms with absorptive nutrition. Their vegetative body consists of slender, branching filamentous threads called **Hyphae**, an aggregate of which constitutes the **Mycelium**.
* **Cell Wall Composition:** Composed of **Chitin** (polymer of $N$-acetylglucosamine) and **$\beta$-Glucans**.
* **Reserve Food:** Stored as **Glycogen** and lipid droplets (never starch).
* **Symbiotic Associations:**
  1. **Lichens:** Mutualistic, obligate consortium between an alga/cyanobacterium (**Phycobiont** / Photobiont) which photosynthesizes organic nutrients, and a fungus (**Mycobiont**, predominantly Ascomycetes) which provides structural shelter, anchorage, and absorption of water/minerals.
     * **Pioneer Species:** Secrete organic acids (lichen acids) that weather bare rock in primary ecological succession.
     * **Pollution Bioindicators:** Sensitive to **Sulfur Dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$)**; completely disappear in industrial polluted zones (cannot grow in cities).
  2. **Mycorrhiza:** Mutualistic association between fungal mycelia and the roots of higher gymnosperm/angiosperm plants (e.g., *Pinus* seeds cannot germinate without mycorrhizae; genus *Glomus* absorbs soil **Phosphorus** for the host plant).

```
                             THE FOUR CLASSES OF FUNGI
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                            ▼                            ▼
    [PHYCOMYCETES]               [ASCOMYCETES]               [BASIDIOMYCETES]
    - Coenocytic, Aseptate       - Septate, Branched         - Septate, Branched
    - Algal fungi                - "Sac Fungi"               - "Club Fungi"
    - Rhizopus, Albugo           - Ascospores in Ascus       - Basidiospores on Basidium
                                 - Penicillium, Yeast        - Agaricus, Rusts, Smuts
                                        │
                                        ▼
                                [DEUTEROMYCETES]
                                - "Fungi Imperfecti"
                                - Sexual stage ABSENT or unknown
                                - Alternaria, Trichoderma
```

| Class | Mycelium Morphology | Asexual Spores | Sexual Spores & Fruiting Body | Diagnostic High-Yield Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phycomycetes** (Oomycetes + Zygomycetes) | **Aseptate & Coenocytic** (multinucleated continuous tubes without cross-walls). | Motile **Zoospores** (aquatic) or non-motile **Aplanospores** formed endogenously in sporangia. | **Zygospores** formed by fusion of two gametes; thick-walled resting spore. | *Rhizopus stolonifer* (black bread mold); *Mucor*; *Albugo candida* (white rust of crucifers). |
| **Ascomycetes** ("Sac Fungi") | **Septate & Branched** | Non-motile **Conidia** produced exogenously on tips of specialized hyphae (**Conidiophores**). | **Ascospores** produced **endogenously** (typically 8) inside a sac-like **Ascus**, aggregated into **Ascocarps**. | *Penicillium notatum* (penicillin antibiotic); *Aspergillus niger* (citric acid); *Neurospora crassa* (genetic research, "Drosophila of plant kingdom"); *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (unicellular budding yeast; baker's/brewer's yeast); Edible morels (*Morchella*) and truffles. |
| **Basidiomycetes** ("Club Fungi") | **Septate & Branched**; characteristic **Clamp Connections**. | Asexual spores generally absent; vegetative fragmentation common. | **Basidiospores** produced **exogenously** (typically 4) on club-shaped **Basidium**, aggregated into **Basidiocarps**. | *Agaricus bisporus* (edible button mushroom); *Puccinia graminis* (black stem rust of wheat); *Ustilago maydis* (corn smut); Bracket fungi and puffballs. |
| **Deuteromycetes** ("Fungi Imperfecti") | **Septate & Branched** | **Conidia** only. | **Sexual reproduction completely unknown / absent**. (If discovered, reclassified into Ascomycetes or Basidiomycetes). | *Alternaria solani* (early blight of potato); *Colletotrichum falcatum* (red rot of sugarcane); *Trichoderma*; *Fusarium*. |

## 22.6 SUB-CELLULAR ACYCLIC AGENTS: VIRUSES, VIROIDS & PRIONS

Non-cellular infectious biological entities standing at the threshold between chemistry and life; excluded from Whittaker's Five-Kingdom classification.

```
                  STRUCTURAL SPECTRUM OF ACYCLIC PATHOGENS
                  
       [VIRUS]   ──► Nucleic Acid (DNA OR RNA) + Protein Coat (Capsid)
       [VIROID]  ──► Naked, circular single-stranded RNA ONLY (No protein coat)
       [PRION]   ──► Abnormally folded, infectious PROTEIN ONLY (No nucleic acid)
```

### 22.6.1 Viruses
* **Discovery Milestones:**
  * **Dmitri Ivanowsky (1892):** Recognized that mosaic disease of tobacco was caused by an infectious pathogen smaller than bacteria, passing through porcelain bacterial-retentive filters.
  * **M.W. Beijerinck (1898):** Demonstrated that filtered sap from diseased tobacco plants could infect healthy plants; coined the term **"Contagium vivum fluidum"** (contagious living fluid).
  * **W.M. Stanley (1935):** First crystallized **Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV)**, demonstrating that viral crystals consist largely of proteins.
* **Ultrastructure & Genomic Architecture:**
  * **Acellular & Inert Outside Host:** Possess no metabolic machinery, no ATP synthesis, no ribosomes; act as obligate intracellular molecular parasites.
  * **Nucleocapsid:** Consists of a central nucleic acid core surrounded by a protective protein shell called a **Capsid**, built of individual repeating protein subunits called **Capsomeres**.
  * **Genome Rule:** A virus contains **EITHER DNA OR RNA, NEVER BOTH!**
    * *Plant Viruses:* Mostly single-stranded RNA (ssRNA), e.g., TMV.
    * *Animal Viruses:* Can be single- or double-stranded RNA, or double-stranded DNA (dsDNA). E.g., Influenza (segmented ssRNA), HIV (ssRNA retrovirus with reverse transcriptase), Hepatitis B (partially dsDNA), Herpes (dsDNA), SARS-CoV-2 (enveloped positive-sense ssRNA).
    * *Bacterial Viruses (Bacteriophages):* Usually **double-stranded DNA (dsDNA)**; possess an icosahedral head, collar, and contractile sheath with tail pins.

### 22.6.2 Viroids
Discovered by **Theodor O. Diener (1971)**:
* Infectious pathogens **smaller than viruses**.
* Composed exclusively of **naked, circular single-stranded RNA (ssRNA)**; **completely lack a protein capsid**.
* The RNA has an exceptionally **low molecular weight** ($246 - 467\text{ nucleotides}$) and does not code for any proteins; replicates via host RNA Polymerase II through a rolling-circle mechanism.
* Cause severe plant diseases: **Potato Spindle Tuber Disease (PSTD)**, Chrysanthemum stunt disease, Citrus exocortis.

### 22.6.3 Prions (Proteinaceous Infectious Particles)
Discovered by **Stanley B. Prusiner (1982)** (Nobel Prize 1997):
* Exceptionally small, infectious agents composed **entirely of abnormally folded proteins**; **contain zero nucleic acid (neither DNA nor RNA)**!
* The normal cellular prion protein ($\text{PrP}^\text{C}$, rich in $\alpha$-helices) undergoes a pathological post-translational conformational change into a misfolded, $\beta$-sheet-rich isoform ($\text{PrP}^\text{Sc}$). $\text{PrP}^\text{Sc}$ acts as a conformational template, binding normal $\text{PrP}^\text{C}$ and converting it into $\text{PrP}^\text{Sc}$, which aggregates into insoluble, protease-resistant amyloid fibrils in neural tissue.
* Incredibly resistant to heat, formaldehydes, UV radiation, and proteases (survives standard autoclaving).
* **Clinical Neuropathologies (Transmissible Spongiform Encephalopathies - TSEs):**
  * **Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy (BSE):** Commonly known as **"Mad Cow Disease"** in cattle.
  * **Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD):** Fatal human neurodegenerative dementia.
  * **Kuru ("Laughing Death"):** Transmitted through ritualistic mortuary cannibalism among the Fore people of Papua New Guinea.
  * **Scrapie:** Chronic wasting disease in sheep and goats.

## 22.7 OVERVIEW OF PLANTAE & ANIMALIA ARCHITECTURE

### 22.7.1 Kingdom Plantae: Evolutionary Trajectory
1. **Algae (Thallophytes):** Simplest aquatic plants; undifferentiated vegetative thallus, devoid of vascular tissues (xylem/phloem); non-jacketed unicellular sex organs (e.g., *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*, *Chara*).
2. **Bryophytes ("Amphibians of the Plant Kingdom"):** First terrestrial plants, but require external water film for flagellated antherozoids to swim to the archegonium for fertilization. Dominant stage is the haploid **Gametophyte**; diploid sporophyte is physically dependent on gametophyte (e.g., *Riccia*, *Marchantia*, *Sphagnum* [peat moss, water-retention capacity]).
3. **Pteridophytes:** First terrestrial plants with **true vascular tissue (xylem with tracheids, phloem with sieve cells)**. Dominant stage is the independent diploid **Sporophyte** (e.g., Ferns, *Selaginella*, *Equisetum* [horsetails]).
4. **Gymnosperms ("Naked Seeds"):** Seeds develop exposed on ovuliferous scales without being enclosed by an ovary wall (no fruit). Possess non-motile siphonogamous pollen tubes (e.g., *Pinus*, *Cycas*, *Ginkgo biloba* [living fossil], *Sequoia sempervirens* [giant redwood]).
5. **Angiosperms ("Enclosed Seeds / Flowering Plants"):** Ovules enclosed within an ovary that matures into a **fruit**. Exhibit unique **Double Fertilization**:
   * Generative fertilization (Syngamy): $\text{Egg } (n) + \text{Sperm } (n) \longrightarrow \text{Zygote } (2n)$.
   * Triple fusion: $\text{Secondary Nucleus } (2n) + \text{Sperm } (n) \longrightarrow \text{Endosperm } (3n, \text{Triploid nutritive tissue})$.

### 22.7.2 Kingdom Animalia: Diagnostic Body Plan Criteria

```
                        ANIMAL KINGDOM CLASSIFICATION TREE
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                         ▼
    [PARAZOA / Cellular]                                     [EUMETAZOA / Tissue-Organ]
    - Phylum Porifera (Sponges)                                      │
    - Asymmetrical, Cellular grade,                                  │
      Choanocytes, Water canal system                                │
                                         ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
                                         ▼                                                       ▼
                              [RADIATA / Diploblastic]                                [BILATERIA / Triploblastic]
                              - Radial symmetry, 2 germ layers                         - Bilateral symmetry, 3 germ layers
                              - Cnidaria (Hydra, Jellyfish)                                       │
                              - Ctenophora (Comb jellies)                                         │
                                                                 ┌────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┐
                                                                 ▼                                ▼                                ▼
                                                          [ACOELOMATE]                   [PSEUDOCOELOMATE]                   [EUCOELOMATE]
                                                          - Solid body mesoderm          - Body cavity from blastocoel       - True coelom lined
                                                          - Platyhelminthes (Flatworms)    (unlined by peritoneum)             by mesoderm
                                                                                         - Aschelminthes (Roundworms)        - Annelida to Chordata
```

* **Body Symmetry:**
  * *Asymmetrical:* Sponges (*Porifera*).
  * *Radial Symmetry:* Body can be divided into identical halves by any plane passing through central axis (Cnidarians, Ctenophores, and **Adult Echinoderms**).
  * *Bilateral Symmetry:* Divided into identical left and right halves along a single sagittal plane (Platyhelminthes to Chordates, and **Larval Echinoderms**).
* **Germ Layers:**
  * *Diploblastic:* Ectoderm and Endoderm separated by non-cellular gelatinous mesoglea (Cnidaria, Ctenophora).
  * *Triploblastic:* Ectoderm, Mesoderm, and Endoderm (Platyhelminthes through Chordata).
* **Coelom (Body Cavity):**
  * *Acoelomates:* No cavity between body wall and gut; space filled with mesenchymal parenchyma (*Platyhelminthes*).
  * *Pseudocoelomates:* Cavity is not lined by mesodermal peritoneum; mesoderm exists as scattered pouches (*Aschelminthes / Nematodes*, e.g., *Ascaris*).
  * *Eucoelomates (True Coelom):* Fluid-filled cavity completely lined by mesoderm. Divided into:
    * *Schizocoelous:* Coelom formed by splitting of embryonic mesodermal mass (Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca).
    * *Enterocoelous:* Coelom formed by outpocketing of archenteron gut (Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata).
* **Notochord:** A flexible, rod-like dorsal mesodermal structure present during embryonic or adult stages in Phylum **Chordata** (absent in non-chordates).

## 22.8 HIGH-YIELD PRACTICE QUESTIONS

### Question 1 (UPSC CSE Prelims Standard)
Consider the following organisms:
1. *Nostoc*
2. *Mycoplasma*
3. *Saccharomyces*
4. *Agaricus*

Which of the above organisms lack a cell wall?
A) 1 and 2 only  
B) 2 only  
C) 2 and 3 only  
D) 3 and 4 only  

**Analytical Answer with Epistemic Breakdown:**
* *Nostoc* is a cyanobacterium possessing a rigid peptidoglycan cell wall covered by a gelatinous sheath.
* *Mycoplasma* (PPLO) is unique among all cellular organisms in **completely lacking a cell wall**; bounded only by a sterol-containing plasma membrane.
* *Saccharomyces* (yeast) and *Agaricus* (mushroom) are fungi and possess cell walls composed of **chitin and $\beta$-glucans**.
* **Correct Option:** **B) 2 only**.

### Question 2 (Competitive State PSC Standard)
Which of the following biological agents contains neither DNA nor RNA, yet is infectious and causes transmissible fatal neurodegenerative disease in mammals?
A) Viroid  
B) Retrovirus  
C) Prion  
D) Bacteriophage  

**Analytical Answer:**
* **Prions** are infectious proteinaceous particles entirely devoid of any nucleic acid genome. They propagate by inducing pathological conformational refolding of normal endogenous cellular prion proteins into protease-resistant $\beta$-sheet aggregates (causing BSE, CJD, and Kuru).
* **Correct Option:** **C) Prion**.

### Question 3 (Advanced Concept Standard)
Match List-I (Organism / Group) with List-II (Diagnostic Epistemic Characteristic):
* List-I:
  * a. Diatoms
  * b. Dinoflagellates
  * c. Archaebacteria
  * d. Lichens
* List-II:
  * 1. Ether-linked branched lipids in cell membrane
  * 2. Frustule shell of crystalline silica
  * 3. Sensitive bioindicators of $\text{SO}_2$ air pollution
  * 4. Saxitoxin neurotoxin causing red tides

Select the correct code:
A) a-2, b-4, c-1, d-3  
B) a-4, b-2, c-1, d-3  
C) a-2, b-1, c-4, d-3  
D) a-3, b-4, c-1, d-2  

**Analytical Answer:**
* Diatoms have silica-impregnated soapbox frustules $\to$ 2.
* Dinoflagellates (e.g., *Gonyaulax*) produce saxitoxin and cause toxic red tides $\to$ 4.
* Archaebacteria survive extreme habitats due to ether-linked branched membrane lipids $\to$ 1.
* Lichens are unable to grow in the presence of sulfur dioxide, serving as air pollution bioindicators $\to$ 3.
* **Correct Option:** **A) a-2, b-4, c-1, d-3**.
