## 24.1 THE HUMAN DIGESTIVE SYSTEM & ENTERIC BIOCHEMISTRY

Digestion is the hydrolytic breakdown of complex, insoluble organic macromolecules into simple, diffusible, and absorbable micromolecules, mediated by mechanical mastication and hydrolytic digestive enzymes.

```
                         THE ALIMENTARY TRACT
                         
  Mouth / Buccal Cavity (Teeth, Tongue, Salivary Amylase)
          │
          ▼
  Pharynx & Esophagus (Deglutition, Peristalsis, Epiglottis)
          │
          ▼  [Gastroesophageal Sphincter]
  Stomach (Cardia, Fundus, Body, Pylorus; HCl, Pepsin, Intrinsic Factor)
          │
          ▼  [Pyloric Sphincter]
  Small Intestine: Duodenum ──► Jejunum ──► Ileum (Crypts of Lieberkuhn, Villi)
          │        ▲         ▲
          │        │ (Bile)  │ (Pancreatic Juice)
          │     [Liver]   [Pancreas]
          ▼  [Ileocecal Valve]
  Large Intestine: Cecum (Appendix) ──► Colon (Ascending, Transverse, Descending, Sigmoid) ──► Rectum
```

### 24.1.1 Dentition & Oral Digestion
* **Human Dental Architecture:**
  * **Thecodont:** Teeth are embedded in deep bony sockets of the maxilla and mandible.
  * **Diphyodont:** Two successive sets of teeth develop during lifetime: deciduous/milk teeth ($20\text{ teeth}$) replaced by permanent adult dentition ($32\text{ teeth}$).
  * **Heterodont:** Four morphologically and functionally distinct dental morphotypes:
    1. *Incisors (I):* Chisel-shaped; specialized for cutting and biting.
    2. *Canines (C):* Dagger-shaped, sharp; specialized for tearing flesh.
    3. *Premolars (PM):* Bicuspid crowns; specialized for grinding and crushing.
    4. *Molars (M):* Broad triturating surfaces; crushing and chewing.
* **Adult Human Dental Formula:**
  $$\text{Dental Formula} = \frac{I\text{ }2,\text{ }C\text{ }1,\text{ }PM\text{ }2,\text{ }M\text{ }3}{I\text{ }2,\text{ }C\text{ }1,\text{ }PM\text{ }2,\text{ }M\text{ }3} \times 2 = \frac{2123}{2123} \times 2 = \mathbf{32\text{ Permanent Teeth}}$$
  *(Deciduous Child Formula: $\frac{2102}{2102} \times 2 = 20\text{ teeth}$; premolars are completely absent).*
* **Enamel:** The outermost hard, acellular, calcified layer covering the tooth crown; derived from ectoderm (ameloblasts). Composed of **Calcium Hydroxyapatite [$\text{Ca}_{10}(\text{PO}_4)_6(\text{OH})_2$]**; the **hardest biological substance in the human body**.
* **Salivary Digestion:** Three pairs of salivary glands—**Parotid** (largest, below ear; infected by Mumps paramyxovirus), **Submandibular / Submaxillary** (at the angle of lower jaw), and **Sublingual** (below tongue)—secrete $1.0 - 1.5\text{ liters/day}$ of saliva ($\text{pH} \approx 6.8$):
  * **Ptyalin (Salivary $\alpha$-Amylase):** Hydrolyzes approximately **$30\%$ of dietary starch** into the disaccharide maltose:
    $$\text{Starch} \xrightarrow[\text{pH } 6.8, \text{ Cl}^- \text{ activator}]{\text{Salivary Amylase}} \text{Maltose} + \text{Isomaltose} + \alpha\text{-limit dextrins}$$
  * **Lysozyme:** Cleaves $\beta(1 \to 4)$ bonds in bacterial peptidoglycan walls, acting as an antimicrobial barrier.

### 24.1.2 Gastric Digestion & Mucosal Cytology
The stomach is a J-shaped muscular reservoir churning food into a semi-fluid acidic slurry called **Chyme**. The gastric mucosa features gastric pits lined by specialized secretory cells:
1. **Parietal (Oxyntic) Cells:**
   * Secrete **Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)** ($\text{pH} \approx 1.5 - 2.0$), which creates an acidic medium killing ingested pathogens, denatures dietary proteins, and activates inactive zymogens.
   * Secrete **Castle's Intrinsic Factor (CIF)**: A glycoprotein essential for the receptor-mediated endocytosis of **Vitamin $\text{B}_{12}$ (Cyanocobalamin)** in the terminal ileum. Autoimmune destruction of parietal cells causes **Pernicious Anemia**.
2. **Peptic (Chief / Zymogen) Cells:** Secrete inactive proenzymes:
   * **Pepsinogen:** Cleaved autocatalytically by $\text{HCl}$ into active **Pepsin**, an endopeptidase hydrolyzing proteins into proteoses and peptones:
     $$\text{Pepsinogen} \xrightarrow{\text{HCl / Pepsin}} \text{Pepsin}; \quad \text{Proteins} \xrightarrow{\text{Pepsin}} \text{Proteoses} + \text{Peptones}$$
   * **Prorennin:** Secreted in infant gastric juice; converted by $\text{HCl}$ to **Rennin** (chymosin), which curdles soluble milk casein into insoluble calcium paracaseinate in the presence of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, preventing rapid gastric emptying. (Absent in adult humans).
3. **Mucous Neck Cells:** Secrete alkaline bicarbonate-rich mucus that coats gastric mucosa, shielding it against excoriation and autodigestion by concentrated $\text{HCl}$ and pepsin. Breakdown of this protective mucosal barrier by *Helicobacter pylori* or NSAIDs results in **Peptic Ulcers**.

### 24.1.3 Hepatobiliary & Pancreatic Digestion
* **The Liver (Largest Gland, $\approx 1.5\text{ kg}$):** Composed of hexagonal **Hepatic Lobules** (structural and functional units) covered by **Glisson's Capsule**. Sinusoids contain resident phagocytic macrophages called **Kupffer Cells**, which destroy senescent RBCs and bacteria.
  * *Bile Production:* Hepatocytes continuously synthesize **Bile Juice** ($\text{pH} \approx 7.8 - 8.6$), concentrated and stored in the **Gallbladder**.
  * **Bile Juice Contains ZERO Digestive Enzymes!**
  * *Bile Salts:* **Sodium Glycocholate and Sodium Taurocholate** reduce surface tension, breaking massive lipid globules into microscopic droplets (**Emulsification**), vastly expanding the surface area for lipase activity. Bile salts also form water-soluble **Micelles** required for lipid absorption.
  * *Bile Pigments:* **Bilirubin** (golden-yellow) and **Biliverdin** (green), catabolic degradation products of hemoglobin heme. Hyperbilirubinemia ($>2\text{ mg/dL}$) causes systemic yellow discoloration of sclera and skin (**Jaundice / Icterus**).
* **The Pancreas:** A heterocrine (mixed) gland:
  * *Exocrine Pancreatic Juice ($\text{pH} \approx 8.4$):* Discharged into the duodenum via the Hepatopancreatic Ampulla of Vater, guarded by the **Sphincter of Oddi**:
    1. **Trypsinogen:** Activated to **Trypsin** by intestinal brush-border enzyme **Enterokinase (Enteropeptidase)**. Trypsin then autocatalytically activates all other pancreatic zymogens:
       $$\text{Trypsinogen} \xrightarrow{\text{Enterokinase}} \text{Trypsin}$$
       $$\text{Chymotrypsinogen} \xrightarrow{\text{Trypsin}} \text{Chymotrypsin}; \quad \text{Procarboxypeptidase} \xrightarrow{\text{Trypsin}} \text{Carboxypeptidase}$$
    2. **Pancreatic Amylase (Amylopsin):** Hydrolyzes remaining starches into disaccharides.
    3. **Pancreatic Lipase (Steapsin):** The primary enzyme hydrolyzing emulsified triglycerides into monoglycerides and free fatty acids.
    4. **Nucleases:** Deoxyribonuclease (DNase) and Ribonuclease (RNase) hydrolyze nucleic acids into nucleotides.

### 24.1.4 Intestinal Digestion & Absorption Kinetics
The small intestine is the definitive organ of complete enzymatic digestion and nutrient absorption:
* **Succus Entericus (Intestinal Juice):** Secreted by the **Crypts of Lieberkühn**: contains disaccharidases (**Maltase, Lactase, Sucrase**), aminopeptidases, dipeptidases, nucleosidases, and intestinal lipases.
* **Surface Area Amplification:** The absorptive surface of the small intestine is amplified $>600\text{-fold}$ through three architectural levels:
  1. *Plicae Circulares (Valves of Kerckring):* Macroscopic permanent circular mucosal folds ($3\times$ amplification).
  2. *Villi:* Millions of finger-like projections ($10\times$ amplification).
  3. *Microvilli:* Apical brush border displaying $\sim 200\text{ million microvilli/mm}^2$ ($20\times$ amplification), yielding a net surface area exceeding $250\text{ m}^2$.
* **Nutrient Absorption Pathways:**
  * *Glucose and Galactose:* Absorbed via **Secondary Active Transport** coupled to $Na^+$ influx via the **SGLT-1** symporter on the apical membrane, followed by facilitated diffusion via **GLUT-2** into mesenteric capillaries.
  * *Fructose:* Absorbed exclusively by **Facilitated Diffusion** via **GLUT-5** (no energy required).
  * *Amino Acids:* Absorbed predominantly via $Na^+$-dependent active transport symporters.
  * *Lipids:* Free fatty acids and 2-monoglycerides diffuse passively from micelles into mucosal enterocytes, where they are re-esterified into triglycerides within the SER, packaged with apolipoproteins and cholesterol into spherical water-soluble droplets called **Chylomicrons** ($\approx 1000\text{ \AA}$ diameter). Chylomicrons are discharged via exocytosis into central lymphatic capillaries called **Lacteals**, bypassing the hepatic portal circulation to enter the bloodstream via the thoracic duct.

## 24.2 THE HUMAN RESPIRATORY SYSTEM & GAS KINETICS

Respiration involves external ventilation, alveolar gas diffusion, cardiovascular transport, systemic tissue exchange, and cellular oxidative phosphorylation.

```
                      THE RESPIRATORY PASSAGEWAY
                      
  External Nostrils ──► Nasal Chamber ──► Pharynx (Nasopharynx) ──► Glottis (Epiglottis)
          │
          ▼
  Larynx ("Voice Box", Thyroid / Cricoid Cartilages, Vocal Cords)
          │
          ▼
  Trachea (Incomplete C-shaped Hyaline Cartilaginous Rings; bifurcates at T5 vertebra)
          │
          ▼
  Right & Left Primary Bronchi ──► Secondary / Tertiary Bronchi ──► Terminal Bronchioles
          │
          ▼
  Respiratory Bronchioles ──► Alveolar Ducts ──► ALVEOLAR SACS (Terminal Respiratory Unit)
```

### 24.2.1 Biomechanics of Pulmonary Ventilation
Lungs are situated in the airtight thoracic cavity enclosed laterally by ribs and intercostal muscles, dorsally by the vertebral column, ventrally by the sternum, and inferiorly by the dome-shaped muscular **Diaphragm**.
* **Inspiration (Active Process):**
  1. Contraction of the **Diaphragm** causes it to flatten inferiorly, increasing the antero-posterior thoracic volume.
  2. Contraction of the **External Intercostal Muscles** lifts the ribs and sternum upward and outward, expanding the dorso-ventral thoracic diameter.
  3. Increased thoracic volume decreases intra-pulmonary pressure below atmospheric pressure ($\approx -1\text{ to }-3\text{ mmHg}$ relative to atmosphere), sucking air into the lungs.
* **Expiration (Passive Process at Rest):**
  1. Relaxation of the diaphragm (resuming its convex dome shape) and external intercostals reduces thoracic volume.
  2. Elastic recoil of the pulmonary parenchyma elevates intra-pulmonary pressure above atmospheric pressure ($+1\text{ to }+3\text{ mmHg}$), expelling air.
  3. *Forced Expiration:* Active process recruiting **Internal Intercostal Muscles** and abdominal wall muscles (rectus abdominis).
* **Pulmonary Surfactant:** Synthesized by **Type II Alveolar Pneumocytes**; composed predominantly of **Dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine (DPPC)**. Dramatically reduces surface tension at the alveolar air-liquid interface, preventing alveolar collapse (atelectasis) during expiration. Deficiency in premature neonates causes fatal **Infant Respiratory Distress Syndrome (IRDS)**.


| Spirometric Metric | Mathematical Definition & Normal Adult Value | Physiological & Clinical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tidal Volume (TV)** | Volume inspired or expired during a normal resting breath: **$\approx 500\text{ mL}$** (Healthy adult: $\approx 6000 - 8000\text{ mL/min}$). | Includes **$\approx 150\text{ mL}$ Anatomical Dead Space** (air filling conducting airways that does not participate in gas exchange). |
| **Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV)** | Extra volume inhaled forcefully beyond normal tidal volume: **$\approx 2500 - 3000\text{ mL}$**. | Reflects maximal inspiratory muscle effort and pulmonary compliance. |
| **Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV)** | Extra volume exhaled forcefully beyond normal tidal expiration: **$\approx 1000 - 1100\text{ mL}$**. | Diminished in restrictive pulmonary disorders and obesity. |
| **Residual Volume (RV)** | Volume of gas remaining in lungs even after maximal, forced expiration: **$\approx 1100 - 1200\text{ mL}$**. | **CANNOT be measured by standard spirometry!** (requires helium dilution or plethysmography); prevents alveolar collapse. |
| **Vital Capacity (VC)** | Maximum volume exhaled after deepest possible inspiration: $\mathbf{\text{VC} = \text{TV} + \text{IRV} + \text{ERV}} \approx \mathbf{4600\text{ mL}}$. | Paramount clinical index of functional respiratory capacity. |
| **Functional Residual Capacity (FRC)** | Volume remaining in lungs after normal passive expiration: $\mathbf{\text{FRC} = \text{ERV} + \text{RV}} \approx \mathbf{2200 - 2300\text{ mL}}$. | Acts as a constant oxygen reservoir buffering blood gas fluctuations between breaths. |
| **Total Lung Capacity (TLC)** | Total gas volume contained in lungs after maximal forced inspiration: $\mathbf{\text{TLC} = \text{VC} + \text{RV}} \approx \mathbf{5800 - 6000\text{ mL}}$. | Expanded in emphysema; constricted in pulmonary fibrosis. |

### 24.2.3 Alveolar Gas Exchange & Diffusion Barrier
Gas exchange across the alveolar-capillary membrane operates strictly via **Simple Passive Diffusion** driven by partial pressure gradients:

$$\text{Diffusion Rate } \propto \frac{\Delta P \cdot A \cdot S}{d \cdot \sqrt{\text{MW}}}$$

* **Respiratory Membrane Layers (Total Thickness $< 0.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$):**
  1. Squamous epithelium of the alveolar wall.
  2. Non-cellular basement membrane.
  3. Capillary endothelial basement membrane.
  4. Endothelial cell layer of the pulmonary blood capillary.
* **Partial Pressure Gradients:**
  * Alveolar Gas: $pO_2 = 104\text{ mmHg}, \quad pCO_2 = 40\text{ mmHg}$.
  * Deoxygenated Venous Blood: $pO_2 = 40\text{ mmHg}, \quad pCO_2 = 45\text{ mmHg}$.
  * Oxygenated Arterial Blood: $pO_2 = 95\text{ mmHg}, \quad pCO_2 = 40\text{ mmHg}$.
  * Systemic Tissues: $pO_2 \le 40\text{ mmHg}, \quad pCO_2 \ge 45\text{ mmHg}$.
* **Solubility Differential:** The solubility coefficient of **$\text{CO}_2$ is $20–25\text{ times}$ higher** than that of $\text{O}_2$. Consequently, despite a minute partial pressure gradient for $\text{CO}_2$ ($\Delta P = 5\text{ mmHg}$) compared to $\text{O}_2$ ($\Delta P = 64\text{ mmHg}$), equivalent molar quantities of $\text{CO}_2$ diffuse across the respiratory membrane per unit time.

### 24.2.4 Gas Transport Biophysics in Blood
#### 1. Oxygen ($O_2$) Transport
* **Dissolved in Plasma:** Only $\approx 1.5 - 3\%$ of total $O_2$ is transported physically dissolved in plasma water due to low solubility.
* **Bound to Hemoglobin:** $\approx 97 - 98.5\%$ is transported as **Oxyhemoglobin ($\text{Hb}_4\text{O}_8$)**. Each of the four heme $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ iron moieties in adult hemoglobin binds one $O_2$ reversibly:
  $$\text{Hb}_4 + 4\text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Hb}_4(\text{O}_2)_4$$
  * *Oxygen Carrying Capacity:* $1\text{ gram of pure Hb}$ binds **$1.34\text{ mL of } O_2$**. With normal hemoglobin levels ($15\text{ g/dL}$), $100\text{ mL of oxygenated arterial blood}$ carries $\approx \mathbf{20\text{ mL of } O_2}$. Under resting conditions, tissues extract $\approx 5\text{ mL of } O_2$ per $100\text{ mL}$ of blood ($25\%$ delivery).

```
       % Hb Saturation
          ▲
      100 ┼                                . - - ' ' 
          │                            . '
          │                         . '      <-- LEFT SHIFT: High Affinity (Alveoli: Low Temp, Low CO2, High pH)
       50 ┼ - - - - - - - - - - . '          
          │                   . '            <-- RIGHT SHIFT (BOHR EFFECT): Low Affinity (Tissues: High Temp, High CO2, Low pH, 2,3-BPG)
          │               . '   │
          │           . '       │
          └─────────┼───────────┼─────────────────────────► pO2 (mmHg)
                   P50         P50
```

* **The Oxygen-Hemoglobin Dissociation Curve (Sigmoid Shape):** The sigmoidal contour reflects **Positive Cooperativity**—binding of the first $O_2$ molecule induces an allosteric conformational shift from the Tense ($T$) to the Relaxed ($R$) state, progressively augmenting the affinity of the remaining heme sites.
* **$P_{50}$ Value:** The partial pressure of oxygen at which hemoglobin is $50\%$ saturated with oxygen (normally $\approx 26.6\text{ mmHg}$).
* **The Bohr Effect (Right Shift $\implies$ Decreased Affinity $\implies O_2$ Unloading in Tissues):**
  * Shift to the **RIGHT** occurs when $P_{50}$ increases, favoring oxygen delivery to active tissues. Induced by:
    $$\uparrow p\text{CO}_2, \quad \uparrow [\text{H}^+] \text{ (decreased pH / Acidosis)}, \quad \uparrow \text{Temperature}, \quad \uparrow \text{2,3-Bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG)}.$$
  * Shift to the **LEFT** occurs in pulmonary alveoli (decreased $p\text{CO}_2$, elevated pH, cooler temperature), maximizing oxygen loading.
  * *Fetal Hemoglobin ($\text{HbF}, \alpha_2\gamma_2$):* Lacks binding sites for 2,3-BPG, giving it a much **higher $O_2$ affinity than adult HbA**, ensuring placental oxygen transfer from maternal blood to fetal blood (curve shifted permanently to the left; $P_{50} \approx 19\text{ mmHg}$).

#### 2. Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$) Transport
Transported in blood via three physiological mechanisms:
1. **Dissolved in Physical Solution:** $\approx 7\%$ dissolved in plasma water.
2. **Carbamino-Hemoglobin ($\text{HbCO}_2$):** $\approx 20 - 23\%$ binds reversibly to uncharged terminal amino groups ($-\text{NH}_2$) of globin polypeptide chains:
   $$\text{Hb-NH}_2 + \text{CO}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Hb-NH-COO}^- + \text{H}^+$$
   *(Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Distinction: Carbon monoxide binds the $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ heme ring with $>200\times$ higher affinity than $O_2$, forming **Carboxyhemoglobin**, starving tissues of oxygen).*
3. **Bicarbonate Ions ($\text{HCO}_3^-$):** $\approx \mathbf{70\%}$ (the dominant mechanism).
   * Inside erythrocytes, the zinc-metalloenzyme **Carbonic Anhydrase** accelerates the reversible hydration of $\text{CO}_2$ by a factor of $10^6$:
     $$\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \underset{\text{Anhydrase}}{\overset{\text{Carbonic}}{\rightleftharpoons}} \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{HCO}_3^- + \text{H}^+$$
   * Released protons ($\text{H}^+$) are buffered by deoxygenated hemoglobin.
   * **The Chloride Shift (Hamburger's Phenomenon):** As $\text{HCO}_3^-$ accumulates inside erythrocytes at the tissue level, it diffuses down its concentration gradient out into the plasma via the **Anion Exchanger 1 (AE1 / Band 3)** protein. To maintain electrical neutrality, **Chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$) diffuse from plasma into the erythrocyte**. In pulmonary capillaries, the entire cascade reverses completely.
* **The Haldane Effect:** Deoxygenation of hemoglobin increases its capacity to carry $\text{CO}_2$ and protons, facilitating $\text{CO}_2$ uptake in tissues; conversely, oxygenation of hemoglobin in the lungs promotes the prompt displacement and release of $\text{CO}_2$.

### 24.3.1 Blood Composition & Hemostasis
Whole blood represents $\approx 7 - 8\%$ of total body weight ($\sim 5\text{ liters}$ in adults; $\text{pH} \approx 7.35 - 7.45$).
* **Plasma ($55\%$ by volume):** Pale yellow liquid containing $90–92\%$ water and $6–8\%$ proteins:
  1. *Serum Albumin ($4.5\text{ g/dL}$):* Primary determinant of **Colloid Osmotic (Oncotic) Pressure** ($\approx 25\text{ mmHg}$), retaining intravascular fluid volume; hypoproteinemia results in generalized tissue **Edema**.
  2. *Globulins ($2.5\text{ g/dL}$):* $\alpha$- and $\beta$-globulins (transport proteins); **$\gamma$-Globulins (Immunoglobulins)** synthesized by plasma B-cells acting as humoral antibodies.
  3. *Fibrinogen ($0.3\text{ g/dL}$):* Inactive soluble clotting precursor synthesized by the liver.
  * **Serum:** Defibrinated plasma: $\mathbf{\text{Serum} = \text{Plasma} - \text{Clotting Factors}}$.
* **Formed Cellular Elements ($45\%$ by volume):**
  1. **Erythrocytes (RBCs):** Non-nucleated, biconcave circular discs ($7.2\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ diameter); average lifespan **$120\text{ days}$**. Count: $5.0 - 5.5\text{ million/mm}^3$ (males), $4.5 - 5.0\text{ million/mm}^3$ (females). Destroyed in the spleen ("graveyard of RBCs").
  2. **Leukocytes (WBCs):** Nucleated cells ($6000 - 8000/\text{mm}^3$):
     * *Granulocytes:* **Neutrophils** ($60–65\%$, active phagocytes with multi-lobed nucleus), **Eosinophils** ($2–3\%$, bilobed, fight parasitic helminth infections and allergic reactions), **Basophils** ($0.5–1\%$, secrete **Histamine** [vasodilator], **Serotonin** [vasoconstrictor], and **Heparin** [anticoagulant]).
     * *Agranulocytes:* **Lymphocytes** ($20–25\%$, B-cells [humoral immunity] and T-cells [cell-mediated immunity]), **Monocytes** ($6–8\%$, largest WBCs; differentiate into tissue macrophages).
  3. **Thrombocytes (Platelets):** Non-nucleated cytoplasmic fragments pinched off from giant bone marrow **Megakaryocytes** ($150,000 - 400,000/\text{mm}^3$); lifespan $7–10\text{ days}$.

### 24.3.2 Blood Group Genetics & Rh Incompatibility
* **ABO Blood Groups (Karl Landsteiner, Nobel 1930):** Determined by genetically dictated oligosaccharide antigens on erythrocyte membranes, governed by multiple alleles ($I^A, I^B, i$):
  * **Group A:** Antigen A on RBCs; Anti-B isoantibodies in plasma (Genotypes: $I^A I^A$ or $I^A i$).
  * **Group B:** Antigen B on RBCs; Anti-A isoantibodies in plasma (Genotypes: $I^B I^B$ or $I^B i$).
  * **Group AB:** Both Antigens A and B; **NO antibodies** in plasma ($I^A I^B$). **Universal Recipient**.
  * **Group O:** **NO antigens** on RBCs; both Anti-A and Anti-B antibodies in plasma ($ii$). **Universal Donor**.
* **The Rh Factor & Erythroblastosis Fetalis:**
  * Discovered by Landsteiner and Wiener (1940) in Rhesus macaque monkeys. An individual possessing the D-antigen on RBCs is **$\text{Rh}^+$** ($\sim 85\%$ of humans); lacking it is **$\text{Rh}^-$**.
  * **Erythroblastosis Fetalis (Hemolytic Disease of the Newborn, HDN):** Occurs strictly when an **$\text{Rh}^-$ mother carries an $\text{Rh}^+$ fetus** (inherited from an $\text{Rh}^+$ father):
    1. During first delivery, fetomaternal micro-hemorrhage exposes the $\text{Rh}^-$ mother to fetal $\text{Rh}^+$ antigens, sensitizing her immune system to produce maternal **anti-Rh IgG antibodies**.
    2. In subsequent $\text{Rh}^+$ pregnancies, maternal anti-Rh IgG antibodies cross the placenta into fetal circulation, destroying fetal erythrocytes, resulting in severe hemolytic anemia, fatal kernicterus (brain damage from bilirubin), or intrauterine death.
    3. *Prophylaxis:* Administering **Anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)** to the $\text{Rh}^-$ mother within $72\text{ hours}$ of delivering every $\text{Rh}^+$ neonate, destroying escaped fetal RBCs before maternal B-cell sensitization occurs.

### 24.3.3 Cardiac Electrophysiology & The Cardiac Cycle
* **Nodal Conduction System:**
  * **Sinoatrial (SA) Node:** Located in the superior wall of right atrium near superior vena cava entry. Generates intrinsic rhythmic action potentials at the highest frequency ($\mathbf{70 - 75\text{ beats/min}}$) due to spontaneous diastolic $Na^+$ leakage (funny currents). Acts as the primary **Pacemaker of the Heart**.
  * **Atrioventricular (AV) Node:** Located in interatrial septum; introduces a crucial physiological delay ($\approx 0.1\text{ second}$), ensuring complete atrial emptying before ventricular contraction begins. Acts as the **Pacesetter**.
  * **Bundle of His & Purkinje Fibers:** Rapidly propagate action potentials through the interventricular septum to ventricular myocardium, ensuring synchronized apex-to-base ventricular ejection.
* **The Cardiac Cycle ($0.8\text{ Second}$ at $75\text{ bpm}$):**
  1. *Joint Diastole ($0.4\text{ s}$):* All four chambers relaxed; tricuspid and bicuspid (mitral) valves open; blood flows passively from atria into ventricles ($70\%$ passive ventricular filling).
  2. *Atrial Systole ($0.1\text{ s}$):* SA node fires; atria contract simultaneously, pumping remaining $30\%$ of blood into ventricles.
  3. *Ventricular Systole ($0.3\text{ s}$):* Ventricles contract; intraventricular pressure spikes:
     * **First Heart Sound ("LUB"):** Sudden closure of the **Atrioventricular (Tricuspid and Mitral) Valves** at the onset of ventricular systole. Low-pitch, long-duration ($0.14\text{ s}$).
     * Isovolumetric contraction transitions to rapid ejection into aorta and pulmonary artery.
  4. *Ventricular Diastole:* Ventricular pressure plummets:
     * **Second Heart Sound ("DUB"):** Sudden closure of the **Semilunar (Aortic and Pulmonary) Valves** at the onset of ventricular diastole. Sharp, high-pitch, short-duration ($0.08\text{ s}$).
* **Hemodynamic Formulas:**
  * **Stroke Volume (SV):** $\text{End-Diastolic Volume (EDV: } 120\text{ mL)} - \text{End-Systolic Volume (ESV: } 50\text{ mL)} = \mathbf{70\text{ mL}}$.
  * **Cardiac Output (CO):** Volume of blood pumped by each ventricle per minute:
    $$\mathbf{\text{Cardiac Output}} = \text{Stroke Volume (SV)} \times \text{Heart Rate (HR)} = 70\text{ mL} \times 72\text{ bpm} \approx \mathbf{5040\text{ mL/min}} \approx \mathbf{5\text{ Liters/min}}$$

* **ECG Deflection Waves:**
  * **P Wave:** Represents **Atrial Depolarization** (electrical excitation spreading from SA node across atria).
  * **QRS Complex:** Represents **Ventricular Depolarization** (initiates ventricular systole). Pathological widening ($>0.12\text{ s}$) indicates bundle branch block.
  * **T Wave:** Represents **Ventricular Repolarization** (relaxation of ventricles). Flattening or inversion signals myocardial ischemia.
  * *Counting Heart Rate:* The number of QRS complexes occurring per minute yields the heart rate.

## 24.4 HIGH-YIELD PRACTICE QUESTIONS

### Question 1 (UPSC CSE Prelims Standard)
Consider the following statements regarding human respiration and circulation:
1. Carbon monoxide poisoning results from the preferential formation of carboxyhemoglobin, which binds oxygen with irreversible stability.
2. The functional residual capacity (FRC) of lungs can be directly measured using standard clinical spirometry.
3. During the cardiac cycle, the first heart sound ("lub") is produced by the simultaneous closure of the semilunar valves.
4. Erythroblastosis fetalis occurs when an $\text{Rh}^-$ mother carries an $\text{Rh}^+$ fetus during a subsequent pregnancy.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A) 4 only  
B) 1 and 4 only  
C) 2 and 3 only  
D) 1, 2 and 4 only  

**Analytical Answer with Epistemic Breakdown:**
* Statement 1 is **INCORRECT**: Carbon monoxide binds hemoglobin's iron to form carboxyhemoglobin, but it does NOT bind oxygen with irreversible stability; it physically competes with and blocks oxygen from binding hemoglobin due to its $>200\times$ higher binding affinity.
* Statement 2 is **INCORRECT**: Residual Volume (RV) cannot be exhaled and therefore **cannot be measured by standard spirometry**. Because $\text{FRC} = \text{ERV} + \text{RV}$, FRC also cannot be measured by simple spirometry.
* Statement 3 is **INCORRECT**: The first heart sound ("lub") is produced by the closure of the **Atrioventricular (Tricuspid and Mitral) valves**, not semilunar valves (which produce the second sound "dub").
* Statement 4 is **CORRECT**: An $\text{Rh}^-$ mother previously sensitized to $\text{Rh}^+$ fetal blood mounts anti-Rh IgG antibodies that cross the placenta to attack fetal RBCs in subsequent $\text{Rh}^+$ gestations.
* **Correct Option:** **A) 4 only**.

### Question 2 (Competitive State PSC Standard)
Which of the following enzymes present in human gastrointestinal secretions does NOT directly break down dietary nutrients, but is strictly required to activate pancreatic zymogens?
A) Pepsin  
B) Enterokinase (Enteropeptidase)  
C) Pancreatic Amylase  
D) Ptyalin  

**Analytical Answer:**
* **Enterokinase** (enteropeptidase) is a brush-border enzyme secreted by the duodenal mucosa whose solitary, indispensable function is to cleave inactive pancreatic **Trypsinogen into active Trypsin**, which subsequently activates chymotrypsinogen, procarboxypeptidase, and proelastase.
* **Correct Option:** **B) Enterokinase (Enteropeptidase)**.

### Question 3 (Advanced Concept Standard)
If a healthy individual has a resting heart rate of $75\text{ beats/min}$, an end-diastolic volume of $130\text{ mL}$, and an end-systolic volume of $50\text{ mL}$, what is their Cardiac Output?
A) $3.75\text{ L/min}$  
B) $5.00\text{ L/min}$  
C) $6.00\text{ L/min}$  
D) $9.75\text{ L/min}$  

**Analytical Answer:**
* Stroke Volume ($\text{SV}$) = $\text{End-Diastolic Volume (EDV)} - \text{End-Systolic Volume (ESV)}$
  $$\text{SV} = 130\text{ mL} - 50\text{ mL} = 80\text{ mL}$$
* Cardiac Output ($\text{CO}$) = $\text{Stroke Volume} \times \text{Heart Rate}$
  $$\text{CO} = 80\text{ mL} \times 75\text{ bpm} = 6000\text{ mL/min} = \mathbf{6.00\text{ L/min}}$$
* **Correct Option:** **C) 6.00 L/min**.
