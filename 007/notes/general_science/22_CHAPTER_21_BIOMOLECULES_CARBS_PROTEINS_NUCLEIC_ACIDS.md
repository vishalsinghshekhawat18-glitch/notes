### Epistemic Stance & Learning Objectives
Living organisms are chemical systems operating far from thermodynamic equilibrium. All physiological mechanisms—from neurotransmission to oxidative phosphorylation—are governed by the stereochemistry, thermodynamics, and catalytic behavior of biomacromolecules. This chapter establishes the rigorous molecular architecture of carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids, details the kinetics and regulatory thermodynamics of enzyme catalysis, categorizes metabolic vitamins and cofactors, and exposes the specific molecular traps recurrent in civil services and competitive examinations.

## 21.1 CHEMICAL CONSTITUENTS & BIOCHEMICAL ANALYSIS

Living tissue (plant, animal, or microbial) subjected to elemental analysis reveals carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus as predominant constituents. However, living matter differs profoundly from non-living earth crust in the relative abundance of carbon and hydrogen relative to other elements.

```
                      TISSUE FRACTIONATION PROTOCOL
                      
                 Living Tissue (e.g., Liver / Plant Leaf)
                                    │
                                    ▼  Grind in Mortar & Pestle with
                                       Trichloroacetic Acid (Cl3C-COOH)
                                    │
                                    ▼  Filter through Cheesecloth / Miracloth
                                    │
                 ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
                 ▼                                     ▼
     [Acid-Soluble Pool / Filtrate]       [Acid-Insoluble Pellet / Retentate]
     - Molecular weight: 18 - 800 Da       - Molecular weight: > 10,000 Da
     - Monosaccharides, Amino acids,       - Polysaccharides, Polypeptides (Proteins),
       Nucleotides, Simple organic acids     Polynucleotides (Nucleic Acids)
     - Represents the CYTOPLASMIC POOL     - *Exception:* LIPIDS (MW < 800 Da, but
                                             form insoluble vesicular membranes)
```

> [!LAW / PRINCIPLE]
> **The Lipid Exception in Macromolecular Analysis**
> Lipids possess low molecular weights ($\text{MW} < 800\text{ Da}$) and are chemically micromolecules. However, upon tissue disruption, their amphipathic nature causes them to aggregate into stable vesicular lipid bilayer fragments that fail to dissolve in aqueous trichloroacetic acid and are retained in the **acid-insoluble retentate**. Thus, lipids are categorized functionally as biomacromolecules in fractionation, despite strictly being micromolecules by molecular weight.

## 21.2 CARBOHYDRATES: STRUCTURE, CLASSIFICATION & ENERGETICS

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes, polyhydroxy ketones, or compounds that yield these upon hydrolysis. Empirical formula: $\text{C}_n(\text{H}_2\text{O})_m$ (with notable exceptions such as deoxyribose $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_4$ and rhamnose $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_5$).

```
                                  CARBOHYDRATES
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                            ▼                            ▼
   [Monosaccharides]             [Oligosaccharides]           [Polysaccharides]
   - 1 Sugar Unit                - 2 to 10 Sugar Units        - > 10 Units (Polymers)
   - Trioses (Glyceraldehyde)    - Disaccharides:             - Homopolysaccharides:
   - Pentoses (Ribose)             * Maltose (Glc-Glc)          * Starch, Glycogen,
   - Hexoses (Glucose,             * Lactose (Gal-Glc)            Cellulose, Chitin
     Fructose, Galactose)          * Sucrose (Glc-Fru)        - Heteropolysaccharides:
                                                                * Hyaluronic acid, Heparin
```

### 21.2.1 Monosaccharides
The simplest carbohydrates, incapable of further enzymatic hydrolysis:
1. **Classification by Carbon Number:**
   * *Trioses ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_3$):* Glyceraldehyde (aldotriose), Dihydroxyacetone (ketotriose) — key intermediates in glycolysis.
   * *Pentoses ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5$):* Ribose (RNA, ATP, $\text{NAD}^+$), Deoxyribose ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_4$ in DNA), Ribulose ( $\text{CO}_2$ acceptor RuBP in Calvin cycle).
   * *Hexoses ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$):*
     * **D-Glucose (Dextrose / Grape Sugar):** Primary cellular metabolic fuel; aldohexose with 4 chiral carbons ($2^4 = 16$ stereoisomers).
     * **D-Fructose (Fruit Sugar / Levulose):** Ketohexose; the sweetest naturally occurring sugar. Marker sugar in mammalian seminal fluid providing energy for sperm motility.
     * **D-Galactose (Brain Sugar):** Aldohexose; essential constituent of glycolipids (cerebrosides) in nervous tissue.

### 21.2.2 Disaccharides & Glycosidic Linkages
Formed by the condensation of two monosaccharides through a **Glycosidic Bond** ($-\text{C}-\text{O}-\text{C}-$) with the elimination of a water molecule ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$):
1. **Maltose (Malt Sugar):** $\alpha\text{-D-Glucose} + \alpha\text{-D-Glucose}$ linked via **$\alpha(1 \to 4)$ glycosidic bond**. Reducing sugar with a free hemiacetal group. Produced during starch digestion by salivary and pancreatic $\alpha$-amylase.
2. **Lactose (Milk Sugar):** $\beta\text{-D-Galactose} + \beta\text{-D-Glucose}$ linked via **$\beta(1 \to 4)$ glycosidic bond**. Reducing sugar. Hydrolyzed by intestinal lactase. *Lactose Intolerance* results from adult lactase deficiency, leading to osmotic diarrhea and bacterial fermentation.
3. **Sucrose (Table Sugar / Cane Sugar):** $\alpha\text{-D-Glucose} + \beta\text{-D-Fructose}$ linked via an **$\alpha(1 \leftrightarrow 2)\beta$ glycosidic bond** involving the anomeric carbons of both monosaccharides.
   * **Non-Reducing Sugar:** Lacks a free aldehyde or ketone group.
   * **Inversion of Sugar:** Dextrorotatory sucrose ($[\alpha]_D = +66.5^\circ$) upon acid/enzymatic hydrolysis yields an equimolar mixture of D-glucose ($+52.5^\circ$) and D-fructose ($-92.4^\circ$). Because the levorotation of fructose dominates, the net optical rotation shifts from positive to negative ($-19.9^\circ$). The product is termed **Invert Sugar**.

### 21.2.3 Polysaccharides (Glycans)
High-molecular-weight condensation polymers of monosaccharides linked by glycosidic bonds:

| Polysaccharide | Monomer Unit | Linkage Architecture | Biological Role & Diagnostic Test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starch (Amylose)** | $\alpha$-D-Glucose | Linear, unbranched; **$\alpha(1 \to 4)$** glycosidic bonds. Helical secondary structure. | Traps molecular iodine ($\text{I}_2$) in helical core, producing an intense **Deep Blue/Black Color**. |
| **Starch (Amylopectin)** | $\alpha$-D-Glucose | Branched; linear **$\alpha(1 \to 4)$** chains with **$\alpha(1 \to 6)$** branch points every $24–30$ residues. | Primary energy storage in plants; insoluble in cold water; stains purplish-red with iodine. |
| **Glycogen ("Animal Starch")** | $\alpha$-D-Glucose | Highly branched; **$\alpha(1 \to 4)$** backbone with **$\alpha(1 \to 6)$** branches every $8–12$ residues. | Primary energy storage in animal liver ($\sim 100\text{ g}$) and skeletal muscle ($\sim 400\text{ g}$); stains **Red-Brown** with iodine. |
| **Cellulose** | $\beta$-D-Glucose | Linear, rigid, unbranched; **$\beta(1 \to 4)$** linkages stabilized by extensive inter-chain H-bonds. | Most abundant organic biomolecule on Earth; structural plant cell walls. Cannot form helices, **gives NO color with iodine**. |
| **Chitin** | $N$-Acetylglucosamine (NAG) | Linear polymer of NAG joined by **$\beta(1 \to 4)$** linkages. | Second most abundant biomolecule; forms fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons. |

### 21.2.4 Chemical Diagnostic Tests for Carbohydrates
1. **Molisch’s Test:** General test for all carbohydrates. Concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ dehydrates sugars into furfural or hydroxymethylfurfural, which condenses with $\alpha$-naphthol to produce a **purple/violet ring** at the liquid junction.
2. **Benedict’s & Fehling’s Tests:** Tests for **Reducing Sugars** (all monosaccharides, maltose, lactose). Alkaline cupric ions ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$, blue) are reduced by free anomeric hemiacetal/hemiketal groups to cuprous oxide ($\text{Cu}_2\text{O}$), forming a **brick-red precipitate**. (Sucrose and polysaccharides yield negative results).
3. **Iodine Test:** Starch forms a charge-transfer complex with triiodide ($\text{I}_3^-$) inside the amylose helix, yielding a **navy-blue coloration** which disappears upon heating (denaturation of helix) and reappears on cooling.

## 21.3 LIPIDS: FATTY ACIDS, TRIGLYCERIDES & STEROLS

Lipids are water-insoluble, hydrophobic or amphipathic biological esters of fatty acids and alcohols, soluble in nonpolar organic solvents (chloroform, ether, benzene).

### 21.3.1 Fatty Acid Chemistry
Carboxylic acids with long hydrocarbon chains ($\text{R}-\text{COOH}$, typically $\text{C}_{12}$ to $\text{C}_{24}$).
* **Saturated Fatty Acids:** Contain no carbon-carbon double bonds ($\text{C}-\text{C}$ single bonds only). Straight conformation packs tightly, yielding high melting points (solid at room temperature):
  * Palmitic acid ($\text{C}_{16}\text{H}_{32}\text{O}_2$, 16 carbons).
  * Stearic acid ($\text{C}_{18}\text{H}_{36}\text{O}_2$, 18 carbons).
* **Unsaturated Fatty Acids:** Contain one or more double bonds ($\text{C}=\text{C}$), predominantly in the *cis* stereochemical configuration, inducing kinks that prevent tight crystalline packing, yielding low melting points (liquid oils at room temperature):
  * Oleic acid: Monounsaturated (MUFA, $18:1, \Delta^9$).
  * Linoleic acid: Polyunsaturated (PUFA, $18:2, \Delta^{9,12}$) — **Essential Fatty Acid ($\omega-6$)**.
  * $\alpha$-Linolenic acid: PUFA ($18:3, \Delta^{9,12,15}$) — **Essential Fatty Acid ($\omega-3$)**.
  * Arachidonic acid: PUFA ($20:4, \Delta^{5,8,11,14}$) — precursor of prostaglandins, thromboxanes, and leukotrienes (eicosanoids).
* **Hydrogenation & Trans-Fats:** Industrial catalytic hydrogenation of vegetable oils (hardening) partially isomerizes natural *cis* bonds into *trans* double bonds. **Trans-fatty acids** increase serum low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol and decrease high-density lipoprotein (HDL), accelerating coronary atherosclerosis.

### 21.3.2 Neutral Fats & Phospholipids
* **Triglycerides (Triacylglycerols / TAGs):** Esters of 1 molecule of **Glycerol** (propane-1,2,3-triol) with 3 fatty acid molecules via ester bonds ($-\text{COO}-$). Primary metabolic energy storage; yields $9.3\text{ kcal/g}$ upon complete oxidation (compared to $4.1\text{ kcal/g}$ for carbs/proteins) due to higher reduction state and anhydrous storage.
* **Phospholipids (Phosphoglycerides):** Glycerol esterified to two fatty acids, with the third hydroxyl group esterified to phosphoric acid bound to a nitrogenous base:
  * **Lecithin (Phosphatidylcholine):** Crucial component of cellular membranes and pulmonary surfactant (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine), which lowers alveolar surface tension to prevent lung collapse.

### 21.3.3 Steroids & Sterols
Lipids characterized by a tetracyclic cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene (CPPP) ring system:
* **Cholesterol:** Major sterol in animal membranes; precursor of:
  1. Bile acids and salts (cholic acid, chenodeoxycholic acid).
  2. Steroid hormones (cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, estradiol, progesterone).
  3. Vitamin D ($\text{7-dehydrocholesterol} \xrightarrow{\text{UV light in skin}} \text{Cholecalciferol / Vitamin D}_3$).

## 21.4 PROTEINS: AMINO ACIDS, STRUCTURAL HIERARCHY & DYNAMICS

Proteins are unbranched heteropolymers of L-$\alpha$-amino acids linked by peptide bonds. They constitute over $50\%$ of cellular dry weight and mediate nearly all biological functions.

```
                         BASIC AMINO ACID STRUCTURE
                                   H
                                   │
                    H3N+ ─────── C ─────── COO-
                                   │
                                   R  <-- Variable Side Chain
```

### 21.4.1 Amino Acid Classification & Zwitterion Ionization
All standard 20 amino acids possess a central $\alpha$-carbon bonded to four groups: a basic amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$), an acidic carboxyl group ($-\text{COOH}$), a hydrogen atom ($-\text{H}$), and a distinctive side-chain ($-\text{R}$).
* **Chirality:** Except **Glycine** ($\text{R} = -\text{H}$, achiral), all standard amino acids are optically active and exist in the **L-stereochemical configuration** in natural proteins.
* **Zwitterion Behavior:** In neutral aqueous solution ($\text{pH} \approx 7.0$), the carboxyl group dissociates ($-\text{COO}^-$) and the amino group protonates ($-\text{NH}_3^+$), forming an internal dipolar ion called a **Zwitterion** with net zero charge at its **Isoelectric Point (pI)**.

```
       Cationic Form (Low pH)      Zwitterion (Isoelectric pI)      Anionic Form (High pH)
              H                               H                               H
              │                               │                               │
       H3N+ ─ C ─ COOH                 H3N+ ─ C ─ COO-                 H2N ─ C ─ COO-
              │                               │                               │
              R                               R                               R
          (Net: +1)                       (Net: 0)                        (Net: -1)
```

* **Nutritional Classification:**
  * **Essential Amino Acids (Cannot be synthesized de novo by human body; must be ingested):**
    * Mnemonic: **PVT TIM HaLL** (Phenylalanine, Valine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Isoleucine, Methionine, Histidine, Leucine, Lysine). *Note: Histidine and Arginine are semi-essential.*
  * **Non-Essential Amino Acids:** Synthesized via transamination pathways (Alanine, Asparagine, Aspartate, Cysteine, Glutamate, Glutamine, Glycine, Proline, Serine, Tyrosine).
* **Chemical Side-Chain Properties:**
  * *Aromatic:* Phenylalanine, Tyrosine, Tryptophan (absorb UV light strongly at $\lambda = 280\text{ nm}$).
  * *Sulfur-Containing:* **Cysteine** (forms covalent disulfide bridges $-\text{S}-\text{S}-$ stabilizing tertiary structure) and **Methionine** (universal initiator amino acid).
  * *Basic (Positively charged at pH 7):* Lysine, Arginine, Histidine.
  * *Acidic (Negatively charged at pH 7):* Aspartate, Glutamate.
  * *Imino Acid:* **Proline** (rigid pyrrolidine ring disrupts $\alpha$-helices, acts as a "helix breaker").

### 21.4.2 The Four Levels of Protein Structural Organization

```
[Primary Structure]   --> Linear sequence of amino acids (Covalent PEPTIDE BONDS).
        │
        ▼
[Secondary Structure] --> Local folding into Alpha-Helix & Beta-Sheet (HYDROGEN BONDS).
        │
        ▼
[Tertiary Structure]  --> Overall 3D globular architecture (Disulfide, Hydrophobic, Ionic).
        │
        ▼
[Quaternary Structure]--> Multimeric assembly of multiple polypeptide subunits (Hemoglobin).
```

1. **Primary ($1^\circ$) Structure:** Linear sequence of amino acids joined exclusively by covalent **Peptide Bonds** ($-\text{CO}-\text{NH}-$, possessing partial double-bond character due to resonance, preventing free rotation). Determines completely the higher-order folding. (Discovered by Frederick Sanger sequencing insulin, Nobel 1958).
2. **Secondary ($2^\circ$) Structure:** Localized spatial conformation of polypeptide backbone stabilized strictly by **Hydrogen Bonds** between the carbonyl oxygen ($-\text{C}=\text{O}$) of one peptide bond and the amide hydrogen ($-\text{N}-\text{H}$) of another:
   * **$\alpha$-Helix:** Right-handed spiral coil with $3.6$ amino acids per turn and a pitch of $0.54\text{ nm}$. Hydrogen bonds form between the $n$-th residue and the $(n+4)$-th residue (e.g., $\alpha$-Keratin in hair, nails, feathers).
   * **$\beta$-Pleated Sheet:** Polypeptide chains aligned side-by-side in parallel or anti-parallel orientations, stabilized by inter-chain hydrogen bonds (e.g., Fibroin in spider silk).
3. **Tertiary ($3^\circ$) Structure:** The overall three-dimensional folding of a single polypeptide chain into a compact globular or fibrous architecture, essential for biological activity (e.g., catalytic clefts of enzymes). Stabilized by:
   * Hydrophobic interactions (nonpolar side chains cluster in core).
   * Electrostatic salt bridges (ionic interactions between $-\text{NH}_3^+$ and $-\text{COO}^-$).
   * Hydrogen bonds.
   * Covalent **Disulfide Bonds** ($-\text{S}-\text{S}-$) between adjacent Cysteine residues.
4. **Quaternary ($4^\circ$) Structure:** Multi-subunit spatial assembly of two or more distinct polypeptide chains (protomers) stabilized by non-covalent forces:
   * **Adult Hemoglobin ($\text{HbA}$):** Tetramer composed of two identical $\alpha$-subunits ($141\text{ aa}$ each) and two identical $\beta$-subunits ($146\text{ aa}$ each) ($\alpha_2\beta_2$), exhibiting allosteric cooperativity in oxygen binding.

### 21.4.3 Protein Denaturation
Disruption of secondary, tertiary, and quaternary protein structures by physical (heat, radiation) or chemical (strong acids/bases, heavy metal ions $\text{Hg}^{2+}, \text{Pb}^{2+}$, urea, detergents) agents without cleaving the covalent peptide bonds.
* **Result:** Loss of biological/catalytic activity, loss of solubility, and precipitation/coagulation. (E.g., coagulation of egg white albumin upon boiling, curdling of milk by lactic acid).
* **Primary structure remains intact** during denaturation.

## 21.5 ENZYMES: MECHANISM, KINETICS & REGULATION

Enzymes are biocatalysts that accelerate chemical reaction rates by factors of $10^6$ to $10^{12}$ without undergoing permanent chemical alteration or altering the overall thermodynamic equilibrium ($\Delta G^\circ$) of the reaction. With the exception of catalytic RNAs (**Ribozymes**, e.g., 23S rRNA peptidyl transferase, RNase P), all enzymes are globular proteins.

```
                              THE ENZYME PROFILE
                              
            Apoenzyme (Inactive Protein Part)
                         +
            Cofactor (Non-Protein Chemical Helper)
                         ║
                         ▼
            HOLOENZYME (Fully Active Catalytic Complex)
```

### 21.5.1 Enzyme Composition
* **Apoenzyme:** The thermolabile, catalytically inactive proteinaceous component.
* **Cofactor:** The heat-stable, non-protein component required for catalytic competence:
  1. *Prosthetic Group:* Tightly and permanently bound organic moiety via covalent bonds (e.g., **Heme** in Catalase and Peroxidase).
  2. *Coenzyme:* Loosely and transiently bound organic molecule, functioning as a cosubstrate carrying chemical groups; usually derived from water-soluble B-vitamins (e.g., $\text{NAD}^+$ and $\text{NADP}^+$ contain **Niacin**; FAD contains **Riboflavin**; Coenzyme A contains **Pantothenic acid**; Thiamine pyrophosphate contains **Thiamine**).
  3. *Metal Ion Activators:* Inorganic metal ions that form coordination bonds with substrate and enzyme active-site residues (e.g., $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ in Carbonic Anhydrase and Alcohol Dehydrogenase; $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ in Hexokinase; $\text{Fe}^{2+}/\text{Fe}^{3+}$ in Cytochromes).

### 21.5.2 Catalytic Mechanism: Lowering Activation Energy
Enzymes do not alter the free energy change ($\Delta G$) or standard free energy ($\Delta G^\circ$) between reactants and products. Instead, they stabilize the transition state, dramatically **lowering the Activation Energy ($E_a$)** required to initiate the reaction.

```
       Energy
         ▲
         │           / \  <-- Activation Energy WITHOUT Enzyme (High Ea)
         │          /   \
         │         /  /\ \  <-- Activation Energy WITH Enzyme (Low Ea)
         │        /  /  \ \
         │  [S] ─┘  /    \ └──► [P]
         │         /      \
         │                 \
         └─────────────────────────► Reaction Coordinate
                 [Delta G remains strictly unchanged!]
```

* **Catalytic Models:**
  1. *Lock and Key Hypothesis (Emil Fischer, 1894):* The active site possesses a rigid, pre-formed complementary geometry to the substrate.
  2. *Induced Fit Hypothesis (Daniel Koshland, 1958):* The active site is structurally flexible; substrate binding induces a precise conformational transition aligning catalytic residues.

### 21.5.3 Enzyme Kinetics & The Michaelis-Menten Constant ($K_m$)
For an enzyme-catalyzed reaction:
$$E + S \underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}} ES \xrightarrow{k_2} E + P$$
The rate equation is defined by the **Michaelis-Menten Equation**:
$$V_0 = \frac{V_{max}[S]}{K_m + [S]}$$

```
       Velocity (V)
          ▲
     Vmax ┼ - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
          │                           . - - ' ' (Hyperbolic Curve)
  Vmax/2  ┼ - - - - - - - - - - - . '
          │                     │
          │                     │
          └─────────────────────┼─────────────────────────► [Substrate]
                                Km
```

* **Michaelis Constant ($K_m$):** The specific substrate concentration at which the initial reaction velocity reaches **half its maximum value** ($V_0 = \frac{1}{2}V_{max}$).
  * $K_m$ is an intrinsic constant for a given enzyme-substrate pair, independent of enzyme concentration.
  * **Significance:** $K_m$ is an **inverse measure of enzyme-substrate affinity**. A **low $K_m$** indicates high affinity (enzyme requires low substrate levels to saturate); a **high $K_m$** indicates low affinity.

### 21.5.4 Enzyme Inhibition Dynamics

| Inhibition Type | Mechanism & Active Site Interaction | Kinetic Effect on $V_{max}$ | Kinetic Effect on $K_m$ | Clinical / Classic Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Competitive** | Inhibitor chemically resembles substrate; binds **reversibly to the catalytic active site**, competing directly with substrate. | **Unchanged ($V_{max}$ constant)**; overcome by elevating $[S]$. | **Increases ($K_m$ higher)**; apparent substrate affinity decreases. | **Malonate** competes with Succinate for Succinate Dehydrogenase; **Statins** inhibit HMG-CoA Reductase; **Sulfa drugs** inhibit bacterial folate synthesis. |
| **Non-Competitive** | Inhibitor binds reversibly to a distinct **allosteric site** (away from active site) on both free enzyme ($E$) and $ES$ complex. | **Decreases ($V_{max}$ lower)**; maximum catalytic speed crippled. | **Unchanged ($K_m$ constant)**; substrate still binds with normal affinity. | Heavy metals ($\text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Hg}^{2+}, \text{Ag}^+$) binding sulfhydryl ($-\text{SH}$) groups; Cyanide inhibiting Cytochrome c Oxidase. |
| **Allosteric (Feedback)** | End-product of a multienzyme pathway binds allosterically to the first committed-step enzyme, inducing conformational inactivation. | Regulated dynamically; transitions between relaxed ($R$) and tense ($T$) states. | Often sigmoidal kinetics; shifts $K_{0.5}$. | ATP and Citrate inhibiting **Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)** in glycolysis; Isoleucine inhibiting Threonine Deaminase. |

## 21.6 NUCLEIC ACIDS: DNA, RNA & CHARGAFF'S RULES

Nucleic acids are unbranched informational heteropolymers composed of repeating monomeric units termed **Nucleotides**.

```
                         NUCLEOTIDE COMPOSITION
                         
                 [Nitrogenous Base] + [Pentose Sugar]
                               │
                               ▼  (N-Glycosidic Bond)
                          [NUCLEOSIDE]
                               │
                               ▼  + Phosphoric Acid (Ester Bond at C-5')
                          [NUCLEOTIDE]
```

### 21.6.1 Nitrogenous Bases & Pentose Sugars
* **Purines (Double-ringed, 9-membered heterocyclic):** **Adenine (A)** and **Guanine (G)**. Found in both DNA and RNA. Attached to C-1' of pentose via an **$N-9$ glycosidic bond**.
* **Pyrimidines (Single-ringed, 6-membered heterocyclic):** **Cytosine (C)** (in DNA & RNA), **Thymine (T)** (strictly in DNA; 5-methyluracil), and **Uracil (U)** (strictly in RNA). Attached to C-1' of pentose via an **$N-1$ glycosidic bond**.
* **Pentose Sugars:** $\beta$-D-Deoxyribose in DNA (lacks oxygen at C-2': $-\text{H}$); $\beta$-D-Ribose in RNA (contains $-\text{OH}$ group at C-2'). The $2'\text{-OH}$ group makes RNA chemically reactive and susceptible to alkaline hydrolysis, explaining why DNA evolved as the more chemically stable genetic repository.

### 21.6.2 The Watson-Crick B-DNA Model (1953)
Based on X-ray diffraction patterns generated by **Rosalind Franklin** and **Maurice Wilkins**, James Watson and Francis Crick elucidated the secondary structure of double-helical B-DNA:
1. **Antiparallel Strands:** Two polynucleotide chains run in opposite directions ($5' \to 3'$ and $3' \to 5'$). The backbone consists of alternating deoxyribose sugars and phosphate groups linked by **$3',5'$-phosphodiester bonds**.
2. **Complementary Base Pairing:** Nitrogenous bases project inwardly, oriented perpendicular to the helical axis:
   * Adenine pairs strictly with Thymine via **two hydrogen bonds** ($A = T$).
   * Guanine pairs strictly with Cytosine via **three hydrogen bonds** ($G \equiv C$).
   * Because $G \equiv C$ pairs have three hydrogen bonds, DNA with high GC content has a higher melting temperature ($T_m$).
3. **Helical Dimensions (B-DNA):**
   * Diameter of double helix: **$2.0\text{ nm}$ ($20\text{ \AA}$)**.
   * Pitch of helix (one complete $360^\circ$ turn): **$3.4\text{ nm}$ ($34\text{ \AA}$)**.
   * Number of base pairs per turn: **$10\text{ bp}$**.
   * Distance between adjacent base pairs: **$0.34\text{ nm}$ ($3.4\text{ \AA}$)**.
   * Helical handedness: **Right-handed helix** with alternating **Major Grooves** and **Minor Grooves**.

> [!LAW / PRINCIPLE]
> **Chargaff's Rules of DNA Equivalence (Erwin Chargaff, 1950)**
> Valid *strictly* for double-stranded DNA (dsDNA):
> 1. The total sum of purines equals the total sum of pyrimidines:
>    $$\text{Purines } (A + G) = \text{Pyrimidines } (T + C) \implies \frac{A + G}{T + C} = 1.0$$
> 2. The molar amount of Adenine equals Thymine ($A = T \implies \frac{A}{T} = 1.0$), and Guanine equals Cytosine ($G = C \implies \frac{G}{C} = 1.0$).
> 3. The base ratio $\frac{A + T}{G + C}$ is constant for a given species, but varies widely across species (dissimilarity ratio).
> *Note:* Chargaff's rules do NOT hold for single-stranded DNA (e.g., Bacteriophage $\phi \times 174$) or single-stranded RNA.

### 21.6.3 Structural Types of RNA
RNA is single-stranded, contains ribose sugar, and replaces Thymine with Uracil:
1. **Messenger RNA (mRNA, $\sim 5\%$ of total cellular RNA):** Carries genetic codons from nuclear DNA to ribosomes for translation. Undergoes post-transcriptional $5'$-capping ($7$-methylguanosine) and $3'$-polyadenylation (poly-A tail).
2. **Transfer RNA (tRNA / Soluble RNA, $\sim 10–15\%$):** Smallest RNA ($\sim 70–90\text{ nucleotides}$, $4\text{S}$).
   * *Secondary Structure:* **Cloverleaf model** (Holley, 1965) containing:
     * **Acceptor Stem:** Conserved un-paired $5'\text{-CCA-}3'$ sequence at $3'$-terminus where specific amino acids attach covalently via aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases.
     * **Anticodon Loop:** Contains a three-base triplet complementary to the mRNA codon.
     * **$T\psi C$ Loop:** Contains pseudouridine ($\psi$); binds ribosome 50S/60S subunit.
     * **DHU Loop:** Contains dihydrouridine; binds aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase.
   * *Tertiary Structure:* Compact **Inverted L-shape** (Kim & Klug).
3. **Ribosomal RNA (rRNA, $\sim 80\%$ of total cellular RNA):** Most abundant, stable, and structural RNA constituent of ribosomes. Catalyzes peptide bond formation (peptidyl transferase ribozyme).

## 21.7 VITAMINS & MINERAL BIOCHEMISTRY

Vitamins are organic trace micronutrients that cannot be synthesized in adequate amounts by humans and function primarily as coenzymes or precursors in metabolic pathways.

```
                                    VITAMINS
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                         ▼
  [Fat-Soluble: A, D, E, K]                                 [Water-Soluble: B-Complex & C]
```

## 21.8 COMPARATIVE MATRICES & EXAM TRAPS

### 21.8.1 Comparative Matrix: DNA vs. RNA

| Parameter | Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) | Ribonucleic Acid (RNA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pentose Sugar** | $\beta$-D-2-Deoxyribose (lacks $2'\text{-OH}$). | $\beta$-D-Ribose (has reactive $2'\text{-OH}$). |
| **Nitrogenous Bases** | Adenine, Guanine, Cytosine, **Thymine**. | Adenine, Guanine, Cytosine, **Uracil**. |
| **Strandedness** | Double-stranded double helix (in all eukaryotes). | Single-stranded (folds into secondary stem-loops). |
| **Chemical Stability** | Highly stable; resistant to alkaline hydrolysis. | Chemically labile and easily degraded by bases. |
| **Chargaff’s Rule** | Strictly obeyed ($A = T$, $G = C$). | Does not apply (except transient dsRNA). |
| **Biological Role** | Permanent genetic storage repository. | Gene expression messenger, adaptor, and catalyst. |

> [!EXAM TRAP & WARNING]
> 1. **The "Reducing Sugar" Ambiguity:**
>    * All monosaccharides (both aldoses and ketoses) are reducing sugars! Fructose is a ketone, yet reduces Benedict's reagent because its $\alpha$-hydroxy ketone group tautomerizes into an aldose via **enolization** in alkaline solutions.
>    * Disaccharides: Maltose and Lactose are reducing sugars. **Sucrose is strictly NON-REDUCING** because its glycosidic bond locks the anomeric carbons of both glucose and fructose.
> 2. **Chargaff Calculation Pitfall:**
>    * If a double-stranded DNA sample contains $20\%$ Cytosine, calculate Adenine percentage:
>      $$\text{Cytosine } (C) = 20\% \implies \text{Guanine } (G) = 20\%$$
>      $$\text{Total } G + C = 40\% \implies \text{Remaining } A + T = 100\% - 40\% = 60\%$$
>      $$\text{Since } A = T \implies \text{Adenine } (A) = \frac{60\%}{2} = 30\%$$
>    * *Exam Trap:* If the problem states the genome is **single-stranded RNA or ssDNA**, Chargaff's rules CANNOT be applied!
> 3. **The Enzyme Inhibition Coordinate Trap:**
>    * In competitive inhibition, the inhibitor can be displaced by flooding with excess substrate $\implies V_{max}$ is **completely restored / unchanged**, while $K_m$ **increases**.
>    * In non-competitive inhibition, the inhibitor binds an allosteric site and poisons the catalytic turnover $\implies V_{max}$ **decreases permanently**, but remaining uninhibited active sites bind substrate normally $\implies K_m$ is **unchanged**.

## 21.9 HIGH-YIELD PRACTICE QUESTIONS

### Question 1 (UPSC CSE Standard)
Consider the following statements regarding biomolecules and enzyme actions:
1. Malonate competitively inhibits succinate dehydrogenase because of its structural similarity to succinate.
2. In competitive inhibition, the apparent Michaelis constant ($K_m$) decreases while the maximum velocity ($V_{max}$) remains unaltered.
3. Cellulose gives a deep blue color when treated with iodine solution due to its helical amylose-like coils.
4. All enzymes are chemically globular proteins with no exceptions.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
A) 1 only  
B) 1 and 2 only  
C) 2, 3 and 4 only  
D) 1 and 4 only  

**Analytical Answer with Epistemic Breakdown:**
* Statement 1 is **CORRECT**: Malonate is a classic competitive inhibitor of the citric acid cycle enzyme succinate dehydrogenase because of close structural similarity to the substrate succinate.
* Statement 2 is **INCORRECT**: In competitive inhibition, $K_m$ **increases** (indicating reduced apparent affinity), while $V_{max}$ remains unchanged.
* Statement 3 is **INCORRECT**: Cellulose does not possess helical coils; its unbranched $\beta(1 \to 4)$ chains form rigid linear microfibrils that cannot entrap iodine. Starch gives the blue color.
* Statement 4 is **INCORRECT**: Not all enzymes are proteins; **ribozymes** (such as 23S rRNA in the bacterial ribosome and RNase P) are catalytic RNA molecules.
* **Correct Option:** **A) 1 only**.

### Question 2 (Competitive State PSC Standard)
Which vitamin deficiency is clinically associated with the classic triad of Dermatitis, Diarrhea, and Dementia?
A) Thiamine ($\text{B}_1$)  
B) Riboflavin ($\text{B}_2$)  
C) Niacin ($\text{B}_3$)  
D) Pyridoxine ($\text{B}_6$)  

**Analytical Answer:**
* Niacin ($\text{B}_3$) deficiency causes **Pellagra**, which classically manifests as the "3 Ds": photosensitive symmetrical pigmented dermatitis, gastrointestinal diarrhea, and neurological dementia (progressing to death if untreated).
* **Correct Option:** **C) Niacin ($\text{B}_3$)**.

### Question 3 (Advanced Epistemic Standard)
A double-stranded DNA fragment has $120$ thymine bases and $120$ guanine bases. What is the total number of nucleotides and the total number of hydrogen bonds in this DNA molecule?
A) 240 nucleotides; 240 hydrogen bonds  
B) 480 nucleotides; 600 hydrogen bonds  
C) 480 nucleotides; 480 hydrogen bonds  
D) 240 nucleotides; 600 hydrogen bonds  

**Analytical Answer:**
* According to Chargaff's rules for double-stranded DNA:
  * Thymine ($T$) = $120 \implies \text{Adenine } (A) = 120$.
  * Guanine ($G$) = $120 \implies \text{Cytosine } (C) = 120$.
  * Total nucleotides = $A + T + G + C = 120 + 120 + 120 + 120 = \mathbf{480\text{ nucleotides}}$.
* Hydrogen bonds:
  * Each $A = T$ base pair contributes 2 hydrogen bonds: $120 \times 2 = 240\text{ H-bonds}$.
  * Each $G \equiv C$ base pair contributes 3 hydrogen bonds: $120 \times 3 = 360\text{ H-bonds}$.
  * Total hydrogen bonds = $240 + 360 = \mathbf{600\text{ hydrogen bonds}}$.
* **Correct Option:** **B) 480 nucleotides; 600 hydrogen bonds**.
