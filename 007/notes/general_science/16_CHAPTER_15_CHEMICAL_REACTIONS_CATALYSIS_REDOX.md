## 15.1 Anatomy of Chemical Reactions & The Five Fundamental Reaction Types

A **Chemical Reaction** is a process in which one or more substances (**Reactants**) undergo bond breaking and bond making to transform into one or more new substances (**Products**) with entirely distinct chemical identities.
* **Law of Conservation of Mass (Antoine Lavoisier, 1789)**: Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. The total mass of products must identically equal the total mass of reactants:
  $$\sum \text{Mass of Reactants} = \sum \text{Mass of Products}$$
  *(This is why every chemical equation MUST be balanced with stoichiometric coefficients!).*

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        CHEMICAL REACTION TAXONOMY                      │
├─────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┬───────────────────────┤
│ Reaction Type       │ General Equation         │ High-Yield Real World │
│                     │                          │ Example               │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ 1. Combination      │ A + B ──► AB             │ Quicklime + Water     │
│    (Synthesis)      │ (Two or more form ONE)   │ CaO + H₂O ──► Ca(OH)₂ │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ 2. Decomposition    │ AB ──► A + B             │ Thermal, Electrolytic,│
│                     │ (ONE splits into two+)   │ Photolytic            │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ 3. Displacement     │ A + BC ──► AC + B        │ Iron nail in blue     │
│    (Single)         │ (More reactive kicks out)│ CuSO₄ solution        │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ 4. Double           │ AB + CD ──► AD + CB      │ Precipitation /       │
│    Displacement     │ (Mutual ion exchange)    │ Neutralization        │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ 5. Redox            │ Oxidation + Reduction    │ Rusting, Respiration, │
│    (Electron Trans.)│ occur SIMULTANEOUSLY     │ Combustion            │
└─────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

### Detailed Mechanics of the Reaction Types

#### 1. Combination Reactions (Exothermic Heat Release)
Two or more elements or compounds combine to synthesize a single new compound:
* **Slaking of Lime**:
  $$\text{CaO (Quicklime / Calcium Oxide)} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Ca(OH)}_2\text{ (Slaked Lime / Calcium Hydroxide)}} + \text{Enormous Heat!}$$
* **White-Washing Chemistry of Walls**:
  When slaked lime solution ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$) is painted onto household walls, it reacts slowly with atmospheric Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) over 2 to 3 days to form a thin, hard, shiny layer of **Calcium Carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$)**, giving walls a brilliant white sheen:
  $$\text{Ca(OH)}_2\text{ (aq)} + \text{CO}_2\text{ (g)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{CaCO}_3\text{ (s, Limestone / Marble)}} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$

#### 2. Decomposition Reactions (Endothermic Energy Absorption)
A single reactant breaks down to yield simpler products, requiring energy input in one of three forms:
1. **Thermal Decomposition (Heat Input)**:
   - Green Vitriol (Ferrous Sulfate): Heating green crystals of $\text{FeSO}_4 \cdot 7\text{H}_2\text{O}$ drives off crystal water, turning white, then decomposes into reddish-brown Ferric oxide and pungent suffocating gases:
     $$2\text{FeSO}_4\text{ (s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\text{ (s)}} + \text{SO}_2\text{ (g)} + \text{SO}_3\text{ (g)}$$
   - Limestone to Quicklime:
     $$\text{CaCO}_3\text{ (s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathbf{\text{CaO (s)}} + \text{CO}_2\text{ (g)} \quad (\text{Industrial manufacture of cement})$$
2. **Electrolytic Decomposition (Electric Current Input - Electrolysis)**:
   - Electrolysis of Acidified Water:
     $$2\text{H}_2\text{O (l)} \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} \mathbf{2\text{H}_2\text{ (g) [at Cathode (-)]} + \text{O}_2\text{ (g) [at Anode (+)]}}$$
     *Crucial Stoichiometric Ratio*: Volume of Hydrogen gas collected at the cathode is **exactly TWICE (2:1)** the volume of Oxygen collected at the anode!
3. **Photolytic Decomposition (Light Energy Input)**:
   - Black & White Photography Chemistry: White Silver Chloride ($AgCl$) or pale-yellow Silver Bromide ($AgBr$) turns dark gray when exposed to sunlight due to decomposition into metallic silver:
     $$2\text{AgCl (s)} \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} \mathbf{2\text{Ag (s, Gray)}} + \text{Cl}_2\text{ (g)}$$
     $$2\text{AgBr (s)} \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} \mathbf{2\text{Ag (s)}} + \text{Br}_2\text{ (g)} \quad (\text{Used in black-and-white photographic film})$$

#### 3. Displacement Reactions & The Reactivity Series
A more chemically reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its aqueous salt solution:

$$\mathbf{K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au}$$

```
     High Reactivity (Violent with cold water) ──► K, Na, Ca
     Moderate Reactivity (React with steam/acids)──► Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb
     Hydrogen Reference Boundary [ H ]
     Noble Low Reactivity (Unreactive with acids) ─► Cu, Hg, Ag, Au
```

* **The Iron Nail in Copper Sulfate Experiment**:  
  When a clean iron nail is dipped into a blue solution of Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$):
  $$\text{Fe (s)} + \text{CuSO}_4\text{ (aq, Blue)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{FeSO}_4\text{ (aq, Pale Green)}} + \mathbf{\text{Cu (s, Reddish-brown deposit on nail)}}$$
  *Why*: Iron is placed higher in the reactivity series than Copper ($\text{Fe} > \text{Cu}$); iron displaces copper. If a copper strip is dipped into $\text{FeSO}_4$, **NO reaction occurs**!

#### 4. Double Displacement Reactions & Precipitation
Mutual exchange of ions between two ionic compounds in aqueous solution:
* **Formation of Insoluble Precipitates**:
  $$\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (aq)} + \text{BaCl}_2\text{ (aq)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{BaSO}_4\text{ (s, Dense White Insoluble Precipitate)}} + 2\text{NaCl (aq)}$$
  *(Barium Sulfate is insoluble and opaque to X-rays, serving as the "Barium Meal" imaging contrast agent for human digestive tract radiography!).*
* **Lead Nitrate and Potassium Iodide**:
  $$\text{Pb(NO}_3)_2\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{KI (aq)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{PbI}_2\text{ (s, Brilliant Yellow Precipitate)}} + 2\text{KNO}_3\text{ (aq)}$$

## 15.2 Oxidation, Reduction & Redox Systems

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        REDOX DUALITY (OIL RIG)                         │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│ OXIDATION (OIL: Oxidation Is Loss)│ REDUCTION (RIG: Reduction Is Gain) │
├───────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Addition of Oxygen             │ 1. Removal of Oxygen               │
│ 2. Removal of Hydrogen            │ 2. Addition of Hydrogen            │
│ 3. LOSS of Electrons (e⁻)         │ 3. GAIN of Electrons (e⁻)          │
│ 4. INCREASE in Oxidation Number   │ 4. DECREASE in Oxidation Number    │
│ The substance oxidized is the     │ The substance reduced is the       │
│ REDUCING AGENT (Reductant)        │ OXIDIZING AGENT (Oxidant)          │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Comprehensive Redox Analysis
In any real chemical system, **oxidation and reduction can NEVER occur in isolation**; they occur simultaneously as a **Redox Reaction**:

$$\text{CuO} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

* $\text{CuO}$ loses Oxygen $\longrightarrow$ **Reduced to $\text{Cu}$** ($\text{CuO}$ is the **Oxidizing Agent**).
* $\text{H}_2$ gains Oxygen $\longrightarrow$ **Oxidized to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$** ($\text{H}_2$ is the **Reducing Agent**).

### Rules for Assigning Oxidation Numbers
1. Any element in its uncombined elemental state has an oxidation number of **$0$** ($O_2, N_2, H_2, Fe, Cu, S_8, P_4 = 0$).
2. Fluorine in all compounds is always **$-1$** (most electronegative element).
3. Oxygen in compounds is usually **$-2$** (Exceptions: in peroxides like $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, Oxygen is **$-1$**; in superoxides like $\text{KO}_2$, Oxygen is **$-\frac{1}{2}$**; in $OF_2$, Oxygen is **$+2$**).
4. Hydrogen is **$+1$** when bonded to non-metals ($HCl, H_2O$); but **$-1$** in metallic hydrides ($NaH, CaH_2$).
5. The sum of oxidation numbers in a neutral molecule is always **$0$**; in a polyatomic ion, it equals the net ionic charge.

## 15.3 Everyday Redox Phenomena: Corrosion & Rancidity

### 1. Corrosion of Metals & The Chemistry of Rusting
Corrosion is the slow, electrochemically driven oxidation of metals by atmospheric moisture and gases ($\text{O}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{SO}_2$):

```
       Anode (Fe oxidation):       Fe ──► Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻
       Cathode (O₂ reduction):     O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ ──► 2H₂O
       Hydrated Rust Formation:    4Fe²⁺ + O₂ + (4+2x)H₂O ──► 2Fe₂O₃ · xH₂O (Rust)
```

* **Chemical Formula of Rust**: **Hydrated Ferric Oxide ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, a flaky, reddish-brown non-adherent powder.
* **Two Mandatory Conditions for Rusting**:
  1. Presence of **Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)**.
  2. Presence of **Water / Moisture ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**.  
  *(Iron will NEVER rust in boiled, distilled water sealed under oil [zero oxygen], nor in dry air with silica gel [zero moisture]!).*
* **Corrosion of Other Common Metals**:
  - **Copper**: Reacts with moist atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ to form a **green coating of Basic Copper Carbonate ($\text{CuCO}_3 \cdot \text{Cu(OH)}_2$)** (seen on brass vessels, bronze statues, and the Statue of Liberty).
  - **Silver**: Reacts with traces of atmospheric Hydrogen Sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) gas to form a **tarnished black coating of Silver Sulfide ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$)**.
  - **Aluminum**: Forms a continuous, microscopically thin, airtight self-protective film of **Aluminum Oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$)** that completely passivates the metal against further corrosion (**Anodizing process**).

#### Industrial Methods of Rust Prevention
1. **Galvanization**: Coating steel or iron with a thin protective layer of **metallic Zinc ($Zn$)**.
   - *The Sacrificial Protection Principle*: Zinc has a higher oxidation potential than iron (more reactive). Even if the galvanized zinc layer is scratched and steel is exposed to air, zinc acts as a **sacrificial anode**, corroding preferentially to protect the underlying iron from rusting!
2. **Tin Plating**: Coating iron food cans with non-toxic tin ($Sn$).
3. **Alloying**: Mixing iron with Chromium and Nickel to manufacture **Stainless Steel** ($18\% Cr, 8\% Ni, Fe$), which forms a self-healing invisible chromium oxide passivation layer.

---

### 2. Rancidity: The Oxidation of Fats & Oils
When foods containing fats and edible oils are left exposed to atmospheric oxygen, unsaturated fatty acids undergo auto-oxidation, breaking down into volatile short-chain aldehydes and carboxylic acids with a foul, repulsive smell and rancid taste:
* **Commercial Prevention Strategies**:
  - **Flushing Food Packets with Nitrogen Gas**: Potato chip bags are puffed up not to deceive consumers, but to displace atmospheric oxygen with chemically inert **Nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$)**, preventing oxidative rancidity for months.
  - **Antioxidants**: Synthetic chemical preservatives added to food that oxidize preferentially, protecting lipids: **BHA** (Butylated Hydroxyanisole) and **BHT** (Butylated Hydroxytoluene), or natural Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid) and Vitamin E (Tocopherol).
  - Vacuum packaging and airtight refrigeration.

## 15.4 Catalysis: Acceleration Without Consumption

A **Catalyst** is a substance that alters (usually accelerates) the rate of a chemical reaction without undergoing any permanent chemical change or mass consumption itself.

```
       Energy
         ▲
         │           /‾‾\  Uncatalyzed Reaction (High Activation Energy E_a)
         │          /    \
         │         /  /\  \
         │        /  /  \  \  CATALYZED REACTION (LOW ACTIVATION ENERGY E_a')
         │       /  /    \  \
         │  ────●──/──────\──● Reactants ──► Products (ΔH remains strictly UNCHANGED!)
         └──────────────────────────────────────────► Reaction Coordinate
```

### The Mechanism of Catalytic Action
> A catalyst accelerates a reaction solely by providing an **alternative reaction pathway with a significantly LOWER Activation Energy ($E_a$)**.

* **What a Catalyst CANNOT Do**:
  1. It CANNOT alter the overall thermodynamic enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) of the reaction.
  2. It CANNOT shift the equilibrium constant ($K_{\text{eq}}$) of a reversible reaction; it merely accelerates the speed at which equilibrium is reached by speeding up forward and reverse reactions equally.
  3. It CANNOT initiate a thermodynamically non-spontaneous reaction ($\Delta G > 0$).

### Classification of Catalysts & High-Yield Industrial Examples
* **Positive Catalyst**: Accelerates reaction rate (e.g., $MnO_2$ in decomposition of $KClO_3$).
* **Negative Catalyst (Inhibitor)**: Retards reaction rate (e.g., Phosphoric acid retarding decomposition of $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$).
* **Catalytic Promoters vs. Poisons**:
  - **Promoter**: Enhances the activity of a catalyst (e.g., Molybdenum $Mo$ acts as a promoter for Iron $Fe$ catalyst in the Haber process).
  - **Poison**: Destroys catalytic activity (e.g., Arsenic or Carbon Monoxide poisoning platinum catalysts).
* **Industrial Catalytic Processes**:
  1. **Haber Process (Ammonia Synthesis)**: $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$  
     Catalyst: **Finely divided Iron ($Fe$)** with Molybdenum ($Mo$) promoter.
  2. **Contact Process (Sulfuric Acid $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$)**: $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3$  
     Catalyst: **Vanadium Pentoxide ($\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$)** (replaces expensive Platinum).
  3. **Hydrogenation of Vegetable Oils (Vanaspati Ghee)**:  
     Liquid vegetable oil $+ \text{H}_2 \longrightarrow$ Solid vegetable ghee  
     Catalyst: **Finely divided Nickel ($Ni$)** at $200^\circ\text{C}$ (or Platinum/Palladium).
  4. **Ostwald Process (Nitric Acid $\text{HNO}_3$)**: Oxidation of ammonia to nitric oxide.  
     Catalyst: **Platinum-Rhodium gauze**.

### Enzymes: Biological Catalysts
Enzymes are complex globular protein molecules synthesized by living cells that catalyze biochemical metabolic reactions with phenomenal specificity under mild physiological conditions ($37^\circ\text{C}$, $\text{pH} \approx 7.4$):
* **Lock-and-Key Mechanism (Emil Fischer)**: Highly specific substrate binding.
* **Key Human Digestive Enzymes**:
  - **Ptyalin / Salivary Amylase**: Starch $\longrightarrow$ Maltose.
  - **Pepsin** (Stomach, requires acidic $HCl$ pH 1.5): Proteins $\longrightarrow$ Peptones/Proteoses.
  - **Trypsin** (Pancreas, requires alkaline pH 8): Proteins $\longrightarrow$ Peptides.
  - **Lipase**: Emulsified Fats $\longrightarrow$ Fatty acids + Glycerol.
  - **Zymase** (in Yeast): Glucose $\longrightarrow$ Ethanol + $\text{CO}_2$ (Alcoholic Fermentation).

## 15.5 Stoichiometry & The Mole Concept

### The Mole: The Chemist's Counting Unit
A **Mole** is the SI base unit representing the amount of substance containing exactly the same number of elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions, electrons) as there are atoms in exactly $12\text{ grams}$ of pure Carbon-12 ($^{12}C$):

$$\mathbf{1\text{ Mole} = N_A = 6.02214076 \times 10^{23}\text{ Entities (Avogadro Constant)}}$$

```
                                  [ 1 MOLE OF ANY SUBSTANCE ]
                                              │
              ┌───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
              ▼                               ▼                               ▼
     6.022 × 10²³ Particles        Gram Atomic / Molar Mass       22.4 Litres of Any Gas
  (Atoms, Molecules, or Ions)      (Mass of 1 mole in grams)      at Standard Temp & Press.
   Ex: 6.022 × 10²³ H₂O molecules   Ex: H₂O = 18 g; CO₂ = 44 g    (STP: 0°C, 1 atm pressure)
```

### High-Yield Stoichiometric Formulas

$$\mathbf{\text{Number of Moles } (n) = \frac{\text{Given Mass } (m)}{\text{Molar Mass } (M)} = \frac{\text{Number of Particles } (N)}{N_A} = \frac{\text{Volume of Gas at STP in Litres}}{22.4\text{ L}}}$$

> [!TIP]
> **Classic Exam Numerical Check**:  
> *Problem*: How many molecules are present in $36\text{ grams}$ of pure water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)?  
> Molar mass of $\text{H}_2\text{O} = (2 \times 1) + 16 = 18\text{ g/mol}$.
> $$n = \frac{36\text{ g}}{18\text{ g/mol}} = 2\text{ Moles}$$
> $$\text{Total Molecules} = 2 \times N_A = 2 \times (6.022 \times 10^{23}) = \mathbf{1.2044 \times 10^{24}\text{ Molecules}}$$

## 15.6 Master Chapter Distinction Matrix

| Feature | Physical Change | Chemical Reaction (Combination/Decomp) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **New Substance Formed?**| **Strictly NO** (Same molecular identity). | **YES** (Entirely new chemical formula & properties). |
| **Atomic Bonds** | Intermolecular physical bonds break/form. | Intra-molecular chemical covalent/ionic bonds break/form. |
| **Reversibility** | Easily reversible (freeze/melt water). | Irreversible by simple physical means. |
| **Mass Proportions** | Any mixing ratio (salt + sand). | Strictly obeys **Law of Constant Proportions**. |

## 15.7 High-Yield Diagnostic Examination Traps

1. **Electrolysis Gas Volume Ratio Trap**:
   - *Trap*: "During electrolysis of water, Oxygen collected is twice the volume of Hydrogen."
   - *Correction*: **Hydrogen is TWICE the volume of Oxygen!** Water is $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($2:1$ ratio). Cathode ($-$) collects **$2\text{ volumes of } H_2$**; Anode ($+$) collects **$1\text{ volume of } O_2$**.
2. **Zinc Galvanization Scratching Trap**:
   - *Trap*: "If galvanized iron is scratched, the exposed iron rusts immediately."
   - *Correction*: **False!** Zinc has a higher oxidation potential than iron and acts as a **sacrificial anode**, continuing to protect the iron even when scratched.
3. **Catalyst and Equilibrium Fallacy**:
   - *Trap*: "Adding a catalyst shifts chemical equilibrium to produce more product."
   - *Correction*: A catalyst **CANNOT alter the equilibrium yield or constant ($K_{\text{eq}}$)**; it merely accelerates forward and reverse reaction rates equally, reaching the same equilibrium faster!
4. **Rusting Weight Change Trap**:
   - *Trap*: "When an iron nail rusts completely, its weight decreases."
   - *Correction*: **Its weight INCREASES!** Rust is hydrated iron oxide ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$), which incorporates the additional mass of atmospheric oxygen and water molecules.
