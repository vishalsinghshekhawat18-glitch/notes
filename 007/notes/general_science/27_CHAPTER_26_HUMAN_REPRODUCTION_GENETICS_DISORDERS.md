## 26.1 HUMAN REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM & EMBRYOLOGY

Human beings are viviparous, sexually reproducing organisms exhibiting marked sexual dimorphism.

```
                           MALE DUCTAL CONTINUUM
                           
  Seminiferous Tubules (Spermatogenesis: Sertoli + Leydig Cells)
            │
            ▼
  Rete Testis ──► Vasa Efferentia ──► Epididymis (Caput -> Corpus -> Cauda: Maturation/Storage)
                                            │
                                            ▼
  Urethra ◄── Ejaculatory Duct ◄── Vas Deferens (Ascends via Inguinal Canal)
     │
     └── Receives Secretions: Seminal Vesicles (60%) + Prostate (30%) + Cowper's (Lubrication)
```

### 26.1.1 The Male Reproductive Apparatus
* **Scrotum & Testes:** The testes reside outside the abdominal cavity within the pouch-like **Scrotum**, maintaining an intra-scrotal temperature **$2.0 - 2.5^\circ\text{C}$ lower** than normal core body temperature—an absolute physiological prerequisite for viable **Spermatogenesis**. Failure of the testes to descend during fetal development is termed **Cryptorchidism** (induces sterility and elevated testicular malignancy risk).
* **Seminiferous Tubule Histology:**
  1. *Male Germ Cells (Spermatogonia):* Undergo meiotic divisions to yield mature spermatozoa.
  2. *Sertoli Cells (Sustentacular / "Nurse" Cells):* Provide structural and nutritional support to developing spermatids; form the **Blood-Testis Barrier** via tight junctions, shielding foreign haploid germ cells from autoimmune destruction; secrete **Inhibin** (suppresses pituitary FSH release) and **Androgen Binding Protein (ABP)**.
  3. *Leydig (Interstitial) Cells:* Located in the connective tissue spaces outside seminiferous tubules; stimulated by **Luteinizing Hormone (LH / ICSH)** to synthesize and secrete androgenic steroid hormones, primarily **Testosterone**.
* **Accessory Sex Glands:**
  * **Seminal Vesicles (Paired, $\sim 60\%$ of semen volume):** Alkaline fluid rich in **Fructose** (exclusive metabolic fuel for sperm motility; forensic marker for sexual assault), prostaglandins, and clotting enzymes.
  * **Prostate Gland (Single, $\sim 30\%$ of semen volume):** Milky, slightly acidic fluid containing citric acid, acid phosphatase, and **Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA)** (fibrinolysin that liquefies coagulated semen). Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH) causes urinary outflow obstruction in aging males.
  * **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) Glands (Paired):** Secrete clear, alkaline, lubricating mucus that neutralizes residual acidic urine in the penile urethra prior to ejaculation.

### 26.1.2 The Female Reproductive Apparatus & The Ovarian Cycle
* **Ovaries & Fallopian Tubes (Oviducts):**
  * The oviduct consists of the **Infundibulum** with finger-like terminal **Fimbriae** (which sweep the ovulated secondary oocyte into the lumen), the dilated **Ampulla**, and the narrow **Isthmus**.
  * **Site of Fertilization:** Occurs specifically at the **Ampullary-Isthmic Junction** (or Ampulla) of the Fallopian tube.
* **The Menstrual Cycle (Average Duration: $28\text{ Days}$):**

```
               THE 28-DAY HUMAN MENSTRUAL CYCLE
               
  Phase 1: MENSTRUAL PHASE (Days 1 - 5)
  - Progesterone & Estrogen levels plunge
  - Endometrial functionalis layer sheds (50 - 100 mL blood & tissue)
               │
               ▼
  Phase 2: FOLLICULAR / PROLIFERATIVE PHASE (Days 6 - 13)
  - Pituitary FSH stimulates primary follicle -> Graafian follicle
  - Growing follicle secretes ESTROGEN -> Endometrium proliferates and thickens
               │
               ▼
  Phase 3: OVULATORY PHASE (Day 14)
  - Extreme positive feedback: ESTROGEN PEAK triggers profound **LH SURGE**
  - Rupture of Graafian follicle -> Release of SECONDARY OOCYTE into pelvic cavity
               │
               ▼
  Phase 4: LUTEAL / SECRETORY PHASE (Days 15 - 28)
  - Ruptured follicle converts to endocrine **CORPUS LUTEUM**
  - Massive secretion of **PROGESTERONE** (and moderate estrogen)
  - Endometrium becomes highly vascular, secretory, and coiled (ready for implantation)
  - If no fertilization: Corpus luteum degenerates into fibrous scar **Corpus Albicans**;
    progesterone drops, precipitating next menstruation.
```

### 26.1.3 Gametogenesis: Spermatogenesis vs. Oogenesis

```
          SPERMATOGENESIS                                 OOGENESIS
          (Continuous from Puberty)                       (Initiated during Embryonic Fetal Life)
          
   Spermatogonium (2n)                             Oogonium (2n)
          │ Mitosis                                       │ Mitosis (in fetal ovary; no new oogonia
          ▼                                               ▼          formed after birth!)
   Primary Spermatocyte (2n)                       Primary Oocyte (2n)
          │                                               │ Arrested in PROPHASE I (Diplotene)
          │ Meiosis I                                     │ until Puberty
          ▼                                               ▼ Meiosis I (Completed just prior to ovulation)
   2 Secondary Spermatocytes (n)                   Secondary Oocyte (n)  +  First Polar Body (n)
          │                                               │ Arrested in METAPHASE II
          │ Meiosis II                                    │ (Completed ONLY upon sperm penetration)
          ▼                                               ▼ Meiosis II
   4 Equal Spermatids (n)                          OOTID / OVUM (n)      +  Second Polar Body (n)
          │ Spermiogenesis                                (Retains >95% cytoplasm)
          ▼
   4 Functional SPERMATOZOA (n)
```

### 26.1.4 Fertilization, Implantation & Placentation
* **Capacitation:** Physiological conditioning of spermatozoa inside the female reproductive tract ($\sim 7\text{ hours}$), stripping inhibitory glycoprotein coats and elevating membrane $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ permeability to permit hyperactivation and the acrosome reaction.
* **Fertilization Cascade:**
  1. *Acrosomal Reaction:* Spermatozoa bind to the **Zona Pellucida** (via ZP3 receptors); release hydrolytic **Hyaluronidase** and **Acrosin**, digesting a pathway through the corona radiata and zona pellucida.
  2. *Block to Polyspermy:* Entry of the first sperm triggers the **Cortical Reaction**—exocytosis of cortical granules hardens the zona pellucida into an impenetrable fertilization envelope, permanently blocking additional sperm entry.
  3. Sperm entry stimulates the secondary oocyte to promptly **complete Meiosis II**, extruding the Second Polar Body and forming the haploid female pronucleus, which fuses with the male pronucleus (**Syngamy**), restoring diploidy ($2n = 46$).
* **Cleavage & Implantation:** The zygote undergoes rapid mitotic cleavages traveling down the oviduct, transforming into a solid ball of $16\text{ cells}$ (**Morula**), which cavitates into a hollow **Blastocyst**:
  * *Trophoblast (Outer layer):* Invades the maternal endometrium on **Day $6 - 7$ post-fertilization (Implantation)**; forms the fetal contribution to the placenta.
  * *Inner Cell Mass (Embryoblast):* Pluripotent stem cells differentiating into the three primary germ layers (**Ectoderm, Mesoderm, Endoderm**) via **Gastrulation**.
* **Endocrine Functions of the Placenta:** Secretes **Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**—which rescues the maternal corpus luteum from degeneration during the first trimester to maintain progesterone secretion (basis of all **urinary pregnancy test strips**); **Human Placental Lactogen (hPL)**; Estrogens; Progesterone; and **Relaxin** (softens the pubic symphysis for parturition).

## 26.2 MENDELIAN GENETICS & CHROMOSOMAL THEORY

Gregor Johann Mendel (1866) elucidated the fundamental laws of particulate genetic inheritance through controlled hybridizations of the garden pea (*Pisum sativum*), choosing 7 distinct pairs of contrasting traits.

```
                         MENDELIAN CROSS RATIOS
                         
  [Monohybrid Cross]  ──► F2 Phenotypic:  3 : 1      (Dominant : Recessive)
                      ──► F2 Genotypic:   1 : 2 : 1  (Pure Dom : Heterozygote : Pure Rec)
                      
  [Dihybrid Cross]    ──► F2 Phenotypic:  9 : 3 : 3 : 1
                      ──► F2 Genotypic:   1:2:2:4:1:2:1:2:1
                      
  [Monohybrid Test Cross] ──► Heterozygote (Tt) x Recessive (tt) ──► 1 : 1
  [Dihybrid Test Cross]   ──► TtRr x ttrr ──► 1 : 1 : 1 : 1
```

### 26.2.1 The Classic Mendelian Laws
1. **Law of Dominance:** In a monohybrid cross between two pure-breeding parents displaying contrasting alleles, only one parental character (**Dominant allele**) manifests in the $F_1$ generation, while the alternative character (**Recessive allele**) remains unexpressed.
2. **Law of Segregation (Purity of Gametes):** Alleles of a gene do not blend or contaminate each other; during meiosis and gametogenesis, the two alleles segregate from each other such that each gamete receives strictly one allele with equal probability ($50\%$). **Universally valid with zero exceptions in all sexually reproducing diploid organisms.**
3. **Law of Independent Assortment:** When two pairs of traits are combined in a dihybrid hybrid, the segregation of alleles of one gene pair is completely independent of the segregation of alleles of the other gene pair during gamete formation.
   * *Limitation / Exception:* Valid **only for genes located on different non-homologous chromosomes** or genes situated far apart on the same chromosome; invalidated by **Genetic Linkage**.

### 26.2.2 Non-Mendelian Extensions & Exceptions
* **Incomplete Dominance (Carl Correns, 1900):** Neither allele is completely dominant; the heterozygous phenotype is an intermediate blend between both homozygous phenotypes:
  * E.g., Flower color in Snapdragon (*Antirrhinum majus*) and Four O'Clock plant (*Mirabilis jalapa*):
    $$\text{Red } (RR) \times \text{White } (rr) \longrightarrow F_1 \text{ All Pink } (Rr)$$
    $$F_2 \text{ Ratio: Phenotypic } = \mathbf{1\text{ Red } : 2\text{ Pink } : 1\text{ White}} \equiv \text{Genotypic } = \mathbf{1\text{ }RR : 2\text{ }Rr : 1\text{ }rr}$$
* **Codominance:** Both alleles express themselves fully and simultaneously in the heterozygote without phenotypic blending:
  * E.g., **ABO Blood Groups** in humans: Alleles $I^A$ and $I^B$ both produce their respective surface antigens; heterozygote $I^A I^B$ expresses **both Antigen A and Antigen B** simultaneously (Blood Group AB).
  * E.g., Sickle-cell trait ($Hb^A Hb^S$ individuals possess both normal and sickled erythrocytes).
* **Multiple Allelism:** Existence of three or more alternative allelic forms of a gene occupying the same genetic locus across a population (e.g., ABO system governed by $I^A, I^B, i$, generating 6 genotypes and 4 phenotypes).
* **Pleiotropy:** A single mutant gene influences multiple, seemingly unrelated phenotypic traits:
  * E.g., **Phenylketonuria (PKU):** Mutation in the single gene encoding the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase causes severe mental retardation, hair depigmentation, and skin hypopigmentation due to systemic tyrosine and neurotransmitter deficiency.
* **Polygenic (Quantitative / Metric) Inheritance:** Phenotypic traits governed additively by multiple independent gene pairs, producing a continuous Gaussian bell-shaped distribution:
  * E.g., Human skin pigmentation (governed by 3 gene pairs: $A, B, C$; Davenport), human adult height, intelligence.

### 26.2.3 Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance & Morgan’s Linkage
* **Chromosomal Theory (Walter Sutton & Theodor Boveri, 1902):** Correlated the physical behavior of Mendelian factors (genes) with the meiotic behavior of chromosomes:
  * Chromosomes exist in homologous pairs; genes exist in allelic pairs.
  * Homologous chromosomes segregate during Anaphase I; gene alleles segregate into gametes.
* **Thomas Hunt Morgan’s Drosophila Experiments (Nobel Prize 1933):**
  * Proved the chromosomal theory using the fruit fly *Drosophila melanogaster* (short 2-week generation time, high fecundity, clear sexual dimorphism, distinct morphological markers, only 4 pairs of chromosomes).
  * **Linkage:** The physical association of two or more genes located on the same chromosome, tending to be inherited together as a unit, **violating Mendel’s Law of Independent Assortment**.
  * **Recombination (Crossing Over):** Physical exchange between homologous non-sister chromatids during pachytene of Meiosis I breaks linkage, producing non-parental recombinant offspring.
  * *Recombination Frequency:* Recombination percentage is directly proportional to the physical distance separating two genes on a chromosome:
    $$\mathbf{1\%\text{ Recombination}} = \mathbf{1\text{ Centimorgan (cM)}} = \mathbf{1\text{ Map Unit (mu)}}$$
    (Alfred Sturtevant utilized this principle to construct the world's first **Genetic Linkage Maps**).

### 26.2.4 Mechanisms of Sex Determination
1. **XX-XY Mechanism:** Female homogametic ($XX$), male heterogametic ($XY$). Present in humans, most mammals, and *Drosophila melanogaster*. (In humans, the **SRY gene** on the Y chromosome encodes Testis Determining Factor, driving male sex differentiation).
2. **XX-XO Mechanism:** Female homogametic ($XX$), male heterogametic ($XO$, possessing only one X chromosome). Present in **Grasshoppers, Cockroaches, and true bugs**.
3. **ZZ-ZW Mechanism:** **Female heterogametic ($ZW$)**, male homogametic ($ZZ$). Present in **Birds, Reptiles, and Butterflies**.
4. **Haplodiploidy:** Sex determined strictly by chromosome ploidy:
   * **Honeybees & Ants:** Unfertilized haploid eggs develop by **Parthenogenesis** into fertile **Haploid Males (Drones, $n = 16$)**; fertilized diploid eggs develop into **Diploid Females (Workers or Queen, $2n = 32$)**.
   * *Epistemic Consequence:* Male drones have no father and cannot have sons, but have a grandfather and can have grandsons!

## 26.3 MOLECULAR GENETICS & THE CENTRAL DOGMA

```
                 THE CENTRAL DOGMA OF MOLECULAR BIOLOGY (Francis Crick)
                 
                      ┌─────────────── Replication ──────────────┐
                      ▼                                          │
                   [ DNA ] ──────────────────────────────────────┘
                      │
                      ▼  TRANSCRIPTION (RNA Polymerase)
                   [ mRNA ]  ◄── (Reverse Transcription / Teminism: Retroviruses)
                      │
                      ▼  TRANSLATION (Ribosomes & tRNA)
                  [ PROTEIN ]
```

### 26.3.1 Semi-Conservative DNA Replication
Proven conclusively by **Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl (1958)** using *E. coli* cultured in heavy nitrogen isotope ($^{15}\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$) followed by regular $^{14}\text{N}$, analyzed via **Cesium Chloride ($\text{CsCl}$) density gradient ultracentrifugation**:
* Generation 0: $100\%$ Heavy ($^{15}\text{N}-^{15}\text{N}$).
* Generation 1 ($20\text{ min}$): $100\%$ Hybrid ($^{15}\text{N}-^{14}\text{N}$), disproving conservative replication.
* Generation 2 ($40\text{ min}$): $50\%$ Hybrid ($^{15}\text{N}-^{14}\text{N}$) and $50\%$ Light ($^{14}\text{N}-^{14}\text{N}$), confirming semi-conservative mechanism.

```
                           THE REPLICATION FORK
                           
  Leading Strand Template (3' -> 5') ──► Continuous Synthesis (5' -> 3')
                                         ▲
                                         │  (DNA Polymerase III)
  Lagging Strand Template (5' -> 3') ──► Discontinuous Synthesis of OKAZAKI FRAGMENTS (5' -> 3')
                                         │
                                         ▼  RNA Primers removed by DNA Pol I;
                                            Fragments sealed by DNA LIGASE
```

* **Key Replication Enzymes:**
  * **DNA Helicase:** Unwinds the double-helical parental duplex by severing hydrogen bonds, consuming ATP.
  * **Topoisomerase (DNA Gyrase in bacteria):** Relieves positive supercoiling strain ahead of the replication fork by making transient cuts in phosphodiester backbones.
  * **Single-Stranded Binding (SSB) Proteins:** Prevent unwound single strands from re-annealing.
  * **RNA Primase:** Synthesizes short complementary RNA primers ($\approx 10\text{ nt}$) providing a free $3'\text{-OH}$ group required by DNA polymerases.
  * **DNA Polymerase III:** Main catalytic enzyme; synthesizes new strands strictly in the **$5' \to 3'$ direction** with high fidelity ($3' \to 5'$ exonuclease proofreading).
  * **DNA Polymerase I (Kornberg Enzyme):** Removes RNA primers via $5' \to 3'$ exonuclease activity and replaces them with deoxyribonucleotides.
  * **DNA Ligase:** Catalyzes the formation of phosphodiester bonds to covalently seal nicks between adjacent Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand.

### 26.3.2 Transcription in Eukaryotes
Synthesis of complementary RNA from a DNA template strand ($3' \to 5'$) by DNA-dependent RNA polymerases. The resulting RNA strand is identical in sequence to the **Coding Strand ($5' \to 3'$)**, replacing thymine with uracil:
* **Eukaryotic RNA Polymerase Specialization:**
  * **RNA Polymerase I:** Transcribes precursor **rRNA** ($28\text{S}, 18\text{S}, 5.8\text{S}$).
  * **RNA Polymerase II:** Transcribes **pre-mRNA (heterogeneous nuclear RNA, hnRNA)**.
  * **RNA Polymerase III:** Transcribes **tRNA**, **$5\text{S}$ rRNA**, and small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs).
* **Post-Transcriptional Processing of Eukaryotic hnRNA:**
  1. **$5'$-Capping:** Addition of an unusual nucleotide—**$7$-Methylguanosine triphosphate ($m^7\text{Gppp}$)**—to the $5'$-end, protecting mRNA from $5'$ exonucleases and facilitating ribosome recruitment.
  2. **$3'$-Polyadenylation (Tailing):** Enzymatic addition of $\sim 200 - 300$ adenylic acid residues (**Poly-A Tail**) at the $3'$-end in a template-independent manner.
  3. **RNA Splicing:** Eukaryotic genes are split genes containing coding sequences (**Exons**) interrupted by non-coding intervening sequences (**Introns**). The **Spliceosome** (complex of snRNPs, "snurps") excises introns and ligates exons together to yield mature translatable mRNA.

### 26.3.3 The Universal Genetic Code
Cracked by Marshall Nirenberg, Har Gobind Khorana, and Robert Holley (Nobel Prize 1968):
* **Triplet Codon:** 64 total codons ($4^3 = 64$ combinations of 4 bases).
* **61 Sense Codons** code for 20 amino acids; **3 Nonsense / Stop Codons** terminate translation: **UAA (Ochre), UAG (Amber), UGA (Opal)**.
* **Initiator Codon:** **AUG** serves a dual function: codes for **Methionine** and acts as the universal **Start Codon**.
* **Key Properties of the Genetic Code:**
  1. *Degeneracy:* Multiple distinct codons code for the same amino acid (e.g., Leucine and Arginine are coded by 6 different codons; only Methionine [AUG] and Tryptophan [UGG] have single codons).
  2. *Unambiguity & Specificity:* One particular codon codes strictly for one and only one amino acid.
  3. *Universality:* The code is identical from bacteria to humans (with minor exceptions in mitochondrial codons and certain protozoans; e.g., UGA codes for tryptophan in mitochondria instead of stop).
  4. *Comma-less & Non-Overlapping:* Read continuously three nucleotides at a time without punctuation.

### 26.3.4 Regulation of Gene Expression: The Lac Operon (Jacob & Monod, Nobel 1965)
The polycistronic transcription unit in *E. coli* metabolizing lactose:

```
                            THE LAC OPERON
                            
           [ p ] ──► [ i ] ──► [ p ] ──► [ o ] ──► [ z ] ──► [ y ] ──► [ a ]
                     Regulatory Promoter Operator
                     Gene (i)
```

1. **Constitutive Regulator Gene ($i$):** Continuously transcribes the **Lac Repressor Protein**.
2. **In the ABSENCE of Inducer (Lactose):**
   * Active repressor binds tightly to the **Operator ($o$) locus**.
   * Sterically blocks **RNA Polymerase** from transcribing structural genes.
   * **Operon is OFF (Repressed)**.
3. **In the PRESENCE of Inducer (Lactose / Allolactose):**
   * Allolactose binds allosterically to the repressor protein, inducing a conformational change that inactivates it.
   * Inactive repressor dissociates from the operator.
   * RNA Polymerase transcribes the polycistronic mRNA encoding three structural enzymes:
     * **$z$ Gene:** Encodes **$\beta$-Galactosidase** (hydrolyzes lactose into glucose and galactose).
     * **$y$ Gene:** Encodes **$\beta$-Galactoside Permease** (increases cellular membrane permeability to lactose).
     * **$a$ Gene:** Encodes **$\beta$-Galactoside Transacetylase** (transfers an acetyl group to $\beta$-galactosides).
   * **Operon is ON (Induced)**; exemplifies **Negative Inducible Control**.

## 26.4 GENETIC DISORDERS & CHROMOSOMAL ABERRATIONS

Human genetic pathologies are classified into Mendelian gene mutations and Chromosomal aberrations resulting from meiotic nondisjunction.

```
                           HUMAN GENETIC PATHOLOGIES
                                       │
           ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                       ▼
   [MENDELIAN GENE DISORDERS]                             [CHROMOSOMAL DISORDERS]
   - Follow strict Mendelian pedigrees                     - Aneuploidy (2n ± 1) or Polyploidy
   - Single-gene mutations                                 - Meiotic nondisjunction of chromosomes
   - Autosomal or Sex-Linked                               - Microscopic karyotypic changes
```

### 26.4.1 Mendelian Disorders

| Pathology | Inheritance Pattern | Molecular Etiology & Pathophysiology | Clinical Manifestation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sickle Cell Anemia** | **Autosomal Recessive** (Chromosome 11) | Single base pair **Point Mutation (Transversion $A \to T$)** in codon 6 of $\beta$-globin gene: $GAG \to GUG$, replacing **Glutamic acid with Valine** ($\text{Glu6Val}$). | Deoxygenated $\text{HbS}$ polymerizes into rigid helical fibers, deforming RBCs into sickle shapes; vaso-occlusive painful crises; micro-infarctions. *Heterozygotes ($Hb^A Hb^S$) exhibit innate resistance to falciparum malaria.* |
| **Thalassemia** | **Autosomal Recessive** | **Quantitative defect** in globin chain synthesis ($\alpha$-thalassemia: deletion of genes on Chromosome 16; $\beta$-thalassemia: mutations on Chromosome 11). Contrast with Sickle Cell (qualitative defect). | Microcytic hypochromic severe anemia; hepatosplenomegaly; bone marrow expansion ("hair-on-end" skull X-ray); requires lifelong blood transfusions. |
| **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** | **Autosomal Recessive** (Chromosome 12) | Inborn error of metabolism; congenital deficiency of hepatic enzyme **Phenylalanine Hydroxylase (PAH)**. | Inability to convert phenylalanine to tyrosine; accumulation of neurotoxic phenylpyruvic acid causing severe irreversible mental retardation, microcephaly, and mousy body odor. |
| **Cystic Fibrosis** | **Autosomal Recessive** (Chromosome 7) | Deletion of phenylalanine at position 508 ($\Delta F508$) in the **CFTR** chloride ion channel gene. | Defective $\text{Cl}^-$ secretion leads to dehydrating, hyperviscous mucus plugging bronchi, pancreatic ducts, and gut; recurrent pseudomonal pneumonia; salty sweat. |
| **Hemophilia A & B** | **X-Linked Recessive** | **Hemophilia A (Classic / "Royal Disease"):** Deficiency of Clotting **Factor VIII**. **Hemophilia B (Christmas Disease):** Deficiency of Clotting **Factor IX**. | Defective intrinsic clotting cascade; profuse prolonged hemorrhage from minor cuts; painful spontaneous hemarthrosis (bleeding into joints). Transmitted by carrier females to $50\%$ of sons (**Criss-cross inheritance**). |
| **Color Blindness** | **X-Linked Recessive** | Mutation in opsin photopigment genes on X-chromosome. | Inability to distinguish **Red and Green colors** (protanopia/deuteranopia); significantly higher frequency in males ($8\%$) than females ($0.4\%$). |

### 26.4.2 Chromosomal Aneuploidies (Nondisjunction Syndromes)

```
                            ANEUPLOIDY PROFILES
                            
  [Down's Syndrome]      ──► Trisomy 21 (47, XX+21 or 47, XY+21)  [Autosomal]
  [Edward's Syndrome]    ──► Trisomy 18 (47, XX+18 or 47, XY+18)  [Autosomal]
  [Patau's Syndrome]     ──► Trisomy 13 (47, XX+13 or 47, XY+13)  [Autosomal]
  [Klinefelter Syndrome] ──► 47, XXY                              [Allosomal / Sex]
  [Turner Syndrome]      ──► 45, X0                               [Allosomal / Sex]
```

1. **Down's Syndrome (Trisomy 21) (Langdon Down, 1866):**
   * *Karyotype:* $47, XX+21$ or $47, XY+21$ (Incidence correlates exponentially with advancing maternal age $>35\text{ years}$ due to maternal meiotic nondisjunction).
   * *Clinical Features:* Broad flat face with depressed nasal bridge, prominent epicanthic eye folds ("mongoloid slant"), permanently open mouth with furrowed protruding tongue, single transverse palmar crease (**Simian Crease**), short stature, mental retardation, and high incidence of congenital cardiac septal defects.
2. **Turner’s Syndrome (Monosomy X):**
   * *Karyotype:* **$45, X0$** (Only viable monosomy in humans; $44\text{ autosomes} + 1\text{ X-chromosome}$).
   * *Clinical Features:* Phenotypically female; short stature; characteristic **Webbed Neck**; low posterior hairline; shield-shaped broad chest with widely spaced nipples; **Rudimentary Streak Ovaries** lacking germ cells $\implies$ sterile, absence of secondary sexual characteristics (amenorrhea).
3. **Klinefelter’s Syndrome (Sex Chromosome Trisomy):**
   * *Karyotype:* **$47, XXY$** ($44\text{ autosomes} + XXY$).
   * *Clinical Features:* Phenotypically male; tall eunuchoid body habitus with elongated extremities; feminine fat distribution; **Gynecomastia** (development of breast tissue); small atrophic testes with azoospermia (sterility); subnormal intelligence; presence of **one inactive Barr body** in buccal smear nuclei.

## 26.5 HIGH-YIELD PRACTICE QUESTIONS

### Question 1 (UPSC CSE Prelims Standard)
Consider the following statements regarding molecular genetics and inheritance:
1. In sickle cell anemia, the genetic defect is caused by the replacement of glutamic acid by valine at the 6th position of the $\beta$-globin chain due to a point mutation.
2. Turner's syndrome is an autosomal trisomy condition resulting in sterile male phenotypes.
3. The lac operon in *Escherichia coli* is switched on when the inducer allolactose binds to the operator locus, promoting RNA polymerase binding.
4. Human males inherit their X-chromosome exclusively from their mother.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
A) 1 and 4 only  
B) 1, 3 and 4 only  
C) 2 and 3 only  
D) 1 and 3 only  

**Analytical Answer with Epistemic Breakdown:**
* Statement 1 is **CORRECT**: Sickle cell anemia is caused by a point mutation ($GAG \to GUG$) causing a $\text{Glu6Val}$ substitution on the $\beta$-globin polypeptide.
* Statement 2 is **INCORRECT**: Turner's syndrome is an allosomal (sex chromosome) **monosomy ($45, X0$)**, producing sterile **female** phenotypes with streak ovaries.
* Statement 3 is **INCORRECT**: Allolactose binds to the **repressor protein**, NOT the operator locus; its binding causes conformational inactivation of the repressor so it releases from the operator.
* Statement 4 is **CORRECT**: Because human males are XY and the father invariably contributes the Y chromosome to sons, the solitary X chromosome is always inherited from the mother.
* **Correct Option:** **A) 1 and 4 only**.

### Question 2 (Competitive State PSC Standard)
Which of the following hormones is directly detected in maternal urine by home pregnancy test kits?
A) Progesterone  
B) Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)  
C) Human Placental Lactogen (hPL)  
D) Luteinizing Hormone (LH)  

**Analytical Answer:**
* **hCG** is secreted exclusively by the syncytiotrophoblast of the implanted blastocyst and early placenta. Because it is filtered into urine in significant concentrations during the early weeks of gestation, immunochromatographic dipsticks utilize anti-hCG monoclonal antibodies for definitive pregnancy confirmation.
* **Correct Option:** **B) Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**.

### Question 3 (Advanced Epistemic Standard)
A cross between two pink-flowered *Antirrhinum majus* (Snapdragon) plants yields 400 offspring. Based on incomplete dominance, what is the expected phenotypic distribution of the progeny?
A) 400 Pink  
B) 300 Red and 100 White  
C) 100 Red, 200 Pink, and 100 White  
D) 200 Red, 100 Pink, and 100 White  

**Analytical Answer:**
* Snapdragon flower color exhibits incomplete dominance where $RR$ is red, $rr$ is white, and the heterozygote $Rr$ is pink.
* Cross: $Rr \times Rr \implies$ Offspring genotype ratio is $1\text{ }RR : 2\text{ }Rr : 1\text{ }rr$.
* Because phenotypic ratio equals genotypic ratio in incomplete dominance ($1\text{ Red} : 2\text{ Pink} : 1\text{ White}$):
  * Red ($RR$) = $\frac{1}{4} \times 400 = \mathbf{100\text{ Red}}$
  * Pink ($Rr$) = $\frac{2}{4} \times 400 = \mathbf{200\text{ Pink}}$
  * White ($rr$) = $\frac{1}{4} \times 400 = \mathbf{100\text{ White}}$
* **Correct Option:** **C) 100 Red, 200 Pink, and 100 White**.
