## 14.1 Historical Genesis of the Periodic Table

```
        DOBEREINER (1817)        NEWLANDS (1866)          MENDELEEV (1869)           MOSELEY (1913)
        [ Triads ]               [ Law of Octaves ]       [ Atomic Mass Law ]        [ Atomic Number Law ]
        Middle element mass =    Every 8th element repeats Periodic functions of     Periodic functions of
        average of extremes      properties like musical  Atomic Mass; Left gaps     Atomic Number (Z);
        (Li, Na, K)              notes (Failed past Ca)   (Eka-Aluminium/Silicon)    The Modern Periodic Law!
```

### 1. Johann Dobereiner's Triads (1817)
Arranged chemically similar elements in groups of three (**Triads**), where the atomic mass of the middle element was approximately the arithmetic mean of the other two:
* **Alkali Metal Triad**: Lithium ($Li = 7$), Sodium ($Na = \frac{7 + 39}{2} = \mathbf{23}$), Potassium ($K = 39$).
* **Halogen Triad**: Chlorine ($Cl = 35.5$), Bromine ($Br \approx \mathbf{80}$), Iodine ($I = 127$).
* **Alkaline Earth Triad**: Calcium ($Ca = 40$), Strontium ($Sr \approx \mathbf{88}$), Barium ($Ba = 137$).

### 2. John Newlands' Law of Octaves (1866)
Arranged elements in increasing order of atomic mass; found that every eighth element exhibited properties similar to the first, analogous to the musical notes of an octave (*Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni, Sa*).  
*Limitations*: Worked well only up to **Calcium ($Z = 20$)**; assumed only 56 elements existed in nature; placed unlike elements in the same column (Cobalt and Nickel placed under Halogens).

### 3. Dmitri Mendeleev's Periodic Table (1869)
> **Mendeleev's Periodic Law**: The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their **Atomic Masses**.

* **Mendeleev's Genius & Predictions**:
  - Left deliberate gaps in his table for undiscovered elements, predicting their atomic masses and chemical properties with astonishing accuracy:
    - **Eka-Aluminium** $\longrightarrow$ Discovered later as **Gallium ($Ga$)**.
    - **Eka-Silicon** $\longrightarrow$ Discovered later as **Germanium ($Ge$)**.
    - **Eka-Boron** $\longrightarrow$ Discovered later as **Scandium ($Sc$)**.
* **Fatal Anomalies of Mendeleev's Mass Table**:
  1. *Inverted Pairs*: Cobalt (Atomic mass $58.9$) was placed *before* Nickel (Atomic mass $58.7$); Tellurium ($127.6$) placed before Iodine ($126.9$).
  2. *Position of Isotopes*: Isotopes of the same element have different atomic masses (e.g., $^{35}Cl$ and $^{37}Cl$), requiring separate slots in a mass-based table, which Mendeleev could not provide.
  3. *Uncertain Position of Hydrogen*: Exhibited dual properties of Alkali metals (Group 1) and Halogens (Group 17).

## 14.2 The Modern Periodic Table (Henry Moseley, 1913)

In 1913, British physicist Henry Moseley bombarded various metals with high-speed electrons and analyzed the frequencies ($\nu$) of the emitted characteristic X-rays:

$$\sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)$$

Moseley proved conclusively that **Atomic Number ($Z$, Nuclear Charge)** is a far more fundamental property of an element than its atomic mass!

> **The Modern Periodic Law**: The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their **Atomic Numbers ($Z$)**.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   MODERN PERIODIC TABLE ARCHITECTURE                   │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 7 Horizontal Rows (PERIODS)       │ 18 Vertical Columns (GROUPS)       │
├───────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Period 1: 2 elements (Shortest)   │ Group 1: Alkali Metals (Li, Na, K) │
│ Period 2 & 3: 8 elements (Short)  │ Group 2: Alkaline Earth (Mg, Ca)   │
│ Period 4 & 5: 18 elements (Long)  │ Groups 3–12: Transition Metals     │
│ Period 6: 32 elements (Lanthanide)│ Group 16: Chalcogens (O, S, Se)    │
│ Period 7: 32 elements (Actinide)  │ Group 17: Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I)  │
│                                   │ Group 18: Noble / Inert Gases      │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Four Blocks of the Periodic Table
Elements are classified into four blocks based on the subshell into which the **last valence electron enters**:
1. **s-Block (Groups 1 & 2)**: Reactive electropositive metals ($ns^1$ and $ns^2$). Form basic ionic oxides.
2. **p-Block (Groups 13 to 18)**: Contains metals, metalloids, and non-metals ($ns^2 np^{1-6}$).
3. **d-Block (Groups 3 to 12 - Transition Elements)**: Metallic elements with partially filled d-orbitals; exhibit variable oxidation states, form colored catalytic coordination complexes.
4. **f-Block (Inner Transition Elements)**: Placed separately at the bottom in two rows:
   - **Lanthanides (4f series)**: Cerium ($Z=58$) to Lutetium ($Z=71$).
   - **Actinides (5f series)**: Thorium ($Z=90$) to Lawrencium ($Z=103$). All actinides are **radioactive**! Elements beyond Uranium ($Z > 92$) are synthetic, man-made elements called **Transuranic Elements**.

## 14.3 Periodic Trends Across Periods & Down Groups

```
     Atomic Radius (Decreases Across ──►)             Ionization Enthalpy (Increases Across ──►)
     Electronegativity (Increases Across ──►)         Metallic Character (Decreases Across ──►)
     ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
     │ Li    Be    B     C     N     O     F     Ne                         │
   ▲ │                                                                        │
   │ │ Na                                                                     │
   │ │ K     DOWN A GROUP:                                                    │
   │ │ Rb    - Atomic Radius INCREASES (New electron shells added)            │
     │ Cs    - Ionization Energy DECREASES (Valence e⁻ held loosely)          │
     │       - Metallic Character INCREASES (Easier to lose e⁻)               │
     │       - Electronegativity DECREASES                                    │
     └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Atomic Radius / Ionic Radius
* **Across a Period (Left to Right)**: **DECREASES**.  
  *Physical Cause*: Electrons are added to the same main energy shell, while positive nuclear charge ($Z$) increases steadily. This pulls the electron cloud closer to the nucleus (**increasing Effective Nuclear Charge $Z_{\text{eff}}$**).
* **Down a Group (Top to Bottom)**: **INCREASES**.  
  *Physical Cause*: A brand-new principal electron shell ($n$) is added at each step, pushing valence electrons farther from the nucleus, despite increasing nuclear charge.
* **Ionic Radii Rules**:
  - **Cation ($M^+$)** is ALWAYS **smaller than its neutral parent atom** ($Na^+ < Na$) because of loss of electrons and increased effective nuclear pull.
  - **Anion ($X^-$)** is ALWAYS **larger than its neutral parent atom** ($Cl^- > Cl$) because added electron repulsion expands the electron cloud.
  - *Isoelectronic Radii Order*: More negative = larger radius!
    $$\mathbf{N^{3-} > O^{2-} > F^- > Na^+ > Mg^{2+} > Al^{3+}}$$

---

### 2. Ionization Enthalpy (IE)
The minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound valence electron from an isolated gaseous atom in its ground state:
* **Across a Period**: **INCREASES** (higher nuclear charge binds electrons tightly).
* **Down a Group**: **DECREASES** (larger radius and increased shielding make electrons easier to remove).
* **The Francium / Helium Extremes**:
  - **Helium ($He$)**: Has the **highest ionization enthalpy** in the periodic table ($\sim 2372\text{ kJ/mol}$).
  - **Cesium / Francium**: Has the **lowest ionization enthalpy**, losing valence electrons with minimal energy.

### 3. Electron Gain Enthalpy (Electron Affinity - EA)
The energy released when an electron is added to an isolated gaseous atom to form a univalent negative anion.
* **General Trend**: Becomes more negative (increases) across a period; becomes less negative down a group.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Fluorine vs. Chlorine Electron Affinity Trap**:  
> *Trap*: "Fluorine has the highest electron affinity in the periodic table because it is the most electronegative element."  
> *Scientific Reality*: **FALSE! CHLORINE has the highest electron affinity in the periodic table!**  
> *Physical Proof*: Fluorine ($Z=9$) has an extremely compact, tiny $2p$ subshell. Adding an electron creates massive inter-electronic repulsion, destabilizing the incoming charge. Chlorine ($Z=17$) has a larger, roomier $3p$ subshell with negligible repulsion. Hence:
> $$\mathbf{\text{Electron Affinity: } \text{Chlorine } (Cl) > \text{Fluorine } (F) > \text{Bromine } (Br) > \text{Iodine } (I)}$$

---

### 4. Electronegativity (Pauling Scale)
The relative tendency of an atom in a chemical molecule to **attract the shared pair of bonding electrons towards itself**.
* **Linus Pauling Scale**: Electronegativity is an arbitrary dimensionless property:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Fluorine (4.0)} > \text{Oxygen (3.5)} > \text{Nitrogen (3.0)} \approx \text{Chlorine (3.0)} > \text{Bromine (2.8)} > \text{Carbon (2.5)} > \text{Hydrogen (2.1)}}$$
  - **Fluorine ($F$)** is the **most electronegative element in the entire periodic table**!
  - **Cesium ($Cs$)** is the **least electronegative (most electropositive) element**.

## 14.4 Chemical Bonding: The Drive for Noble Gas Octet

Atoms enter chemical combinations to achieve the exceptionally stable, minimum-energy **Noble Gas Electronic Configuration ($ns^2 np^6$ - Octet Rule)** discovered by G.N. Lewis and Irving Langmuir.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        CHEMICAL BONDING TAXONOMY                       │
├─────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┬───────────────────────┤
│ Ionic / Electrovalent│ Covalent Bond           │ Coordinate / Dative   │
├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ Complete TRANSFER   │ Equal MUTUAL SHARING     │ One atom donates BOTH │
│ of electrons from   │ of electron pairs        │ electrons of the bond │
│ Metal to Non-metal  │ between Non-metals       │ pair (Lone pair donor)│
│ Ex: NaCl, MgO, CaCl₂│ Ex: H₂, O₂, N₂, CH₄, H₂O │ Ex: NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺, CO    │
└─────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Ionic (Electrovalent) Bonding
Formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from a metal (low ionization energy) to a non-metal (high electron affinity), held together by powerful **electrostatic Coulomb forces**:

$$Na \ ([2,8,1]) + Cl \ ([2,8,7]) \longrightarrow Na^+ \ ([2,8]) + Cl^- \ ([2,8,8]) \longrightarrow \mathbf{NaCl}$$

#### Characteristic Properties of Ionic Compounds
1. **High Melting & Boiling Points**: Enormous thermal energy required to overcome 3D electrostatic crystal lattice forces ($NaCl\text{ MP} = 801^\circ\text{C}$).
2. **Hard and Brittle**: Strong forces make them hard; shear forces align like charges, causing catastrophic cleavage (brittleness).
3. **Electrical Conductivity Paradox**:
   - **Solid State**: Completely **non-conductive insulators** (ions immobilized in rigid lattice positions).
   - **Molten / Aqueous Solution**: **Exceptional electrical conductors** (free, mobile hydrated ions migrate freely toward electrodes).
4. **Solubility**: Highly soluble in polar solvents (water, high dielectric constant $K \approx 81$); insoluble in non-polar organic solvents (benzene, kerosene, ether).

---

### 2. Covalent Bonding
Formed by the equal, mutual sharing of electron pairs between non-metallic atoms of comparable electronegativity:
* **Single Covalent Bond**: 1 shared pair ($2\text{ electrons}$), e.g., $H-H$, $Cl-Cl$, $CH_4$.
* **Double Covalent Bond**: 2 shared pairs ($4\text{ electrons}$), e.g., $O=O$, $CO_2$, $C_2H_4$.
* **Triple Covalent Bond**: 3 shared pairs ($6\text{ electrons}$), e.g., $N\equiv N$, $C_2H_2$.
  - *Why Nitrogen ($N_2$) is Chemically Inert*: The $N\equiv N$ triple bond has an enormous **Bond Dissociation Energy ($946\text{ kJ/mol}$)**, making atmospheric nitrogen unreactive at ordinary temperatures.

#### Non-Polar vs. Polar Covalent Bonds & Dipole Moment
* **Non-Polar Covalent**: Shared pair sits symmetrically between identical atoms (zero electronegativity difference, e.g., $H_2, O_2, Cl_2$).
* **Polar Covalent**: Formed between atoms with different electronegativities; the more electronegative atom pulls the shared pair closer, acquiring a partial negative charge ($\delta^-$), leaving a partial positive charge ($\delta^+$) on the other (**Dipole**, e.g., $H^{\delta+} - Cl^{\delta-}$).

## 14.5 Hydrogen Bonding: The Miracle of Molecular Cohesion

When Hydrogen is covalently bonded to a **highly electronegative, small-sized atom (strictly Fluorine, Oxygen, or Nitrogen - "FON")**, the bond pair is drawn powerfully away from hydrogen. The bare, exposed hydrogen proton attracts the lone pair of a neighboring electronegative atom:

$$\cdots \text{H}^{\delta+} - \text{O}^{\delta-} \cdots \text{H}^{\delta+} - \text{O}^{\delta-} \cdots \text{H}^{\delta+} - \text{O}^{\delta-} \cdots$$

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        HYDROGEN BONDING PHENOMENA                      │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Intermolecular H-Bonding          │ Intramolecular H-Bonding           │
├───────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Between DIFFERENT molecules       │ Within the SAME molecule           │
│ Causes molecular association      │ Prevents association               │
│ Drastically ELEVATES BP and MP    │ LOWERS boiling point               │
│ Ex: Water (H₂O), Alcohol, HF      │ Ex: o-Nitrophenol, Salicylic acid  │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### High-Yield Natural Phenomena Governed by Hydrogen Bonding
1. **Why Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) is a Liquid while Hydrogen Sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) is a Gas**:  
   Oxygen is small and highly electronegative ($3.5$), forming powerful **intermolecular hydrogen bonds** that cluster $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules into a cohesive liquid at room temperature ($BP = 100^\circ\text{C}$). Sulfur has a larger atomic radius and lower electronegativity ($2.5$), forming zero hydrogen bonds; hence $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ molecules remain separate as a toxic gas ($BP = -60^\circ\text{C}$)!
2. **DNA Double Helix Stability**: The two complementary strands of the human DNA genetic code are held together exclusively by **hydrogen bonds**:
   - Adenine ($A$) pairs with Thymine ($T$) via **2 Hydrogen Bonds** ($A = T$).
   - Guanine ($G$) pairs with Cytosine ($C$) via **3 Hydrogen Bonds** ($G \equiv C$).
3. **Anomalous Expansion & Floating of Ice**: In solid ice, four hydrogen bonds per molecule freeze water into an open, hollow hexagonal cage structure with vacant cavities, expanding volume and lowering density, allowing ice to float.

## 14.7 High-Yield Diagnostic Examination Traps

1. **Chlorine vs. Fluorine Electron Affinity Trap**:
   - *Trap*: "Fluorine has the highest electron affinity in the periodic table."
   - *Correction*: **Chlorine ($Cl$) has the highest electron affinity!** Fluorine's tiny $2p$ subshell creates strong electron-electron repulsion, releasing less energy upon electron capture than chlorine's spacious $3p$ subshell.
2. **Solid Ionic Conduction Fallacy**:
   - *Trap*: "Solid salt ($NaCl$) conducts electricity because it is an ionic compound."
   - *Correction*: Solid $NaCl$ is an **absolute electrical insulator**! Ions are locked rigidly in crystalline lattice points; it conducts electricity **only when molten or dissolved in water**.
3. **Mendeleev vs. Modern Periodic Law**:
   - *Trap*: "Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic number."
   - *Correction*: Mendeleev arranged elements by **Atomic Mass**. **Henry Moseley (1913)** established the Modern Periodic Table based on **Atomic Number ($Z$)**.
4. **Hydrogen Bond Requisite Elements**:
   - *Trap*: "Hydrochloric acid ($HCl$) forms strong hydrogen bonds."
   - *Correction*: **Zero Hydrogen Bonding in $HCl$!** Although chlorine is electronegative ($3.0$), its atomic radius is too large to create the concentrated electric field required for hydrogen bonding. Hydrogen bonding occurs **strictly with Fluorine, Oxygen, and Nitrogen ("FON")**.
