## 25.1 EXCRETION & RENAL OSMOREGULATION

Excretion is the physiological elimination of metabolic nitrogenous byproducts (ammonia, urea, uric acid) and excess electrolytes to maintain water-electrolyte and acid-base homeostasis.

```

                                         │
            ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
            ▼                            ▼                            ▼
     [AMMONOTELISM]                [UREOTELISM]                 [URICOTELISM]
   - Ammonia (NH3)               - Urea (H2N-CO-NH2)          - Uric Acid (Purine ring)
   - Highly toxic                - Moderate toxicity          - Least toxic, insoluble
   - Requires 300 - 500 mL       - Requires ~50 mL H2O/g      - Requires < 10 mL H2O/g
     water per gram NH3            (Synthesized in LIVER        (Excreted as dry pellet / paste)
   - Aquatic invertebrates,        via Ornithine cycle)       - Reptiles, Birds, Insects,
     bony fishes, tadpoles       - Mammals, adult amphibians,   land snails (Xeric adaptation)
                                   marine sharks
```

> [!LAW / PRINCIPLE]
> **The Krebs-Henseleit Ornithine / Urea Cycle (Liver Matrix & Cytosol)**
> Toxic ammonia generated via oxidative deamination of amino acids is converted in hepatocytes into less toxic, water-soluble urea:
> $$2\text{NH}_3 + \text{CO}_2 + 3\text{ATP} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Urea Cycle}} \text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CO}-\text{NH}_2 \text{ (Urea)} + 2\text{ADP} + 4\text{P}_i + \text{AMP}$$
> *Pathway Intermediates:* Ornithine $\to$ Citrulline $\to$ Argininosuccinate $\to$ Arginine $\xrightarrow{\text{Arginase}}$ Urea + Ornithine.
> *Exam Note:* Kidneys do NOT synthesize urea; they strictly filter and excrete urea synthesized by the liver!

### 25.1.1 Renal Anatomy & The Nephron Unit
* **Macroscopic Anatomy:** Bean-shaped retroperitoneal organs located between $T_{12}$ and $L_3$ vertebrae. Divided into an outer **Cortex** and inner **Medulla** consisting of conical **Medullary Pyramids** whose bases face the cortex and apices (renal papillae) project into minor calyces. Cortical tissue projecting between pyramids constitutes the **Columns of Bertin**.
* **The Nephron ($\sim 1\text{ to }1.2\text{ million}$ per kidney):**
  1. *Cortical Nephrons ($85\%$):* Glomeruli in outer cortex; short loops of Henle penetrating only into the superficial medulla; peritubular capillaries surround tubules.
  2. *Juxtamedullary Nephrons ($15\%$):* Glomeruli at the corticomedullary junction; **very long loops of Henle** extending deep into the renal papillae, accompanied by specialized hairpin-shaped capillary loops called **Vasa Recta**. Indispensable for concentrating urine.

```
                                            ▼
```

### 25.1.2 The Three Physiological Steps of Urine Formation
1. **Glomerular Ultrafiltration:**
   * High hydrostatic pressure inside the narrow glomerular capillaries ($\text{GHP} \approx 60\text{ mmHg}$) forces plasma through the trilaminar filtration membrane: (i) fenestrated capillary endothelium, (ii) acellular basement membrane, and (iii) visceral epithelial layer of Bowman's capsule displaying specialized interdigitating cells called **Podocytes** forming micro-porous **Filtration Slits** ($\approx 25\text{ nm}$).
   * **Net Filtration Pressure (NFP):**
     $$\text{NFP} = \text{GHP} - (\text{BCOP} + \text{CHP}) = 60\text{ mmHg} - (32\text{ mmHg} + 18\text{ mmHg}) = \mathbf{10\text{ mmHg}}$$
     *(where $\text{BCOP}$ = Blood Colloid Osmotic Pressure due to albumin; $\text{CHP}$ = Capsular Hydrostatic Pressure).*
   * **Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR):** The volume of ultrafiltrate formed across all nephrons per minute:
     $$\mathbf{\text{GFR} = 125\text{ mL/min}} = \mathbf{180\text{ Liters/day}}$$
2. **Tubular Selective Reabsorption:**
   * Out of $180\text{ L}$ of filtrate formed daily, $\approx 99\%$ is reabsorbed into peritubular capillaries, yielding only $\approx \mathbf{1.5\text{ Liters of final urine/day}}$.
   * *PCT:* Simple cuboidal epithelium with extensive microvillar brush border reabsorbs **$100\%$ of filtered Glucose and Amino Acids** (via $Na^+$-dependent secondary active transport), $\sim 80\%$ of $\text{HCO}_3^-$, and $70–80\%$ of filtered water and $Na^+$.
   * *Threshold Substances:* Glucose has a high renal threshold ($180\text{ mg/dL}$ in arterial plasma); when blood glucose exceeds this threshold, transporter saturation occurs and glucose spills into urine (**Glucosuria**).
3. **Tubular Active Secretion:**
   * Tubular epithelial cells actively secrete protons ($\text{H}^+$), potassium ions ($K^+$), and ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) from peritubular blood into the tubular lumen (PCT and DCT), preserving physiological blood $\text{pH} \approx 7.4$ and potassium balance.

### 25.1.3 The Counter-Current Multiplier & Osmotic Gradient
The mammalian kidney produces hypertonic urine (up to **$1200\text{ mOsm/L}$**—four times the plasma osmolarity of $300\text{ mOsm/L}$) through the physical interplay between the **Loop of Henle** (counter-current multiplier) and the **Vasa Recta** (counter-current exchanger):
1. **Descending Limb of Henle:** Freely permeable to water but virtually impermeable to electrolytes; water diffuses out into the hypertonic medullary interstitium, concentrating tubular fluid from $300\text{ mOsm/L}$ at the cortex to $1200\text{ mOsm/L}$ at the hairpin bend.
2. **Ascending Limb of Henle:** **Impermeable to water**; the thick segment actively extrudes $Na^+, K^+$, and $2\text{Cl}^-$ via apical $\text{Na}^+\text{-K}^+\text{-2Cl}^-$ cotransporters (NKCC2) into the interstitium, establishing the hyperosmotic medullary gradient. The tubular fluid becomes progressively hypotonic ($100 - 200\text{ mOsm/L}$) as it enters the DCT.
3. **Urea Recycling:** Urea diffuses out from the lower collecting duct into the deep medullary interstitium, reinforcing medullary hyperosmolarity, before diffusing into the thin ascending limb to repeat the cycle.
4. **Vasa Recta (Counter-Current Exchanger):** Blood flows down the descending capillary losing water and absorbing $\text{NaCl}$; as it ascends toward the cortex, it reabsorbs water and discharges $\text{NaCl}$, preserving the medullary interstitial hyperosmotic gradient without washing it out.

### 25.1.4 Endocrine Regulation of Renal Function
1. **The Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS):**
   * Triggered by a fall in glomerular blood flow/GFR or low $[Na^+]$, sensed by the **Juxtaglomerular (JG) Cells** of the afferent arteriole and the **Macula Densa** of the DCT (together forming the **Juxtaglomerular Apparatus, JGA**).
   * JG cells secrete the aspartyl protease **Renin** into circulation:
     $$\text{Angiotensinogen (Liver)} \xrightarrow{\text{Renin}} \text{Angiotensin I} \xrightarrow[\text{Vascular Endothelium}]{\text{ACE (Lungs)}} \text{Angiotensin II}$$
   * *Angiotensin II Functions:*
     * Powerful systemic arterial vasoconstrictor, elevating glomerular blood pressure.
     * Stimulates the adrenal cortex to secrete **Aldosterone**, which acts on principal cells of the DCT and collecting duct to stimulate active $Na^+$ and water reabsorption while promoting $K^+$ excretion.
2. **Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin):**
   * Synthesized by hypothalamic supraoptic nuclei, stored in the posterior pituitary. Released in response to elevated plasma osmolarity ($>290\text{ mOsm/L}$) sensed by hypothalamic osmoreceptors or severe hypovolemia.
   * Binds $V_2$ basolateral receptors on collecting ducts, triggering cAMP-mediated exocytic insertion of **Aquaporin-2 (AQP2)** water channels into the apical membrane, inducing massive water reabsorption.
   * *Pathology:* Deficiency or lack of response to ADH causes **Diabetes Insipidus**, characterized by extreme polydipsia and polyuria ($15 - 20\text{ L/day}$ of dilute hypotonic urine, with zero glucose).
3. **Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF / ANP):**
   * Secreted by cardiac atrial myocytes in response to excessive atrial wall stretch (hypervolemia).
   * Causes renal vasodilation, inhibits renin and aldosterone secretion, and stimulates urinary excretion of sodium (**Natriuresis**) and water, acting as an **endogenous antagonist to RAAS**.

### 25.2.1 Neuronal Ultrastructure & The Action Potential
* **The Neuron:** Composed of a **Cell Body (Soma / Cyton)** containing Nissl's granules (aggregates of RER and free ribosomes for protein synthesis; absent in axon hillock and axon), receptive branching **Dendrites**, and an elongating conducting **Axon**.
  * *Myelin Sheath:* In the PNS, axons are insulated by **Schwann Cells**; in the CNS, by **Oligodendrocytes**. Interruptions in the myelin sheath are **Nodes of Ranvier**, enabling rapid **Saltatory Conduction** (action potentials leap from node to node at velocities $>100\text{ m/s}$, consuming substantially less metabolic energy).
* **Biophysics of Nerve Impulse Generation:**

```
       Membrane Potential (mV)
          ▲
      +30 ┼                 /\  <-- Peak Action Potential (Overshoot)
          │                /  \
        0 ┼ - - - - - - - / - -\ - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
          │              /      \
      -55 ┼ - - - - - - / - - - -\ - - - Threshold Potential
          │            /          \
      -70 ┼ ──────────/            \            ────── Resting Potential (-70 mV)
          │  Resting                \          /
          │                          \________/ <-- Hyperpolarization
          └────────────────────────────────────────► Time (milliseconds)
```

1. **Resting Membrane Potential (RMP $\approx -70\text{ mV}$):**
   * The axonal membrane is polarized: interior is electrically negative relative to the extracellular fluid.
   * Maintained by: (i) High membrane resting permeability to $K^+$ via passive leak channels, (ii) Impermeability to intracellular organic anions (proteins, nucleic acids), and (iii) The **$Na^+/K^+$ ATPase pump** ($3\text{ }Na^+$ extruded out for every $2\text{ }K^+$ imported in).
2. **Depolarization (Generation of Action Potential):**
   * A suprathreshold stimulus ($\ge -55\text{ mV}$) triggers the rapid opening of **Voltage-Gated Sodium Channels (VGSCs)**.
   * $Na^+$ ions surge down their steep electrochemical gradient into the axoplasm, reversing the polarity from $-70\text{ mV}$ to an overshoot peak of **$+30\text{ mV}$** within $1\text{ millisecond}$.
3. **Repolarization:**
   * VGSCs inactivate rapidly; **Voltage-Gated Potassium Channels (VGKCs)** open.
   * $K^+$ ions rush out of the axon into the ECF, restoring the negative internal resting potential.
4. **Hyperpolarization & Refractory Period:**
   * Delayed closure of VGKCs causes a transient drop below resting potential ($\approx -80\text{ mV}$). The $Na^+/K^+$ pump actively restores initial ionic distributions.
   * *All-or-None Law:* Action potential magnitude is invariant regardless of stimulus strength above threshold; stimulus intensity is encoded strictly by **firing frequency**, not amplitude.

### 25.2.2 Synaptic Transmission Biophysics
* **Electrical Synapses:** Pre- and post-synaptic membranes are physically bridged by **Gap Junctions (Connexons)**; ionic currents flow directly with zero synaptic delay ($<0.1\text{ ms}$); rare in mammalian CNS (retina, cardiac intercalated discs).
* **Chemical Synapses:** Separated by a fluid-filled **Synaptic Cleft** ($20–40\text{ nm}$):
  1. Action potential arrival depolarizes the pre-synaptic terminal, opening **Voltage-Gated Calcium Channels ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$)**.
  2. $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx drives synaptotagmin/SNARE-mediated fusion of **Synaptic Vesicles** with the pre-synaptic membrane, discharging **Neurotransmitters** into the cleft via exocytosis.
  3. Neurotransmitters diffuse across the cleft to bind specific ligand-gated receptors on the post-synaptic membrane:
     * *Excitatory Neurotransmitters (Acetylcholine, Glutamate, Noradrenaline):* Open ligand-gated $Na^+$ channels, causing depolarization (Excitatory Postsynaptic Potential, EPSP).
     * *Inhibitory Neurotransmitters (GABA [$\gamma$-aminobutyric acid], Glycine):* Open ligand-gated $\text{Cl}^-$ or $K^+$ channels, causing post-synaptic hyperpolarization (IPSP).

### 25.2.3 Functional Neuroanatomy of the Human Brain
Enclosed within the neurocranium and wrapped in three protective fibroelastic **Meninges**: outer **Dura Mater**, middle web-like **Arachnoid Mater**, and vascular inner **Pia Mater**. The subarachnoid space is filled with **Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)**.

```
                          DIVISIONS OF THE HUMAN BRAIN
                                       │
     ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
[FOREBRAIN (Prosencephalon)]     [MIDBRAIN (Mesencephalon)]      [HINDBRAIN (Rhombencephalon)]
- Cerebrum (Hemispheres,          - Cerebral Peduncles            - Cerebellum ("Little Brain",
  Corpus Callosum, Cortex)        - Corpora Quadrigemina            Coordination, Equilibrium)
- Diencephalon:                     (2 Superior Colliculi:        - Pons (Pneumotaxic center)
  * Thalamus (Sensory Relay)         Visual Reflex;               - Medulla Oblongata
  * Hypothalamus (Autonomic,        2 Inferior Colliculi:           (Cardiovascular, Respiratory,
    Homeostasis, Neuroendocrine)     Auditory Reflex)               Vomiting & Deglutition centers)
```

1. **The Forebrain:**
   * **Cerebrum:** Divided into left and right hemispheres by a deep longitudinal fissure, interconnected internally by a massive transverse tract of myelinated nerve fibers called the **Corpus Callosum** (diagnostic mammalian hallmark). The outer convoluted grey matter (**Cerebral Cortex**) contains motor areas, sensory areas, and vast **Association Areas** responsible for complex integrative functions (memory, abstract cognition, communication).
   * **Thalamus:** Major coordinating and relay station for all ascending sensory signals (except olfaction) projecting to the cortex.
   * **Hypothalamus:** Situated at the base of the thalamus:
     * Contains vital centers regulating **Thermoregulation (Body Thermostat)**, hunger, satiety, and thirst.
     * Houses neurosecretory nuclei synthesizing hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones and posterior pituitary hormones (Oxytocin and ADH).
   * **The Limbic System ("Emotional Brain"):** Composed of deep structures—the **Amygdala** (mediates fear, rage, emotional memory), **Hippocampus** (essential for memory consolidation: short-term to long-term memory; damaged in Alzheimer's), and parts of the hypothalamus, regulating emotional expressions and sexual drive.
2. **The Midbrain:**
   * Contains a canal called the **Cerebral Aqueduct of Sylvius**.
   * Dorsal surface displays four rounded lobes called the **Corpora Quadrigemina**: two superior colliculi (visual reflex centers) and two inferior colliculi (auditory reflex tracking).
3. **The Hindbrain & Brainstem:**
   * **Cerebellum ("Little Brain"):** Highly convoluted surface with a branching pattern of white matter called the **Arbor Vitae** ("tree of life"); coordinates involuntary regulation of voluntary movements, motor learning, posture, and equilibrium. Alcohol suppresses cerebellar Purkinje cells, causing ataxia and uncoordinated gait.
   * **Pons Varolii:** Interconnects different brain regions; contains the **Pneumotaxic Center** that modulates respiratory rate and tidal volume.
   * **Medulla Oblongata:** Connects to the spinal cord; houses essential autonomic reflex centers:
     * Cardiovascular center (regulates heart rate and contractility).
     * Respiratory rhythmicity center (sets basal breathing cadence).
     * Vasomotor center (controls arterial diameter).
     * Reflex centers for vomiting, swallowing, coughing, and sneezing.
   * *Brainstem:* Composed of **Midbrain + Pons + Medulla** (Cerebellum is *excluded*).

### 25.2.4 The Somatosensory Apparatus: Eye and Ear
* **The Human Eye:**
  * Three concentric tunics: outer fibrous tunic (**Sclera and transparent Cornea**), middle vascular tunic (**Choroid, Ciliary Body, and pigmented Iris**), and inner neurosensory **Retina**.
  * *Retina Cytology:* Three cellular layers—photoreceptor cells (rods and cones), bipolar neurons, and ganglion cells whose axons converge to form the **Optic Nerve (Cranial Nerve II)**.
  * **Rods:** Contain the photosensitive purplish-red pigment **Rhodopsin (Visual Purple)**, a conjugated protein composed of the protein opsin and **11-cis-retinal** (aldehyde derivative of Vitamin A). Function in scotopic (dim-light / black-and-white) vision.
  * **Cones:** Contain **Iodopsin** pigments; mediate photopic (bright-light) and high-acuity color vision (Red, Green, Blue cones).
  * **Fovea Centralis (Macula Lutea):** A central retinal depression packed **exclusively with densely packed cones**; site of maximal visual acuity and highest resolution.
  * **Blind Spot (Optic Disc):** Point where optic nerve axons exit and retinal blood vessels enter; **completely devoid of photoreceptors (no rods or cones)**; insensitive to light.
* **The Human Ear:**
  * *Middle Ear:* Contains three articulating auditory ossicles that amplify sound pressure $20\text{-fold}$ from the tympanic membrane to the oval window: **Malleus (Hammer) $\longrightarrow$ Incus (Anvil) $\longrightarrow$ Stapes (Stirrup)**. (Stapes is the smallest bone in the human body). The **Eustachian Tube** connects middle ear cavity to nasopharynx, equalizing atmospheric pressure across the eardrum.
  * *Inner Ear (Labyrinth):*
    1. **Cochlea (Organ of Hearing):** Fluid-filled coiled spiral shell; the median scala media contains the **Organ of Corti** resting on the basilar membrane, displaying sensory hair cells with stereocilia capped by the tectorial membrane. Sound vibrations induce fluid waves flexing hair cells, depolarizing auditory nerve fibers.
    2. **Vestibular Apparatus (Organ of Equilibrium):** Composed of **Three Semicircular Canals** (oriented mutually perpendicular to each other; ampullae house the **Crista Ampullaris** for dynamic angular rotational equilibrium) and the **Otolith Organs** (**Utricle and Saccule**, containing the **Macula** with calcium carbonate otoliths for static linear acceleration and gravity detection).

## 25.3 THE HUMAN ENDOCRINE SYSTEM & SIGNAL TRANSDUCTION

Endocrine glands are ductless glands secreting chemical messengers called **Hormones** directly into the interstitial fluid and bloodstream.

```
                           HORMONE RECEPTOR MECHANICS
                                       │
           ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                       ▼
   [PEPTIDE & PROTEIN HORMONES]                             [STEROID & THYROID HORMONES]
   - Water-soluble, lipophobic                              - Lipid-soluble, lipophilic
   - CANNOT cross lipid bilayer                             - Diffuse directly through plasma membrane
   - Bind EXTRACELLULAR membrane receptors                  - Bind INTRACELLULAR receptors (Cytosol / Nucleus)
   - Generate SECOND MESSENGERS:                            - Hormone-Receptor complex binds specific
     cAMP, IP3, DAG, Ca2+                                     Hormone Response Elements (HRE) on DNA
```

### 25.3.1 Master Regulators: Hypothalamus & Pituitary Axis
The pituitary gland (**Hypophysis**) is cradled in a bony depression of the sphenoid bone called the **Sella Turcica**, connected to the hypothalamus via the hypophyseal infundibulum.
* **Adenohypophysis (Anterior Pituitary):** Regulated by hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting neurohormones delivered via the **Hypophyseal Portal System**:
  1. **Growth Hormone (GH / Somatotropin):** Promotes tissue mitosis, protein anabolism, and bone elongation.
     * *Hypersecretion in childhood:* **Gigantism** (proportional skeletal overgrowth).
     * *Hypersecretion in adulthood:* **Acromegaly** (disproportionate enlargement of facial bones, mandible [prognathism], hands, and feet).
     * *Hyposecretion in childhood:* **Pituitary Dwarfism** (stunted growth with normal mental and sexual development, "midgets").
  2. **Prolactin (PRL):** Stimulates mammary gland hyperplasia and milk synthesis.
  3. **Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH):** Stimulates thyroid gland growth and $\text{T}_3/\text{T}_4$ release.
  4. **Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (ACTH):** Stimulates adrenal cortex glucocorticoid secretion.
  5. **Gonadotropins (LH and FSH):** In females, **Luteinizing Hormone (LH)** triggers ovulation and maintains corpus luteum; **Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH)** drives ovarian follicular growth. In males, LH stimulates Leydig cells to secrete testosterone; FSH acts on Sertoli cells to induce spermiogenesis.
* **Neurohypophysis (Posterior Pituitary / Pars Nervosa):** Does NOT synthesize hormones; stores and discharges two hypothalamic octapeptides transported via axonal tracts:
  1. **Oxytocin ("Love / Birth Hormone"):** Induces powerful rhythmic contractions of the myometrium during parturition (positive feedback loop) and stimulates contraction of myoepithelial cells surrounding mammary alveoli, causing **Milk Ejection** ("let-down reflex").
  2. **Vasopressin (Antidiuretic Hormone, ADH):** Mediates renal water conservation via AQP2.

## 25.4 HIGH-YIELD PRACTICE QUESTIONS

### Question 1 (UPSC CSE Prelims Standard)
Consider the following hormonal pairs:
1. Insulin and Glucagon
2. Calcitonin and Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)
3. Melatonin and Prolactin
4. Aldosterone and Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)

Which of the pairs given above act antagonistically to each other in human physiology?
A) 1 and 2 only  
B) 1, 2 and 4 only  
C) 2 and 3 only  
D) 1, 2, 3 and 4  

**Analytical Answer with Epistemic Breakdown:**
* Pair 1: Insulin lowers blood glucose (hypoglycemic), whereas glucagon elevates blood glucose (hyperglycemic) $\implies$ **Antagonistic**.
* Pair 2: Calcitonin lowers blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (hypocalcemic), whereas PTH elevates blood $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (hypercalcemic) $\implies$ **Antagonistic**.
* Pair 3: Melatonin regulates circadian sleep-wake cycles; prolactin stimulates mammary lactation. They have unrelated physiological axes $\implies$ *Not antagonistic*.
* Pair 4: Aldosterone causes sodium and water retention (elevating blood pressure), whereas ANF induces renal sodium excretion (natriuresis) and lowers blood pressure $\implies$ **Antagonistic**.
* **Correct Option:** **B) 1, 2 and 4 only**.

### Question 2 (Competitive State PSC Standard)
Which region of the human brain functions as the primary coordinating center for autonomic thermoregulation (body temperature) and neuroendocrine homeostasis?
A) Cerebellum  
B) Thalamus  
C) Hypothalamus  
D) Medulla Oblongata  

**Analytical Answer:**
* The **Hypothalamus** contains the internal biological thermostat regulating thermogenesis and heat dissipation (sweating/shivering), alongside neurosecretory centers controlling hunger, thirst, satiety, and the endocrine system.
* **Correct Option:** **C) Hypothalamus**.

### Question 3 (Advanced Epistemic Standard)
A patient presents with persistent extreme thirst (polydipsia) and passes $15\text{ liters}$ of clear, dilute urine daily. Laboratory analysis reveals normal fasting blood glucose levels and no glucose in the urine. Which condition does this patient most likely have?
A) Diabetes Mellitus Type 1  
B) Diabetes Mellitus Type 2  
C) Diabetes Insipidus  
D) Addison's Disease  

**Analytical Answer:**
* Profuse dilute polyuria ($15\text{ L/day}$) in the presence of completely normal blood glucose and absence of glucosuria is the clinical hallmark of **Diabetes Insipidus**, caused by deficiency of Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin) or unresponsiveness of renal collecting duct $V_2$ receptors to ADH.
* **Correct Option:** **C) Diabetes Insipidus**.
