## 8. IIBF AFMB Unit 1: Accounting Concepts, Principles (GAAP) & Ind AS Framework

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP)**
> GAAP forms the standardized conceptual foundation that ensures financial statements across all business enterprises are uniform, consistent, comparable, and legally transparent.

## 📊 1. Master Matrix of 11 Fundamental Accounting Concepts

| Accounting Concept | Core Theoretical Definition | Practical Accounting Application in Banking / Business |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business Entity Concept | Business and its owners are distinct separate entities | Owner's drawings are debited to Drawing Account; Capital is shown as a liability on the Balance Sheet. |
| Money Measurement Concept | Only transactions quantifiable in monetary terms are recorded | Employee skill, brand loyalty, or staff health are not recorded in accounts. |
| Going Concern Concept | Business will continue operating indefinitely without liquidation intent | Fixed assets are recorded at historical cost less depreciation, not at current liquidation value. |
| Accounting Period Concept | Business life is divided into discrete time intervals (usually 1 year, 1 Apr to 31 Mar) | Mandates annual preparation of Profit & Loss Account and Balance Sheet. |
| Cost / Historical Cost Concept | Assets are recorded at the original acquisition purchase price | Market fluctuations in land value do not alter balance sheet asset value until realized. |
| Dual Aspect Concept | Every transaction affects at least two accounts with equal Debit and Credit | Fundamental Accounting Equation: \(\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Capital}\). |
| Accrual Concept | Revenue recognized when earned; Expense recognized when incurred | Interest earned on loans is credited as income even if not yet collected in cash. |
| Realization / Revenue Recognition | Revenue recognized when legal title/ownership of goods passes to buyer | Advance payment received from customer is a liability until goods are delivered. |
| Matching Concept | Revenues of a period must be matched with expenses incurred to generate that revenue | Depreciation on machinery used to manufacture goods is charged against the same year's sales revenue. |
| Conservatism / Prudence Concept | Anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible losses | Closing stock valued at **Cost or Net Realizable Value (Market Price), whichever is LOWER**; Provision for Bad Debts. |
| Materiality Concept | Items with significant economic impact must be disclosed separately | Insignificant items (e.g. pencil purchase) charged immediately to expense rather than capitalized as assets. |

## 🏛️ 2. Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) & IFRS

• **Ind AS Framework:** Converged with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) issued by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) under Section 133 of the Companies Act 2013.
• **Key Ind AS for Banks:**.
• • **Ind AS 109 (Financial Instruments):** Mandates **Expected Credit Loss (ECL)** model replacing the traditional incurred loss model for loan loss provisioning.
• • **Ind AS 115 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers):** Standardized 5-step model for revenue recognition.
• • **Ind AS 116 (Leases):** Requires recognition of Right-of-Use (RoU) assets and lease liabilities on the balance sheet for operating leases.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Accounting Concepts:
1. **Valuation of Closing Stock:** Valuing closing stock at *Cost or Market Price, whichever is LOWER* is an application of the **Prudence / Conservatism Concept**.
2. **Capital as a Liability:** Capital is shown as a liability on the Balance Sheet due to the **Business Entity Concept**.
3. **Accrual vs Cash Basis:** Recording accrued interest and outstanding rent at year-end is mandated by the **Accrual and Matching Concepts**.

## 9. IIBF AFMB Unit 2: Golden Rules of Accounting, Journalizing & Ledger Posting

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: The 3 Golden Rules of Double-Entry Bookkeeping**
> Under the double-entry bookkeeping system founded by Luca Pacioli (1494), every financial transaction involves two aspects: a Debit (Dr) and an equal Credit (Cr).

## 📊 1. Master Framework: 3 Types of Accounts & Their Golden Rules

| Account Category | Scope / Examples | Golden Rule for Debit (Dr) | Golden Rule for Credit (Cr) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal Accounts | Individuals (Ram A/c), Companies (TCS Ltd), Banks (SBI A/c), Representative (Outstanding Rent) | **Debit the Receiver** (Who receives value from business) | **Credit the Giver** (Who provides value to business) |
| Real Accounts | Tangible assets (Cash, Furniture, Machinery, Land) & Intangible assets (Goodwill, Patents) | **Debit what Comes In** (Asset acquired / increases) | **Credit what Goes Out** (Asset sold / decreases) |
| Nominal Accounts | Expenses & Losses (Rent, Salary, Interest paid) & Incomes & Gains (Commission, Interest received) | **Debit all Expenses & Losses** | **Credit all Incomes & Gains** |

## 📝 2. Step-by-Step Accounting Flow: Transaction to Ledger

• **Step 1: Source Document:** Transaction occurs supported by voucher, invoice, or receipt.
• **Step 2: Journalizing (Book of Original Entry):** Chronological recording of transactions with debit and credit legs accompanied by a brief explanation (*Narration*).
• **Step 3: Ledger Posting (Principal Book of Accounts):** Transferring debit and credit entries from Journal into individual T-shaped ledger accounts.
• **Step 4: Balancing Ledger Accounts:** Calculating the difference between total debit side and total credit side at month/year end to establish the closing balance (Debit Balance if Debit > Credit; Credit Balance if Credit > Debit).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Golden Rules:
1. **Outstanding Rent Classification:** 'Outstanding Rent A/c' is a **Representative Personal Account**, NOT a Nominal Account. (Rent is Nominal, but Outstanding Rent represents liability to landlords).
2. **Bank Account Classification:** A Bank Account (e.g. SBI Account) is a **Personal Account** (Artificial Legal Person), NOT a Real Account.
3. **Luca Pacioli Origin:** Double-entry bookkeeping was formulated by Italian mathematician **Luca Pacioli in 1494**.

## 10. IIBF AFMB Unit 3: Trial Balance, Rectification of Errors & Suspense Account

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Trial Balance as a Proof of Arithmetical Accuracy**
> A Trial Balance is a statement prepared at a particular date with debit and credit balances extracted from all ledger accounts to test the arithmetical accuracy of bookkeeping entries.

## 📊 1. Master Classification of Accounting Errors

| Error Category | Nature / Mechanism | Impact on Trial Balance Tally | Rectification Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Error of Principle | Transaction recorded in violation of fundamental accounting principles (e.g. Capital expenditure treated as Revenue expenditure) | ❌ **Trial Balance Tallies** (Does NOT affect total debits/credits) | Debit correct Asset A/c; Credit wrong Expense A/c |
| Error of Complete Omission | A transaction is completely omitted from being recorded in Journal and Ledger | ❌ **Trial Balance Tallies** | Pass original journal entry in full |
| Compensating Error | Two or more independent errors cancel each other out in terms of monetary effect | ❌ **Trial Balance Tallies** | Rectify individual erroneous ledger accounts |
| Error of Commission (Posting to Wrong Account) | Amount posted to wrong account of the same class (e.g. Debited to Shyam instead of Ram) | ❌ **Trial Balance Tallies** | Debit correct person; Credit wrong person |
| Error of Partial Omission | Transaction entered in Journal but posted only to one ledger leg | 🚨 **Trial Balance Fails to Tally** | Rectified via Suspense Account |
| Error in Casting / Under-casting | Wrong mathematical totaling of a subsidiary book or ledger account | 🚨 **Trial Balance Fails to Tally** | Rectified via Suspense Account |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Trial Balance & Errors:
1. **Capital Expenditure Charged to Revenue:** Treating installation charges of new machinery as 'Repairs' is an **Error of Principle**. It will **NOT** affect the agreement of the Trial Balance.
2. **Suspense Account Nature:** Suspense Account is a temporary account. If it has a debit balance at final accounts stage, it appears on the **Assets side** of Balance Sheet; if credit balance, on the **Liabilities side**.
3. **Compensating Errors:** When Ram's account is over-debited by ₹500 and Shyam's account is under-debited by ₹500, they are **Compensating Errors**.

## 11. IIBF AFMB Unit 4: Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Dual Perspectives in Bank Reconciliation**
> A Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS) is a periodic statement prepared by an account holder to reconcile the balance shown in their Cash Book (Bank Column) with the balance shown in the Bank's Passbook / Statement.

## 📊 1. Master Reconciling Adjustments Table

| Transaction / Cause of Discrepancy | Cash Book Impact | Pass Book Impact | Action when Starting from Cash Book (+ / −) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cheques issued to suppliers but not yet presented for payment | Deducted (−) | Not yet deducted (Higher) | **ADD (+)** to Cash Book |
| Cheques deposited into bank but not yet cleared / collected | Added (+) | Not yet credited (Lower) | **SUBTRACT (−)** from Cash Book |
| Interest credited directly by bank in Passbook | Not yet recorded (Lower) | Added (+) | **ADD (+)** to Cash Book |
| Direct payment made by bank under standing instructions (Insurance/Rent) | Not yet recorded (Higher) | Deducted (−) | **SUBTRACT (−)** from Cash Book |
| Bank charges and commission debited by bank | Not yet recorded (Higher) | Deducted (−) | **SUBTRACT (−)** from Cash Book |
| Direct deposit by customer into bank account | Not yet recorded (Lower) | Added (+) | **ADD (+)** to Cash Book |
| Dishonor of cheque discounted / deposited | Recorded as cash in hand | Debited (−) | **SUBTRACT (−)** from Cash Book |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on BRS:
1. **Passbook Debit Balance:** A **Debit balance in Bank Passbook** indicates an **OVERDRAFT** (unfavorable balance for customer).
2. **Cash Book Credit Balance:** A **Credit balance in Cash Book** indicates an **OVERDRAFT**.
3. **Cheques Issued but Not Presented:** When starting with a favorable Cash Book balance, cheques issued but not presented must be **ADDED (+)**.

## 12. IIBF AFMB Unit 5: Depreciation Accounting & Mathematical Methods

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Depreciation as Cost Allocation (Ind AS 16 / AS 10)**
> Depreciation is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of a tangible fixed asset over its estimated useful economic life, reflecting wear and tear, passage of time, and obsolescence.

## 🧮 1. Mathematical Formulas for Core Depreciation Methods

$\text{Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Original Cost} - \text{Estimated Scrap Value}}{\text{Useful Life in Years}}$

*Explanation:* Equal amount of depreciation charged every year throughout asset life. Book value reaches exact scrap value at end of useful life.

$D_t = r \times \text{Book Value}_{t-1}, \quad \text{where } r = 1 - \sqrt[n]{\frac{\text{Scrap Value}}{\text{Cost}}}$

*Explanation:* Depreciation is calculated on opening written-down book value each year. Depreciation amount decreases progressively each year. Book value never mathematically reaches zero.

$D_t = \frac{\text{Remaining Life at Start of Year}}{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}} \times (\text{Cost} - \text{Scrap Value})$

*Explanation:* An accelerated depreciation method where higher depreciation is charged in initial years.

## 📊 2. Master Comparison: SLM vs WDV Method

| Parameter | Straight Line Method (SLM) | Written Down Value Method (WDV) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calculation Basis | Calculated on **Original Cost** every year | Calculated on **Written Down Book Value** of that year |
| Annual Depreciation Amount | Constant / Fixed every year | Declines progressively every year |
| Asset Book Value at End of Life | Becomes exactly Equal to Scrap Value (or Zero) | Never reaches Zero |
| Income Tax Act Recognition | Recognized only for Power Generating units | Mandatorily recognized for all business classes under Income Tax Act 1961 |
| Combined Impact (Depreciation + Repairs) | Unfair impact: Total burden increases in later years as repairs rise | Balanced impact: Falling depreciation offsets rising repair costs |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Depreciation:
1. **Depreciation on Land:** Land is **NEVER depreciated** under accounting standards because its useful life is unlimited.
2. **Income Tax Act Requirement:** For tax computation under the Income Tax Act 1961, **WDV method is mandatorily used** across blocks of assets.
3. **WDV Zero Book Value Trap:** Under WDV method, the book value of an asset can **never mathematically reach absolute zero**.
