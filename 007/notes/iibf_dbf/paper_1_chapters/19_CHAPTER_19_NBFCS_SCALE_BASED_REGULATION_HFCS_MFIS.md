# NBFCs SCALE-BASED REGULATION, HFCs & MICROFINANCE (MFIs)

Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) and Microfinance Institutions (MFIs) deliver specialized credit to credit-rationed borrowers, MSMEs, and rural households. The Reserve Bank of India regulates this sector through the four-tiered Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) framework and the harmonized 2022 Microfinance Directions.

---

## § 19.1 Unit 24: Micro Finance Institutions (MFIs)

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 24)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Microfinance delivers collateral-free credit to low-income households. The unified 2022 RBI regulatory framework applies activity-based standards across all lenders, establishing an annual household income cap of ₹3,00,000, a 50% debt-service outflow ceiling, and zero prepayment penalties.

### 1. Delivery Models in Indian Microfinance

• **Self-Help Group – Bank Linkage Programme (SBLP):**
  - Pioneered by **NABARD in 1992** based on the recommendations of the **S.K. Kalia Committee**.
  - A homogeneous group of **10 to 20 rural individuals** (primarily women) pooling periodic small savings into a common fund.
  - After 6 months of successful internal lending, commercial banks extend collateral-free credit linked to savings (scaling from 1:1 up to 1:4 or higher).
• **Joint Liability Groups (JLGs):**
  - An informal group of **4 to 10 individuals** borrowing funds from banks or MFIs under mutual co-guarantee without requiring prior joint savings.

### 2. RBI Harmonized Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans (Effective 1 April 2022)

This framework replaced all previous entity-specific guidelines with an activity-based regulation across ALL Regulated Entities (Commercial Banks, SFBs, RRBs, Co-op Banks, NBFCs, NBFC-MFIs):

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 CORE MANDATES OF THE 2022 RBI MICROFINANCE RULES            │
│                                                                             │
│  1. HOUSEHOLD INCOME CEILING  :  ₹3,00,000 per annum (Urban & Rural)        │
│  2. COLLATERAL REQUIREMENT    :  STRICTLY COLLATERAL-FREE (Zero security)    │
│  3. DEBT-SERVICE OUTFLOW CAP  :  MAXIMUM 50% of monthly household income    │
│  4. PREPAYMENT PENALTY        :  STRICTLY ZERO (Foreclosure fees banned)     │
│  5. NBFC-MFI QUALIFYING ASSETS:  Minimum 75% of total assets (down from 85%)│
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

• **Assessment of Household Income:** A "Household" is defined as an individual unit comprising husband, wife, and their unmarried children. Lenders must conduct a verified board-approved income assessment.  
• **Limit on Outflow (Debt Servicing Burden):** Regulated entities cannot sanction a microfinance loan if the total monthly debt repayment (principal + interest across all existing loans) exceeds **50% of total monthly household income**.  
• **Pricing of Credit:** RBI dismantled the previously prescribed interest rate caps and margin caps for NBFC-MFIs. Interest rates must be determined by a board-approved policy with a transparent component breakdown disclosed in the **Key Fact Statement (KFS)**.

## § 19.2 Unit 25: Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 25)**  
> **Core Proposition:** NBFCs are registered under the Companies Act and regulated under Chapter IIIB of the RBI Act. They must pass the 50-50 Principal Business Test. Under the Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) framework, the Top 10 eligible NBFCs by asset size are automatically placed in the Upper Layer subject to bank-like regulations.

### 1. NBFCs vs. Commercial Banks Core Differences

| Parameter | Commercial Banks | Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Incorporation / Licensing** | Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (Section 22) | Companies Act, 2013 + RBI Act, 1934 (Section 45-IA) |
| **Demand Deposits (CASA)** | **Permitted** (Can accept savings & current deposits) | **Strictly PROHIBITED** (Cannot accept demand deposits) |
| **Payment & Settlement System** | Part of clearing system (Cheque issuance on self) | **Cannot issue cheques drawn on themselves**; not part of payment clearing system |
| **Deposit Insurance (DICGC)** | **Covered up to ₹5 Lakh per depositor** | **Zero deposit insurance cover** (Deposits with NBFCs are NOT insured by DICGC) |
| **Statutory Reserves** | Mandatory CRR & SLR | No CRR; Deposit-taking NBFCs maintain liquid assets (15% of public deposits in approved securities) |

• **The 50-50 Principal Business Test:** An entity qualifies as an NBFC if its **financial assets constitute more than 50% of its total assets**, AND its **income from financial assets constitutes more than 50% of its gross income**.

---

### 2. RBI Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) 4-Layer Pyramid

The Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) framework categorizes NBFCs into four tiers based on size, activity, and systemic risk:

```
                          ┌─────────────┐
                          │  TOP LAYER  │  (Currently Empty; extreme systemic risk)
                       ┌──┴─────────────┴──┐
                       │    UPPER LAYER    │  (TOP 10 ELIGIBLE BY ASSET SIZE +
                    ┌──┴───────────────────┴──┐ Score-based inclusions; Bank-like rules)
                    │       MIDDLE LAYER      │  (All NBFC-D + Non-deposit ≥ ₹1,000 Cr;
                 ┌──┴─────────────────────────┴──┐ HFCs, SPDs, CICs, Infrastructure FIs)
                 │          BASE LAYER           │  (Non-deposit < ₹1,000 Cr; P2P, AA;
                 └───────────────────────────────┘  Net Owned Funds min ₹10 Cr)
```

| SBR Layer | Qualifying Criteria & Scale | Regulatory Stringency & Governance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top Layer (NBFC-TL)** | Systemically extreme risk entities (Currently empty; populated only if RBI identifies extreme contagion/spillover risk). | Highest possible supervisory scrutiny and customized capital buffers. |
| **Upper Layer (NBFC-UL)** | • **Top 10 Eligible NBFCs by Asset Size** shall **ALWAYS be included in the Upper Layer**, irrespective of any other factor.<br>• Additional NBFCs identified based on a parametric scoring methodology (Size 35%, Interconnectedness 25%, Complexity 10%, Nature of Activity 30%). | **Bank-Like Regulations:**<br>• Mandatory **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) of 9.0%**.<br>• Mandatory listing on stock exchange within 3 years.<br>• Standard asset provisioning aligned with banks.<br>• Mandatory Board Risk Committees, CCO, and CRO. |
| **Middle Layer (NBFC-ML)** | • **All Deposit-Taking NBFCs (NBFC-D)** irrespective of asset size.<br>• Non-Deposit taking NBFCs with **Asset Size ≥ ₹1,000 Crore**.<br>• All **Housing Finance Companies (HFCs)**, Standalone Primary Dealers (SPDs), Core Investment Companies (CICs), and Infrastructure Finance Companies (IFCs). | Standard capital adequacy (CRAR 15%), 90-day NPA classification, appointment of Chief Risk Officer (CRO). |
| **Base Layer (NBFC-BL)** | • Non-deposit taking NBFCs with **Asset Size < ₹1,000 Crore**.<br>• NBFC-Peer to Peer (P2P), NBFC-Account Aggregators (AA), and Non-Operative Financial Holding Companies (NOFHC). | Light-touch regulation; Net Owned Funds (NOF) requirement raised in phases to **₹10 Crore**. |

### 3. Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) under RBI Oversight

• **Regulatory Transfer:** Under the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019, regulatory powers over Housing Finance Companies were transferred from the **National Housing Bank (NHB) to the Reserve Bank of India** (NHB retains supervisory inspection).  
• **Principal Business Criteria for HFCs:**
  - Minimum **60% of total assets** must be deployed in providing housing finance.
  - Of this, at least **50% of total assets** must be deployed in **individual housing loans**.

### 4. Co-Lending Model (CLM 80:20)

• **Regulatory Mandate:** RBI Master Circular on Co-Lending by Banks and NBFCs to the Priority Sector.  
• **Mandatory Minimum Risk Retention:** The NBFC must retain a **minimum 20% share of each individual loan** on its balance sheet; the partner Commercial Bank takes up to **80%**.  
• **Single Blended Interest Rate:** The borrower is charged a single blended interest rate reflecting the weighted average of the bank's and NBFC's agreed cost of funds.

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 24 & Unit 25 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — Upper Layer Membership Rule:** Under RBI SBR guidelines, the **Top 10 eligible NBFCs by asset size are AUTOMATICALLY in the Upper Layer**. It is NOT the top 25 or top 50!
2. **Trap — Microfinance Income Limit:** The household annual income limit for microfinance loans is a uniform **₹3,00,000 across BOTH rural and urban areas**. An option stating ₹1.2 Lakh rural and ₹2 Lakh urban is **outdated**.
3. **Trap — Prepayment Penalty in Microfinance:** Prepayment penalties on microfinance loans are **strictly 0% (prohibited)** under RBI Master Directions.
4. **Trap — DICGC Insurance on NBFC Deposits:** Public deposits held with deposit-taking NBFCs are **NOT insured by DICGC**. DICGC covers deposits with commercial and cooperative banks up to ₹5 Lakh, but **zero cover for NBFCs**.
5. **Trap — Co-Lending Risk Retention:** In the Co-Lending Model (CLM), the NBFC must retain **at least 20% risk share** on its own books.

### Unit 24 & Unit 25 Practice Questions (IIBF DB&F Pattern)

**Q3. [CONCEPT-RECURRING / CO-LENDING 80:20] Under the Co-Lending Model (CLM) between Scheduled Commercial Banks and NBFCs for priority sector lending, what is the mandatory minimum share of individual loans that the NBFC must retain on its own balance sheet?**  
A. 10%  
B. 20%  
C. 30%  
D. 50%  

**Q4. [CONCEPT-RECURRING / NBFC DEPOSIT TRAPS] Consider the following statements regarding Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs):**  
Statement I: NBFCs passing the 50-50 Principal Business Test are permitted to accept demand deposits (savings and current accounts) from the general public.  
Statement II: Fixed deposits accepted by deposit-taking NBFCs (NBFC-D) are covered by deposit insurance provided by the DICGC up to ₹5 Lakh per depositor.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?  
A. Statement I only  
B. Statement II only  
C. Both Statement I and Statement II  
D. Neither Statement I nor Statement II  

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under RBI SBR directions, the top ten eligible NBFCs by asset size shall always reside in the Upper Layer, alongside other entities selected via systemic risk scoring.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** The 2022 unified guidelines established a uniform annual household income ceiling of ₹3,00,000 across all geographical areas.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** The co-lending framework mandates that the NBFC must retain a minimum 20% share of the individual loans on its books, with the bank taking up to 80%.  
• **Q4 — Answer: D.** Both statements are false. NBFCs are strictly prohibited from accepting demand deposits (Statement I is false), and NBFC deposits are NOT covered by DICGC deposit insurance (Statement II is false).
