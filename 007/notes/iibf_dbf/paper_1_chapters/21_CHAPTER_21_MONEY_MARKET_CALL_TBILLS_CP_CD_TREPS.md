# MONEY MARKET ARCHITECTURE: CALL, T-BILLS, CP, CD & TREPS

The Money Market facilitates short-term wholesale liquidity allocation for maturities up to one year (365 days) under the regulatory authority of the Reserve Bank of India. This chapter examines the structural segregation of financial markets (Unit 29) alongside money market instruments, Certificates of Deposit, Bills Rediscounting (BRDS), and Long-Term Repo Operations (LTRO) (Unit 30).

---

## § 21.1 Unit 29: Financial Markets - An Overview

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 29)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Financial markets are segmented by instrument maturity into Money Markets (maturity ≤ 1 year, liquidity management under RBI) and Capital Markets (maturity > 1 year, long-term capital formation under SEBI), operating across primary and secondary trading venues.

### 1. Money Market vs. Capital Market Master Matrix

| Parameter | Money Market | Capital Market |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maturity Horizon** | **Short-Term (Up to 1 Year / 365 Days)** | **Medium and Long-Term (Exceeding 1 Year)** |
| **Primary Statutory Regulator** | **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** | **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** |
| **Core Instruments** | Call/Notice Money, T-Bills, Commercial Paper (CP), Certificates of Deposit (CD), TREPS, Commercial Bills (BRDS). | Equity Shares, Preference Shares, Debentures, Corporate Bonds, Government Securities (G-Secs), Financial Derivatives. |
| **Economic Purpose** | Liquidity balancing, reserve requirement management (CRR/SLR), short-term cash management. | Long-term capital expenditure, fixed asset investment, corporate equity expansion. |
| **Risk & Return Profile** | **Low Default Risk, Low Return, High Liquidity** | **Higher Risk, Variable Capital Return, Tenor Risk** |
| **Primary Participants** | RBI, Commercial Banks, Primary Dealers, Financial Institutions, Mutual Funds, Corporates. | Retail Investors, Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs), Mutual Funds, Insurance Companies, Corporates. |

## § 21.2 Unit 30: Money Market Instruments Suite

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 30)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Money market instruments provide short-term liquidity management for banks, the sovereign, and corporate borrowers. Under RBI directions, both Commercial Paper (CP) and Certificates of Deposit (CD) carry a minimum denomination of ₹5 Lakh.

### 1. Call, Notice & Term Money Market

• **Call Money:** Interbank borrowing or lending for **Overnight (1 Day)** to address immediate reserve (CRR) mismatches.  
• **Notice Money:** Interbank borrowing or lending for **2 Days up to 14 Days**.  
• **Term Money:** Interbank borrowing or lending for **15 Days up to 1 Year (365 Days)** without collateral.  
• **Eligible Participants:** Scheduled Commercial Banks, Payment Banks, Small Finance Banks, Cooperative Banks, and Primary Dealers (PDs). Non-bank institutions (corporates, mutual funds) are **prohibited from lending/borrowing** in the uncollateralized call/notice market.

---

### 2. Treasury Bills (T-Bills) & Cash Management Bills (CMBs)

• **Issuing Authority:** Issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Central Government.  
• **Issuance Nature:** Issued at a **discount to face value and redeemed at par (₹100)**; carry zero coupon interest.  
• **Tenors & Auction Schedule (Current RBI Auction Calendar):**
  - **91-Day T-Bills:** Weekly auction (every Wednesday).
  - **182-Day T-Bills:** Weekly auction (every Wednesday).
  - **364-Day T-Bills:** Weekly auction (every Wednesday).
• **Minimum Denomination:** Multiples of **₹10,000**. Eligible for SLR maintenance by commercial banks.  
• **Cash Management Bills (CMBs):** Short-term non-standardized zero-coupon bills issued to meet temporary cash flow mismatches of the Central Government for **maturities of less than 91 days**.

---

### 3. Commercial Paper (CP) vs. Certificates of Deposit (CD)

| Parameter | Commercial Paper (CP) | Certificates of Deposit (CD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Instrument Character** | Unsecured promissory note issued by corporate borrowers | Negotiable, transferable promissory note issued against bank term deposits |
| **Eligible Issuers** | Highly rated Corporates, Primary Dealers (PDs), All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs) | **Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs)** and select All-India Financial Institutions |
| **Minimum Denomination** | **₹5 Lakh** (and in multiples of ₹5 Lakh thereafter) | **₹5 Lakh** (and in multiples of ₹5 Lakh thereafter) (*Current RBI Master Direction*) |
| **Maturity / Tenor Range** | **Minimum 7 Days up to Maximum 1 Year** | • **Issued by Banks:** **Min 7 Days to Max 1 Year**<br>• **Issued by FIs:** **Min 1 Year to Max 3 Years** |
| **Credit Rating Mandate** | Minimum rating of **A3** (or equivalent) by SEBI-registered CRA | Rating mandatory if issued by FIs; banks issue based on board policy |
| **Loans Against Instrument** | **Strictly PROHIBITED** (Banks cannot grant loans against CP) | **Strictly PROHIBITED** (Banks cannot grant loans against their own CDs) |
| **Secondary Market Trading** | Dematerialized format (FBIL benchmark reference / FIMMDA conventions) | Dematerialized format; transferable by delivery and endorsement |

> **Critical Regulatory Update — CD Minimum Denomination:**  
> Under the RBI's Master Direction on Certificates of Deposit, the minimum denomination of a CD is **₹5 Lakh and in multiples of ₹5 Lakh thereafter** (identical to Commercial Paper). An option citing ₹1 Lakh is **outdated and invalid**.

### 4. Triparty Repo (TREPS)

• **Mechanism:** A repurchase agreement where a third party—the **Clearing Corporation of India Limited (CCIL)**—acts as an intermediary to provide collateral valuation, allocation, and settlement services.  
• **Risk Mitigation:** CCIL acts as the **Central Counterparty (CCP)**, executing multilateral netting and trade novation. This **mitigates counterparty credit risk** across interbank repo trades, enabling non-bank entities (Mutual Funds, Insurance companies) to participate in overnight secured lending.

---

### 5. Bills Rediscounting Scheme (BRDS)

• **Origin & Historical Basis:** Introduced by the RBI in **November 1970** following the recommendations of the Narasimham Working Group (Study Group on the Bill Market Scheme) to foster a genuine, liquid commercial trade bill market in India.  
• **Operational Mechanism:**
  1. A seller draws a bill of exchange on the buyer against a genuine domestic trade sale of goods; the buyer accepts the bill.
  2. The seller discounts the trade bill with their commercial bank.
  3. Under BRDS, the discounting bank, to manage short-term liquidity, **rediscounts the eligible commercial bill** with the RBI or approved institutional rediscounting agencies (LIC, GIC, UTI, other commercial banks).
• **Statutory Eligibility Rules:**
  - Must be a **genuine trade bill** arising from the actual physical sale and delivery of goods. **Accommodation bills (kite-flying finance bills) are strictly prohibited**.
  - Must carry at least **two good signatures** (the drawer/seller and the drawee/buyer or an accepting bank).
  - Original tenor of the underlying trade bill cannot exceed **180 days**, while the rediscounting maturity is typically capped at **90 days**.

---

### 6. Long-Term Repo Operations (LTRO & TLTRO)

• **Institutional Purpose:** Introduced by the RBI in **February 2020** as a specialized liquidity injection tool to transmit policy rate cuts directly into corporate borrowing costs when money market spreads widen during stress periods.  
• **Mechanics:**
  - Unlike standard Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) repos which operate overnight or for 7/14-day tenors, LTRO provides banks with durable central bank liquidity for **tenors of 1 year and 3 years at the prevailing policy repo rate**.
  - Banks place Government Securities as collateral and receive term liquidity at a fixed, low borrowing cost.
• **Targeted Long-Term Repo Operations (TLTRO):**
  - Launched during the March 2020 pandemic economic shock.
  - Specified tranches (e.g. TLTRO 1.0, TLTRO 2.0) required borrowing banks to deploy the liquidity into **investment-grade corporate bonds, commercial paper, and non-convertible debentures** of targeted sectors (especially NBFCs, MFIs, and HFCs), successfully reviving corporate debt liquidity.

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 29 & Unit 30 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — CD vs CP Minimum Denomination:** Both Commercial Paper (CP) and Certificates of Deposit (CD) have the **exact same minimum denomination of ₹5 Lakh and in multiples of ₹5 Lakh thereafter**. Do NOT choose ₹1 Lakh for CD.
2. **Trap — Bank Loans Against CD/CP:** Banks are **STRICTLY PROHIBITED from granting loans or advances against their own CDs or against CPs**.
3. **Trap — Call vs Notice vs Term Tenors:** Call Money is **1 day (overnight)**; Notice Money is **2 to 14 days**; Term Money is **15 to 365 days**.
4. **Trap — TREPS Counterparty Risk:** TREPS does not eliminate all systemic risk through magic; it **mitigates counterparty credit risk through CCP multilateral netting and novation by CCIL**.
5. **Trap — BRDS Bill Eligibility:** An accommodation bill (a bill drawn without an underlying commercial trade transaction to raise finance) is **strictly ineligible for rediscounting under BRDS**. Only genuine commercial trade bills qualify.
6. **Trap — LTRO Tenor:** LTROs operate for **1-year and 3-year tenors**, not standard 14-day repo tenors.

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** Under current RBI directions, the minimum denomination for both Commercial Paper (CP) and Certificates of Deposit (CD) is ₹5 Lakh and multiples of ₹5 Lakh thereafter.  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** Under BRDS, only genuine commercial trade bills with two good signatures are eligible; accommodation bills are strictly barred.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** LTRO provides term liquidity for 1-year and 3-year tenors at the policy repo rate, facilitating credit transmission.  
• **Q4 — Answer: A.** Statement I is correct (banks cannot grant loans against their own CDs). Statement II is incorrect because non-banks (corporates, mutual funds) are strictly barred from participating in the uncollateralized Call/Notice money market.
