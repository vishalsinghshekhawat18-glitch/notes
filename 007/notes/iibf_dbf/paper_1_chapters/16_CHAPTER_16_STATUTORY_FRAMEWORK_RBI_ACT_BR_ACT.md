# STATUTORY FRAMEWORK: RBI ACT, 1934 & BANKING REGULATION ACT, 1949

The statutory governance of the Indian banking and monetary system is anchored by two foundational parliamentary enactments: the **Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934** (which established and empowers the central bank) and the **Banking Regulation Act, 1949** (which provides comprehensive prudential oversight, licensing, and management supervision over commercial and cooperative banks).

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 22)**  
> **Core Proposition:** The RBI Act, 1934 (Chapters I–V and Schedules) governs central banking functions, note issuance, minimum reserves, CRR, and the statutory Monetary Policy Committee. ## Part I: Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 (Chapter-by-Chapter Statutory Map)

The RBI Act, 1934 comprises **Chapters I to V** alongside four primary **Schedules**:

### 1. Master Section Index: RBI Act, 1934

| Chapter & Section | Statutory Subject Matter | Key Legal Provision & Examination Point |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chapter I: Sec 1 & 2** | Preliminary & Definitions | • **Sec 2(e):** Defines a **"Scheduled Bank"** as a bank included in the **Second Schedule** of the RBI Act. |
| **Chapter II: Sec 3** | Establishment of the Bank | Mandates establishment of the RBI to regulate bank notes, maintain reserves for monetary stability, and operate the currency and credit system. |
| **Chapter II: Sec 4** | Capital of the Bank | Original share capital was **₹5 Crore** divided into 5 lakh shares of ₹100 each. Entire capital nationalized by GoI on **1 January 1949**. |
| **Chapter II: Sec 7** | Central Government Directions | Empowers Central Government, after consultation with the Governor, to give directions to the RBI in the public interest. |
| **Chapter II: Sec 8** | Composition of Central Board | **21 Members:** Governor + up to 4 Deputy Governors (appointed by GoI for up to 5 yrs); 4 Directors from Local Boards; 10 Directors nominated by GoI; 2 Government officials. |
| **Chapter II: Sec 9** | Local Boards | Four Local Boards (Western, Eastern, Northern, Southern areas based in **Mumbai, Kolkata, New Delhi, Chennai**). 5 members each; 4-year term. |
| **Chapter II: Sec 17** | **Permitted Business of RBI** | • **17(1):** Accepting deposits without interest.<br>• **17(2):** Purchase, sale, and rediscount of commercial bills and export bills.<br>• **17(3):** Buying and selling foreign exchange.<br>• **17(4):** Short-term loans and advances to banks against approved collateral.<br>• **17(5):** **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** to Central and State Govts (repayable within 3 months).<br>• **17(8):** Purchase and sale of Government Securities (OMO). |
| **Chapter II: Sec 18** | Emergency / Special Advances | Direct discounting of bills or loans to banks when normal financial channels are impaired (Lender of Last Resort). |
| **Chapter II: Sec 19** | **Prohibited Business of RBI** | RBI **CANNOT**: engage in direct trade; purchase its own shares or shares of other banks; make loans on mortgage of immovable property; grant unsecured advances; or **pay interest on deposits**. |
| **Chapter III: Sec 20 & 21** | Banker to Central Government | Obligation and right of the RBI to transact banking business of the Central Government. |
| **Chapter III: Sec 21A** | Banker to State Governments | RBI transacts banking business of State Governments by voluntary bilateral agreement. |
| **Chapter III: Sec 22** | **Sole Right to Issue Notes** | RBI possesses the exclusive monopoly to issue bank notes in India (except **₹1 notes and coins**, which are issued by the Ministry of Finance). |
| **Chapter III: Sec 24** | Denominations of Notes | Permitted denominations: ₹2, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500, ₹2000, or any other denomination **not exceeding ₹10,000**. |
| **Chapter III: Sec 26** | **Legal Tender Character** | • **26(1):** Every bank note is legal tender across India.<br>• **26(2):** Central Government, upon recommendation of the Central Board, can declare **any series of bank notes of any denomination to cease to be legal tender** (Statutory basis of Demonetisation). |
| **Chapter III: Sec 27** | Re-issue of Notes | Prohibits RBI from putting into re-circulation notes that are torn, defaced, or excessively soiled. |
| **Chapter III: Sec 28** | Recovery of Lost/Mutilated Notes | RBI is not bound to refund lost or stolen notes; governed by **RBI (Note Refund) Rules**. |
| **Chapter III: Sec 31** | Bearer Demand Bills | Restricts issue of demand bills/promissory notes payable to bearer exclusively to the RBI and Central Government. |
| **Chapter III: Sec 33** | **Minimum Reserve System** | Assets of Issue Department must not be less than **₹200 Crore** at any time: **Gold Bullion/Coins ≥ ₹115 Crore**; Foreign Securities make up the remaining balance (**₹85 Crore**). |
| **Chapter III: Sec 42** | **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** | • **42(1):** Mandates every Scheduled Bank to maintain with RBI an average daily balance of not less than a prescribed percentage of its Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL).<br>• Amendment 2006 **removed the statutory floor of 3% and ceiling of 20%**.<br>• **42(1A):** Incremental CRR (I-CRR) to impound excess liquidity surges.<br>• **42(6):** Qualifying conditions for inclusion in **Second Schedule**: Paid-up capital and reserves **≥ ₹5 Lakh**, and affairs not conducted to the detriment of depositors. |
| **Chapter IIIB: Sec 45-IA** | Regulation of NBFCs | Mandatory Certificate of Registration (CoR) and minimum Net Owned Funds (NOF) to commence NBFC business. |
| **Chapter IIIF: Sec 45ZA–ZN** | **Monetary Policy Framework** | • **45ZA:** Inflation target set once every 5 years ($4\% \pm 2\%$).<br>• **45ZB:** 6-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC).<br>• **45ZG:** Quorum of 4 members.<br>• **45ZH:** At least 4 meetings per year.<br>• **45ZN:** Failure report if inflation breaches target for **3 consecutive quarters**. |
| **Chapter IV: Sec 47** | Allocation of Surplus Profits | After provisions for bad debts and depreciation, RBI transfers surplus net profits to the Central Government. |
| **Chapter IV: Sec 49** | Publication of Bank Rate | Mandates publication of standard rate at which RBI buys or rediscounts eligible bills. |
| **Schedules I to IV** | First to Fourth Schedules | • **First Schedule:** Four regional territories of Local Boards.<br>• **Second Schedule:** Master list of **Scheduled Commercial and Cooperative Banks**.<br>• **Third Schedule:** Declaration of secrecy by Board members.<br>• **Fourth Schedule:** Repealed provisions. |

## Part II: Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (Section-by-Section Statutory Map)

The Banking Regulation Act, 1949 comprises **Sections 1 to 56** organized across **Parts I to V**:

### 1. Master Section Index: Banking Regulation Act, 1949

| Section Number | Statutory Subject Matter | Key Legal Mandate & Exam Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sec 3** | Non-Applicability | The Act does NOT apply to Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS) or Cooperative Land Mortgage Banks. |
| **Sec 5(b)** | **Definition of "Banking"** | *"Accepting, for the purpose of lending or investment, of deposits of money from the public, repayable on demand or otherwise, and withdrawable by cheque, draft, order or otherwise."* |
| **Sec 5(c)** | Definition of Banking Company | Any company which transacts the business of banking in India. |
| **Sec 6** | Permitted Forms of Business | Explicitly lists 17 permitted ancillary activities: borrowing, lending, discounting bills, issuing letters of credit, guarantees, underwriting, safety lockers, acting as agent/trustee, factoring. |
| **Sec 7** | Use of Words "Bank" / "Banker" | Every banking company must use at least one of the words **"bank"**, **"banker"**, **"banking"**, or **"banking company"** as part of its name. Non-banking entities are strictly prohibited from using these words. |
| **Sec 8** | **Prohibition of Trading** | No banking company shall directly or indirectly deal in buying, selling, or bartering of goods (except when realizing security pledged against debts). |
| **Sec 9** | **Disposal of Non-Banking Assets** | Any immovable property acquired by a bank in satisfaction of claims/debts must be disposed of within **7 years**. RBI may extend this period by up to **5 additional years**. |
| **Sec 10A to 10D** | Board of Directors Qualifications | At least **51% of Directors** must possess specialized knowledge or practical experience in accountancy, agriculture, banking, cooperation, economics, finance, law, or small-scale industries. |
| **Sec 12** | Capital Structure & Voting Rights | • Subscribed capital must be at least **50% of Authorized capital**.<br>• Paid-up capital must be at least **50% of Subscribed capital**.<br>• Voting rights of any single shareholder in private banks capped at **26%** by RBI. |
| **Sec 17** | **Statutory Reserve Fund** | Every banking company must transfer **not less than 20% of its net profit** (raised to **25% by RBI Master Direction**) of each year to a Reserve Fund before declaring any dividend. |
| **Sec 18** | Cash Reserve for Non-Scheduled Banks | Non-scheduled banks must maintain a cash reserve of at least **3% of their NDTL** in cash or current account with the RBI. |
| **Sec 19** | **Restriction on Subsidiaries** | • Bank cannot form a subsidiary except for permitted activities under Sec 6.<br>• Bank cannot hold shares in any company exceeding **30% of that company's paid-up share capital + reserves**, OR **30% of the bank's own paid-up share capital + reserves**, whichever is less. |
| **Sec 20** | **Restrictions on Loans and Advances** | • Bank **CANNOT grant loans on the security of its own shares**.<br>• Bank cannot grant loans to its own Directors or firms/entities in which its Directors are interested (without prior Board/RBI approval). |
| **Sec 21** | Control over Advances by RBI | Empowers RBI to issue directions on credit policy, margins on secured loans, maximum exposure limits, and lending interest rates. |
| **Sec 21A** | Protection Against Scrutiny of Rates | Rates of interest charged by banks **cannot be reopened or scrutinized by courts** under the Usurious Loans Act, 1918. |
| **Sec 22** | **Licensing of Banking Companies** | Mandatory requirement to obtain a **License from the RBI** before commencing or carrying on banking business in India. |
| **Sec 23** | Branch Licensing | Prior permission of RBI required for opening a new place of business or changing existing locations. |
| **Sec 24** | **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** | Every banking company must maintain liquid assets (cash, gold, or unencumbered approved G-Secs) equal to a prescribed percentage of its NDTL (Statutory ceiling: **40%**; floor of 25% eliminated in 2007). |
| **Sec 26** | Return of Unclaimed Deposits | Mandates annual return of all deposit accounts that have not been operated for **10 years**. |
| **Sec 26A** | **Depositor Education & Awareness (DEA) Fund** | All bank deposits and balances remaining unclaimed for **over 10 years** must be credited to the DEA Fund within 3 months. Depositors can claim their refunds with interest at any subsequent time. |
| **Sec 29 & 30** | Accounts, Balance Sheet & Audit | • **Sec 29:** Preparation of Balance Sheet (Form A) and Profit & Loss Account (Form B) as on **31 March** under the **Third Schedule**.<br>• **Sec 30:** Mandatory audit by a qualified Chartered Accountant; appointment/re-appointment requires prior approval of the RBI. |
| **Sec 31** | Submission of Audited Returns | Three copies of audited balance sheet and P&L account submitted to RBI within **3 months** from the end of the accounting period. |
| **Sec 35** | **Inspection by RBI** | Empowers RBI to conduct on-site inspection of books and accounts of any banking company; report submitted to Central Government. |
| **Sec 35A** | **Power to Issue Directions** | Apex power of RBI to issue directions to banks in the **public interest**, in the interest of banking policy, or to prevent the affairs of any bank being conducted detrimentally to depositors. |
| **Sec 35AA & 35AB** | Insolvency Resolution Directions | Empowers Central Govt to authorize RBI to direct banks to initiate corporate insolvency resolution under the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016**. |
| **Sec 36AA** | **Removal of Managerial Personnel** | RBI may remove any Chairman, Director, CEO, or employee of a bank if satisfied their continuation is detrimental to depositors. |
| **Sec 36AB** | Appointment of Additional Directors | RBI may appoint up to **5 additional directors** on the Board of a banking company. |
| **Sec 36ACA** | **Supersession of Board of Directors** | RBI may supersede the Board of Directors of a banking company for a period up to **12 months** and appoint an Administrator. |
| **Sec 45** | **Moratorium & Compulsory Amalgamation** | • RBI can apply to Central Govt for order of **Moratorium (up to 6 months)** on a failing bank.<br>• During moratorium, RBI prepares a scheme of reconstruction or **compulsory amalgamation** with another bank (e.g., Yes Bank reconstruction; amalgamation of Lakshmi Vilas Bank into DBS Bank India). |
| **Sec 56** | **Application to Co-operative Banks** | Applies provisions of BR Act to Primary Co-operative Banks (UCBs), State Co-operative Banks, and DCCBs. Substantially expanded by the **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020** to give RBI direct regulatory control over UCB management, audit, and resolution. |

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 22 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — Note Issuance Authority:** The ₹1 note and metallic coins are issued by the **Ministry of Finance (Government of India)**, bearing the signature of the **Finance Secretary**. Bank notes of ₹2 and above are issued by the **RBI under Section 22**, bearing the signature of the **RBI Governor**.
2. **Trap — Prohibited Banking Business:** Under Section 19 of the RBI Act, the RBI **CANNOT pay interest on deposits** placed with it, and under Section 8 of the BR Act, a commercial bank **CANNOT engage in buying and selling goods (trading)**.
3. **Trap — Non-Banking Asset Disposal Period:** Under Section 9 of the BR Act, immovable property acquired by a bank must be disposed of within **7 years** (RBI can grant an extension of up to **5 years**). Total maximum holding period is **12 years**.
4. **Trap — Statutory Reserve Fund Transfer:** Section 17 of the BR Act specifies **20% of net profits**, but the RBI's Master Direction mandates a higher prudential transfer of **25% of net profits**.
5. **Trap — Loan on Own Shares:** Under Section 20 of the BR Act, a bank is **STRICTLY PROHIBITED from granting any loan or advance against the security of its own shares**.
6. **Trap — Unclaimed Deposits Timeline:** Deposits inactive for **10 years** are transferred to the **DEA Fund under Section 26A** of the BR Act.

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** The ₹1 currency note and all coins are issued by the Ministry of Finance, Government of India. The RBI has the sole monopoly to issue bank notes of ₹2 and higher under Section 22 of the RBI Act.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Section 9 mandates disposal within 7 years (the RBI may extend this by up to 5 additional years).  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** Section 17 of the BR Act mandates a statutory transfer of at least 20%, but the RBI Master Direction requires commercial banks to transfer 25% of net profit to the Reserve Fund before declaring dividends.  
• **Q4 — Answer: B.** Statement I is incorrect because Section 20 strictly prohibits a bank from lending on the security of its own shares. Statement II is correct (Section 36AA grants RBI powers to remove managerial personnel).  
• **Q5 — Answer: D.** Accounts inoperative for 10 years are classified as unclaimed deposits and transferred to the DEA Fund under Section 26A.
