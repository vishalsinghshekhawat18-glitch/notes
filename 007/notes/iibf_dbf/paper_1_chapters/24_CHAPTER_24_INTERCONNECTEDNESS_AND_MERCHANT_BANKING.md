# FINANCIAL MARKET INTERCONNECTEDNESS & MERCHANT BANKING

Modern financial architecture operates as an interconnected ecosystem where liquidity shocks transmit across money, debt, foreign exchange, and equity markets. This chapter covers the Interconnection of Financial Markets, Integrated Bank Treasury operations, and Contagion Dynamics (Unit 34) alongside Merchant Banking Services and SEBI regulations (Unit 35).

---

## § 24.1 Unit 34: Interconnection of Financial Markets & Market Dynamics

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 34)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Financial market integration enables rapid capital mobility and interest rate parity, but exposes the economy to systemic contagion. Commercial banks manage cross-market exposures via Integrated Treasury desks under Asset-Liability Committee (ALCO) oversight.

### 1. Levels of Financial Market Integration

| Degree of Integration | Market Characteristics & Capital Mobility | Price Discrepancies & Arbitrage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Segmented / Low Integration** | Strict capital controls, fragmented regulatory silos, information delays, limited cross-border participant access. | Large, persistent price and yield discrepancies across markets. Arbitrage is constrained. |
| **Partially Integrated** | Emerging markets with capital account restrictions (e.g. India under FEMA). | Short-term money and forex markets exhibit strong links, while equity and bond markets experience policy-dampened transmission. |
| **Fully / Highly Integrated** | Unrestricted capital mobility, electronic straight-through processing (STP), transparent price discovery. | Price differentials disappear almost instantaneously via arbitrage. Strong law of one price holds. |

### 3. Integrated Bank Treasury Operations

In modern commercial banking, the **Integrated Treasury** consolidates domestic and foreign currency liquidity management into a unified operations center:
• **Four Core Functional Desks:**
  1. *Domestic Money Market Desk:* Manages daily reserve obligations (CRR with RBI, SLR), Call/Notice money borrowing, issuance of CDs, and TREPS operations.
  2. *Forex Treasury Desk:* Manages cross-border currency flows, covers merchant forex risk, quotes spot/forward rates, and executes currency swaps.
  3. *Investment / G-Sec Desk:* Manages sovereign bond trading, corporate bonds, commercial paper, and statutory SLR investments categorized into HTM (Held to Maturity), AFS (Available for Sale), and HFT (Held for Trading).
  4. *Derivatives Desk:* Structures and trades Interest Rate Swaps (IRS), Forward Rate Agreements (FRAs), and currency options for balance-sheet hedging.
• **Organizational Governance:**
  - **Front Office:** Dealing room executing market trades.
  - **Mid Office:** Independent risk management unit monitoring Value at Risk (VaR), stop-loss limits, counterparty exposure limits, and ALM mismatches. Reports directly to the **Chief Risk Officer (CRO)**.
  - **Back Office:** Confirmation, settlement, accounting, and regulatory reporting to RBI.
  - **Asset-Liability Committee (ALCO):** Senior management operational committee chaired by the CEO/CMD (or Executive Director) that manages asset-liability mismatches, liquidity buffers, and interest rate risk, reporting functionally to the Board's Risk Management Committee (RMC).

### 4. Financial Contagion & Systemic Transmission Mechanisms

**Financial Contagion** refers to the rapid cross-border or cross-institutional transmission of liquidity or solvency shocks:
• **Core Contagion Channels:**
  1. *Direct Bilateral Counterparty Exposure:* Insolvent institution defaults on interbank loans, repos, or derivative contracts, triggering cascading counterparty failures.
  2. *Fire-Sale Externality / Common Asset Exposure:* Distressed institutions dump liquid assets (G-Secs, high-grade bonds) onto the market to raise emergency cash. Collapsing asset prices force other sound banks to record heavy mark-to-market (MTM) losses, depleting regulatory capital.
  3. *Information Asymmetry / Panic Contagion:* Creditors unable to distinguish sound banks from distressed banks execute preemptive runs on all institutions sharing similar business profiles.
  4. *The Sovereign-Bank Nexus ("Doom Loop"):* Commercial banks hold large portfolios of domestic government debt. Sovereign fiscal distress reduces bond values, eroding bank capital; distressed banks then require sovereign capital bailouts, compounding the sovereign crisis.
• **Macroprudential Mitigants:** Countercyclical Capital Buffer (CCyB), Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), and the RBI Large Exposure Framework (capping exposure to a single counterparty at 20% of Tier 1 capital, extendable to 25% for connected groups).

## § 24.2 Unit 35: Merchant Banking Services (2026 Regulatory Regime)

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 35)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Merchant banks provide fee-based corporate financial consultancy, capital issue management, underwriting, and mergers and acquisitions (M&A) advisory under SEBI regulation. Under the **SEBI (Merchant Bankers) (Amendment) Regulations, 2025** (notified 5 December 2025, in force from **3 January 2026**), the legacy four-category framework was restructured into **Two Categories (Category I & Category II)**, with minimum Net Worth enhanced to **₹50 Crore** for Category I and **₹10 Crore** for Category II, alongside mandatory **Liquid Net Worth** requirements. Merchant bankers remain strictly prohibited from deposit-taking or commercial lending.

### 1. Commercial Banking vs. Merchant Banking

| Parameter | Commercial Banking | Merchant Banking |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Nature of Activity** | **Fund-Based Activity:** Mobilizing deposits and extending loans and advances. | **Fee-Based / Advisory Activity:** Managing public issues, underwriting, corporate debt structuring, M&A advisory. |
| **Deposit Mobilization** | **Accepts public deposits** (Savings, Current, Fixed deposits). | **STRICTLY PROHIBITED from accepting deposits** from the public. |
| **Money Lending** | Engages in direct corporate, retail, and agricultural lending. | **STRICTLY PROHIBITED from carrying on money lending**. |
| **Primary Risk Exposure** | **Credit Default Risk** and liquidity/maturity mismatch risk. | **Reputational Risk**, market underwriting risk, and advisory liability. |
| **Primary Statutory Regulator** | **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** | **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** |

• **Historical Evolution:** Grindlays Bank established the first merchant banking division in India in **1969**, followed by the State Bank of India (SBI) in **1972** and ICICI in **1973**.

---

### 2. SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations (2026 Dual-Category Regime)

The SEBI (Merchant Bankers) (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 (effective 3 January 2026) streamlined the registration architecture by abolishing Categories III and IV, instituting a robust two-tier structure:

| Category | Permissible Scope of Activities | Mandatory Minimum Net Worth | Mandatory Liquid Net Worth |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Category I** | • Can act as **Lead Manager to Public Issues (IPOs / FPOs / Rights)**.<br>• Underwriting of issues.<br>• Corporate financial advisory, capital restructuring, and M&A consultancy.<br>• Portfolio management services (subject to separate SEBI PMS registration). | **₹50 Crore**<br>*(Enhanced from obsolete legacy ₹5 Cr)* | **₹12.5 Crore**<br>*(25% of Net Worth)* |
| **Category II** | • Can act as **adviser or consultant** to an issue.<br>• Can act as underwriter or portfolio manager.<br>• *Statutory Restriction:* **CANNOT act as Lead Manager** to an issue. | **₹10 Crore**<br>*(Enhanced from obsolete legacy ₹50 Lakh)* | **₹2.5 Crore**<br>*(25% of Net Worth)* |

> **Implementation Timeline & Phased Compliance (SEBI Circulars Jan 2026 & June 2026 Extension):**  
> • **New Applicants:** Must satisfy the full net worth and liquid net worth requirements immediately upon registration (Category I: ₹50 Crore / ₹12.5 Cr liquid; Category II: ₹10 Crore / ₹2.5 Cr liquid).  
> • **Existing Registrants:** Subject to phased compliance under the structured transition glide path as amended by SEBI's June 2026 extension circular, giving existing players time to augment capital. The legacy ₹5 Crore threshold is an **obsolete historical figure** and must not be treated as current law.

> **Crucial Regulatory Mandate:**  
> Only a **Category I Merchant Banker** is legally permitted to act as the **Lead Manager** to an Initial Public Offering (IPO) or rights issue on an Indian stock exchange!

---

### 3. Mandated Activities in Public Issue Management

• **Pre-Issue Obligations:**
  - Conducting comprehensive due diligence on the issuing company's financials, promoter background, and litigation.
  - Submitting the **Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP)** alongside a statutory **Due Diligence Certificate** to SEBI.
  - Appointing supporting issue intermediaries: Registrars to the Issue, Bankers to the Issue, Advertising Agencies, and Underwriters.
  - Organizing institutional investor roadshows and determining the price band via book-building.
• **Post-Issue Obligations:**
  - Monitoring subscription collections; ensuring compliance with the **minimum 90% subscription mandate** (if subscription falls below 90%, the issuer must refund entire application money within 4 days).
  - Finalizing basis of allotment in coordination with stock exchange authorities within **T+3 days**.
  - Submitting post-issue monitoring reports (initial report within 7 days, final report within 78 days) to SEBI.

---

### 4. SEBI Code of Conduct for Merchant Bankers

• Merchant bankers must observe high standards of integrity, dignity, and fairness in all dealings.  
• Must maintain **arms-length firewalls** to prevent conflict of interest between advisory clients.  
• Strictly prohibited from engaging in **insider trading or front-running** securities of corporate clients.  
• Must not make exaggerated statements, misleading claims, or guarantee assured returns to investors.

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 34 & Unit 35 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — 2026 Merchant Banker Net Worth:** Under the 2026 SEBI regime, Category I Merchant Bankers must have a minimum net worth of **₹50 Crore** (NOT the obsolete ₹5 Crore figure), with **₹12.5 Crore** in liquid net worth. Category II requires **₹10 Crore** net worth and **₹2.5 Crore** liquid net worth.
2. **Trap — Abolition of Categories III & IV:** SEBI has **abolished Categories III and IV**; only Categories I and II exist.
3. **Trap — Merchant Banker Lending Restrictions:** Merchant bankers are **STRICTLY FORBIDDEN from accepting public deposits or engaging in commercial lending**. They are fee-based financial intermediaries.
4. **Trap — Who Can Act as Lead Manager:** Only **Category I Merchant Bankers** can act as Lead Managers to a public issue. Category II is legally barred from lead management.
5. **Trap — Minimum Subscription Mandate:** An IPO must achieve at least **90% subscription** of the net offer to the public; otherwise, all application money must be refunded to investors immediately.
6. **Trap — Treasury Mid-Office Reporting:** The Treasury Mid-Office reports directly to the **Chief Risk Officer (CRO)** or Risk Management Committee, NOT to the Head of Dealing/Treasury.

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: D.** Under the SEBI 2026 regulations, Category I Merchant Bankers must maintain a minimum Net Worth of ₹50 Crore and a Liquid Net Worth of ₹12.5 Crore (the legacy ₹5 Crore net worth requirement is obsolete).  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** The Mid Office operates as an independent risk management unit monitoring dealer exposures, stop-loss triggers, and VaR, reporting directly to the CRO.  
• **Q3 — Answer: A.** Only Category I Merchant Bankers are permitted by SEBI to act as Lead Managers to public issues; Category II entities cannot act as Lead Managers.  
• **Q4 — Answer: B.** Statement I is incorrect because Merchant Bankers are securities market intermediaries regulated exclusively by **SEBI** under the SEBI Act, 1992. Statement II is correct (they are prohibited from deposit-taking and commercial money lending).
