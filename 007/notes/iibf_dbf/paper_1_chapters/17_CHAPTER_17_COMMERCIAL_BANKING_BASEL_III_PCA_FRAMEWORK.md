# COMMERCIAL BANKING, BASEL III & PCA FRAMEWORK

Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs) form the core of the Indian financial intermediation architecture. To preserve depositor confidence and withstand macro-financial shocks, commercial banks operate under strict capital adequacy standards (Basel III) and are subject to immediate regulatory intervention under the Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) framework.

## § 17.1 Unit 21: Indian Banking Structure - Commercial Banking

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 21)**  
> **Core Proposition:** The Indian commercial banking structure comprises Public Sector Banks (PSBs), Private Sector Banks, and Foreign Banks. Indian banks must maintain a minimum Total Capital (CRAR) of 11.5% (including a 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer). Breach of capital, asset quality (Net NPA ≥ 6%), or leverage triggers Prompt Corrective Action (PCA).

### 1. Structure of Scheduled Commercial Banks in India

| Banking Category | Institutional Composition & Numbers | Operating Charter & Regulatory Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

### 2. Basel III Capital Adequacy Architecture (RBI Master Directions)

The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) formulated Basel III following the 2008 crisis. The Reserve Bank of India enforced **more stringent capital standards than the global Basel III baseline**:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 INDIAN BASEL III REGULATORY CAPITAL ARCHITECTURE            │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┬───────────┬───────────────┤
│ CAPITAL COMPONENT                               │ BCBS BASE │ RBI MANDATE   │
│ • Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)                   │ 4.5%      │ 5.5% of RWAs  │
│ • Total Tier 1 Capital                          │ 6.0%      │ 7.0% of RWAs  │
│ • Minimum Total Capital (CRAR without CCB)      │ 8.0%      │ 9.0% of RWAs  │
│ • Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB - pure CET1) │ 2.5%      │ 2.5% of RWAs  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┼───────────┼───────────────┤
│ TOTAL MINIMUM CAPITAL (INCLUDING CCB)           │ 10.5%     │ 11.5% of RWAs │
│ • Maximum Allowable Tier 2 Capital              │ 2.0%      │ 2.0% of RWAs  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┴───────────┴───────────────┘
```

• **Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) Formula:**
  $$\text{CRAR} = \frac{\text{Eligible Tier 1 Capital} + \text{Eligible Tier 2 Capital}}{\text{Credit Risk RWAs} + \text{Market Risk RWAs} + \text{Operational Risk RWAs}} \ge 11.5\%$$

• **Tier 1 Capital (Going-Concern Capital):** Absorbs losses while the bank remains solvent and operational. Comprises **CET1** (paid-up equity shares, statutory reserves, retained earnings) and **Additional Tier 1 (AT1)** capital (Perpetual Non-Cumulative Preference Shares, PNCPS; Perpetual Debt Instruments with statutory loss-absorption write-down/conversion clauses).  
• **Tier 2 Capital (Gone-Concern Capital):** Absorbs losses in the event of winding up or liquidation. Comprises general provisions for standard assets (up to 1.25% of credit RWAs), revaluation reserves (at a 55% discount), and subordinated debt instruments (minimum maturity 5 years). Capped at **2.0% of RWAs**.  
• **Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs):**
  - Banks classified as "Too Big to Fail" based on Size, Interconnectedness, Lack of Substitutability, and Complexity.
  - Currently identified D-SIBs: **State Bank of India (SBI)**, **HDFC Bank**, and **ICICI Bank**.
  - Subject to an additional **CET1 capital surcharge** ranging from 0.20% to 0.80% of RWAs.
• **Basel III Liquidity Standards:**
  - **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR):** Mandates holding sufficient unencumbered High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to survive a 30-day severe liquidity stress scenario:
    $$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{Total Stock of HQLA}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days}} \ge 100\%$$
  - **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR):** Promotes medium/long-term funding stability over a 1-year horizon:
    $$\text{NSFR} = \frac{\text{Available Stable Funding (ASF)}}{\text{Required Stable Funding (RSF)}} \ge 100\%$$
  - **Leverage Ratio:** Non-risk-based backstop: $\frac{\text{Tier 1 Capital}}{\text{Total Exposure}} \ge 3.5\%$ (4.0% for D-SIBs).

### 3. Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework (RBI Framework Effective 1 Jan 2022)

The RBI's **Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework for Scheduled Commercial Banks** (Circular dated 2 Nov 2021, effective 1 Jan 2022) monitors banks across three parameters: **Capital, Asset Quality, and Leverage**.

> **Methodology — Basis-Point Deterioration from Capital Baseline:**  
> In the RBI 2022 framework, PCA capital thresholds are officially measured by **basis-point (bps) deterioration from the applicable regulatory minimum including the Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB)**:
> • Baseline CRAR = **11.5%** (9.0% regulatory minimum + 2.5% CCB).  
> • Baseline CET1 = **8.0%** (5.5% regulatory minimum + 2.5% CCB).

| Parameter & Indicator | Applicable Regulatory Baseline (incl. CCB) | Risk Threshold 1 (Mandatory Dividend Curbs) | Risk Threshold 2 (Threshold 1 + Branch Curbs) | Risk Threshold 3 (Threshold 2 + Remuneration Curbs) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CRAR (Total Capital)** | **11.5%**<br>*(9.0% min + 2.5% CCB)* | **Up to 250 bps below baseline**<br>*(≥ 9.0% to < 11.5%)* | **> 250 bps to ≤ 400 bps below baseline**<br>*(≥ 7.5% to < 9.0%)* | **> 400 bps below baseline**<br>*(< 7.5%)* |
| **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)** | **8.0%**<br>*(5.5% min + 2.5% CCB)* | **Up to 162.5 bps below baseline**<br>*(≥ 6.375% to < 8.0%)* | **> 162.5 bps to ≤ 312.5 bps below baseline**<br>*(≥ 4.875% to < 6.375%)* | **> 312.5 bps below baseline**<br>*(< 4.875%)* |
| **Asset Quality (Net NPA Ratio)** | **< 6.0%** | **≥ 6.0% to < 9.0%** | **≥ 9.0% to < 12.0%** | **≥ 12.0%** |
| **Tier 1 Leverage Ratio** | **3.5%** (Other SCBs)<br>**4.0%** (D-SIBs) | **Up to 50 bps below min**<br>*(3.0% to < 3.5% Other; 3.5% to < 4.0% D-SIBs)* | **> 50 bps to ≤ 100 bps below min**<br>*(2.5% to < 3.0% Other; 3.0% to < 3.5% D-SIBs)* | **> 100 bps below min**<br>*(< 2.5% Other; < 3.0% D-SIBs)* |

> **Critical Supervisory Evolution:**  
> • **Removal of RoA Trigger:** In the revised PCA framework, **Return on Assets (RoA) was officially removed** as a mandatory supervisory trigger.  
> • **Mandatory Corrective Sanctions:**
>   - *Risk Threshold 1:* Mandatory restriction on dividend distribution and remittance of profits; promoters/owners mandated to infuse fresh capital.
>   - *Risk Threshold 2:* Threshold 1 restrictions PLUS mandatory prohibition on opening new domestic or overseas branches.
>   - *Risk Threshold 3:* Threshold 1 & 2 restrictions PLUS mandatory restriction on management compensation and directors' fees.
> • **Discretionary Actions:** Special supervisory inspection, credit portfolio caps, cessation of high-risk lending, or invoking merger/amalgamation under Section 45 of BR Act.

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 21 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — Indian vs BCBS Minimum CRAR:** The Basel Committee (BCBS) baseline minimum CRAR is **8.0%** (10.5% with CCB). The Reserve Bank of India mandates a stricter minimum of **9.0%** (which reaches **11.5% with the 2.5% CCB**). An answer citing 9% as total capital including CCB is **incorrect**.
2. **Trap — Asset Quality PCA Trigger:** The PCA asset quality trigger is based strictly on the **Net NPA Ratio (≥ 6.0%)**, NOT the Gross NPA ratio.
3. **Trap — RoA in Revised PCA:** Return on Assets (RoA) is **NO LONGER a trigger** under the revised PCA framework for commercial banks.
4. **Trap — Tier 2 Capital Cap:** Under Basel III, eligible Tier 2 capital is capped at a maximum of **2.0% of Risk-Weighted Assets (RWAs)**.
5. **Trap — Current D-SIBs:** The three designated Domestic Systemically Important Banks in India are **SBI, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank**.
6. **Trap — PCA Capital Trigger Calibration:** PCA thresholds are measured as basis-point deterioration from the **regulatory baseline including CCB (11.5% CRAR / 8.0% CET1)**. Total CRAR Threshold 1 triggers at up to 250 bps below 11.5% (i.e. **9.0% to < 11.5%**); Threshold 2 is **7.5% to < 9.0%**; Threshold 3 is **< 7.5%**. For CET1, Threshold 1 is **6.375% to < 8.0%**; Threshold 2 is **4.875% to < 6.375%**; Threshold 3 is **< 4.875%**.

Statement I: The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) requires banks to hold High-Quality Liquid Assets to withstand a 30-day severe liquidity stress scenario with a minimum target of 100%.  
Statement II: The Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) measures short-term intraday interbank payment settlement liquidity.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?  
A. Statement I only  
B. Statement II only  
C. Both Statement I and Statement II  
D. Neither Statement I nor Statement II  

**Q4. [PYQ-PATTERN / RECOLLECTED RECONSTRUCTION / PCA EXCLUSIONS] Which of the following parameters was OFFICIALLY REMOVED as a mandatory trigger in the revised Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) framework for commercial banks?**  
A. Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) Ratio  
B. Net NPA Ratio  
C. Return on Assets (RoA)  
D. Tier 1 Leverage Ratio  

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** The RBI mandates a minimum Total CRAR of 9.0% plus a Capital Conservation Buffer of 2.5%, totaling **11.5% of RWAs** (higher than the global BCBS baseline of 10.5%).  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** Under the revised PCA framework, Risk Threshold 1 is triggered when the Net NPA ratio is $\ge 6.0\%$ but $< 9.0\%$.  
• **Q3 — Answer: A.** Statement I is correct (LCR $\ge 100\%$ for 30-day stress). Statement II is incorrect because the Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) is a medium/long-term structural metric covering a **1-year horizon**, not an intraday metric.  
• **Q4 — Answer: C.** In the revised PCA framework, Return on Assets (RoA) was removed as an independent trigger to avoid penalizing temporarily unprofitable banks that maintain sound capital and low net NPAs.
