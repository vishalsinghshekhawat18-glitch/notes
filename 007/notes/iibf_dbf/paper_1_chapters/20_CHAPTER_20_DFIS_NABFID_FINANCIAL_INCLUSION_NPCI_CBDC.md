# DFIs, NaBFID, FINANCIAL INCLUSION & DIGITAL RAILS

Development Financial Institutions (DFIs) provide patient, long-term capital for industrialization and infrastructure, while digital payment rails drive universal financial inclusion. This chapter explores the historical evolution and modern revival of DFIs including NaBFID (Unit 23) alongside Financial Inclusion, FinTech, and Central Bank Digital Currency (Unit 26).

---

## § 20.1 Unit 23: Development Financial Institutions (DFIs)

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 23)**  
> **Core Proposition:** DFIs provide long-gestation project finance. India's early DFIs (IFCI, ICICI, IDBI) faced asset-liability mismatches when concessional funding ended post-1991, prompting their conversion into universal commercial banks. The DFI model was revived in 2021 through the statutory creation of NaBFID for infrastructure financing.

### 1. Historical Evolution of Indian DFIs (1948 – 2000)

| Institution | Inception Year & Statutory Basis | Original Institutional Mandate | Post-Reform Transformation & Current Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Industrial Finance Corporation of India (IFCI)** | **1 July 1948**; IFCI Act, 1948 | **India's FIRST DFI.** Established to provide medium and long-term debt to large industrial manufacturing units. | Converted into a public limited company in 1993; currently functions as a Systemically Important Non-Deposit NBFC (NBFC-ND-SI) under Government ownership. |
| **Industrial Credit and Investment Corporation of India (ICICI)** | **January 1955**; Promoted by World Bank, GoI & private enterprise | Channeled foreign currency loans and equity underwriting to private sector industrial enterprises. | **Reverse Merger in 2002:** The parent DFI (ICICI) merged into its retail banking subsidiary (*ICICI Bank Ltd*), pioneering the **Universal Banking** model in India. |
| **Industrial Development Bank of India (IDBI)** | **July 1964**; IDBI Act, 1964 (Apex DFI) | Established as a wholly-owned subsidiary of RBI to coordinate, refinance, and supervise all industrial DFIs; made autonomous in 1976. | **Commercial Banking Transition in 2004:** Transformed into a full-service commercial bank (*IDBI Bank Ltd*) under the IDBI (Repeal) Act, 2003; LIC acquired majority stake in 2019. |
| **Unit Trust of India (UTI)** | **1964**; UTI Act, 1963 | Pioneer mutual fund mobilizing retail savings for industrial equities; managed the historic US-64 scheme. | Bifurcated in 2002 into **SUUTI** (holding government legacy assets) and **UTI Mutual Fund** (SEBI-registered mutual fund). |

---

### 2. The Universal Banking Transition (Khan & Narasimham Committees)

• **The ALM Crisis of DFIs:** Pre-1991, DFIs received subsidized, low-cost long-term funding through the RBI's **National Industrial Credit (Long Term Operations) - NIC-LTO funds** and government-guaranteed bonds. In the post-reform era, these concessional windows ceased. DFIs were forced to borrow at market interest rates via 3–5 year bonds to fund 15–20 year industrial infrastructure loans, triggering acute **Asset-Liability Mismatch (ALM)**.  
• **The Solution (Universal Banking):** The **Khan Committee (1998)** and **Narasimham Committee II (1998)** recommended phasing out specialized DFIs in favor of **Universal Banks**—institutions offering under a single corporate umbrella the full continuum of commercial banking, investment banking, long-term project finance, and asset management.

### 4. Modern DFI Revival: NaBFID (Act 2021)

• **Establishment:** Set up under the **National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development Act, 2021** as a specialized statutory DFI to fund the National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP).  
• **Capital Structure:**
  - Authorized Share Capital: **₹1,00,000 Crore**.
  - Initial Paid-Up Capital: **₹20,000 Crore** (Government of India holds minimum 26% equity).
  - Initial Sovereign Grant: **₹5,000 Crore** provided by the Central Government.
• **Regulatory Mandate:** Regulated and supervised by the RBI as the **Fifth All-India Financial Institution (AIFI)** under Sections 45L and 45N of the RBI Act, 1934.  
• **Dual Objective:** (1) Financial objective: Direct lending, syndication, and refinancing of infrastructure projects; (2) Developmental objective: Deepening the domestic long-term corporate debt and derivatives market for infrastructure.

## § 20.2 Unit 26: Emerging Intermediaries, Financial Inclusion & FinTech

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 26)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Financial inclusion integrates unbanked segments via PMJDY, measured by the RBI FI-Index. Digital public infrastructure managed by NPCI and the introduction of the Digital Rupee (CBDC) underpin modern retail banking rails.

### 1. Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)

• **Core Mandate:** National Mission for Financial Inclusion launched on **28 August 2014**.  
• **Key Financial Inclusion Features:**
  - Universal access to basic banking services via **Basic Savings Bank Deposit Accounts (BSBDA)** with zero minimum balance requirement.
  - **Overdraft (OD) Facility:** Up to **₹10,000** to eligible account holders (one per household, preferably woman of house; no conditions attached for OD up to ₹2,000; age bracket: **18 to 65 years**).
  - **RuPay Debit Card:** Includes an inbuilt **Accidental Insurance Cover of ₹2 Lakh** (for accounts opened after 28 August 2018).

### 2. The RBI Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index)

Introduced by the RBI in **August 2021** to measure the extent of financial inclusion across the country:
• **Scoring Metric:** A single value ranging between **0 and 100**, where **0 represents complete financial exclusion** and **100 represents complete financial inclusion**.  
• **Three Dimensional Weights:**
  $$\text{FI-Index} = \mathbf{Access (35\%)} + \mathbf{Usage (45\%)} + \mathbf{Quality (20\%)} = 100\%$$
• **Unique Design Property:** Calculated annually in July **without a base year**, reflecting cumulative historical achievements across banking, investments, insurance, and postal services.

### 3. Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC / Digital Rupee - e₹)

• **Statutory Basis:** Enabled via amendments to Section 22 and Section 2 of the RBI Act, 1934, passed under the **Finance Act, 2022**, recognizing CBDC as sovereign legal tender currency.  
• **Key Design Dimensions:**
  - **Wholesale CBDC ($e₹-W$):** Launched November 2022; restricted to institutional interbank settlement of secondary market transactions in Government Securities.
  - **Retail CBDC ($e₹-R$):** Launched December 2022; digital token representing sovereign currency issued to retail users via authorized bank digital wallets for peer-to-peer (P2P) and peer-to-merchant (P2M) payments.
  - **Interest-Free Nature:** To prevent disintermediation of commercial bank deposits, **the Digital Rupee earns ZERO interest**.

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 23 & Unit 26 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — India's First DFI:** **IFCI (established in 1948)** was India's first DFI, NOT ICICI (1955) or IDBI (1964).
2. **Trap — DFI to Universal Bank Pioneer:** **ICICI** was the first Indian DFI to execute a reverse merger into a commercial bank (2002), followed by IDBI (2004).
3. **Trap — NaBFID Initial Capital & Grant:** NaBFID was established with **₹20,000 Crore initial paid-up capital** and a **₹5,000 Crore sovereign grant**.
4. **Trap — FI-Index Base Year:** The RBI Financial Inclusion Index has **NO BASE YEAR**. It tracks cumulative progress from zero to 100.
5. **Trap — PMJDY Overdraft Age Limit:** The age bracket for availing the ₹10,000 PMJDY overdraft is **18 to 65 years** (expanded from the earlier 18 to 60 years limit).
6. **Trap — Interest on CBDC:** The Reserve Bank of India pays **ZERO interest on CBDC (Digital Rupee)** balances to protect commercial bank deposits from flight to central bank currency.

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** IFCI was established on 1 July 1948 by an Act of Parliament as India's first DFI. ICICI followed in 1955, and IDBI in 1964.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** NaBFID was established with an authorized share capital of ₹1,00,000 Crore and an initial paid-up capital of ₹20,000 Crore, supplemented by a ₹5,000 Crore central government grant.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** The RBI FI-Index assigns 45% weight to Usage, 35% to Access, and 20% to Quality.  
• **Q4 — Answer: A.** Statement I is correct. Statement II is false because the Digital Rupee earns zero interest to prevent depositors from emptying commercial bank savings accounts to hold risk-free central bank digital cash.
