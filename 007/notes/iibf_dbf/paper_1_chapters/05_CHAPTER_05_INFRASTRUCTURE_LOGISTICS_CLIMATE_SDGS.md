# INFRASTRUCTURE, LOGISTICS & CLIMATE SDGs

Infrastructure represents the foundational capital asset enabling economic production, logistics efficiency, and human capital formation. This chapter integrates Physical and Social Infrastructure (Unit 05) with Climate Action, Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), and Corporate Social Responsibility mandates (Unit 10).

## § 5.1 Unit 05: Infrastructure including Social Infrastructure

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 05)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Infrastructure investments bridge economic supply-chain bottlenecks and foster social capability. The funding architecture has evolved through Public-Private Partnership (PPP) frameworks, notably the Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM), while social infrastructure is anchored by national health insurance and universal foundational education.

### 1. Physical Infrastructure & National Planning Frameworks

| Strategic Initiative | Launch Year & Institutional Lead | Financial Outlay / Operational Scope | Strategic Policy Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP)** | 2019–2025; Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) | **₹111 Lakh Crore** projected outlay across 6,800+ projects | **Capital Sharing:** Centre (39%), States (40%), Private Sector (21%). Energy (24%), Roads (19%), Urban (16%), Railways (13%). |
| **PM GatiShakti National Master Plan** | October 2021; DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce & Industry) | GIS-based digital multi-modal planning platform | Synchronizes 7 infrastructure engines (Roads, Railways, Airports, Ports, Mass Transport, Waterways, Logistics). |
| **National Logistics Policy (NLP)** | September 2022; Ministry of Commerce & Industry | Comprehensive logistics efficiency framework | *Policy Aspirations:* Reduce national logistics cost from ~13–14% of GDP toward single digits (~8%); place India among top 25 nations in the World Bank Logistics Performance Index (LPI). |

### 2. Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Financing Models & Risk Allocation

| Model | Capital Investment Sharing | Operational / Revenue Mechanism | Risk Allocation Diagnosis (Construction vs. Traffic) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EPC (Engineering, Procurement, Construction)** | **100% Government funded** | Government pays contractor based on construction milestones | **Government bears 100% financing and traffic risk**; Contractor bears only project execution risk. |
| **BOT - Toll (Build-Operate-Transfer)** | **100% Private Concessionaire funded** | Private developer collects user toll fees over 20–30 year concession period | **Private concessionaire bears 100% traffic and commercial revenue risk**. Severe NPAs in banking history. |
| **BOT - Annuity** | **100% Private Concessionaire funded** | Government pays fixed semi-annual annuity payments to private developer | Government bears traffic risk; Concessionaire bears construction and maintenance risk. |
| **Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM)** | **40% Government grant; 60% Private funding** | Government provides 40% inflation-indexed cash grant during construction; remaining 60% paid as annuity with interest | **Optimal Risk Sharing:** Government collects toll and bears **100% traffic risk**; Private developer bears execution and maintenance risk. Low banking stress. |

### 3. Social Infrastructure: Health & Education Architecture

• **Ayushman Bharat — Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY):**
  - World's largest government-funded health assurance scheme.
  - Provides a defined health cover of **₹5 Lakh per family per year** for secondary and tertiary care hospitalization to bottom 40% vulnerable households.
  - Cashless, paperless access at empaneled public and private hospitals across India.
  - *Current Expansion:* Health coverage of ₹5 Lakh per year extended to **all senior citizens aged 70 years and above**, irrespective of income bracket.
• **National Education Policy (NEP) 2020:**
  - Restructured pedagogical framework from 10+2 to **5+3+3+4** (Foundational: 5 yrs, Preparatory: 3 yrs, Middle: 3 yrs, Secondary: 4 yrs).
  - Public education expenditure target set at **6% of GDP** (Centre and States combined).

## § 5.2 Unit 10: Climate Change, Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 10)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Decarbonization and climate commitments are operationalized through India's COP26 Panchamrit targets, Sovereign Green Bond issuances, SEBI Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR), and statutory Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) obligations.

### 1. India's COP26 / COP28 'Panchamrit' Climate Commitments

At COP26 (Glasgow) and reaffirmed in updated Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs):
1. **500 GW Non-Fossil Energy:** Reach 500 GW non-fossil energy capacity by **2030**.
2. **50% Renewable Electricity:** Meet 50% of aggregate energy requirements from renewable energy by **2030**.
3. **1 Billion Tonnes Carbon Reduction:** Reduce total projected carbon emissions by 1 billion tonnes from now through **2030**.
4. **45% Carbon Intensity Reduction:** Reduce carbon intensity of GDP by **45% by 2030** (over 2005 baseline levels).
5. **Net Zero Carbon Target:** Achieve **Net Zero carbon emissions by 2070**.

### 2. Green Finance & ESG Disclosure Architecture

• **Sovereign Green Bonds (SGrBs):** Issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Central Government. Proceeds are credited to the Consolidated Fund of India and exclusively deployed into public sector projects that reduce carbon intensity (Renewable energy, clean transport, green buildings).  
• **Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR):** Mandated by SEBI for the **Top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalization** on Indian stock exchanges. Includes **BRSR Core** requiring third-party reasonable assurance across 9 key ESG performance attributes.

### 3. Statutory Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) — Section 135, Companies Act 2013

| Statutory Parameter | Legal Threshold / Mandatory Requirement | Operational Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Applicability Thresholds** (Immediately preceding Financial Year) | • Net Worth **≥ ₹500 Crore**, OR<br>• Turnover **≥ ₹1,000 Crore**, OR<br>• Net Profit **≥ ₹5 Crore** | Any company satisfying ANY ONE of the three criteria must comply with Section 135. |
| **Mandatory Annual Spend** | At least **2% of average net profits** of the company made during the **3 immediately preceding financial years**. | Calculated under Section 198 of the Companies Act, 2013. |
| **CSR Committee Constitution** | 3 or more Directors, with **at least 1 Independent Director**. | Exemption: If total CSR obligation is **≤ ₹50 Lakh**, the Board of Directors itself discharges CSR committee functions. |
| **Treatment of Unspent CSR Funds** | • *Ongoing Projects:* Transfer to a special bank account ("Unspent CSR Account") within **30 days** of FY end, spent within 3 FYs.<br>• *Other than Ongoing Projects:* Transfer to a Fund specified in **Schedule VII** within **6 months** of FY end. | Unspent CSR funds cannot be retained in general company reserves. |

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 05 & Unit 10 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM) Traffic Risk:** In HAM road projects, the private developer **DOES NOT bear traffic risk**. The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) / Government collects tolls and absorbs 100% of the traffic risk.
2. **Trap — Net Zero Target Year:** India's official commitment for Net Zero greenhouse gas emissions is **2070** (not 2050 as pledged by Western economies).
3. **Trap — CSR Applicability Criteria:** A company is covered if it breaches **any one** of the three criteria: Net Worth ₹500 Cr, Turnover ₹1,000 Cr, OR Net Profit ₹5 Cr.
4. **Trap — CSR Committee Exemption:** If a company's annual CSR obligation does not exceed **₹50 Lakh**, it is **not mandatory** to constitute a separate CSR Committee; the Board discharges these functions directly.

### Unit 05 & Unit 10 Practice Questions (IIBF DB&F Pattern)

**Q1. Under the Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM) widely deployed for highway infrastructure financing in India, what proportion of the project capital cost is provided as an inflation-linked grant by the government during the construction phase?**  
A. 20%  
B. 40%  
C. 60%  
D. 100%  

**Q2. Under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013, which of the following companies is NOT legally mandated to spend at least 2% of its average net profits on Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)?**  
A. A company having a net worth of ₹550 Crore and net profit of ₹2 Crore  
B. A company having an annual turnover of ₹1,200 Crore and net profit of ₹3 Crore  
C. A company having a net worth of ₹300 Crore, turnover of ₹800 Crore, and net profit of ₹4 Crore  
D. A company having a net profit of ₹6 Crore and turnover of ₹600 Crore  

**Q3. Under India's 'Panchamrit' climate pledges, what is the mandated national target year to achieve Net Zero carbon emissions?**  
A. 2030  
B. 2047  
C. 2050  
D. 2070  

**Q4. Consider the following statements regarding the Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY health insurance scheme:**  
Statement I: The scheme provides health coverage up to ₹5 Lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary care hospitalization.  
Statement II: Individuals aged 70 years and above are eligible for ₹5 Lakh health cover under PM-JAY only if their annual household income is below ₹3 Lakh.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?  
A. Statement I only  
B. Statement II only  
C. Both Statement I and Statement II  
D. Neither Statement I nor Statement II  

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under HAM, 40% of the capital cost is disbursed by the government in five equal milestones during the construction period, while the remaining 60% is funded by the concessionaire and repaid via semi-annual annuities.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Company C meets none of the Section 135 thresholds (Net worth < ₹500 Cr, turnover < ₹1,000 Cr, net profit < ₹5 Cr). Companies A, B, and D each breach at least one statutory threshold.  
• **Q3 — Answer: D.** India's target year for achieving Net Zero emissions under the Panchamrit declaration is 2070.  
• **Q4 — Answer: A.** Statement I is correct. Statement II is incorrect because under the expanded PM-JAY guidelines, all senior citizens aged 70 years and above are covered **irrespective of income status**.
