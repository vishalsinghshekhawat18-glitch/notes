## § 8.1 Unit 12: Fundamentals of Economics, Microeconomics, Macroeconomics & Types of Economies

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 12)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Economic theory bifurcates into Microeconomics (individual consumer and firm optimization) and Macroeconomics (aggregate national income, inflation, and employment). Market structures determine the pricing power of firms, ranging from price-taking Perfect Competition to interdependent Oligopolies.

### 1. Fundamental Dichotomies in Economics

| Dichotomy Category | Dimension A | Dimension B | Core Analytical Distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scope of Inquiry** | **Microeconomics** (Adam Smith, Alfred Marshall) | **Macroeconomics** (John Maynard Keynes) | Micro examines individual decision-makers (households, firms, specific markets). Macro studies economy-wide aggregates (GDP, national savings, general price level, aggregate employment). |
| **Methodological Nature** | **Positive Economics** ("What is") | **Normative Economics** ("What ought to be") | Positive economics is descriptive, objective, and testable against empirical data. Normative economics involves value judgments, ethical preferences, and prescriptive policy goals. |
| **Economic Systems** | **Capitalist / Market Economy** | **Socialist / Command Economy** | Capitalism allocates resources through decentralized price mechanisms and private property ("Invisible Hand"). Socialism centralizes resource allocation via state planning bureaus. |
| **Mixed Economy** | Coexistence of Public and Private Sectors | India's Constitutional & Economic Model | State controls strategic core infrastructure and natural monopolies; private capital operates freely in competitive consumer goods, agriculture, and services. |

### 2. Comprehensive Market Structures Master Matrix

| Market Structure | Number of Sellers & Buyers | Nature of Product | Barriers to Entry & Exit | Pricing Power of Firm | Demand Curve Faced by Firm |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Perfect Competition** | Very large number of small buyers and sellers | **Homogeneous / Identical** (Perfect substitutes) | **Zero barriers** (Complete freedom of entry & exit) | **Zero (Price Taker)**. Price is set by market supply and demand. | **Perfectly Elastic (Horizontal line)**: $$P = AR = MR$$ |
| **Monopolistic Competition** | Large number of firms | **Differentiated products** (Branding, packaging, design) | **Low barriers** to entry and exit | **Limited pricing power** due to product uniqueness. | **Downward sloping, highly elastic**. High selling/advertising expenses. |
| **Oligopoly** | **Few large dominant sellers** | Homogeneous (Steel/Cement) or Differentiated (Automobiles/Telecom) | **High barriers** (Capital intensity, patents, scale) | **Interdependent pricing**. Firms react strategically to rivals' moves. | **Kinked Demand Curve** (Sweezy Model) explaining price rigidity. |
| **Pure Monopoly** | **Single seller** (Sole producer) | **Unique product** with zero close substitutes | **Extreme / Insurmountable barriers** (Legal, natural, technological) | **Complete (Price Maker)**. Can set either price or quantity, not both. | **Downward sloping, inelastic**. Can practice **Price Discrimination**. |

### 3. Oligopoly Dynamics & Sweezy's Kinked Demand Curve

• **Mutual Interdependence:** In an oligopoly, no single firm can make a pricing or output decision without forecasting the retaliatory reactions of its rival competitors.  
• **Paul Sweezy's Kinked Demand Curve Model (Price Rigidity):**
  - Explains why prices remain "sticky" or rigid in oligopolistic markets even when input costs fluctuate.
  - **Upper Segment (Above Current Price $P^*$):** Highly **Elastic**. If a firm raises its price, competitors will *not follow*, causing the price-raising firm to lose substantial market share.
  - **Lower Segment (Below Current Price $P^*$):** Highly **Inelastic**. If a firm cuts its price, all competitors *immediately follow* to prevent losing their customers, triggering a price war with minimal volume gains.
  - **The Discontinuous Marginal Revenue ($MR$) Gap:** The sudden change in elasticity creates a vertical break (gap) in the $MR$ curve directly below the kink. As long as the firm's Marginal Cost ($MC$) curve shifts within this vertical gap, the profit-maximizing equilibrium price and output remain completely unchanged.

### 4. Monopoly Price Discrimination (A.C. Pigou's Three Degrees)

1. **First-Degree (Perfect Price Discrimination):** The monopolist extracts the entire consumer surplus by charging each consumer the exact maximum price they are willing to pay.
2. **Second-Degree (Block Pricing):** Monopolist charges different unit prices based on the quantity consumed (e.g., declining block tariffs in electricity billing).
3. **Third-Degree (Market Segmentation):** Monopolist segments consumers into distinct sub-markets with different price elasticities of demand (e.g., student vs corporate software discounts; peak vs off-peak train fares). A higher price is charged in the market segment with lower price elasticity.

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 12 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — Perfect Competition Equality:** Under Perfect Competition, the firm is a price taker, which means:
   $$\text{Price} (P) = \text{Average Revenue} (AR) = \text{Marginal Revenue} (MR)$$
   In all other market structures (Monopoly, Monopolistic Competition, Oligopoly), Price equals Average Revenue, but is **greater than Marginal Revenue ($P = AR > MR$)**.
2. **Trap — Sweezy Model Reaction Assumption:** Rivals **ignore price increases** (making the upper curve elastic) but **match price cuts** (making the lower curve inelastic). An option stating rivals match price increases is **false**.
3. **Trap — Normative vs Positive Statements:** Statements containing "should", "ought to", or "is fair" are **normative**. Statements reporting observable empirical facts (e.g., "India's inflation rate rose to 5.4%") are **positive**.

### Unit 12 Practice Questions (IIBF DB&F Pattern)

**Q1. [CONCEPT-RECURRING] In Paul Sweezy's Kinked Demand Curve model of oligopolistic market behavior, the demand curve above the prevailing market price is:**  
A. Highly inelastic because rivals match any price increase  
B. Perfectly inelastic because consumers have zero substitutes  
C. Relatively elastic because rivals will not follow a price increase  
D. A horizontal straight line indicating infinite supply at zero cost  

**Q2. [PYQ-RECOLLECTED] Under perfect competition, which of the following mathematical identities holds true for an individual profit-maximizing firm in the short run?**  
A. Price = Average Revenue = Marginal Revenue  
B. Price > Marginal Revenue = Marginal Cost  
C. Price = Average Cost > Marginal Revenue  
D. Marginal Revenue = Average Cost > Price  

**Q3. [CONCEPT-RECURRING] Consider the following economic statements:**  
Statement I: "The Reserve Bank of India reduced the policy repo rate by 25 basis points in response to falling inflation."  
Statement II: "The central government ought to provide universal basic income to protect unorganized workers from inflation."  
Which of the following correctly classifies these statements?  
A. Both Statement I and Statement II are Positive economic statements  
B. Both Statement I and Statement II are Normative economic statements  
C. Statement I is a Positive economic statement, while Statement II is a Normative economic statement  
D. Statement I is a Normative economic statement, while Statement II is a Positive economic statement  

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** In the Sweezy model, rivals do not match price hikes, so an individual firm faces an elastic demand segment above the kink, losing sales rapidly if it raises prices.  
• **Q2 — Answer: A.** In perfect competition, the firm takes market price as given, meaning every additional unit sold adds exactly the market price to total revenue ($P = AR = MR$).  
• **Q3 — Answer: C.** Statement I states a verifiable empirical action (Positive). Statement II involves an ethical value judgment ("ought to"), representing Normative economics.
