# GLOBALIZATION, FOREIGN TRADE POLICY & GLOBAL INSTITUTIONS

India's external economic engagement is governed by trade liberalization, foreign capital flows, and multilateral institutional frameworks. This chapter covers the impact of Globalisation on India (Unit 06), Foreign Trade Policy and Foreign Investments (Unit 08), and International Economic Organisations including the IMF, World Bank, WTO, and Regional Multilateral Banks (Unit 09).

---

## § 6.1 Unit 06: Globalisation - Impact on India

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 06)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Globalisation opened India's economy to international merchandise trade, cross-border services outsourcing, and foreign capital flows. While boosting productivity and consumer choice, it exposes the domestic economy to external demand shocks and currency volatility.

### 1. The Core Dimensions of Globalisation

• **Trade Dimension:** Dismantling of quantitative import restrictions (QRs), rationalization of customs tariff schedules, and expansion of services exports (software, BPM, GCCs).  
• **Financial & Investment Dimension:** Transition from reliance on external debt to non-debt capital inflows: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) and Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI).  
• **Remittance Channel:** India is the world's largest recipient of inward personal remittances (> $115–120 Billion annually), stabilizing the domestic current account.  
• **Modern Structural Headwinds:** Geoeconomic fragmentation, supply chain reshoring/friendshoring, protectionist non-tariff barriers, and Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanisms (CBAM).

## § 6.2 Unit 08: Foreign Trade Policy, Foreign Investments & Economic Development

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 08)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) 2023 shifted from fixed five-year horizons to a dynamic, continuous framework targeting $2 Trillion in aggregate exports by 2030, supported by Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVA) for currency internationalization.

### 1. Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) 2023 Core Pillars

1. **Continuous Policy Architecture:** Unlike earlier 5-year plans (e.g. FTP 2015–20), FTP 2023 has **no fixed end date** and is updated dynamically based on real-time trade feedback.
2. **Export Target:** Mandates achieving **$2 Trillion in total exports** ($1 Trillion merchandise + $1 Trillion services) by **2030**.
3. **Four Strategic Pillars:**
   - *Incentive to Remission:* Transition from subsidy schemes to automated duty remissions (RoDTEP, RoSCTL).
   - *Export Promotion via Collaboration:* Exporters, States, Districts, and Indian Missions abroad.
   - *Ease of Doing Business:* Paperless digital processing, reduced transaction costs, electronic certificates of origin.
   - *Emerging Sectors:* E-Commerce exports, Towns of Export Excellence (TEE), and Districts as Export Hubs (DEH).
4. **Key Facilitation Measures:**
   - **E-Commerce Export Consignment Cap:** Raised from ₹5 Lakh to **₹10 Lakh per consignment** via courier/postal modes.
   - **Towns of Export Excellence (TEE):** 43 designated industrial clusters eligible for financial assistance under Market Access Initiative (MAI).

### 2. International Trade Settlement in Indian Rupee (SRVA Mechanism)

In July 2022, the RBI introduced a regulatory framework permitting invoice, payment, and settlement of international trade in **Indian Rupees (INR)**:
• **Mechanism:** An authorized dealer (AD Category-I) bank in India opens a **Special Non-Resident Rupee (Vostro) Account (SRVA)** of the partner country's correspondent bank.  
• **Trade Flow:** Indian importers pay in INR credited to the foreign bank's SRVA; Indian exporters are paid in INR debited from the foreign bank's SRVA.  
• **Surplus Balance Deployment:** Surplus INR balances in SRVA can be invested in Indian Government Securities (G-Secs), Treasury Bills, or local corporate bonds.

### 3. Balance of Payments (BoP) Architecture & Convertibility Framework

| BoP Component | Sub-Accounts Included | Economic Nature & Convertibility Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Current Account** | • Merchandise Trade Balance (Exports – Imports)<br>• Invisibles: Services, Transfers (Remittances), Income (Profits, Interest) | **Full Convertibility:** India accepted IMF **Article VIII obligations on 19 August 1994**, establishing full Current Account Convertibility without restrictions on trade/travel payments. |
| **Capital Account** | • Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)<br>• Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)<br>• External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)<br>• Non-Resident Deposits (NRE, FCNR(B)) | **Partial Convertibility:** Controlled via RBI/FEMA regulations. Tarapore Committee (1997 & 2006) established pre-conditions (fiscal deficit < 3%, inflation 3–5%, low gross NPAs) for full capital account convertibility. |

## § 6.3 Unit 09: International Economic Organisations

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 09)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Multilateral financial stability and trade governance are anchored by the Bretton Woods institutions (IMF and World Bank Group), the World Trade Organization (WTO), and regional development banks.

### 1. International Monetary Fund (IMF)

• **Establishment:** Bretton Woods Conference (1944); operations commenced in December 1945; Headquarters in **Washington, D.C.**  
• **Core Purpose:** Safeguard international monetary stability, oversee the fixed/floating exchange rate system, and provide balance-of-payments financing.  
• **Quotas & Voting Power:** Each member is assigned a Quota based on economic size (GDP, openness, variability, reserves). Quotas determine subscription payments, voting power, and maximum borrowing limits. India's quota share is ~2.75% (8th largest).  
• **Special Drawing Rights (SDR):**
  - Created by IMF in 1969 as a supplementary international reserve asset.
  - **Basket of 5 Currencies:** US Dollar (USD), Euro (EUR), Chinese Renminbi (CNY), Japanese Yen (JPY), and British Pound Sterling (GBP). The value of SDR is determined daily.

### 2. The World Bank Group (Five Specialized Institutions)

1. **IBRD (International Bank for Reconstruction and Development):** Provides development loans and policy guarantees to creditworthy middle-income and developing sovereign governments.
2. **IDA (International Development Association):** Known as the **'Soft Loan Window'** of the World Bank. Provides interest-free loans (credits) and grants to the poorest 75+ low-income countries with long repayment tenors (30–40 years).
3. **IFC (International Finance Corporation):** Exclusively finances and advises **private sector enterprises** in developing economies without sovereign government guarantees.
4. **MIGA (Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency):** Promotes cross-border FDI by providing **political risk insurance (guarantees)** against non-commercial risks (war, expropriation, breach of contract).
5. **ICSID (International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes):** Conciliation and arbitration of investment disputes between sovereign states and foreign investors. (**India is NOT a member** of ICSID, citing lack of appeals mechanism).

### 3. World Trade Organization (WTO) & Agreement on Agriculture (AoA)

• **Establishment:** Created by the **Marrakesh Agreement**, replacing GATT on **1 January 1995**; Headquarters in **Geneva, Switzerland**.  
• **AoA Domestic Support Subsidies (The 3 Boxes):**
  - **Green Box:** Subsidies that cause **no or minimal trade distortion** (government R&D, agricultural pest control, environmental programs, decoupled direct income support). **Allowed without any financial limits**.
  - **Blue Box:** Direct payments under **production-limiting programs** based on fixed areas and yields. Permitted without reduction commitments.
  - **Amber Box:** **Trade-distorting subsidies** that directly affect market prices and production (Minimum Support Prices, input subsidies on fertilizers, power, seeds). Subject to *De Minimis* caps: **10% of total agricultural production value** for developing countries, and **5% for developed countries**.
  - *The Peace Clause (Bali Ministerial, 2013):* Protects developing nations like India from legal disputes if public food procurement programs (food security buffer stocks) breach Amber Box de minimis caps.

### 4. Regional Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs)

| Institution | Establishment Year & HQ | Leading Shareholders & Voting Power | Primary Development Mission |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asian Development Bank (ADB)** | 1966; Manila, Philippines | Japan (15.6%) and USA (15.6%) are largest; India is 4th largest shareholder. | Social and economic development in Asia-Pacific region. |
| **New Development Bank (NDB)** | 2014 (Fortaleza BRICS Summit); Shanghai, China | Equal shareholding (20% each) among the five founding BRICS members. | Infrastructure and sustainable development in BRICS and emerging economies. |
| **Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)** | 2016; Beijing, China | China is largest shareholder (~26.6% voting power); **India is 2nd largest shareholder** (~7.6% voting power). | Sovereign and non-sovereign infrastructure financing across Asia. |

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 06, 08 & 09 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — World Bank Group Membership:** India is a member of 4 out of the 5 World Bank institutions: IBRD, IDA, IFC, and MIGA. **India is NOT a member of ICSID**.
2. **Trap — Current vs Capital Convertibility:** India achieved **full Current Account Convertibility in 1994** (under Article VIII of IMF). India has **NOT implemented full Capital Account Convertibility**.
3. **Trap — WTO Green Box Subsidies:** Green Box subsidies are **completely exempt from reduction limits** because they are non-trade-distorting. Do not confuse Green Box with Amber Box (which contains MSP and input subsidies capped at 10% de minimis).
4. **Trap — AIIB Shareholding:** India is the **second-largest shareholder** in the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) after China.

### Unit 06, 08 & 09 Practice Questions (IIBF DB&F Pattern)

**Q1. [PYQ-RECOLLECTED] Under the World Trade Organization (WTO) Agreement on Agriculture (AoA), which category of agricultural support subsidies is considered trade-distorting and subject to strict 'De Minimis' percentage reduction limits?**  
A. Green Box subsidies  
B. Blue Box subsidies  
C. Amber Box subsidies  
D. Special and Differential Treatment Box subsidies  

**Q2. [PYQ-RECOLLECTED] Which of the following institutions comprising the World Bank Group is India NOT a member of?**  
A. International Development Association (IDA)  
B. Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (MIGA)  
C. International Finance Corporation (IFC)  
D. International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID)  

**Q3. [CONCEPT-RECURRING] Under the Reserve Bank of India's Special Rupee Vostro Account (SRVA) mechanism introduced in July 2022, surplus INR balances held by foreign correspondent banks can be invested in:**  
A. Only overseas foreign currency sovereign bonds  
B. Government Securities (G-Secs) and Treasury Bills issued by the Government of India  
C. Real estate residential properties in metropolitan cities  
D. Speculative foreign exchange currency futures contracts  

**Q4. [CONCEPT-RECURRING] Consider the following statements regarding the Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) 2023:**  
Statement I: The FTP 2023 sets an official national target of achieving $2 Trillion in aggregate exports by the year 2030.  
Statement II: The maximum value limit for individual exports through courier and postal modes for e-commerce exporters has been set at ₹10 Lakh per consignment.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?  
A. Statement I only  
B. Statement II only  
C. Both Statement I and Statement II  
D. Neither Statement I nor Statement II  

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** Amber Box contains all trade-distorting price support (such as MSP) and direct input subsidies, capped at 10% of agricultural production value for developing countries.  
• **Q2 — Answer: D.** India is not a signatory to the 1965 ICSID Convention, maintaining that international commercial arbitration should not bypass domestic courts without an appellate provision.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** RBI guidelines permit foreign correspondent banks to deploy surplus rupee balances in Indian G-Secs, T-Bills, and bank fixed deposits.  
• **Q4 — Answer: C.** Both statements are correct under FTP 2023 provisions.
