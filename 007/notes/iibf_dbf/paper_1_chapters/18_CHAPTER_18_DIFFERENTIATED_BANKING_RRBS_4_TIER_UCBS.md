# DIFFERENTIATED BANKING: RRBs & 4-TIER COOPERATIVE BANKS

To serve diverse regional, rural, and niche financial inclusion needs, the Indian banking architecture includes differentiated and localized banking institutions. This chapter covers Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), the RBI's 4-Tier regulatory framework for Primary Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs), and niche banks including Small Finance Banks (SFBs) and Payment Banks.

---

## § 18.1 Unit 21b: Differentiated Banking Architecture

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 21b)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Differentiated banks operate under targeted mandates. RRBs focus on rural credit (75% PSL target, 50:15:35 shareholding). Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs) are organized into a 4-Tier scale-based structure under the amended Banking Regulation Act, with a revised 60% PSL target. Small Finance Banks extend small credit (75% PSL), while Payment Banks handle payments and deposits (capped at ₹2 Lakh) with a strict prohibition on lending.

### 1. Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) Statutory Architecture

• **Legislative Enactment:** Established under the **Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976** (following the Narasimham Working Group recommendations; first RRB, *Prathama Bank* in Moradabad, UP, was established on 2 October 1975, sponsored by Syndicate Bank).  
• **Statutory Shareholding Distribution (Section 6, RRB Act):**
  $$\text{Central Government: } \mathbf{50\%} \quad\Big|\quad \text{Sponsor Commercial Bank: } \mathbf{35\%} \quad\Big|\quad \text{State Government: } \mathbf{15\%}$$
• **Supervision & Regulation:** Regulated by the **Reserve Bank of India** under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, and statutorily inspected and supervised by **NABARD** under Section 35 of the BR Act.  
• **Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Mandate:** RRBs are mandated to achieve a total PSL target of **75% of ANBC or CEOBE**, with 18% dedicated to Agriculture (10% to Small & Marginal Farmers) and 15% to Weaker Sections.  
• **Capital Adequacy:** Mandated to maintain a minimum **CRAR of 9.0%**.

---

### 2. Primary Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs): The 4-Tier Regulatory Framework

Following the recommendations of the **N.S. Vishwanathan Expert Committee (2022)**, the RBI instituted a four-tiered regulatory framework based on deposit size to apply proportional regulation across UCBs:

| Tier Classification | Deposit Size Threshold | Minimum Net Worth Requirement | Mandatory CRAR Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1 UCBs** | Deposits **up to ₹100 Crore** | • **₹2 Crore** (for unit UCBs operating in a single district)<br>• **₹5 Crore** (for all other UCBs) | **9.0%** (Standard baseline capital) |
| **Tier 2 UCBs** | Deposits **> ₹100 Crore and up to ₹1,000 Crore** | **₹5 Crore** | **12.0%** (Phased compliance) |
| **Tier 3 UCBs** | Deposits **> ₹1,000 Crore and up to ₹10,000 Crore** | **₹5 Crore** | **12.0%** |
| **Tier 4 UCBs** | Deposits **exceeding ₹10,000 Crore** | **₹5 Crore** | **12.0%** (Subject to comprehensive bank-like controls) |

> **Critical Regulatory Update — UCB Priority Sector Lending (PSL):**  
> Under the RBI's revised directions, the overall Priority Sector Lending target for Primary Urban Co-operative Banks is set at **60% of ANBC or CEOBE** (moderated from the earlier 75% aspirational path to preserve lending stability across municipal and cooperative centers).

• **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020 & UCB Governance:**
  - Resolved the historic "Dual Control" friction between State Registrars of Co-operative Societies (RCS) and the RBI.
  - Empowered RBI with direct statutory authority over UCBs regarding:
    1. Licensing, management appointments, and CEO qualifications under fit-and-proper criteria.
    2. Powers to supersede the Board of Directors of a cooperative bank for up to 5 years (under Section 56).
    3. Directing statutory audits by professional chartered accountants.
    4. Formulating schemes of reconstruction or amalgamation under Section 45 without placing the bank under initial moratorium.

### 3. Niche Differentiated Banks: Small Finance Banks vs. Payment Banks

Following the recommendations of the **Committee on Comprehensive Financial Services for Small Businesses and Low Income Households (Chaired by Dr. Nachiket Mor, 2014)**, the RBI introduced two differentiated banking licenses:

| Parameter | Small Finance Banks (SFBs) | Payment Banks (PBs) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Objective** | Financial inclusion through **savings mobilization and small credit delivery** to unserved segments. | Financial inclusion through **high-volume, low-value payments, remittances, and savings**. |
| **Minimum Paid-Up Capital** | **₹200 Crore** (₹100 Crore for UCBs voluntarily converting into SFBs) | **₹100 Crore** |
| **Lending Activity** | **PERMITTED** across retail, agriculture, MSME, and microfinance segments. | **STRICTLY PROHIBITED:** **Cannot lend, cannot grant loans, cannot issue credit cards!** |
| **Permitted Deposits** | Both Demand Deposits (CASA) and Time Deposits (FD/RD) without statutory ceiling. | **Demand Deposits only** (Savings & Current). Maximum balance: **₹2,00,000 per customer**. |
| **Portfolio Loan Restrictions** | At least **50% of total loan portfolio must comprise loans up to ₹25 Lakh**. | N/A (Zero loans permitted). |
| **Rural Branch Mandate** | At least **25% of physical branches must be in unbanked rural centres** (population < 10,000). | At least **25% of physical access points must be in unbanked rural centres**. |
| **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** | **75% of ANBC or CEOBE** | N/A |
| **Capital Adequacy (CRAR)** | Minimum **15.0% of RWAs** (Tier 1 min 7.5%) | Minimum **15.0% of RWAs** |
| **Statutory Liquidity Deployment** | Normal CRR and SLR requirements. | • Mandatory CRR with RBI.<br>• Must invest **minimum 75% of demand deposits in G-Secs/T-Bills** (up to 1 yr tenor).<br>• Remaining max 25% in current/term deposits with SCBs. |
| **Promoter Lock-in** | Minimum 40% promoter equity locked for 5 years. | Minimum 40% promoter equity locked for 5 years. |

### 4. Local Area Banks (LABs) Architecture

• **Origin & Policy Mandate:** Introduced in **August 1996** following the 1996 Union Budget to mobilize rural and semi-urban household savings and channel local resources into local investments.  
• **Statutory & Operational Boundaries:**  
  - **Geographical Jurisdiction:** Strictly restricted to operate across a maximum of **three contiguous districts**.  
  - **Capital Requirement:** Minimum paid-up equity capital of **₹5 Crore** (with promoters contributing at least ₹2 Crore).  
  - **Regulatory Status:** Licensed under Section 22 of Banking Regulation Act, 1949 as Non-Scheduled Commercial Banks; mandated to maintain **15.0% CRAR** and comply with prudential priority sector norms. Active surviving LABs include *Coastal Local Area Bank Ltd* and *Krishna Bhima Samruddhi LAB Ltd*.

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 21b High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — Payment Bank Credit Cards:** Payment Banks **CANNOT issue Credit Cards**. They can issue ATM/Debit cards, Net Banking, and UPI handles, but **zero credit products**.
2. **Trap — Payment Bank Deposit Ceiling:** The maximum balance limit per customer in a Payment Bank is **₹2,00,000** (raised by RBI from the initial ₹1,00,000 limit in April 2021).
3. **Trap — RRB Shareholding Breakdown:** Central Government: **50%**, Sponsor Bank: **35%**, State Government: **15%**. (Do not confuse the Sponsor Bank's 35% with the State's 15%).
4. **Trap — UCB 2025 PSL Target:** In current RBI Master Directions, the overall PSL target for Urban Co-operative Banks is **60% of ANBC**, NOT 75% or 40%.
5. **Trap — Small Finance Bank Small Loan Quota:** An SFB must ensure that at least **50% of its loan portfolio comprises loans up to ₹25 Lakh**.
6. **Trap — Local Area Bank Jurisdiction:** LABs are restricted to a maximum of **3 contiguous districts** with minimum capital of **₹5 Crore**; they are NOT multi-state scheduled banks.

### Unit 21b Practice Questions (IIBF DB&F Pattern)

**Q1. [PYQ-PATTERN / RECOLLECTED RECONSTRUCTION / RRB SHAREHOLDING] What is the statutory equity shareholding pattern in Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) established under the Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976?**  
A. Central Govt: 51%, State Govt: 25%, Sponsor Bank: 24%  
B. Central Govt: 50%, Sponsor Bank: 35%, State Govt: 15%  
C. Central Govt: 60%, Sponsor Bank: 20%, State Govt: 20%  
D. Central Govt: 33.3%, Sponsor Bank: 33.3%, State Govt: 33.4%  

**Q2. [CONCEPT-RECURRING / 4-TIER UCB] Under the Reserve Bank of India's 4-Tier regulatory framework for Primary Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs), a cooperative bank with total deposits of ₹450 Crore is classified under:**  
A. Tier 1 UCB  
B. Tier 2 UCB  
C. Tier 3 UCB  
D. Tier 4 UCB  

**Q3. [PYQ-PATTERN / RECOLLECTED RECONSTRUCTION / PAYMENT BANK PROHIBITIONS] Under RBI guidelines governing Payment Banks, which of the following activities is strictly prohibited?**  
A. Issuing Rupay Debit Cards linked to savings accounts  
B. Accepting demand deposits up to ₹2,00,000 per customer  
C. Providing utility bill payment and domestic inward remittance services  
D. Granting personal housing loans or issuing revolving credit cards  

**Q4. [2026 BENCHMARK / SFB PORTFOLIO] Consider the following regulatory requirements for Small Finance Banks (SFBs):**  
Statement I: SFBs are required to achieve a Priority Sector Lending target of 75% of ANBC/CEOBE.  
Statement II: SFBs must deploy at least 50% of their total loan portfolio in advances of up to ₹25 Lakh.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?  
A. Statement I only  
B. Statement II only  
C. Both Statement I and Statement II  
D. Neither Statement I nor Statement II  

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under Section 6 of the RRB Act, 1976, the capital is shared in the ratio of 50% (Central Government), 35% (Sponsor Bank), and 15% (State Government).  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** Under the N.S. Vishwanathan framework, Tier 2 UCBs are those with deposits greater than ₹100 Crore and up to ₹1,000 Crore. A bank with ₹450 Crore deposits is a **Tier 2 UCB**.  
• **Q3 — Answer: D.** Payment Banks are strictly prohibited from undertaking any form of lending activity, including housing loans, overdrafts, or credit cards.  
• **Q4 — Answer: C.** Both statements are correct under the RBI operating guidelines for Small Finance Banks.
