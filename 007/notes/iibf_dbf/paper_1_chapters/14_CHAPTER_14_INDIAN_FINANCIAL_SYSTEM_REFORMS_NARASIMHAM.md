# INDIAN FINANCIAL SYSTEM & BANKING SECTOR REFORMS

The Indian Financial System mobilizes financial savings and channels them into productive economic investments through institutional intermediaries and organized markets. This chapter covers the architecture of the Indian Financial System (Unit 20) and Banking Sector Reforms, Bad Bank infrastructure (NARCL-IDRCL), and the EASE agenda (Unit 28).

---

## § 14.1 Unit 20: Indian Financial System - An Overview

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 20)**  
> **Core Proposition:** The financial system operates through four interdependent pillars: Financial Institutions, Financial Markets, Financial Instruments, and Financial Services. Its primary economic function is efficient capital allocation and liquidity transformation under apex regulatory oversight.

### 1. The Four Pillars of the Financial System

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE FOUR PILLARS OF THE INDIAN FINANCIAL SYSTEM             │
├──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. INSTITUTIONS      │ 2. MARKETS           │ 3. INSTRUMENTS & SERVICES     │
│ • Commercial Banks   │ • Money Market       │ • Equity & Debt Securities    │
│ • Cooperative Banks  │ • Capital Market     │ • T-Bills, CP, CD, Repos      │
│ • DFIs (NaBFID)      │ • Forex Market       │ • Derivatives (Swaps, Futures)│
│ • NBFCs & MFIs       │ • Derivatives Market │ • Mutual Funds & AIFs         │
│ • Primary Dealers    │                      │ • Merchant Banking & Custody  │
└──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘
```

• **Financial Intermediation:** Intermediaries bridge the gap between surplus economic units (household savers) and deficit economic units (corporate and government borrowers).  
• **Maturity Transformation:** Commercial banks transform short-term liquid liabilities (demand deposits, savings accounts) into long-term illiquid assets (housing loans, infrastructure project credit), creating asset-liability management (ALM) liquidity risk that necessitates prudential regulation.

## § 14.2 Unit 28: Reforms and Developments in the Banking Sector

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 28)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Post-1991 banking reforms dismantled financial repression under the Narasimham Committee recommendations. Recent structural consolidation is anchored by the Bad Bank framework (NARCL-IDRCL), IBC resolution, and the EASE governance reforms across Public Sector Banks.

### 1. The Landmark Narasimham Committee Frameworks

| Parameter | Narasimham Committee I (1991) | Narasimham Committee II (1998) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Official Title** | Committee on the Financial System (CFS) | Committee on Banking Sector Reforms (CBSR) |
| **Mandate** | Address severe balance sheet weakness and structural repression across public sector banks. | Consolidate post-reform banking system and enhance global competitiveness. |
| **Statutory Reserve Norms** | Recommended phased reduction of **CRR to 3%–5%** and **SLR to 25%** to release commercial lending funds. | Recommended further deregulation of reserves; greater autonomy for public sector bank boards. |
| **Prudential Standards** | • Introduced **90-Day NPA Classification Norm**.<br>• Mandated **8% Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR)** aligned to Basel I norms.<br>• Phased provisioning for sub-standard, doubtful, and loss assets. | • Recommended raising minimum **CRAR to 9% by 2000** and **10% by 2002**.<br>• Mandated **100% Mark-to-Market (MTM)** valuation of government securities portfolios. |
| **Structural Architecture** | • Permitted entry of **new private sector commercial banks** (HDFC, ICICI, UTI/Axis).<br>• Recommended establishing specialized **Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs)** (enacted via RDDBFI Act 1993).<br>• Creation of the Board for Financial Supervision (BFS) inside RBI. | • Proposed a **Three-Tier Banking Structure**: 2–3 large global Indian banks; 8–10 national banks; specialized regional/local banks.<br>• Laid ground for SARFAESI Act, 2002.<br>• Targeted branch computerization and core banking. |

### 2. Asset Quality Review (AQR, 2015) & Legacy Cleanup

• **Context:** Between 2008 and 2014, commercial banks engaged in "evergreening" stressed corporate infrastructure loans via forbearance schemes (Corporate Debt Restructuring - CDR, Strategic Debt Restructuring - SDR, 5:25 refinancing, and Scheme for Sustainable Structuring of Stressed Assets - S4A).  
• **The 2015 AQR:** The RBI conducted an exhaustive asset quality review across commercial bank loan books, withdrawing regulatory forbearance and mandating banks to classify delinquent corporate exposures as Non-Performing Assets (NPAs).  
• **Outcome:** Transparent recognition caused Gross NPAs across Scheduled Commercial Banks to peak at **11.5% in March 2018** (over ₹10 Lakh Crore), creating the "Twin Balance Sheet Problem" (stressed corporate balance sheets alongside stressed public sector bank balance sheets).

### 3. The Bad Bank Architecture: NARCL & IDRCL

To resolve large stressed corporate assets exceeding ₹500 Crore, the Government of India and the banking industry established a dual "Bad Bank" institutional mechanism:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  DUAL BAD BANK INSTITUTIONAL ARCHITECTURE                   │
├────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│ NATIONAL ASSET RECONSTRUCTION CO.      │ INDIA DEBT RESOLUTION CO. LTD.     │
│ LTD. (NARCL)                           │ (IDRCL)                            │
│ • Registered as an ARC under SARFAESI  │ • Operates as an exclusive Asset   │
│ • Public Sector Banks hold 51% EQUITY  │   Management Company (AMC)         │
│ • Acquires stressed loans from banks   │ • Private Sector FIs hold 51%      │
│ • Pays: 15% CASH + 85% SECURITY        │ • Drives turnaround, restructuring,│
│   RECEIPTS (SRs)                       │   and resolution of assets         │
└────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```

• **Sovereign Guarantee on Security Receipts:**
  - The Central Government approved a **sovereign guarantee of up to ₹30,600 Crore** backing the Security Receipts (SRs) issued by NARCL.
  - **Operational Mechanism:** The guarantee is valid for **5 years**. If the actual recovery from resolution falls short of the face value of the SRs, the sovereign guarantee liquidates the difference, protecting bank balance sheets against recovery shortfalls.

### 4. Enhanced Access and Service Excellence (EASE) Agenda

The **EASE Agenda** is an annual reform roadmap jointly driven by the **Department of Financial Services (DFS, Ministry of Finance)** and the **Indian Banks' Association (IBA)** to institutionalize clean, technology-driven governance across Public Sector Banks (PSBs):

| EASE Edition | Core Theme & Strategic Focus | Key Banking Deliverables |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EASE 1.0** (2018) | **Clean and Smart Banking** | Transparent NPA recognition, clean provisioning, recovery monitoring. |
| **EASE 2.0** (2019) | **Clean, Smart and Inclusive** | Dedicated Stressed Asset Management Verticals (SAMVs), PSB consolidation. |
| **EASE 3.0** (2020) | **Smart, Tech-Enabled Banking** | 'Dial-a-Loan' for MSMEs, digital retail lending, co-lending platforms. |
| **EASE 4.0** (2021) | **24x7 Tech Banking & Data Analytics** | Cloud adoption, digital agricultural financing (KCC digitization), dividend payouts. |
| **EASE 5.0** (2022) | **Digital Customer Experience** | Integrated omnichannel banking, localized digital service points. |
| **EASE 6.0 / 7.0** (Current) | **Customer-Centric Digital Banking & AI** | AI-driven fraud prevention, automated MSME underwriting, cyber security, ESG risk integration. |

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 20 & Unit 28 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — NARCL vs IDRCL Shareholding:** Public Sector Banks hold the **majority 51% equity in NARCL** (the asset reconstruction vehicle), while Private Sector Financial Institutions hold the **majority 51% equity in IDRCL** (the resolution/asset management vehicle). Do not invert these shareholding structures!
2. **Trap — Bad Bank Cash-to-SR Ratio:** Under the NARCL acquisition model, banks receive **15% in upfront cash and 85% in Security Receipts (SRs)** (not 50:50 or 100% cash).
3. **Trap — Government Guarantee Ceiling on SRs:** The sovereign guarantee backing NARCL Security Receipts is capped at **₹30,600 Crore**, valid for **5 years**.
4. **Trap — Universal Banking Recommendation:** The blueprint for Universal Banking (merging the operational roles of commercial banks and DFIs) was formulated by the **Khan Committee (1998)**, not the Narasimham Committee.

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** NARCL (the ARC) is majority-owned (51%) by Public Sector Banks, while IDRCL (the asset manager) is majority-owned (51%) by Private Sector Financial Institutions.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Acquisition consideration consists of 15% upfront cash and 85% in Security Receipts backed by a ₹30,600 Crore government guarantee.  
• **Q3 — Answer: A.** Statements I and II are core recommendations of Narasimham Committee I. Statement III is false because the committee recommended permitting the entry of **new private sector banks**, not nationalization.  
• **Q4 — Answer: B.** EASE 1.0 focused on "Clean and Smart Banking", enforcing transparent NPA classification, ring-fencing non-performing loans, and setting up Stressed Asset Management Verticals.
