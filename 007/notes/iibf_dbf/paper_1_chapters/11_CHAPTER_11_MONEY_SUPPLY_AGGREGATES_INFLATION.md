# MONEY SUPPLY MEASURES (M0-M4, L1-L3) & INFLATION

The creation, regulation, and circulation of money forms the foundation of commercial banking and central monetary management. The Reserve Bank of India regulates money supply aggregates to calibrate systemic liquidity, stabilize the purchasing power of the currency, and steer headline inflation within the statutory target framework.

---

## § 11.1 Unit 14: Money Supply and Inflation

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 1 Benchmark (Unit 14)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Money supply is classified into standard measures ($M0$ to $M4$), New Monetary Aggregates ($NM1$ to $NM3$), and Liquidity Aggregates ($L1$ to $L3$). The money multiplier expands central bank reserve money through fractional reserve commercial banking. Inflation targeting in India is legally anchored to headline CPI-Combined ($4\% \pm 2\%$).

### 1. Currency Issuance & Section 33 Minimum Reserve System

Under **Section 33 of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934**, the Issue Department of the RBI issues currency notes against backing assets under the **Minimum Reserve System (instituted in 1956)**:
• **Total Statutory Reserve:** The RBI must maintain aggregate backing assets of not less than **₹200 Crore** at all times.  
• **Gold Requirement:** Of this ₹200 Crore, the value of **Gold Coin and Gold Bullion must NOT be less than ₹115 Crore**.  
• **Foreign Securities:** The remaining balance of **₹85 Crore** is held in foreign securities (bonds, foreign exchange reserves).

### 2. Traditional Money Supply Aggregates (1977 RBI Framework)

| Measure | Classification | Components Included | Economic & Banking Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$M0$** | **Reserve Money / Monetary Base** (High-Powered Money) | $$\text{Currency in Circulation} + \text{Bankers' Deposits with RBI} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$$ | Central bank money; the sovereign liquidity base supporting credit creation. |
| **$M1$** | **Narrow Money** | $$\text{Currency with the Public} + \text{Demand Deposits with Banking System} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$$ | Most liquid monetary measure; immediately spendable transaction money. |
| **$M2$** | Semi-Narrow Money | $$M1 + \text{Post Office Savings Bank Deposits}$$ | Incorporates postal transaction accounts. |
| **$M3$** | **Broad Money** | $$M1 + \text{Time Deposits with Banking System}$$ | **Primary monetary indicator** tracked by RBI for macroeconomic policy and credit expansion. |
| **$M4$** | Comprehensive Broad Money | $$M3 + \text{Total Post Office Deposits (EXCLUDING National Savings Certificates - NSC)}$$ | Broadest historical liquidity measure. |

> **Critical Composition Note:**  
> • **Currency with the Public:** $\text{Currency in Circulation} - \text{Cash held by banks in vaults}$.  
> • **Demand Deposits:** Pure current accounts and demand liability portions of savings accounts (excluding inter-bank deposits).  
> • **Time Deposits:** Fixed deposits and recurring deposits of the public held across commercial and cooperative banks.

### 3. New Monetary Aggregates ($NM$) & Liquidity Aggregates ($L$) (1998 Y.V. Reddy Committee)

The 1998 Working Group introduced New Monetary Aggregates based on residency and maturity profiles:

| Measure | Formula / Aggregation Layer | Key Inclusions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$NM1$** | $$\text{Currency with Public} + \text{Demand Deposits} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$$ | Resident transaction money. |
| **$NM2$** | $$NM1 + \text{Short-Term Time Deposits of Residents (Maturity up to 1 Year)}$$ | Captures interest-rate sensitive retail deposits. |
| **$NM3$** | $$NM2 + \text{Long-Term Time Deposits (> 1 Year)} + \text{Call/Term Funding from Financial Institutions}$$ | Resident broad money measure. |
| **$L1$** | $$NM3 + \text{All Deposits with Post Office Savings Banks (Excluding NSC)}$$ | Baseline liquidity aggregate. |
| **$L2$** | $$L1 + \text{Term Deposits with All-India FIs (NABARD, EXIM, SIDBI, NHB)} + \text{Term Borrowings by FIs} + \text{CDs issued by FIs}$$ | Institutional wholesale funding layer. |
| **$L3$** | $$L2 + \text{Public Deposits of Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)}$$ | Comprehensive macro-financial liquidity metric. |

### 4. The Money Multiplier Mechanism ($m$)

The **Money Multiplier** reflects the extent to which commercial bank credit creation expands high-powered reserve money ($M0$) into broad money ($M3$):

$$m = \frac{M3}{M0} = \frac{1 + c}{c + r}$$

Where:
• $c = \frac{C}{D}$ is the **Currency-to-Deposit Ratio** (public's preference for holding physical cash relative to bank deposits).  
• $r = \frac{R}{D}$ is the **Reserve-to-Deposit Ratio** of commercial banks:
  $$r = \text{Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)} + \text{Excess Reserve Ratio}$$

• **Multiplier Dynamics:**  
  - If the RBI increases the **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)**, $r$ rises, reducing the money multiplier $m$ and contracting total money supply ($M3$).  
  - If the public holds less physical cash and deposits more into banks ($c \downarrow$), the banking system's deposit creation capacity expands, driving the multiplier $m$ upward.

### 5. Inflation Dynamics: CPI vs. WPI

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services, leading to a loss in purchasing power:

| Parameter | Consumer Price Index (CPI-Combined) | Wholesale Price Index (WPI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Official Publishing Body** | **National Statistical Office (NSO), MoSPI** | **Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce)** |
| **Current Base Year** | **2012 = 100** | **2011–12 = 100** |
| **Targeting Role** | **Official Headline Anchor for RBI MPC** ($4\% \pm 2\%$) | Monitored for input costs and wholesale producer pricing. |
| **Inclusion of Services** | **INCLUDED** (Healthcare, education, transport, housing, etc.) | **STRICTLY EXCLUDED** (Measures physical commodities only). |
| **Food & Beverages Weight** | **High Weight: 45.86%** (High sensitivity to food price shocks) | Grouped in Primary Articles & Manufactured Food (~24.4%). |
| **Major Commodity Groups** | Food & Beverages (45.86%), Housing (10.07%), Fuel & Light (6.84%), Clothing (6.53%), Misc/Services (28.32%). | Manufactured Products (**64.23%**), Primary Articles (**22.62%**), Fuel & Power (**13.15%**). |

• **Headline vs. Core Inflation:**
  - *Headline Inflation:* Total inflation reported via the complete Consumer Price Index, including volatile sub-components.
  - *Core Inflation:* Headline CPI **excluding Food and Fuel components** ($\text{Core CPI} = \text{Headline CPI} - [\text{Food \& Beverages} + \text{Fuel \& Light}]$), reflecting underlying structural demand pressures.
• **Inflation Classifications:**
  - *Creeping Inflation:* Mild, manageable price rise (2% to 3% annually), considered healthy for economic activity.
  - *Galloping / Hyperinflation:* Rapid, uncontrollable price escalation (double/triple-digit rates), leading to currency breakdown.
  - *Stagflation:* Dangerous economic condition combining **stagnant economic growth, high unemployment, and high inflation**.
  - *Disinflation:* A decrease in the **rate of inflation** (prices are still rising, but at a slower pace).
  - *Deflation:* A persistent **fall in the absolute price level** (negative inflation rate).

### Examiner Trap Vault: Unit 14 High-Yield Distractors

1. **Trap — Exclusions in $M4$ and $L1$:** National Savings Certificates (NSC) are **strictly excluded** from postal deposit calculations in $M4$ and $L1$.
2. **Trap — Services in WPI:** The Wholesale Price Index **DOES NOT include services**. If an exam question asks whether IT or banking charges are captured in WPI, the answer is **NO**; services are captured exclusively in CPI.
3. **Trap — RBI Statutory Inflation Anchor:** The Monetary Policy Committee targets **Headline CPI (Combined)**, not Core CPI and not WPI.
4. **Trap — Minimum Reserve System Split:** Under Section 33 of the RBI Act, total reserves are ₹200 Crore, of which **Gold must be at least ₹115 Crore** (not ₹85 Crore).

**Q3. [CONCEPT-RECURRING] Which of the following statements correctly differentiates the Consumer Price Index (CPI-Combined) from the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) in India?**  
Statement I: CPI measures price movements at the retail consumer level and includes services, whereas WPI measures commodity prices at the wholesale stage and excludes services.  
Statement II: The Reserve Bank of India uses the Wholesale Price Index as its nominal anchor for flexible inflation targeting.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?  
A. Statement I only  
B. Statement II only  
C. Both Statement I and Statement II  
D. Neither Statement I nor Statement II  

**Q4. [PYQ-PATTERN / RECOLLECTED RECONSTRUCTION] If commercial banks decide to hold higher excess reserves and the public's preference for holding physical currency increases relative to bank deposits, what is the impact on the Money Multiplier ($m$)?**  
A. The money multiplier increases significantly  
B. The money multiplier remains strictly constant  
C. The money multiplier decreases  
D. The money multiplier becomes equal to infinity  

---

### Answer Key & Explanations

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under the Minimum Reserve System (1956), out of the total statutory reserve of ₹200 Crore, at least ₹115 Crore must be in gold bullion/coins, with the remaining ₹85 Crore in foreign securities.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Broad Money $M3$ equals Narrow Money $M1$ (Currency with public + Demand deposits + Other deposits with RBI) plus Time deposits with commercial and cooperative banks.  
• **Q3 — Answer: A.** Statement I is correct. Statement II is incorrect because the RBI's flexible inflation targeting framework legally anchors to **Headline CPI-Combined** ($4\% \pm 2\%$), not WPI.  
• **Q4 — Answer: C.** Both an increase in the currency-deposit ratio ($c$) and an increase in the reserve-deposit ratio ($r$) reduce the money multiplier ($m = \frac{1+c}{c+r}$), contracting the credit creation capacity of the banking system.
