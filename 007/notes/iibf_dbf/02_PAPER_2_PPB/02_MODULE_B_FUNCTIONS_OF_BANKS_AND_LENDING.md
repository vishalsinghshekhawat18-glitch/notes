## 2. 📌 Module 2: Credit Risk, Prudential Lending & Fair Lending Frameworks

Prudential credit guidelines govern credit risk underwriting, portfolio provisioning, and borrower protections across Scheduled Commercial Banks, NBFCs, and Housing Finance Companies.

• Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Ind-AS 109 / IFRS 9 Framework: Replaces ex-post Incurred Loss Model with ex-ante forward-looking provisioning based on macroeconomic forecasts. Three stages: Stage 1 (Performing, 0-30 DPD) = 12-Month ECL.
• Stage 2 (Underperforming, 31-89 DPD / Significant Increase in Credit Risk - SICR) = Lifetime ECL.
• Stage 3 (Credit-Impaired / Default, 90+ DPD) = Lifetime ECL based on PD x LGD x EAD. 5-year capital transition glide path.
• RBI Co-Lending Model (CLM 1 & CLM 2): Combines low-cost bank capital with last-mile NBFC sourcing reach for Priority Sector. CLM 1 = Joint Lending; CLM 2 = Direct Assignment / Sourcing takeover. Mandatory Invariant: NBFC MUST retain minimum 20% credit risk on its own balance sheet. End-borrower pays blended interest rate. All repayments routed via tripartite Escrow account.
• Fair Practices Code on Penal Charges: Abolished compounding penal interest. Only reasonable, board-approved Penal Charges permitted. Penal charges cannot be capitalized or added to principal balance (no interest charged on penal charges). For individual non-business loans, charges cannot exceed commercial defaults.
• Digital Lending Guidelines 2025 (DLG): Regulated Entities (REs) fully responsible for LSPs/DLAs. Fund disbursals & repayments must flow directly between RE bank account and borrower bank account without synthetic pass-through pool accounts. Key Fact Statement (KFS) disclosing APR mandatory. Minimum cooling-off window: 3 days (tenor >=7 days) or 1 day (<7 days). First Loss Default Guarantee (FLDG) strictly capped at 5% of portfolio.
• Mutual Credit Guarantee Scheme (MCGS) & CGTMSE: Credit guarantee for MSEs up to ₹5 Crore (₹10 Cr for high-tech). Tiered guarantee: 85% for micro loans up to ₹5 Lakh, women, SC/ST, and NER; 75% for standard MSEs.
• Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS): Short-term crop loans up to ₹3 Lakh at benchmark 7.0% p.a. GoI provides 1.5% subvention to banks + 3.0% Prompt Repayment Incentive (PRI) to farmers -> effective net rate of 4.0% p.a.
• PM Vishwakarma Scheme: Collateral-free loans up to ₹3 Lakh (Tranche 1: ₹1L, Tranche 2: ₹2L) at concessional 5.0% interest rate (MoMSME subvention up to 8%).
• Priority Sector Lending (PSL) 2025-26 Overhaul: Housing loan limits categorized into 3 population tiers: >=50 Lakh pop (₹50 Lakh limit), 10L-50L pop (₹45 Lakh limit), <10L pop (₹35 Lakh limit). Urban Co-operative Banks PSL target fixed at 60% ANBC/CEOBE. Renewable Energy PSL limit: ₹35 Crore for utilities, ₹10 Lakh for households.
• Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) Revised Norms: Public deposit ceiling reduced from 3x to 1.5x Net Owned Funds (NOF); liquid assets requirement raised from 13% to 15%; maximum deposit tenor reduced from 10 to 5 years; co-branded credit cards and interest rate hedging permitted.

> 🎯 Exam Angle → 🔥 HIGH — Pay close attention to statutory classifications, founding years, nodal ministries, and numerical thresholds in 📌 Module 2: Credit Risk, Prudential Lending & Fair Lending Frameworks.

## 63. IIBF PPB Unit 8: Principles of Lending, Working Capital Assessment & Term Loans

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Working Capital vs Term Loan Appraisal**
> Banks provide credit under two distinct operational horizons: Working Capital (short-term liquidity for day-to-day operating cycle) and Term Loans (medium-to-long-term capital expenditure for creating productive fixed assets).

## 📊 1. Master Framework: Tandon Committee MPBF Methods

| Method of Lending | Borrower Minimum Margin (NWC Contribution) | MPBF Calculation Formula | Benchmark Current Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Method 1 (Tandon) | 25% of the **Working Capital Gap (WCG)** [where WCG = Current Assets (CA) − Current Liabilities (CL excluding bank borrowings)] | MPBF = 0.75 \times (CA - CL) | 1.17 : 1 |
| Method 2 (Tandon / Chore Standard) | 25% of the **Total Current Assets (TCA)** funded from long-term sources / Net Working Capital | MPBF = (0.75 \times CA) - CL | 1.33 : 1 |
| Method 3 (Tandon - Restricted) | 100% of Core Current Assets (CCA) + 25% of remaining Real/Fluctuating Current Assets | MPBF = 0.75 \times (CA - CCA) - CL | 1.79 : 1 |

## 🧮 2. Key Mathematical Formulas for Credit Appraisal

$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{Net Profit After Tax} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Interest on Term Loan}}{\text{Interest on Term Loan} + \text{Principal Term Loan Installment}}$

*Explanation:* Measures borrower's ability to service annual term debt obligations. Benchmark: Average DSCR should ideally be between 1.50 and 2.00. Minimum acceptable DSCR is generally 1.20.

$\text{BEP (\% of capacity)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Costs}}{\text{Installed Capacity Output} \times (\text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost per unit})} \times 100$

*Explanation:* Indicates the level of production/sales where Total Revenue equals Total Cost (Zero Profit, Zero Loss). Lower BEP indicates higher margin of safety for lenders.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Working Capital & Term Loans:
1. **Tandon Method 2 Formula Trap:** Method 2 margin is 25% of **Total Current Assets**, NOT 25% of Working Capital Gap. Formula is: \(\text{MPBF} = (0.75 \times CA) - CL\).
2. **Current Ratio Benchmark:** Method 1 gives a Current Ratio of **1.17:1**, whereas Method 2 mandates a minimum Current Ratio of **1.33:1**.
3. **Nayak Committee Parameters:** Nayak Committee applies to credit limits up to **₹5 Crores** for MSEs. Projected annual turnover \(T\) is assessed; Total Working Capital Requirement = \(25\% \text{ of } T\), of which Bank Finance = \(20\% \text{ of } T\) and Borrower Margin = \(5\% \text{ of } T\).

## 64. IIBF PPB Unit 9: Types of Collaterals & Modes of Creating Charges

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Charge on Securities**
> A charge is a legal right created by a borrower in favor of a lending bank over assets (movable, immovable, or actionable claims) to secure the repayment of financial advances.

## 📊 1. Master Comparison Matrix of the 5 Primary Security Charges

| Charge Type | Nature of Asset | Physical Possession | Ownership Title | Governing Statute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pledge | Movable goods, Gold, Goods in warehouse | With the **Bank (Pawnee)** | With Borrower (Pawnor) | Section 172, Indian Contract Act 1872 |
| Hypothecation | Movable assets (Stock, Machinery, Vehicles) | With the **Borrower (Hypothecator)** | With Borrower | Section 2(1)(n), SARFAESI Act 2002 |
| Mortgage | Immovable property (Land, Buildings) | Constructive / Physical / Symbolic | With Mortgagor (transfers interest) | Section 58, Transfer of Property Act 1882 |
| Assignment | Actionable claims (LIC policies, Book debts, Supply bills) | Documentary assignment transferred to bank | Transferred to Bank (Assignee) | Section 130, Transfer of Property Act 1882 |
| Lien (General) | Securities, Commercial paper, Goods | With the **Bank** (in ordinary course of business) | With Customer | Section 171, Indian Contract Act 1872 |

## 🏛️ 2. The 6 Types of Mortgages (Section 58, Transfer of Property Act 1882)

| Mortgage Type | Section in TP Act | Creation Method & Formalities | Bank's Remedy / Sale Power without Court |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equitable Mortgage (Deposit of Title Deeds) | Section 58(f) | Delivery of original title deeds to the bank with intent to create security in notified towns. No stamp duty/registration needed in most states. | Enforceable via SARFAESI Act 2002 without court intervention; or via civil court decree for sale. |
| Simple Mortgage | Section 58(b) | Registered mortgage deed without delivery of possession. Mortgagor binds himself personally to pay the mortgage money. | Bank has no right to sell property without obtaining a Civil Court decree. |
| Mortgage by Conditional Sale | Section 58(c) | Ostensible sale of property with condition that sale becomes void on repayment, or becomes absolute on default. | Remedy is **Foreclosure** (court order debarring mortgagor from redeeming), NOT sale. |
| Usufructuary Mortgage | Section 58(d) | Delivery of possession to mortgagee who receives rents and profits in lieu of interest/principal. | Mortgagee cannot sue for sale or foreclosure; recovers debt strictly from property usufruct (rents/yields). |
| English Mortgage | Section 58(e) | Absolute transfer of property to mortgagee with agreement to re-transfer upon repayment on a fixed date. | Mortgagee has power to sell property without court intervention under Section 69 TP Act in specific conditions. |
| Anomalous Mortgage | Section 58(g) | A mortgage which is not any of the above 5 types (e.g. combination of simple and usufructuary mortgage). | Terms governed strictly by the custom and express terms in the mortgage deed. |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Collaterals & Charges:
1. **Pledge vs Hypothecation Possession:** In a Pledge, possession is **ALWAYS with the Bank**. In Hypothecation, possession is **ALWAYS with the Borrower**.
2. **Equitable Mortgage Requirements:** 3 essential requisites for a valid Equitable Mortgage under Sec 58(f): (a) a debt, (b) deposit of **ORIGINAL title deeds**, and (c) execution in a town notified by the State Government.
3. **Foreclosure Remedy:** The remedy of **Foreclosure** is available ONLY in *Mortgage by Conditional Sale* and certain *Anomalous Mortgages*. It is **NEVER** available in Simple or Equitable mortgages.

## 65. IIBF PPB Unit 10: Non-Fund Based Facilities — Letters of Credit (LC) & UCPDC 600

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Letter of Credit (Documentary Credit)**
> A Letter of Credit is an irrevocable undertaking issued by a bank (Issuing Bank) at the request of a buyer (Applicant) to pay the seller (Beneficiary) a specified sum, provided compliant documents stipulated in the credit are presented within the validity period.

## 👥 1. The 6 Key Parties in a Letter of Credit Transaction

| Party | Role & Description | Primary Statutory Obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applicant (Opener) | The Buyer / Importer who requests his bank to open the LC | Reimburses the Issuing Bank upon presentation of compliant documents. |
| Issuing Bank (Opening Bank) | The Buyer's bank that issues the LC and gives the primary payment undertaking | Under primary, irrevocable liability to pay beneficiary upon presentation of compliant documents. |
| Advising Bank | Bank in seller's country that authenticates and communicates the LC to the beneficiary | Responsible only for verifying the apparent authenticity of the credit. |
| Confirming Bank | A bank that adds its own independent payment undertaking to the LC at issuing bank's request | Assumes same irrevocable liabilities as the Issuing Bank (dual bank guarantee for seller). |
| Negotiating / Nominated Bank | The bank authorized to examine documents and pay / advance funds to the beneficiary | Examines documents under UCPDC 600 and negotiates compliant drafts. |
| Beneficiary | The Seller / Exporter in whose favor the credit is issued | Entitled to receive payment upon presenting strictly compliant documents within LC validity. |

## 📜 2. Types of Letters of Credit & Special Clauses

| LC Type | Operational Mechanism | Exam Significance / Commercial Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Irrevocable LC | Cannot be amended or cancelled without the consent of all parties (Issuing Bank, Confirming Bank, Beneficiary) | Under UCPDC 600, all LCs are **presumed Irrevocable** unless stated otherwise. |
| Confirmed LC | Confirming bank adds its independent undertaking to pay | Protects seller against Issuing Bank default and country/political risk. |
| Transferable LC | Can be transferred in whole or part by the 1st Beneficiary to 2nd Beneficiary(ies) | Used by middleman/trading agents. Can be transferred **only ONCE** unless stated. |
| Back-to-Back LC | A second separate LC opened by seller's bank on the security of the master incoming LC | Used when the master LC is non-transferable. |
| Red Clause LC | Contains an express clause permitting the advising bank to grant **pre-shipment clean advance** to the seller | Provides working capital to exporter before manufacturing/shipping goods. |
| Green Clause LC | Permits pre-shipment advance for **warehousing and storage** at port of shipment in addition to manufacturing | Requires warehouse receipts as collateral. |
| Standby LC (SBLC) | Operates as a financial guarantee; payable only upon default/non-performance of applicant | Governed by UCPDC 600 or ISP98 (International Standby Practices). |
| Revolving LC | Amount is automatically reinstated after utilization without requiring fresh amendments | Used for regular, repetitive commercial shipments. |

## ⏱️ 3. Strict Examination Timelines under UCPDC 600

• **Article 14(b) - 5 Banking Days Rule:** The Issuing Bank, Confirming Bank, or Nominated Bank each has a maximum of **5 banking days** following the day of receipt of documents to determine whether the presentation is compliant.
• **Article 16 - Notice of Refusal:** If documents are non-compliant, the bank must give a single notice of refusal stating all discrepancies by telecommunication (SWIFT MT734) before the close of the 5th banking day. Failure to do so precludes the bank from claiming non-compliance.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Letters of Credit:
1. **Document Examination Timeline:** Under UCPDC 600, the maximum time to examine documents is **5 banking days**, NOT 7 days (7 days was the old UCP 500 rule).
2. **Presumption of Irrevocability:** Under UCPDC 600 Article 3, an LC is **ALWAYS irrevocable** even if it does not state so on its face.
3. **Red Clause vs Green Clause:** Red Clause covers **pre-shipment manufacturing advance**. Green Clause covers **pre-shipment advance PLUS storage/warehousing at port of origin**.

## 66. IIBF PPB Unit 11: Bank Guarantees (BG), Co-Acceptance & Deferred Payments

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Contract of Guarantee (Section 126, Indian Contract Act 1872)**
> A contract of guarantee is a tripartite contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default.

## 📊 1. Classification & Characteristics of Bank Guarantees

| Guarantee Type | Purpose / Commercial Use Case | Bank Risk Profile & Margin Guidelines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financial Guarantee | Guarantees repayment of a financial debt (e.g. advance payment guarantee, loan repayment, customs/excise duty payment) | High financial risk. RBI mandates higher cash margin / tangible collateral. |
| Performance Guarantee | Guarantees timely completion of a commercial contract, project construction, or service delivery | Relatively lower financial risk; bank must evaluate applicant's technical competence and past execution track record. |
| Deferred Payment Guarantee (DPG) | Guarantees payment of machinery/equipment purchases in installments over a medium-to-long-term horizon | Treated as a Term Loan equivalent; requires full financial appraisal and DSCR evaluation. |
| Bid Bond / Earnest Money Guarantee | Guarantees that bidder will not withdraw tender and will execute contract if awarded | Short-term validity (typically 3–6 months); converts to Performance Guarantee on award. |

## ⚖️ 2. Invocation of Bank Guarantee & Section 28 Limitation Period

A Bank Guarantee is an independent contract between the Bank and the Beneficiary. The bank must honor invocation on demand without inquiring into the underlying dispute between buyer and seller:

• **Rule of Absolute Independence:** The bank is not concerned with whether the principal debtor has committed breach or whether the claim is justified, provided invocation strictly complies with guarantee terms.
• **Court Injunction Exceptions:** Courts grant injunctions against BG invocation ONLY under 2 exceptional circumstances (established in *U.P. State Sugar Corp v. Sumac International Ltd*): (1) **Established Fraud of egregious nature** known to the bank, or (2) **Irretrievable Injustice / Irreparable Harm**.
• **Section 28 of Indian Contract Act (Amendment):** Guarantees can stipulate a specific claim period. If the guarantee provides for a claim period, it must allow a **minimum claim period of 1 year** beyond the guarantee expiry date for enforcing rights against the bank.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Bank Guarantees:
1. **Restraint on Invocation:** Banks **CANNOT refuse** payment on an unconditional bank guarantee merely because the customer (principal debtor) disputes the claim or requests stop-payment.
2. **Section 28 Claim Period Rule:** Under Section 28 of ICA, any clause curtailing the claim period to less than **1 year** from the expiry date is legally void.
3. **DPG Nature:** Deferred Payment Guarantee is evaluated on **Term Loan Appraisal principles** because installments mature over multiple years.

## 67. IIBF PPB Unit 12: Export Finance & Foreign Exchange Operations for Banks

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Export Credit Architecture in Indian Banking**
> Under RBI Master Directions on Export Credit, banks provide financial assistance to exporters at concessional interest rates across two distinct operational stages.

## 📊 1. Master Matrix: Pre-Shipment vs Post-Shipment Export Credit

| Parameter | Pre-Shipment Credit (Packing Credit / EPC) | Post-Shipment Export Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligibility Basis | Valid export order or confirmed irrevocable Letter of Credit (LC) from overseas buyer | Shipping documents (Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, Customs Shipping Bill, Commercial Invoice) |
| Permissible Tenor | Normally up to **180 days**; extendable by bank up to **270 days**; beyond 270 days on commercial rates | Up to **9 months (270 days)** from date of shipment for realization under FEMA regulations |
| Liquidation / Repayment | Liquidated out of export proceeds, negotiation of export bills, or conversion into Post-Shipment Credit | Liquidated strictly upon realization of export proceeds in foreign exchange via banking channels |
| Concessional Interest Cap | Eligible under Interest Equalization Scheme (IES) for MSME manufacturer exporters | Eligible for concessional subvention / equalization up to regulatory realization limits |

## 🛡️ 2. Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (ECGC) Covers

ECGC provides credit insurance covers to banks and direct exporters to mitigate risks associated with cross-border trade:

• **1. Packing Credit Guarantee (WTPCG):** Protects banks against losses due to insolvency or default of the exporter in repaying pre-shipment advances.
• **2. Post-Shipment Export Credit Guarantee (WTPSG):** Protects banks against non-realization of export bills discounted/purchased.
• **3. Standard Policy to Exporters:** Protects exporters against **Commercial Risks** (insolvency of buyer, protracted default, refusal to accept goods) and **Political Risks** (war, civil strife, import restrictions, exchange transfer blockage by foreign government).
• **Risks NOT Covered by ECGC:** Quality disputes, buyer's non-payment due to exporter's fault, foreign exchange fluctuations.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Export Finance:
1. **Liquidation of Packing Credit:** Packing credit **CANNOT be repaid with local cash/funds**. It MUST be liquidated from export proceeds or by converting into post-shipment bills.
2. **Export Proceeds Realization Deadline:** Under FEMA 1999 regulations, export proceeds must be realized within **9 months** from the date of shipment (15 months for warehouses established abroad).
3. **Gold Card Scheme:** Valid for **3 years** (renewable), issued to exporters with good track record of 3 years, offering 20% standby limits and faster processing within 25 days for fresh limits.

• • **General Category (Loans up to ₹1,000 Lakhs):** Up to **75%** guarantee cover.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on PSL & MSME:
1. **SFB and RRB PSL Targets:** SFBs and RRBs have a Total PSL Target of **75% of ANBC**, NOT 40%.
2. **Export Turnover Exclusion in MSME:** When calculating annual turnover for MSME classification, **export of goods and services is EXCLUDED** from the total turnover.
3. **Weaker Section SGB Sub-Target:** The weaker sections target for Domestic Banks is **12% of ANBC**, while for RRBs it is **15%**.

## 69. IIBF PPB Unit 14: NPA Management, SARFAESI Act & Recovery Mechanisms

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Prudential Norms on Income Recognition & Asset Classification (IRAC)**
> Under RBI IRAC norms, an asset becomes Non-Performing when it ceases to generate income for the bank.

## 📊 1. Master Matrix of Asset Classification & Mandatory Provisioning Norms

| Asset Category | Period / Criteria | Mandatory Provision on Secured Portion | Mandatory Provision on Unsecured Portion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard Asset | Performing normally without default | 0.40% (General) / 0.25% (Direct Agri & SME) / 1.00% (Commercial Real Estate) | 0.40% to 1.00% |
| Sub-Standard Asset | NPA for a period (le 12	ext{ months}) | **15%** | **25%** (20% for infrastructure loans) |
| Doubtful-1 (D1) | Remained in Sub-standard category for 12 months (NPA up to 1 yr) | **25%** | **100%** |
| Doubtful-2 (D2) | NPA for more than 1 year and up to 3 years | **40%** | **100%** |
| Doubtful-3 (D3) | NPA for more than 3 years | **100%** | **100%** |
| Loss Asset | Identified as uncollectible by bank, internal/external auditors, or RBI inspection | **100%** (Write-off or full provision) | **100%** |

## ⚖️ 2. SARFAESI Act, 2002 Enforcement Architecture

| Section in SARFAESI | Enforcement Stage | Statutory Timelines & Powers | Borrower / Guarantor Rights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Section 13(2) | Demand Notice to Borrower | Mandatory **60-day notice** issued to discharge liabilities in full after account is classified as NPA. | Borrower can submit representation/objection within 60 days. |
| Section 13(3A) | Disposal of Objections | Bank must reply to borrower's representation within **15 days** of receipt. | Rejection of objection gives no immediate right to appeal in DRT. |
| Section 13(4) | Enforcement / Possession Measures | Upon failure to pay after 60 days, bank takes symbolic/physical possession of secured asset, takes over management, or appoints manager. | Borrower can appeal to DRT within **45 days** under Section 17. |
| Section 14 | Magistrate Assistance | Chief Metropolitan Magistrate (CMM) or District Magistrate (DM) takes physical possession of asset and hands over to bank. | Administrative act; Magistrate cannot adjudicate dispute on merits. |
| Section 17 | Appeal to DRT (Securitisation Application) | Borrower files application against Sec 13(4) measures within 45 days. | Appeal to Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) under Sec 18 requires **50% pre-deposit** (can be reduced to 25%). |

## 🏛️ 3. Comparative Recovery Forums: Lok Adalats vs DRT vs IBC

| Recovery Forum | Governing Act | Monetary Claim Jurisdiction | Nature of Order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lok Adalat | Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 | Up to **₹20 Lakhs** (for bank compromise settlements) | Consent decree; No appeal lies against award |
| Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT) | RDB Act, 1993 (Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy) | **₹20 Lakhs and above** | Issues Recovery Certificate; Appeal to DRAT within 30 days |
| Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code (IBC) | Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (NCLT) | Default threshold of **₹1 Crore and above** | CIRP process: Resolution within 180 + 90 days (max 330 days) |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on NPA Management & SARFAESI:
1. **SARFAESI Agricultural Land Exemption:** Under Section 31(i), the SARFAESI Act does **NOT apply to agricultural land**.
2. **SARFAESI Debt Thresholds:** SARFAESI cannot be invoked if: (a) remaining debt is less than **₹1 Lakh**, or (b) remaining principal and interest is less than **20% of the original principal debt**.
3. **DRAT Pre-Deposit Requirement:** To appeal before the Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) under Section 18 of SARFAESI, the borrower must deposit **50% of the debt determined by DRT** (DRAT can reduce it to minimum 25%).

## 93. IIBF PPB Unit 20: Credit Appraisal Mathematics & Failed Electronic Transaction TAT Rules

> 🧠 **Key Concept — IIBF Core Foundation: Working Capital Assessment & TAT Redressal**
> Commercial credit appraisal determines working capital limits using standardized regulatory methods, while electronic banking enforces statutory compensation for failed payment turnaround times.

### 🏭 1. Working Capital Assessment Methodologies

#### A. Turnover Method (Nayak Committee Recommendations for MSMEs)
• *Applicability:* For working capital loan limits up to **₹5 Crore** for MSMEs and small businesses.
• **Total Working Capital Requirement:** Estimated at **25% of projected annual turnover**.
• **Promoter Margin (Borrower Contribution):** Minimum **5% of projected turnover**.
• **Maximum Bank Finance:** **20% of projected turnover**.

$$\text{Bank Working Capital Limit} = \text{Projected Annual Turnover} \times 20\%$$

#### B. Tandon Committee: Maximum Permissible Bank Finance (MPBF)
*Working Capital Gap (WCG) = Current Assets (CA) - Current Liabilities other than Bank Borrowings (CL)*

| Method | Bank Finance Share | Borrower Long-Term Margin Share | Minimum Current Ratio Benchmark |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Method 1** | $$\text{MPBF}_1 = 0.75 \times (\text{CA} - \text{CL})$$ | 25% of Working Capital Gap (WCG) | **1.17 : 1** |
| **Method 2** | $$\text{MPBF}_2 = (0.75 \times \text{CA}) - \text{CL}$$ | 25% of total Current Assets (CA) | **1.33 : 1** |

### 📈 2. Term Loan Viability: Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

$$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{Net Profit After Tax} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Annual Term Loan Interest}}{\text{Annual Principal Installment} + \text{Annual Term Loan Interest}}$$
• **Ideal Industry Benchmark:** Between **1.5 : 1 and 2.0 : 1**.
• **Minimum Acceptable Ratio:** **1.25 : 1**. A DSCR below 1.0 indicates that company cash flows are insufficient to service debt without external infusion.

### ⏱️ 3. RBI Harmonisation of Turn Around Time (TAT) & Penalty Compensation

*Under RBI's circular, if a customer's account is debited but the transaction fails, the bank must reverse the funds within the prescribed TAT or pay mandatory auto-compensation without requiring customer complaint:*

| Payment Channel | Authorized Resolution TAT | Mandatory Penalty for Delay |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ATM / Cash Recycler** (Cash not dispensed) | **T + 5 calendar days** | **₹100 per day** of delay |
| **Point of Sale (PoS) / E-Commerce** | **T + 5 calendar days** | **₹100 per day** of delay |
| **UPI Transactions** (Beneficiary not credited) | **T + 1 calendar day** | **₹100 per day** of delay |
| **IMPS (Instant Interbank Transfer)** | **T + 1 calendar day** | **₹100 per day** of delay |
| **NACH / Direct Debit Reversals** | **T + 1 calendar day** | **₹100 per day** of delay |
