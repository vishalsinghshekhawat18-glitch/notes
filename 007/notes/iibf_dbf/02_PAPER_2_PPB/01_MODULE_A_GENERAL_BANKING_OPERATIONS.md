## 1. 📌 Module 1: Deposit Operations, Customer Rights & Statutory Governance

Deposit operations and customer protection form the bedrock of statutory banking law in India. Recent legislative overhauls under the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2025, DICGC Act, and the Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS) 2026 have modernized customer rights, expedited distress payouts, and streamlined estate transmissions.

• Nomination under Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025 (Effective 01.11.2025): Depositors can nominate up to 4 persons per deposit account, safe custody article, or safe deposit locker under either Simultaneous Nomination (with explicit % shares summing to 100%) or Successive Nomination (Nominee 1 -> Nominee 2 -> Nominee 3 -> Nominee 4). For single locker hirers, successive nomination applies; for joint hirers, nomination delivers contents to survivors along with nominee.
• Legal Status of Nominee: A nominee acts as a trustee/custodian for legal heirs, not the absolute beneficial owner (Supreme Court ruling in Sarbati Devi vs Usha Devi). Bank obtains valid discharge under Section 45ZA, but legal heirs retain succession rights.
• RBI Revised Norms for Deposit Accounts of Minors: Minors of any age can open/operate savings & term deposits with natural/legal guardian, with mothers explicitly recognized on par with fathers. Literate minors aged 10+ can independently operate accounts with board-approved velocity limits. Section 11 of Indian Contract Act strictly prohibits overdrafts/credit to minors. Fresh KYC and signature verification mandatory upon turning 18.
• DICGC Deposit Insurance System (DICGC Act 1961): Insures up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank (principal + interest). Covers Commercial Banks, RRBs, Co-op Banks, SFBs, and PBs. Excludes foreign/state/central govt and inter-bank deposits. Account coverage ratio: 97.6%; Value coverage ratio: 41.5%.
• DICGC Section 18A 90-Day Mandatory Interim Payout: When a bank is placed under All-Inclusive Directions (AID) / moratorium by RBI, the bank has 45 days to submit claims, and DICGC has 45 days to verify and disburse up to ₹5 Lakh directly into depositors alternate bank accounts.
• DICGC Risk-Based Premium (RBP) Transition (FY27): Transitioning from flat 12p per ₹100 p.a. to CAMELS-linked risk rating (Tier 1/Tier 2 models offering up to 33.33% risk discount and 25% vintage discount).
• SEBI Revised Nomination Norms for Demat/MFs: Incapacitated single investors can authorize 1 nominee to operate folios. AMC/DP official must conduct physical verification. Strict zero-alteration rule on mobile/email/bank details; redemptions flow exclusively to linked investor bank account.
• Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS) 2026 (Effective 01.07.2026): One Nation One Ombudsman architecture covering SCBs, RRBs, SFBs, PBs, NBFCs (>=₹100 Cr assets), and UCBs (>=₹50 Cr deposits). Consequential financial loss compensation increased from ₹20 Lakh to ₹30 Lakh, plus up to ₹1 Lakh for mental harassment/loss of time. Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC) at RBI Chandigarh.

## 56. IIBF PPB Unit 1: Banker-Customer Relationship, Rights & Duties

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Multi-Faceted Legal Relationships in Banking**
> The relationship between a banker and a customer is fundamentally contractual and depends upon the exact nature of the transaction being executed.

## 📊 1. Master Matrix of Banker-Customer Legal Relationships

| Transaction / Activity | Bank's Legal Status | Customer's Legal Status | Governing Statute / Principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deposit of Money in Savings / Current / FD | Debtor (Borrower) | Creditor (Lender) | Common Law of Banking |
| Borrowing via Loan / Cash Credit / Overdraft | Creditor (Lender) | Debtor (Borrower) | Indian Contract Act, 1872 |
| Articles / Valuables in Safe Custody | Bailee | Bailor | Section 148, Indian Contract Act 1872 |
| Hiring Safe Deposit Locker | Lessor (Licensor) | Lessee (Licensee) | Section 45ZE, BR Act / Transfer of Property Act |
| Collection of Cheques, Drafts & Bills | Agent | Principal | Section 182, Indian Contract Act 1872 |
| Pledge of Goods / Securities for a Loan | Pawnee (Pledgee) | Pawnor (Pledgor) | Section 172, Indian Contract Act 1872 |
| Hypothecation of Movable Assets | Hypothecatee | Hypothecator | SARFAESI Act 2002 / Contract Act |
| Mortgage of Immovable Property | Mortgagee | Mortgagor | Section 58, Transfer of Property Act 1882 |
| Issuance of Demand Draft / Pay Order | Debtor | Creditor (Purchaser / Payee) | Negotiable Instruments Act 1881 |
| Money Left for Specific Unexecuted Purpose | Trustee | Beneficiary | Indian Trusts Act 1882 |

## ⚖️ 2. Core Rights of a Banker

Under Indian law and banking conventions, bankers enjoy specific statutory rights to protect their advances and manage operational risks:

• **1. Right of General Lien (Section 171, Indian Contract Act 1872):** A general lien allows a banker to retain any goods, securities, or commercial paper delivered in the ordinary course of business as security for a general balance of account. Unlike a particular lien (which attaches only to the specific goods repaired/handled), a general lien applies across all outstanding obligations unless there is an express contract to the contrary.
• **Exclusions from General Lien:** Safe custody articles, securities left by mistake, money deposited for a specific unfulfilled purpose, and shares deposited for sale through broker.
• **2. Right of Set-Off:** The legal right to merge/combine two accounts of the same customer (one in debit and one in credit) to determine the net liability. Conditions: Debts must be mutual, due and payable immediately, ascertained in sum, and in the *same right and same capacity*.
• **3. Right of Appropriation (Clayton's Rule):** Governed by Sections 59, 60, and 61 of the Indian Contract Act. Under Clayton's Case (1816), in running cash credit/overdraft accounts with a deceased or retired partner/guarantor, payments into the account extinguish the earliest debits first. To preserve guarantor liability, the bank must immediately break the old account and open a new separate account.
• **4. Right to Charge Interest & Incidental Charges:** Permitted under express/implied contract and RBI Prudential Norms.

## 🔒 3. Banker's Duty of Secrecy & Statutory Exceptions

Section 13 of the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1970/1980 mandates strict confidentiality of customer account affairs. Established under Tournier v. National Provincial and Union Bank of England (1924), exceptions exist only under 4 specific heads:

• **1. Disclosure under Compulsion of Law:** Orders under Section 226(3) Income Tax Act, Section 91 CrPC, Section 43C PMLA, Banker's Books Evidence Act 1891, and Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA).
• **2. Disclosure in Public Interest:** Prevention of national security threats, terror financing, or major public frauds.
• **3. Disclosure in the Bank's Own Interest:** Filing a legal recovery suit against a defaulting borrower, enforcing SARFAESI, or claiming insurance.
• **4. Disclosure with Customer's Express or Implied Consent:** Providing trade opinions upon written authorization, credit bureau sharing (CIBIL/Experian) under Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act 2005.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Banker-Customer Relationship:
1. **Locker Relationship vs Safe Custody:** Hiring a locker creates a **Lessor-Lessee** relationship, NOT Bailor-Bailee. The bank does not take possession or knowledge of the locker contents. Safe custody, conversely, is **Bailee-Bailor**.
2. **Right of Set-off Capacity Trap:** A bank CANNOT set off a customer's individual credit balance against a partnership debit balance without specific authorization, nor set off trust accounts against personal liabilities.
3. **Clayton's Rule Operation:** If a partner in a firm dies and the bank continues the running overdraft account without breaking it, subsequent deposits by surviving partners automatically reduce the deceased partner's liability, releasing his estate.

## 57. IIBF PPB Unit 2: AML / KYC Norms & Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: 4 Structural Pillars of KYC Policy**
> Under RBI Master Direction on KYC (updated under PMLA 2002 and PML Rules 2005), every regulated entity must establish a Board-approved KYC framework built upon 4 mandatory pillars.

## 📊 1. Master KYC Risk Categorization & Periodic Updation Schedule

| Risk Category | Customer Profile Examples | Mandatory Periodic Re-KYC Cycle | Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low Risk | Salaried individuals with clear source of income, Govt departments, PSUs, Regulated financial institutions | Once every 10 Years | Standard CDD; self-declaration allowed if no change in address/identity |
| Medium Risk | Blind persons, Stock brokers, Non-resident customers (NRIs), High net-worth individuals without PEP links | Once every 8 Years | Periodic review of transaction volumes vs declared income |
| High Risk | Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs), Non-Face-to-Face accounts, Trusts/Charities, Bullion dealers, Cross-border correspondents | Once every 2 Years | Mandatory Senior Management approval for account opening and source of funds verification |

## 📑 2. Officially Valid Documents (OVD) & Central KYC (CKYCR)

Under PML Rule 2(1)(d), only the following **6 documents** qualify as proof of identity and address (OVD):

• **1. Passport**.
• **2. Driving Licence (DL)**.
• **3. Proof of possession of Aadhaar Number** (with Aadhaar number masked/redacted unless voluntarily provided for DBT).
• **4. Voter's Identity Card** issued by the Election Commission of India.
• **5. Job Card issued by NREGA** signed by an officer of the State Government.
• **6. Letter issued by the National Population Register (NPR)** containing details of name and address.
• **Central KYC Registry (CKYCR):** Managed by CERSAI under Section 12 of PMLA. Regulated entities must upload KYC data of individual customers within 10 days of account opening to generate a unique 14-digit **KYC Identifier (KIN)**.

## 🚨 3. Regulatory Reporting Thresholds to FIU-IND

| Report Type | Statutory Trigger Threshold | Submission Frequency / Deadline | Special Rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash Transaction Report (CTR) | All individual or integrally connected cash transactions > ₹10 Lakhs in a calendar month | By 15th of the succeeding month | Both deposits and withdrawals in cash are aggregated |
| Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) | Any transaction irrespective of amount giving rise to reasonable suspicion of illicit proceeds/terror financing | Within 7 working days of arriving at conclusion of suspicion | Strict 'Tipping-off' prohibition under Section 12 PMLA (Bank cannot inform customer) |
| Cross Border Wire Transfer Report (CWTR) | All cross-border wire transfers > ₹5 Lakhs or foreign currency equivalent | By 15th of the succeeding month | Both inward and outward wire transfers monitored |
| Non-Profit Organization Transaction Report (NTR) | All receipts by Non-Profit Organizations (NPOs) > ₹10 Lakhs | By 15th of the succeeding month | Applies to registered NPOs/NGOs |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on AML / KYC:
1. **PAN is NOT an OVD for Address:** PAN card serves only as proof of identity (and financial transaction verification), NOT as proof of address since it does not print address details.
2. **STR Threshold Trap:** STR has **NO monetary threshold**. A transaction of even ₹5,000 can trigger an STR if it has no economic rationale or fits money laundering typologies.
3. **Re-KYC Frequency Trap:** High Risk = **2 Years**, Medium Risk = **8 Years**, Low Risk = **10 Years**. Low risk accounts do not require physical branch visits if self-declaration confirms unchanged details.

## 58. IIBF PPB Unit 3: Operations in Accounts of Special Customers

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Legal Capacity to Contract (Section 11, Indian Contract Act)**
> Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject (18 years, or 21 years if court guardian appointed), of sound mind, and not disqualified by law.

## 📊 1. Master Rules for Special Categories of Individual Customers

| Customer Type | Legal Framework / Capacity | Cheque Book / Overdraft Rules | Operational Guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minor (Under 10 Years) | Account opened and operated by natural guardian (Father/Mother) or Court Guardian | No overdraft; No personal liability on minor | Guardian signs on behalf of minor. Balance belongs to minor. |
| Minor (10+ Years, Literate) | Permitted by RBI to open and operate self-managed savings/FD account | Cheque book permitted; Overdraft strictly prohibited | Cannot be declared insolvent; Cannot ratify debts upon attaining majority. |
| Illiterate Person | Competent to contract; signs via Left Thumb Impression (LTI) for male / Right Thumb Impression (RTI) for female | Cheque book generally not issued unless with crossed order; cash withdrawal in person only | Thumb impression must be attested by independent witness known to bank. |
| Blind Person | Full legal capacity; equal rights to banking facilities under Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 | Cheque book, ATM/Debit Card, NetBanking fully permissible with talking ATM aids | Witness required during account opening; no discrimination permitted. |
| Purdahnashin Woman | Strict seclusion; transaction treated with heightened caution by courts | Cheque book permitted; withdrawals via authorized agent or witnessed mandate | Bank must ensure independent legal/financial understanding without undue influence. |
| Insolvent Person | Disqualified from contracting; property vests in Official Assignee / Receiver | Account must be immediately frozen upon receipt of Adjudication Order | Any credit balance must be handed over to the Official Receiver. |

## 👥 2. Joint Account Survivorship Mandates

• **1. Either or Survivor (E or S):** Either party can operate during joint lifetime. Upon death of one, the survivor operates the account without requiring legal representation from deceased's heirs.
• **2. Former or Survivor (F or S):** Only the *Former* (first account holder) can operate during lifetime. The *Survivor* can operate only after the death of the Former upon producing death certificate.
• **3. Anyone or Survivors (A or S):** Used for 3 or more holders. Any single holder can operate. On death of one, remaining survivors continue operations.
• **4. Jointly Operated Accounts:** All holders must sign every withdrawal/cheque. On death of one holder, the balance is payable to the survivor(s) **AND** the legal heirs of the deceased joint holder jointly.

## 🏢 3. Non-Corporate Entities: Partnership & HUF

| Parameter | Partnership Firm (Partnership Act 1932) | Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governing Law | Indian Partnership Act, 1932 | Hindu Law (Mitakshara / Dayabhaga) & Hindu Succession Act |
| Creation / Membership | Created by Agreement / Contract between partners | Created by status of birth in the Hindu coparcenary |
| Management Head | All partners have implied authority to bind the firm | Exclusively managed by the **Karta** (eldest coparcener) |
| Liability | Unlimited joint and several liability across all partners | Karta has unlimited liability; Coparceners liable only to extent of their share in joint family property |
| Female Membership | Women can be full partners | Daughters are coparceners by birth post 2005 Amendment |
| Dissolution on Death | Death of partner dissolves firm unless partnership deed specifies otherwise | Death of Karta does not dissolve HUF; next senior coparcener becomes Karta |

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Special Customers:
1. **Stopping Payment in Joint Accounts:** In an 'Either or Survivor' account, **ANY** of the joint holders can instruct the bank to stop payment of a cheque drawn by the other holder. But revocation of stop-payment requires consent of **ALL** holders.
2. **Minor as Partner Trap:** Under Section 30 of Indian Partnership Act, a minor CANNOT be a full partner, but can be admitted only to the **benefits of an existing partnership** with consent of all partners. Minor's personal property is not liable.
3. **Delegation by Karta:** A Karta cannot appoint an outsider as agent through Power of Attorney to manage HUF business unless strictly authorized by all adult coparceners.

## 59. IIBF PPB Unit 4: Operations in Accounts of Companies, Trusts, Clubs & Societies

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Corporate Legal Personality & Constitution**
> A company registered under the Companies Act 2013 is an artificial legal person with perpetual succession and common seal (optional). Its operational perimeter is strictly governed by its charter documents.

## 📊 1. Master Matrix: Companies vs Trusts vs Societies

| Entity Type | Governing Act | Constitutive Document | Mandatory Account Opening Requirements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Private Limited Company | Companies Act, 2013 | MoA & AoA + Certificate of Incorporation | Min 2, Max 200 members. Board resolution, KYC of all directors & authorized signatories, CIN, PAN. |
| Public Limited Company | Companies Act, 2013 | MoA & AoA + Certificate of Incorporation | Min 7 members, no upper limit. Board resolution, Certificate of Commencement of Business, PAN, CIN. |
| One Person Company (OPC) | Companies Act, 2013 | MoA & AoA with nominee details | Single member/director who must be an Indian citizen and resident. |
| Section 8 Company | Companies Act, 2013 (Non-profit) | License from Central Govt + MoA/AoA | Formed for charitable/scientific/educational purposes; dividend distribution strictly prohibited. |
| Public / Private Trust | Indian Trusts Act, 1882 (Private) / State Public Trust Acts | Registered Trust Deed | Trust Deed specifying powers of trustees. Trustees must act jointly unless deed explicitly allows single operation. |
| Societies & Clubs | Societies Registration Act, 1860 | Memorandum of Association & Rules/Bylaws | Min 7 members. Registration certificate, list of managing committee members, governing body resolution. |

## ⚖️ 2. Company Borrowing Powers & Registration of Charges (CERSAI & RoC)

When lending to companies, banks must ensure borrowing is within the limits authorized under Section 180(1)(c) of the Companies Act 2013 and register charges timely:

• **Section 180(1)(c) Borrowing Limits:** Board of Directors cannot borrow money exceeding the aggregate of paid-up share capital, free reserves, and securities premium without prior approval of shareholders by special resolution.
• **Registration of Charges with RoC (Section 77, Companies Act 2013):** Every charge created by a company on its assets (mortgage, hypothecation, pledge) must be registered with the Registrar of Companies (RoC) within **30 days** of creation (extendable up to 60 days on additional fees). Unregistered charges are **VOID** against the liquidator and creditors.
• **CERSAI Registration:** All equitable mortgages and security interests must also be registered on the CERSAI portal within 30 days under Chapter IV-A of the SARFAESI Act 2002.

## 📜 3. Legal Doctrines Governing Corporate Banking

• **Doctrine of Ultra Vires:** Any contract entered into by a company beyond the powers given in its MoA is Ultra Vires (beyond powers) and void ab initio. It cannot be ratified even by 100% shareholder consent.
• **Doctrine of Constructive Notice:** Third parties dealing with a company are presumed to have read and understood its public documents (MoA and AoA registered with RoC).
• **Doctrine of Indoor Management (Turquand's Rule):** Outsiders dealing with a company are entitled to assume that internal procedures and approvals required by AoA have been properly complied with.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Companies and Trusts:
1. **Trustee Delegation Trap:** A trustee **CANNOT delegate** duties or discretionary powers to co-trustees or outsiders unless the Trust Deed expressly permits it (*Delegatus non potest delegare*).
2. **Registration of Charges Deadline:** Standard registration period with RoC is **30 days**. An unregistered charge makes the bank an **unsecured creditor** in the event of company liquidation.
3. **Director's Personal Cheque into Company Account:** A bank should never credit a cheque drawn in favor of a company to the personal account of a director without verified authority; doing so forfeits statutory protection under Section 131 NI Act.

## 60. IIBF PPB Unit 5: Mandate, Power of Attorney & Legal Orders (Garnishee vs Attachment)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Third-Party Operational Authorities & Court Orders**
> Banks frequently receive third-party operational authorizations or statutory attachment orders that freeze or direct the payment of customer funds.

## 📊 1. Deep Comparison: Garnishee Order vs Attachment Order

| Parameter | Garnishee Order (Order 21 Rule 46 CPC) | Income Tax Attachment Order (Sec 226(3) IT Act) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issuing Authority | Civil Court in execution of decree | Income Tax Officer / Tax Recovery Officer (TRO) |
| Two-Stage Process | Yes: (1) **Order Nisi** (Freeze/Show-cause) followed by (2) **Order Absolute** (Payment direction) | No: Single direct order directing immediate remittance to Govt treasury |
| Application to Future Credits | ❌ **No.** Attaches only funds existing *at the exact moment and date of service* of the order. | ✅ **Yes.** Attaches current credit balance **AND all subsequent future credits** until the full tax demand is cleared. |
| Joint Accounts Rule | Cannot attach joint account (A & B) for individual debt of A unless specified in order | Can attach proportionate share of joint holder (50% presumed unless specified) |
| Priority of Orders | Subordinate to Income Tax Attachment Orders | Enjoys **Statutory Priority** (Crown Debt) over Civil Court Garnishee Orders |
| Effect on Un-cleared Cheques | Does not attach cheques deposited but not yet cleared/credited | Attaches proceeds as soon as they clear into the account |
| Right of Set-off by Bank | Bank can exercise prior right of set-off before calculating attachable balance | Bank can exercise set-off for dues already due and payable before order date |

## ⚖️ 2. Deceased Account Claim Settlement Framework

Under RBI Master Directions on Customer Service, banks follow structured protocols to settle claims of deceased depositors without causing undue hardship to survivors:

• **1. Accounts with Valid Nomination / Survivorship Mandate:** Claim settled in favor of the nominee or surviving joint holder within **15 days** of receipt of death certificate and KYC proof, without requiring legal representation (Probate, Letters of Administration, or Succession Certificate).
• **2. Accounts without Nomination / Survivorship (Below Threshold Limit):** Banks settle claims based on simple Indemnity Bond, Letter of Disclaimer from other legal heirs, and KYC proof up to board-approved threshold limits (typically ₹1 Lakh to ₹5 Lakhs).
• **3. Accounts with Substantial Balances & Disputes:** Legal representation (Succession Certificate under Indian Succession Act 1925) is mandatory.
• **4. Safe Deposit Lockers of Deceased:** Nominee is allowed to open the locker and inventory the contents in the presence of 2 independent witnesses; nominee receives contents as custodian for legal heirs.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Legal Orders:
1. **Future Deposits in Garnishee Order:** A deposit made in the afternoon *after* a Garnishee Order Nisi was served in the morning is **NOT** attached. The bank must open a separate new account for subsequent transactions.
2. **IT Order Priority:** If both a Garnishee Order and an IT Attachment Order arrive, the **Income Tax Attachment Order takes absolute precedence**.
3. **Deceased Account with Mandate / PoA:** A Mandate or Power of Attorney terminates **immediately** on the death, insanity, or insolvency of the principal. Any cheque signed by a PoA holder presented after the principal's death must be returned with remark 'Drawer Deceased'.

## 61. IIBF PPB Unit 6: Safe Deposit Lockers, Safe Custody & Nomination Facility

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Safe Custody vs Safe Deposit Lockers**
> While both services provide physical security for valuable customer assets, their legal contracts, liability thresholds, and operational rules are fundamentally distinct.

## 📊 1. Revised RBI Master Directions on Safe Deposit Lockers (2023 Norms)

| Operational Parameter | RBI Statutory Rule / Standard | Legal Basis / Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank's Liability for Locker Incidents | Capped at **100 times the annual locker rent** in case of fire, theft, burglary, dacoity, building collapse, or staff fraud | RBI Circular on Safe Deposit Lockers (2023) |
| Exemption from Liability | Bank not liable for loss arising from natural calamities / Act of God (earthquake, flood) or customer negligence | Common Law of Torts / Contractual terms |
| Term Deposit as Security for Locker | Bank can obtain Term Deposit covering **3 years' rent plus break-open charges** from new hirers | RBI Locker Master Directions |
| Model Locker Agreement | Mandatory revised stamped agreement based on IBA model executed with all hirers | Indian Stamp Act / Contract Act |
| Locker Operation Inactivity Alert | If a locker remains unoperated for **7 years** (even if rent is paid), bank can break open after due notice | RBI Unclaimed Accounts & Lockers Norms |
| Break-Open Protocol | Notice via registered post/email; newspaper publication in 2 dailies; executed in presence of 2 independent witnesses and Videography | SOP on Locker Break-Open |

## 📑 2. Nomination Rules under Banking Regulation Act (Section 45ZA to 45ZF)

Governed by the Banking Companies (Nomination) Rules 1985, nomination provides smooth succession and eliminates probate requirements:

| Form Number | Statutory Purpose | Eligible Facility | Key Legal Provision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Form DA-1 | Registration of Fresh Nomination | Deposit Accounts, Safe Custody, Safe Deposit Lockers | Nomination can be in favor of only ONE individual (except Lockers hired jointly with specific survivorship). |
| Form DA-2 | Cancellation of Existing Nomination | Deposit Accounts, Safe Custody, Lockers | Can be cancelled at any time during depositor's lifetime in writing. |
| Form DA-3 | Variation / Modification of Nomination | Deposit Accounts, Safe Custody, Lockers | Changes nominee name/details without cancelling the facility. |

• **Nominee is a Trustee:** The Supreme Court (Sarojini Ramaswami v. Union of India / Ram Chander Talwar v. Devinder Kumar Talwar) established that the nominee is merely an authorized receiver/trustee for the legal heirs. Nomination does not override the Indian Succession Act.
• **Minor as Nominee:** A minor can be appointed as nominee provided the depositor appoints a major guardian in Form DA-1 to receive funds on the minor's behalf in the event of the depositor's death during minor's minority.
• **Nomination in Partnership / Company Accounts:** Strictly **PROHIBITED**. Nomination is available ONLY to individual depositors (sole or joint).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Lockers and Nomination:
1. **Locker Compensation Cap:** The maximum compensation a bank is legally liable to pay for locker theft/building collapse is **100 times the annual locker rent** (not the claimed value of gold/jewelry inside).
2. **Nomination for Non-Individuals:** Nomination facility is **NOT available** for Partnership firms, Companies, Trusts, HUFs, or Societies. It applies solely to natural persons.
3. **Multiple Nominees in Deposit Accounts:** Under Section 45ZA, a deposit account can have **ONLY ONE nominee**. Having two nominees (e.g. 50% to son, 50% to daughter) in a single deposit account is legally invalid.

## 62. IIBF PPB Unit 7: Cash Operations, Clean Note Policy & Counterfeit Currency

> 🧠 **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Clean Note Policy & Currency Integrity**
> Under Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act 1949 and RBI Act 1934, the Reserve Bank implements policies to maintain the quality, authenticity, and hygiene of circulating Indian currency.

## 🚨 1. Detection and Impounding of Counterfeit / Forged Banknotes

Under RBI Master Directions on Counterfeit Currency, failure by a bank teller to impound a forged note is treated as a severe compliance failure:

| Parameter | Single Transaction: Up to 4 Forged Notes | Single Transaction: 5 or More Forged Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impounding Action | Teller stamps 'COUNTERFEIT BANKNOTE IMPOUNDED' and issues signed receipt to presenter | Teller stamps 'COUNTERFEIT BANKNOTE IMPOUNDED' and issues signed receipt to presenter |
| Return of Note to Customer | ❌ **Strictly Prohibited.** No credit/refund given under any circumstances | ❌ **Strictly Prohibited.** No credit/refund given under any circumstances |
| Police Reporting Protocol | Send consolidated report with impounded notes to Nodal Police Station at the **end of the month** | File an **immediate First Information Report (FIR)** with local police station for criminal investigation |
| Preservation of Forged Notes | Maintained in safe custody under joint custodianship until handed over to police | Handed over to Police Investigation Officer against official acknowledgement |

## 💵 2. RBI (Note Refund) Rules, 2009: Soiled vs Mutilated Notes

| Category | Definition / Characteristics | Value Payable (Adjudication Norms) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soiled Note | A banknote which has become dirty due to normal usage, or a two-piece note pasted together where both pieces belong to the same note and form an entire note without any essential feature missing | Full Value payable across all bank branches free of charge |
| Mutilated Note | A banknote of which a portion is missing or which is composed of more than two pieces pasted together | Adjudicated based on the **surface area of the single largest undivided piece** according to RBI Note Refund Rules |
| Extremely Brittle / Charred / Burnt Notes | Notes severely burnt, charred, or stuck together that cannot be handled without crumbling | Cannot be accepted across bank counters; sent directly to RBI Issue Department for special adjudication |
| Deliberately Cut / Slogans Inscribed | Notes bearing political/religious slogans or intentionally cut/defaced | ❌ **Zero Value.** Rejected and impounded under Note Refund Rules |

## 📐 3. Area Benchmarks for Mutilated Note Refund (Mahatma Gandhi New Series)

• **For Denominations of ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500:**.
• • **Full Value:** Single largest undivided piece has an area (ge 80%) of the original note dimensions.
• • **Half Value:** Single largest undivided piece has an area (ge 40%) and (< 80%) of the original note dimensions.
• • **Zero Value:** Single largest undivided piece has an area (< 40%).
• **For Lower Denominations (₹5, ₹10, ₹20):**.
• • **Full Value:** Single largest undivided piece has an area (ge 50%). (No half-value refund for denominations below ₹50).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Top Exam Traps on Cash Operations:
1. **Police Reporting Slabs for Fake Notes:** Up to 4 notes = **Monthly consolidated report**. 5 or more notes in a single transaction = **Immediate FIR**.
2. **Returning Fake Notes:** A bank employee who returns a counterfeit note to the customer without impounding can be prosecuted under Section 39 of the CrPC for concealing a cognizable offence.
3. **Half-Value Refund Rule:** Half-value refund is available ONLY for denominations of **₹50 and above**. For ₹5, ₹10, and ₹20 notes, there is NO half value (it is either full value if (ge 50%) area or zero value if (< 50%)).

## 92. IIBF PPB Unit 19: Negotiable Instruments Act 1881 — Cheque Protections, Forgery & Sec 138 Dishonour

> 🧠 **Key Concept — IIBF Core Foundation: Cheque Operations & Legal Protections**
> The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 balances the operational speed of the banking clearing system with statutory protections for paying and collecting bankers against forged instruments.

### 🛡️ 1. Statutory Protections to Paying Banker

• **Section 85(1) (Order Cheque):** Where an order cheque purports to be endorsed by or on behalf of the payee, the paying banker is discharged by **Payment in Due Course**, even if an endorsement turns out to be forged.
• **Section 85(2) (Bearer Cheque):** A cheque originally drawn as a bearer cheque remains payable to bearer regardless of any subsequent endorsements (*"Once a bearer, always a bearer"*). Paying banker is discharged if paid to the bearer in due course.
• **Section 85A (Bank Drafts):** Draft drawn by one branch of a bank upon another branch; paying branch is fully protected if paid in due course despite forged endorsements.
• **Section 128 (Crossed Cheques):** Paying banker is discharged if payment of a crossed cheque is made in good faith and without negligence to a banker (in general crossing) or to the specified banker (in special crossing).

### ✍️ 2. The Absolute Rule on Forged Drawer Signature

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 FORGED ENDORSEMENT vs. FORGED DRAWER SIGNATURE              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. FORGED ENDORSEMENT (Sec 85) : Bank IS PROTECTED if paid in due course.   │
│ 2. FORGED DRAWER SIGNATURE    : Bank IS NOT PROTECTED. Payment is a NULLITY.│
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

• *Legal Reality:* A forged signature of the drawer is completely void (*nullity*). There is no mandate from the customer. The paying banker cannot debit the customer's account, even if the forgery was executed with extreme skill (*Bihta Cooperative Bank vs. Bank of Bihar*).

### 📥 3. Statutory Protection to Collecting Banker (Section 131)

A collecting banker who receives payment of a crossed cheque for a customer incurs no liability to the true owner in case of title defect, provided:
1. The cheque was **crossed before it came into the bank's hands**.
2. The bank collected the cheque **in good faith and without negligence** (proper KYC, verification of apparent alterations).
3. The bank acted purely as an **agent for collection on behalf of a customer** (not as a holder for value).

### ⚖️ 4. Criminal Dishonour of Cheques: Section 138 of NI Act

*Dishonour of a cheque due to insufficiency of funds is a criminal offense punishable with imprisonment up to 2 years, fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both:*

```
  Cheque Presented (Within 3 Months) ──► Returned Dishonoured (Memo Received)
                                                      │
         ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┘
         ▼ (Within 30 Days)
  Demand Notice Issued to Drawer in Writing
         │
         ▼ (Wait 15 Days for Drawer to Pay)
  Drawer Fails to Clear Dues within 15 Days
         │
         ▼ (Within 1 Month)
  Criminal Complaint Filed before Judicial / Metropolitan Magistrate
```

> 🎯 **Top IIBF Traps for Unit 19:**
> 1. **Protection Condition for Collecting Banker:** Protection under Section 131 is available **ONLY if the cheque was crossed prior to deposit**. If an uncrossed cheque is crossed by the bank, Section 131 protection is forfeited.
> 2. **Section 138 Timelines:** Demand notice must be issued within **30 days** of dishonour; drawer is granted **15 days** to pay; complaint must be filed within **1 month** thereafter.
