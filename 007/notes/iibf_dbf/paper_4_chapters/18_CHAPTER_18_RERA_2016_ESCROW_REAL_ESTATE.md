> **Paper:** 4 (Retail Banking and Wealth Management)

Real estate represents the physical collateral anchor of retail housing finance. Historically, the absence of standardized building definitions, diversion of buyer funds by developers, and delayed construction severely impacted home loan borrowers and commercial lenders. The enactment of the **Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA 2016)** established structural discipline through mandatory project registration, dedicated bank escrow accounts, standardized carpet area pricing, and defect liability. Parallel to RERA, banks employ rigorous **Real Property Valuation Methodologies** and statutory tax frameworks (Section 24b, 80C, 80EEA, 54/54EC) to appraise mortgage security.

---

## 1. RERA 2016: Core Statutory Architecture & Mandatory Provisions

Enacted by Parliament to protect home-buyers and establish transparency in real estate transactions, RERA enforces five structural mandates:

### 1. Mandatory Project Registration Thresholds *(Statutory / Regulatory Requirement)*
- Every residential and commercial real estate project where:
  - The total land area to be developed exceeds **500 square meters**, OR
  - The number of apartments proposed to be developed exceeds **8 apartments** (inclusive of all phases),
  must be registered with the State Real Estate Regulatory Authority **prior to advertising, marketing, booking, or selling**.

### 2. 70% Dedicated Bank Escrow Account Architecture *(Statutory / Regulatory Requirement)*
To eliminate fund siphoning into other projects, Section 4(2)(l)(D) mandates:
- The promoter must deposit **70% of all amounts realized from allottees** into a **dedicated escrow account** maintained with a scheduled commercial bank.
- Funds in this escrow account can be withdrawn **strictly to cover the cost of construction and land cost**.
- **Three-Tier Withdrawal Certification:** Every withdrawal from the escrow account must be certified by:
  1. An **Engineer** certifying construction progress.
  2. An **Architect** certifying physical percentage completion.
  3. A practicing **Chartered Accountant (CA)** certifying that withdrawal is proportional to project completion.

### 3. Standardized Carpet Area Mandate *(Statutory / Regulatory Requirement)*
Promoters are legally prohibited from selling properties based on ambiguous "Super Built-Up Area" or "Built-Up Area". Pricing must be based strictly on **Carpet Area**:
- **Statutory Definition:** The net usable floor area of an apartment, **excluding** the area covered by external walls, areas under service shafts, exclusive balcony/verandah area, and exclusive open terrace area, but **including** the area covered by the internal partition walls of the apartment.

### 4. 5-Year Structural Defect Liability *(Statutory / Regulatory Requirement)*
- If any structural defect or defect in workmanship, quality, or provision of services is brought to the promoter's notice within **5 years from the date of handing over possession**, the promoter is legally bound to rectify the defect **without further charge within 30 days**.
- If the promoter fails to rectify within 30 days, aggrieved allottees are entitled to receive full statutory compensation.

### 5. 10% Advance Booking Cap & Dispute Redressal *(Statutory / Regulatory Requirement)*
- **10% Cap:** Promoters cannot accept a sum exceeding **10% of the cost of the apartment** as an advance payment or application fee without first executing and registering a written **Agreement for Sale**.
- **Appellate Tribunal Pre-Deposit:** Under Section 43(5), if a promoter appeals against an order or penalty imposed by the Regulatory Authority or Adjudicating Officer, the appeal cannot be entertained unless the promoter deposits **at least 30% of the penalty** or **100% of the total amount directed to be paid to the allottee** (including interest and compensation).

## 2. Real Property Valuation Methodologies in Bank Appraisal *(Valuation / Appraisal Practice)*

Commercial banks rely on certified property valuations to determine the Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio and distress liquidation value:

### 1. Land and Building Method (Cost Approach)
$$\text{Total Property Value} = \text{Market Value of Land} + \text{Depreciated Replacement Cost of Building}$$
- **Land Valuation:** Assessed using the comparative sales method (recent registered transactions in the vicinity) or circle/guideline rates.
- **Building Replacement Cost:** Assessed based on prevailing construction rates (Plinth Area Rate) minus accumulated physical depreciation.

### 2. Rent Capitalization Method (Income Capitalization Approach)
Used for commercial properties or residential flats generating stable rental cash flows:
$$\text{Capitalized Property Value} = \text{Net Annual Rental Income} \times \text{Year's Purchase (YP)}$$
$$\text{Year's Purchase (YP)} = \frac{1}{\text{Capitalization Rate } r}$$
Where Net Annual Rent equals Gross Rent minus Outgoings (property taxes, repair allowance of 10%–15%, collection charges, insurance).

### 3. Belting Method for Plot Valuation
Applied when valuing deep plots of land where highway/road frontage commands premium value:
- **Belt 1 (Front Belt):** Up to a depth of typically 50–100 feet; valued at **100%** of prime road rate.
- **Belt 2 (Middle Belt):** Land behind Belt 1; valued at **66.6% to 75%** of the Belt 1 rate.
- **Belt 3 (Rear Belt):** Deepest portion of plot; valued at **50%** of the Belt 1 rate.

### 4. Economic Life of Structures & Sinking Fund Formula
- **Estimated Structural Life:**
  - RCC Framed Buildings: **75 to 100 Years**.
  - Load-Bearing Brick Masonry: **50 to 60 Years**.
- **Sinking Fund for Replacement:** An annual sum set aside at compound interest to accumulate the replacement cost of the building at the end of its economic life:
  $$I = S \times \frac{i}{(1 + i)^n - 1}$$
  Where $I$ is the annual sinking fund installment, $S$ is the total replacement sum required, $i$ is the annual interest rate, and $n$ is structural life in years.

## 3. Comprehensive Worked Numerical: Real Property Valuation & Sinking Fund

**Problem Statement:**  
A commercial property produces a gross annual rental income of ₹12,00,000.
- Outgoings (municipal taxes, repairs, insurance, management expenses): $20\%$ of gross rent.
- Prevailing market yield (capitalization rate) for similar real estate: $8.00\%$ per annum.
- At the end of its 30-year remaining useful life, the building will require a replacement fund of ₹50,00,000.
- The annual interest rate yielded by safe sinking fund investments is $6.00\%$.

Calculate: (1) Net Annual Income, (2) Year's Purchase (YP), (3) Capitalized Value of Property, and (4) Annual Sinking Fund Installment required to accumulate ₹50,00,000 over 30 years (given that $(1.06)^{30} = 5.74349$).

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Calculate Net Annual Rental Income:**
   $$\text{Outgoings} = 12,00,000 \times 20\% = ₹2,40,000$$
   $$\text{Net Annual Income} = 12,00,000 - 2,40,000 = ₹9,60,000$$

2. **Calculate Year's Purchase (YP):**
   $$\text{YP} = \frac{1}{\text{Capitalization Rate}} = \frac{1}{0.08} = 12.50$$

3. **Compute Capitalized Property Value:**
   $$\text{Capitalized Value} = \text{Net Income} \times \text{YP} = 9,60,000 \times 12.50 = ₹1,20,00,000 \quad (₹1.20\text{ Crores})$$

4. **Compute Annual Sinking Fund Installment ($I$):**
   $$I = S \times \frac{i}{(1 + i)^n - 1} = 50,00,000 \times \frac{0.06}{5.74349 - 1} = 50,00,000 \times \frac{0.06}{4.74349} = \frac{3,00,000}{4.74349} = ₹63,244.69 \approx ₹63,245$$

The capitalized property value is **₹1.20 Crores**, and the required annual sinking fund deposit is **₹63,245**.

## 4. Housing Finance Tax Deductions & Capital Gains Framework

| Section | Nature of Tax Concession | Maximum Statutory Deduction Cap | Mandatory Qualifying Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 24(b)** | Interest on Housing Loan (Self-Occupied) | **₹2,00,000** per financial year | Available for acquisition/construction completed within 5 years from end of FY in which loan was borrowed |
| **Section 24(b)** | Interest on Housing Loan (Rented / Let-Out) | **Actual Interest Paid (No Upper Cap)** | Loss under House Property that can be set off against other heads capped at ₹2,00,000 per year |
| **Section 80C** | Principal Repayment of Housing Loan | **₹1,50,000** (Within aggregate 80C cap) | Also covers stamp duty and registration charges paid in year of purchase; property cannot be sold for 5 years |
| **Section 80EEA** | Additional Interest Deduction for Affordable Housing | **₹1,50,000** (Over and above Section 24b) | Loan sanctioned between 1 April 2019 and 31 March 2022; Stamp Duty value $\le ₹45\text{ Lakhs}$; first-time home buyer |
| **Section 54** | Capital Gains Exemption on Sale of Residential House | Exemption equal to capital gains invested | Reinvest in another residential house within 1 yr before, 2 yrs after sale, or construct within 3 yrs |
| **Section 54EC** | Capital Gains Exemption via Specified Bonds | **₹50,00,000** per financial year | Invest in bonds of REC, PFC, NHAI, or IRFC within **6 months** of property transfer (5-year lock-in) |

## 5. Solved Examination Questions

**Q1.** Under Section 4 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016 (RERA), what percentage of amounts realized from allottees must the promoter deposit into a dedicated scheduled bank escrow account?
- (A) 50%
- (B) 60%
- (C) 70%
- (D) 80%
*Answer:* **(C)**  
*Explanation:* RERA Section 4(2)(l)(D) mandates that 70% of all funds collected from buyers must be deposited in a dedicated bank escrow account for construction and land costs.

**Q2.** Under the Rent Capitalization method of property valuation, if the capitalization rate in a commercial market is $10.00\%$, what is the Year's Purchase (YP)?
- (A) 5
- (B) 10
- (C) 12.5
- (D) 20
*Answer:* **(B)**  
*Explanation:* Year's Purchase is calculated as $\text{YP} = \frac{1}{\text{Capitalization Rate}} = \frac{1}{0.10} = 10$.
