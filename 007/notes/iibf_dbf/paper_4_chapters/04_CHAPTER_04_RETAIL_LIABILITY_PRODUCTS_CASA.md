# RETAIL LIABILITY PRODUCTS: CASA, TIME DEPOSITS & SPECIAL SCHEMES

> **Paper:** 4 (Retail Banking and Wealth Management)  
> **Standard:** Macmillan Courseware & IIBF 2026 Master Benchmark • Duplex A4 Monochrome Print Edition

Liability products represent the foundation of commercial banking, providing the low-cost deposit funding necessary to finance asset origination. Retail liabilities fall into two broad statutory classes: **Demand Liabilities** (Current and Savings Accounts - CASA), payable on demand, and **Time Liabilities** (Fixed Deposits and Recurring Deposits), payable after a contracted maturity. In India, retail liability administration is strictly governed by RBI Master Directions, statutory operational mandates (minors, nomination, joint accounts, deceased claims), the dual-track direct tax framework, and DICGC insurance protections.

---

## 1. Master Classification of Retail Liability Deposits

| Deposit Class | Account Type | Interest Bearing Status | Tenor Structure | Target Segment & Primary Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demand Liabilities** | **Current Account** | **Zero Interest** (Strictly prohibited by RBI) | Indefinite; payable instantly on demand | Traders, businesses, corporate entities for high-velocity daily transactions |
| **Demand Liabilities** | **Savings Bank Account** | **Interest Payable** (Calculated on daily end-of-day balance) | Indefinite; payable on demand | Individuals, households, clubs, trusts for routine savings and payments |
| **Time Liabilities** | **Fixed / Term Deposit (FD)** | **Interest Payable** (Cumulative or periodic payout) | **Min 7 Days, Max 10 Years** | Individuals and institutions seeking fixed yield and capital safety |
| **Time Liabilities** | **Recurring Deposit (RD)** | **Interest Payable** (Compounded quarterly) | **Min 6 Months, Max 10 Years** | Salaried earners depositing a fixed monthly installment toward a future goal |

## 2. Current Accounts: Regulatory Architecture & Operational Rules

- **Statutory Prohibition of Interest:** RBI regulations prohibit banks from paying interest on current account balances, whether held by residents or non-residents.
- **Permitted Entities:** Sole proprietorships, partnerships, LLPs, private/public limited companies, trusts, and government departments. Commercial current accounts cannot be opened in the name of deceased persons or insolvent entities.

### RBI Revised Guidelines on Opening Current Accounts
To enforce credit discipline and curb fund diversion by corporate borrowers, the RBI mandates:
1. **Borrowers with CC/OD Facilities:**
   - **Exposure $\ge ₹5\text{ Crores}$:** No bank can open a current account for a borrower who has availed Cash Credit (CC) / Overdraft (OD) facilities from the banking system. All transactions must be routed through the CC/OD account.
   - Banks holding $\ge 10\%$ of the borrower's total banking exposure may open collection/escrow accounts, but funds must be remitted back to the primary CC/OD account.
2. **Borrowers without CC/OD Facilities:**
   - If total credit exposure is $\ge ₹50\text{ Crores}$, an **Escrow Mechanism** is mandatory; only the escrow-managing bank can open a current account.
   - If exposure is between ₹5 Crores and ₹50 Crores, lending banks can open current accounts; non-lending banks cannot open current accounts.

## 3. Savings Bank Accounts: Interest Mechanics & BSBDA Mandate

### 1. Daily Product Method of Interest Calculation
Under RBI directives, savings bank interest must be calculated on a **daily end-of-day product basis**:
$$\text{Interest Amount} = \frac{\sum \text{Daily Closing Product} \times \text{Annual Interest Rate}}{365 \times 100}$$
- **Credit Frequency:** Interest must be credited to customer accounts at least **quarterly** (or on shorter intervals like monthly).

### 2. Minimum Balance Maintenance & Penalty Norms
- Banks must give advance notice (via SMS or Email) to customers when account balances drop below the mandatory minimum threshold.
- Penalties levied for non-maintenance of minimum balance must be **proportional** to the shortfall and **cannot turn the account into a negative balance**.

### 3. Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account (BSBDA) / PMJDY Mandates
- **Zero Minimum Balance:** No initial deposit requirement; zero minimum balance maintenance.
- **Service Inclusions:** Free ATM-cum-debit card; cash deposits at branch and ATMs free of charge.
- **Withdrawal Limits:** Minimum **4 free withdrawals per month** (including branch cash withdrawals and ATM withdrawals). The rule establishes a statutory floor of 4 free withdrawals; banks cannot levy charges for basic transactions within this minimum threshold.

## 4. Special Customer Segments & Operational Procedures

### 1. Accounts of Minors
Under Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, an agreement with a minor is void ab initio. However, banks open savings accounts for minors under protective rules:
- **Operated by Guardian:** A minor of any age can have an account opened and operated by a natural guardian (father or mother) or court-appointed legal guardian.
- **Independent Operation (Age 10+):** Minors aged **10 years and above** who can read and write are permitted to open and operate savings accounts independently in their own name, subject to bank-prescribed balance caps and debit card issuance.
- **Strict Prohibition of Overdraft:** No overdraft or advance facility can ever be sanctioned to a minor account, as a minor cannot be held personally liable for debt.
- **Attainment of Majority:** Upon reaching 18 years of age, the guardian's operational mandate lapses immediately. The former minor must confirm the account balance, submit fresh KYC documents, and provide fresh specimen signatures.

### 2. Nomination Rules in Bank Deposits
Governed by Sections 45ZA to 45ZF of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 and the Banking Companies (Nomination) Rules, 1985:
- **Statutory Forms:** **Form DA-1** (making a nomination), **Form DA-2** (cancellation of nomination), and **Form DA-3** (modification/variation of nomination).
- **Single Nominee Rule:** Only **one individual** can be nominated per deposit account (whether held individually or jointly). Joint nominations are prohibited for deposits (permitted only for safe deposit lockers).
- **Legal Status of Nominee:** A nominee is legally an **agent/trustee** holding the funds on behalf of the legal heirs of the deceased depositor under Indian succession laws; the nominee does not become the absolute beneficial owner. However, payment by the bank to the nominee constitutes a **valid legal discharge** for the bank.

### 3. Joint Accounts & Operational Mandates
- **Either or Survivor (E or S):** Either account holder can operate the account singly during their lifetime. On the demise of one holder, the balance is payable to the survivor without probate.
- **Former or Survivor (F or S):** The former operates the account during their lifetime; the survivor can operate or claim the balance only after the demise of the former.
- **Jointly:** Both/all account holders must sign for every withdrawal, mandate, or instruction.

### 4. Settlement of Claims in Deceased Depositor Accounts
- Under RBI Master Directions, banks must fix board-approved monetary thresholds for settlement of claims in deceased accounts without production of legal representation (Probate, Letters of Administration, or Succession Certificate) against a Letter of Indemnity and surety.
- Banks must settle complete claims within **15 days** of receipt of all necessary claim documents.

## 5. Time Deposits: Operations, Overdue Rules & Advances

### 1. Tenor and Premature Withdrawal
- **Permissible Tenor:** Minimum **7 days** (minimum 15 days for bulk deposits $> ₹3\text{ Crores}$), maximum **10 years** (can extend to 20 years under court orders).
- **Premature Penalty:** Banks typically charge a penalty of $0.5\% - 1.0\%$ below the applicable interest rate for the actual period run. Non-callable deposits (without premature withdrawal option) are permitted for retail deposits exceeding prescribed thresholds (e.g., $\ge ₹1\text{ Crore}$).

### 2. Overdue Term Deposits (RBI Master Direction)
If a term deposit matures and proceeds remain unclaimed and unrenewed:
$$\text{Overdue Interest Rate} = \min(\text{Contracted FD Rate}, \text{Prevailing Savings Bank Rate})$$
The bank pays interest from the maturity date at the savings account rate or contracted rate, whichever is lower.

### 3. Advances Against Bank's Own Term Deposit
- **Margin:** Typically **$10\% - 15\%$** (loan sanction of $85\% - 90\%$ of FDR principal plus accrued interest).
- **Interest Rate:** Usually **$1.0\% - 2.0\%$** above the deposit interest rate.
- **No Third-Party Advance without Consent:** If a deposit is held in joint names or in the name of a third party, advance requires explicit written discharge from all depositors.

## 6. Statutory Tax Framework & Unclaimed Deposit Administration

### 1. Dual-Track Direct Tax Architecture (TDS on Bank Deposits)
Direct tax withholding on bank deposit interest spans two distinct frameworks:

#### A. Historical Framework / AY 2026–27 (Income-tax Act, 1961):
- **Section 194A TDS Threshold:** TDS applies if annual deposit interest across all branches of a bank exceeds **₹40,000** for general citizens, or **₹50,000** for senior citizens (aged 60+).
- **Exemption Declarations:** **Form 15G** (for individuals below 60 years whose total income is below basic exemption) and **Form 15H** (for senior citizens aged 60+ whose net tax liability is NIL).

#### B. Current Framework / Tax Year 2026–27 Onward (Income-tax Act, 2025):
- **Enhanced Interest Thresholds:** TDS on interest corresponding provisions apply if annual deposit interest exceeds **₹50,000** for general citizens, or **₹1,00,000** for senior citizens.
- **Form 121 Mandate:** Self-declaration of nil tax liability is consolidated into **Form 121**, replacing Forms 15G and 15H under the modernized rules.
- **Section 206AA Mandate:** If the depositor does not furnish a valid Permanent Account Number (PAN), TDS must be deducted at the higher rate of **$20\%$**.

### 2. Inoperative / Dormant Accounts (2-Year Rule)
- A savings or current account is classified as **inoperative / dormant** if there are **no customer-induced transactions** (debit or credit) for **over 2 years**.
- *Crucial Rule:* System-generated transactions (crediting interest, debiting service charges, dividend mandates) are **NOT customer-induced** and cannot restart the 2-year dormancy clock.
- **No Penalties:** Banks are strictly prohibited from levying charges for activating inoperative accounts; interest must continue to be credited regularly on inoperative savings and term deposits.

### 3. Unclaimed Deposits & The DEA Fund (10-Year Rule)
- Balances in accounts inoperative for **10 years or more**, or term deposits unclaimed for 10 years after maturity, must be transferred to the **Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund** under **Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949**.
- Transfers occur on the last working day of each subsequent month along with accrued interest. Depositors retain the right to claim refund from their bank at any time.
- **RBI UDGAM Portal:** Centralized digital portal (*Unclaimed Deposits - Gateway to Access inforMation*) enabling citizens to search unclaimed balances across multiple banks.

### 4. Deposit Insurance Cover (DICGC)
- Under the DICGC Act 1961, all commercial bank deposits are insured up to **₹5,00,000** per depositor per bank (covering principal plus interest) in the same right and same capacity.

---

> [!CAUTION]
> **Examiner Trap Alert & Regulatory Pitfalls:**
> 1. **Savings Balance Negative Trap:** Banks are **prohibited** from turning a savings bank account balance negative due to non-maintenance penalty charges.
> 2. **Dormancy Clock Trigger:** System credits of interest do **not** restart the 2-year dormancy clock; only active customer-induced actions qualify.
> 3. **Nominee vs Legal Heir:** A nominee is legally a **custodian/trustee** for the legal heirs and does not automatically become the beneficial owner under inheritance laws, though payment to a nominee provides the bank with a valid legal discharge.
> 4. **Overdue Deposit Interest:** Unrenewed overdue term deposits earn interest from maturity date at the **savings bank rate or contracted rate, whichever is lower**.

## 7. Worked Numerical: Daily Product Savings Interest Calculation

**Problem Statement:**  
A customer maintains a savings account with a commercial bank at an interest rate of $3.65\%$ per annum. The ledger closing balances for a 90-day quarter were as follows:
- Days 1 to 30 (30 days): Closing balance ₹50,000
- Days 31 to 60 (30 days): Closing balance ₹80,000
- Days 61 to 90 (30 days): Closing balance ₹1,20,000

Calculate the total quarterly interest to be credited to the account.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Calculate Daily Closing Products:**
   - Period 1 Product: $50,000 \times 30 = ₹15,00,000$
   - Period 2 Product: $80,000 \times 30 = ₹24,00,000$
   - Period 3 Product: $1,20,000 \times 30 = ₹36,00,000$

2. **Calculate Total Aggregate Product:**
   $$\sum \text{Product} = 15,00,000 + 24,00,000 + 36,00,000 = ₹75,00,000$$

3. **Compute Interest Amount:**
   $$\text{Interest} = \frac{\sum \text{Product} \times \text{Rate}}{365 \times 100} = \frac{75,00,000 \times 3.65}{36,500} = \frac{2,73,75,000}{36,500} = ₹750.00$$

Total quarterly interest credited = **₹750.00**.

## 8. Solved Examination Questions

**Q1.** Under Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act 1949, deposit balances remaining unclaimed for how many years must be transferred to the Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund?
- (A) 3 Years
- (B) 5 Years
- (C) 7 Years
- (D) 10 Years
*Answer:* **(D)**  
*Explanation:* Any deposit account remaining inoperative or unclaimed for 10 years or more must have its entire balance transferred to the RBI's DEA Fund on the last working day of each subsequent month.

**Q2.** Under the current statutory tax framework (Income-tax Act, 2025 / updated thresholds), what is the TDS threshold on bank fixed deposit interest for senior citizens (aged 60 years and above)?
- (A) ₹40,000
- (B) ₹50,000
- (C) ₹1,00,000
- (D) ₹2,00,000
*Answer:* **(C)**  
*Explanation:* Under the modernized tax framework, the threshold for TDS on deposit interest for senior citizens is ₹1,00,000 (up from the historical ₹50,000 under the 1961 Act), while the regular depositor threshold is ₹50,000 (up from historical ₹40,000).

**Q3.** Under the Banking Companies (Nomination) Rules, which statutory form is used by a depositor to cancel an existing nomination?
- (A) Form DA-1
- (B) Form DA-2
- (C) Form DA-3
- (D) Form 15G
*Answer:* **(B)**  
*Explanation:* Form DA-1 is used for making a nomination, Form DA-2 is used for cancellation of nomination, and Form DA-3 is used for variation/modification of nomination.

## 9. Active Recall & Self-Diagnostic Prompts

<details>
<summary>1. State the RBI rule regarding overdue term deposits that remain unrenewed.</summary>

If a term deposit matures and proceeds remain unpaid and unrenewed, the bank pays interest from the maturity date at the prevailing savings bank interest rate or the contracted fixed deposit interest rate, whichever is lower.
</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Why are system-generated credits excluded when determining account dormancy?</summary>

Because dormancy is defined strictly by the absence of active customer intention. Automated credits like interest or service fee debits do not demonstrate customer awareness or operational activity.
</details>

<details>
<summary>3. What is the legal distinction between a nominee and a legal heir in bank deposit settlement?</summary>

A nominee is a designated custodian/trustee who receives deposit proceeds from the bank upon the depositor's demise. Payment to the nominee gives the bank a valid legal discharge, but the nominee must hold the funds for the benefit of the legal heirs entitled under succession laws.
</details>
