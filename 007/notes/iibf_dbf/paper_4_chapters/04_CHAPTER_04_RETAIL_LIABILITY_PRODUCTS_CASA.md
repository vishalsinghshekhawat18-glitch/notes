# RETAIL LIABILITY PRODUCTS: CASA, TIME DEPOSITS & SPECIAL SCHEMES

> **Paper:** 4 (Retail Banking and Wealth Management)  
> **Standard:** Macmillan Courseware & IIBF 2026 Master Benchmark • Duplex A4 Monochrome Print Edition

Liability products represent the foundation of commercial banking, providing the low-cost deposit funding necessary to finance asset origination. Retail liabilities fall into two broad statutory classes: **Demand Liabilities** (Current and Savings Accounts - CASA), payable on demand, and **Time Liabilities** (Fixed Deposits and Recurring Deposits), payable after a contracted maturity. In India, retail liability administration is strictly governed by RBI Master Directions, statutory tax frameworks, and DICGC insurance protections.

---

## 1. Master Classification of Retail Liability Deposits

| Deposit Class | Account Type | Interest Bearing Status | Tenor Structure | Target Segment & Primary Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demand Liabilities** | **Current Account** | **Zero Interest** (Strictly prohibited by RBI) | Indefinite; payable instantly on demand | Traders, businesses, corporate entities for high-velocity daily transactions |
| **Demand Liabilities** | **Savings Bank Account** | **Interest Payable** (Calculated on daily end-of-day balance) | Indefinite; payable on demand | Individuals, households, clubs, trusts for routine savings and payments |
| **Time Liabilities** | **Fixed / Term Deposit (FD)** | **Interest Payable** (Cumulative or periodic payout) | **Min 7 Days, Max 10 Years** | Individuals and institutions seeking fixed yield and capital safety |
| **Time Liabilities** | **Recurring Deposit (RD)** | **Interest Payable** (Compounded quarterly) | **Min 6 Months, Max 10 Years** | Salaried earners depositing a fixed monthly installment toward a future goal |

## 2. Current Accounts: Regulatory Architecture & Operational Rules

- **Statutory Prohibition of Interest:** RBI regulations prohibit banks from paying interest on current account balances, whether held by residents or non-residents.
- **Permitted Entities:** Sole proprietorships, partnerships, LLPs, private/public limited companies, trusts, and government departments. Commercial current accounts cannot be opened in the name of deceased persons or insolvent entities.

### RBI Revised Guidelines on Opening Current Accounts
To enforce credit discipline and curb fund diversion by corporate borrowers, the RBI mandates:
1. **Borrowers with CC/OD Facilities:**
   - **Exposure $\ge ₹5\text{ Crores}$:** No bank can open a current account for a borrower who has availed Cash Credit (CC) / Overdraft (OD) facilities from the banking system. All transactions must be routed through the CC/OD account.
   - Banks holding $\ge 10\%$ of the borrower's total banking exposure may open collection/escrow accounts, but funds must be siphoned back to the primary CC/OD account.
2. **Borrowers without CC/OD Facilities:**
   - If total credit exposure is $\ge ₹50\text{ Crores}$, an **Escrow Mechanism** is mandatory; only the escrow-managing bank can open a current account.

## 3. Savings Bank Accounts: Interest Mechanics & BSBDA Mandate

### 1. Daily Product Method of Interest Calculation
Under RBI directives, savings bank interest must be calculated on a **daily end-of-day product basis**:
$$\text{Interest Amount} = \frac{\sum \text{Daily Closing Product} \times \text{Annual Interest Rate}}{365 \times 100}$$
- **Credit Frequency:** Interest must be credited to the customer account at least **quarterly** (or monthly).

### 2. Minimum Balance Maintenance & Penalty Norms
- Banks must give advance notice (SMS/Email) to customers when account balances drop below the mandatory minimum threshold.
- Penalties levied for non-maintenance of minimum balance must be **proportional** to the shortfall and **cannot turn the account into a negative balance**.

### 3. Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account (BSBDA) / PMJDY Mandates
- **Zero Minimum Balance:** No initial deposit requirement; zero minimum balance maintenance.
- **Service Inclusions:** Free ATM-cum-debit card; cash deposits at branch and ATMs free of charge.
- **Withdrawal Limits:** Minimum **4 free withdrawals per month** (including branch cash withdrawals and ATM withdrawals). Banks cannot levy charges for value-added services if the basic four withdrawals are not exceeded.

## 4. Time Deposits: Operations, Overdue Rules & Advances

### 1. Tenor and Premature Withdrawal
- **Permissible Tenor:** Minimum **7 days** (minimum 15 days for bulk deposits $> ₹3\text{ Crores}$), maximum **10 years** (can extend to 20 years under court orders).
- **Premature Penalty:** Banks typically charge a penalty of $0.5\% - 1.0\%$ below the applicable interest rate for the actual period run. Non-callable deposits (without premature withdrawal option) are permitted for deposits $\ge ₹1\text{ Crore}$ (or $\ge ₹15\text{ Lakhs}$ for retail under revised guidelines).

### 2. Overdue Term Deposits (RBI Master Direction)
If a term deposit matures and proceeds remain unclaimed and unrenewed:
$$\text{Overdue Interest Rate} = \min(\text{Contracted FD Rate}, \text{Prevailing Savings Bank Rate})$$
The bank pays interest from the maturity date at the savings account rate or contracted rate, whichever is lower.

### 3. Advances Against Bank's Own Term Deposit
- **Margin:** Typically **$10\% - 15\%$** (meaning loan sanction of $85\% - 90\%$ of FDR principal plus accrued interest).
- **Interest Rate:** Usually **$1.0\% - 2.0\%$** above the deposit interest rate.
- **Tax Advantage:** No TDS is deducted on interest paid by the borrower on such advances.

## 5. Statutory Tax Withholding & Unclaimed Deposit Administration

### 1. Section 194A TDS on Term Deposit Interest
- **General Depositors (Below 60 Years):** TDS applies if total interest earned across all branches of a bank exceeds **₹40,000** in a financial year.
- **Senior Citizens (Aged 60 Years and Above):** TDS applies only if total interest exceeds **₹50,000** in a financial year.
- **Exemption Declarations:**
  - **Form 15G:** Submitted by individuals below 60 years whose estimated total income is below the basic tax exemption limit.
  - **Form 15H:** Submitted by senior citizens (aged 60+) whose net tax liability for the financial year is NIL (even if total income exceeds basic exemption).

### 2. Inoperative / Dormant Accounts (2-Year Rule)
- A savings or current account is classified as **inoperative / dormant** if there are **no customer-induced transactions** (debit or credit) for **over 2 years**.
- *Crucial Rule:* System-generated transactions (crediting interest, debiting service charges) are **NOT customer-induced** and cannot prevent dormancy.
- Reactivation must be executed **free of charge** upon fresh KYC verification.

### 3. Unclaimed Deposits & The DEA Fund (10-Year Rule)
- Balances in accounts inoperative for **10 years or more**, or term deposits unclaimed for 10 years after maturity, must be transferred to the **Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund** under **Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949**.
- **RBI UDGAM Portal:** Centralized digital portal (*Unclaimed Deposits - Gateway to Access inforMation*) enabling citizens to track unclaimed deposits across multiple banks.

### 4. Deposit Insurance Cover (DICGC)
- Under the DICGC Act 1961, all commercial bank deposits are insured up to **₹5,00,000** per depositor per bank (covering principal plus interest) in the same right and same capacity.

---

> [!CAUTION]
> **Examiner Trap Alert & Regulatory Pitfalls:**
> 1. **Savings Balance Negative Trap:** Banks are **prohibited** from turning a savings bank account balance negative due to non-maintenance penalty charges.
> 2. **Dormancy Clock Trigger:** System credits of interest do **not** restart the 2-year dormancy clock; only customer-induced actions qualify.
> 3. **Form 15H vs 15G:** Form 15H is strictly for **Senior Citizens (60+)** and requires zero tax liability; Form 15G is for individuals under 60 whose total taxable income is below the basic threshold.

## 6. Worked Numerical: Daily Product Savings Interest Calculation

**Problem Statement:**  
A customer maintains a savings account with a commercial bank at an interest rate of $3.65\%$ per annum. The ledger closing balances for a 90-day quarter were as follows:
- Days 1 to 30 (30 days): Closing balance ₹50,000
- Days 31 to 60 (30 days): Closing balance ₹80,000
- Days 61 to 90 (30 days): Closing balance ₹1,20,000

Calculate the total quarterly interest to be credited to the account.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:

1. **Calculate Daily Closing Products:**
   - Period 1 Product: $50,000 \times 30 = ₹15,00,000$
   - Period 2 Product: $80,000 \times 30 = ₹24,00,000$
   - Period 3 Product: $1,20,000 \times 30 = ₹36,00,000$

2. **Calculate Total Aggregate Product:**
   $$\sum \text{Product} = 15,00,000 + 24,00,000 + 36,00,000 = ₹75,00,000$$

3. **Compute Interest Amount:**
   $$\text{Interest} = \frac{\sum \text{Product} \times \text{Rate}}{365 \times 100} = \frac{75,00,000 \times 3.65}{36,500} = \frac{2,73,75,000}{36,500} = ₹750.00$$

Total quarterly interest credited = **₹750.00**.

## 7. Solved Examination Questions

**Q1.** Under Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act 1949, deposit balances remaining unclaimed for how many years must be transferred to the Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund?
- (A) 3 Years
- (B) 5 Years
- (C) 7 Years
- (D) 10 Years
*Answer:* **(D)**  
*Explanation:* Any deposit account remaining inoperative for 10 years or more must have its entire balance transferred to the RBI's DEA Fund.

**Q2.** Under current Income Tax regulations, what is the Section 194A TDS threshold on bank fixed deposit interest for senior citizens (aged 60 years and above)?
- (A) ₹10,000
- (B) ₹40,000
- (C) ₹50,000
- (D) ₹1,00,000
*Answer:* **(C)**  
*Explanation:* The TDS threshold on interest from deposits with banks is ₹50,000 for senior citizens, compared to ₹40,000 for non-senior citizens.

<summary>1. State the RBI rule regarding overdue term deposits that remain unrenewed.</summary>

If a term deposit matures and proceeds remain unpaid and unrenewed, the bank pays interest from the maturity date at the prevailing savings bank interest rate or the contracted fixed deposit interest rate, whichever is lower.

Because dormancy is defined strictly by the absence of active customer intention. Automated credits like interest or service fee debits do not demonstrate customer awareness or operational activity.
