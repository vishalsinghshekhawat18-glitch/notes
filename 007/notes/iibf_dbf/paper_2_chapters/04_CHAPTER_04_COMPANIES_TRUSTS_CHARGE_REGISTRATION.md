# COMPANIES, TRUSTS & COMPANIES ACT SECTION 77 CHARGES

Corporate bodies and unincorporated associations possess distinct legal structures governed by company law, trust law, and cooperative society statutes. Lending to companies requires rigorous verification of corporate borrowing powers, authorized signatories, and mandatory registration of charges with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) under Section 77 of the Companies Act, 2013.

---

## § 4.1 Unit 04b: Corporate Entities, Trusts & Clubs

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 04 / 26)**  
> **Core Proposition:** A company is an artificial legal person created by law, with perpetual succession and an optional common seal (under the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2015, common seal is optional; documents can be signed by two directors or one director and the Company Secretary). Ultra vires transactions (beyond the Memorandum of Association) are void and cannot be ratified. Section 77 of the Companies Act, 2013 mandates statutory registration of all charges created by a company within strict timeframes.

### 1. Types of Companies & Key Documents

| Entity Type | Minimum Members / Directors | Key Statutory Documents & Operational Rules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Private Limited Company** | Min 2 Members / Max 200;<br>Min 2 Directors | • **Certificate of Incorporation** issued by ROC.<br>• **Memorandum of Association (MoA):** Defines the external boundary of company's powers and main objects (borrowing power clause).<br>• **Articles of Association (AoA):** Internal governance regulations, board meetings, delegation to directors. |
| **Public Limited Company** | Min 7 Members / No Max;<br>Min 3 Directors | • Requires Certificate of Incorporation.<br>• Share capital freely transferable.<br>• Can invite public deposits (subject to eligibility criteria under Section 76: net worth $\ge$ ₹100 Cr or turnover $\ge$ ₹500 Cr with credit rating) and issue listed debentures. |
| **One Person Company (OPC)** | Exactly 1 Member (Natural Person, Indian Citizen); Min 1 Director | • Sole member must nominate another natural person as **Nominee** in the event of death/incapacity.<br>• Simplified compliance; cannot carry on non-banking financial investment activities. |
| **Section 8 Company (Not-for-Profit)** | Min 2 Members (Private) / 7 (Public); Min 2/3 Directors | • Formed for promotion of commerce, art, charity, education.<br>• Prohibited from paying dividends to members.<br>• Profits must be applied solely to promoting its objects. |

### 2. Board Resolution & Authorized Signatories

Every corporate bank account opening or credit facility must be supported by a **Board Resolution** passed by the Board of Directors:
• Must be certified as a true copy by the Chairman / Company Secretary.
• Explicitly names authorized individuals, operating mandates (singly/jointly), borrowing limits, and authority to mortgage/pledge company property.
• **Doctrine of Indoor Management (*Royal British Bank v. Turquand*):** Outside parties dealing in good faith are entitled to presume that internal regulations have been complied with. However, the doctrine **does not protect a bank if there is actual knowledge of irregularity, forgery, or gross negligence**.

## § 4.2 Unit 26b: Companies Act, 2013 Section 77 Charge Registration

> **P0 Audit Fix — Stop-Ship Regulatory Clearance (C-03)**  
> Section 77 of the Companies Act, 2013 imposes a statutory duty on every company creating a charge on its property or assets (tangible or intangible, in India or abroad) to register the particulars of the charge with the Registrar of Companies (ROC).

### 1. The Statutory Charge Registration Timeline (Section 77 & 87)

• **Normal Statutory Period (Section 77(1)):**
  - Registration within **30 days** of the date of creation of charge.
  - Normal prescribed filing fees payable via MCA portal (Form CHG-1 for assets other than debentures, Form CHG-9 for debentures).
• **First Extension by Registrar (First Proviso to Section 77(1)):**
  - If not registered within 30 days, the Registrar may, on application, allow registration to be made within a period of **sixty (60) days of such creation**.
  - Permitted upon payment of prescribed **additional fees**.
• **Second Extension by Registrar on Ad Valorem Fees (Second Proviso to Section 77(1)):**
  - If not registered within the 60-day period from creation, the Registrar may allow registration within a **further period of sixty (60) days**.
  - Permitted upon application and payment of prescribed **ad valorem fees** based on the value of the charge.
  - **Total Outer Window with RoC:** Exactly **120 days from creation** (60 days from creation + further 60 days).
• **Condonation of Delay by Central Government (Section 87):**
  - Beyond 120 days from the date of creation of the charge, the **Registrar has zero statutory power** to register the charge.
  - The company or any interested person (such as the lending bank) must petition the Central Government (delegated to the Regional Director) under Section 87 for condonation of delay and rectification of the register.

### 2. Consequences of Non-Registration (Section 77(3))

If a charge is not registered with the ROC:
• The charge is **VOID against the Liquidator and any other Creditor** of the company.
• If the company enters insolvency or liquidation, the bank is treated as an **unsecured creditor**, losing its priority over the secured asset!
• **Protection of the Debt:** Non-registration does NOT invalidate the underlying debt. The money secured remains payable immediately.

## § 4.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. A commercial bank sanctions a term loan of ₹10 Crore to a private limited company on 1 October, secured by a hypothecation of plant and machinery. By which date must the charge normally be registered with the Registrar of Companies under Section 77(1) of the Companies Act, 2013?**  
A. Within 15 days (by 16 October)  
B. Within 30 days (by 31 October)  
C. Within 60 days with normal fees  
D. Within 300 days as a standard statutory right  

**Q2. [IIBF-Pattern Diagnostic] Under Section 77(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, if a company fails to register a charge within the initial 30 days of creation, what is the maximum outer timeline within which the Registrar of Companies (ROC) itself is statutorily empowered to allow registration upon payment of additional and ad valorem fees?**  
A. Up to 60 days from creation on additional fees, with no further extension permitted by ROC  
B. Up to 60 days from creation on additional fees, plus a further period of 60 days on ad valorem fees (total 120 days from creation)  
C. Up to 180 days from creation upon payment of standard compounding penalties  
D. Up to 300 days from creation at the sole discretion of the Board of Directors  

**Q3. [Examiner Trap] What is the legal consequence under Section 77(3) of the Companies Act, 2013 if a bank fails to register a mortgage charge created by a borrowing company that subsequently enters liquidation?**  
A. The entire loan advance is declared illegal and forfeited to the government  
B. The charge is void against the liquidator and other creditors, demoting the bank to an unsecured creditor  
C. The bank retains first charge over the property by virtue of common law equitable rights  
D. The directors of the company become criminally liable under Section 138 of NI Act

## § 4.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under Section 77(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, every charge created by a company must be registered with the ROC within **30 days** of its creation with normal filing fees (Form CHG-1 for movable/immovable assets, Form CHG-9 for debentures).  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** Under the provisos to Section 77(1), the statutory staging is: (1) normal registration within 30 days of creation; (2) the Registrar may allow registration within a period of **60 days of such creation** on payment of additional fees; (3) if not registered within 60 days, the Registrar may allow registration within a **further period of 60 days** on payment of prescribed *ad valorem* fees. Thus, the total outer window under ROC jurisdiction is **120 days from creation**. Beyond 120 days, ROC has zero power; condonation by Central Government (Regional Director) under Section 87 is mandatory.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** Section 77(3) explicitly provides that an unregistered charge is void against the liquidator and any creditor. The bank loses its secured status and must rank as an unsecured creditor in liquidation proceedings, though the debt itself remains legally recoverable.
