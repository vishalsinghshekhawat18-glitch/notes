# NON-FUND FACILITIES II: BANK GUARANTEES (BG) & DEFERRED PAYMENTS

A Bank Guarantee is a tripartite collateral contract of guarantee under Section 126 of the Indian Contract Act 1872, where the bank promises to discharge the liability of the applicant upon default. Divided into Financial and Performance Guarantees, BG operations are conditioned by Section 28 of the Contract Act and strict judicial rules on injunction restraint.

## § 12.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

Bank Guarantees (BG), Co-Acceptance & Deferred Payments

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Contract of Guarantee (Section 126, Indian Contract Act 1872)**
> A contract of guarantee is a tripartite contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default.

## 1. Classification & Characteristics of Bank Guarantees

| Guarantee Type | Purpose / Commercial Use Case | Bank Risk Profile & Margin Guidelines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financial Guarantee | Guarantees repayment of a financial debt (e.g. advance payment guarantee, loan repayment, customs/excise duty payment) | High financial risk. RBI mandates higher cash margin / tangible collateral. |
| Performance Guarantee | Guarantees timely completion of a commercial contract, project construction, or service delivery | Relatively lower financial risk; bank must evaluate applicant's technical competence and past execution track record. |
| Deferred Payment Guarantee (DPG) | Guarantees payment of machinery/equipment purchases in installments over a medium-to-long-term horizon | Treated as a Term Loan equivalent; requires full financial appraisal and DSCR evaluation. |
| Bid Bond / Earnest Money Guarantee | Guarantees that bidder will not withdraw tender and will execute contract if awarded | Short-term validity (typically 3–6 months); converts to Performance Guarantee on award. |

## 2. Invocation of Bank Guarantee & Section 28 Limitation Period

A Bank Guarantee is an independent contract between the Bank and the Beneficiary. The bank must honor invocation on demand without inquiring into the underlying dispute between buyer and seller:

• **Rule of Absolute Independence:** The bank is not concerned with whether the principal debtor has committed breach or whether the claim is justified, provided invocation strictly complies with guarantee terms.
• **Court Injunction Exceptions:** Courts grant injunctions against BG invocation ONLY under 2 exceptional circumstances (established in *U.P. State Sugar Corp v. Sumac International Ltd*): (1) **Established Fraud of egregious nature** known to the bank, or (2) **Irretrievable Injustice / Irreparable Harm**.
• **Section 28 of Indian Contract Act (Amendment):** Guarantees can stipulate a specific claim period. If the guarantee provides for a claim period, it must allow a **minimum claim period of 1 year** beyond the guarantee expiry date for enforcing rights against the bank.

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on Bank Guarantees:
1. **Restraint on Invocation:** Banks **CANNOT refuse** payment on an unconditional bank guarantee merely because the customer (principal debtor) disputes the claim or requests stop-payment.
2. **Section 28 Claim Period Rule:** Under Section 28 of ICA, any clause curtailing the claim period to less than **1 year** from the expiry date is legally void.
3. **DPG Nature:** Deferred Payment Guarantee is evaluated on **Term Loan Appraisal principles** because installments mature over multiple years.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** Under Section 28 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 (amended), what is the minimum statutory claim limitation period that a bank can contractually specify for invoking a guarantee?
• (A) 6 months from the expiry of the validity period
• (B) 1 year from the expiry of the guarantee
• (C) 3 years from the date of default
• (D) 30 days from project commissioning

**Q2:** Under settled Supreme Court jurisprudence, in which exceptional circumstances will an Indian court issue an injunction restraining the invocation of an unconditional Bank Guarantee?
• (A) Any commercial dispute between buyer and seller regarding quality of goods
• (B) Only in cases of established egregious fraud of a serious nature or irretrievable injustice/harm
• (C) If the customer files an insolvency petition under IBC
• (D) Delay in supply caused by transport strike

**Q3:** What is the maximum tenor up to which commercial banks are generally permitted by RBI to issue Bank Guarantees without special approvals?
• (A) 3 years
• (B) 5 years
• (C) 10 years (subject to viability and risk mitigation)
• (D) 15 years

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(B) 1 year from the expiry of the guarantee** — Section 28 permits parties to agree that rights will be extinguished if no suit is instituted within a specified period of not less than 1 year from the date of expiry.

2. **(B) Only in cases of established egregious fraud of a serious nature or irretrievable injustice/harm** — An unconditional BG is an autonomous contract; courts will not interfere except in cases of egregious fraud known to the bank or irretrievable injustice (U.P. State Sugar Corp v. Sumac International).

3. **(C) 10 years (subject to viability and risk mitigation)** — Banks should not normally issue guarantees with tenors exceeding 10 years unless supported by specific cash margin, government counter-guarantee, or infrastructure parameters.

<details>
<summary>Distinguish between a Financial Guarantee and a Performance Guarantee.</summary>
Financial Guarantee: The bank guarantees the repayment of money or financial obligation by the borrower (e.g. advance payment guarantee, loan repayment guarantee); 100% credit risk, attracting higher risk weight and cash margin. Performance Guarantee: The bank guarantees the due performance of a technical, construction, or commercial contract; if the contractor fails to perform within timelines, the bank pays liquidated damages.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What is a Deferred Payment Guarantee (DPG) and how does it function in capital goods procurement?</summary>
A DPG is issued when a buyer purchases costly machinery/capital equipment on installment credit. The seller delivers machinery immediately, and the buyer agrees to pay in periodic installments with interest over 3 to 7 years. The buyer’s bank issues a DPG guaranteeing that each installment will be paid on due dates; if the buyer defaults, the bank steps in and honors the installment.
</details>
