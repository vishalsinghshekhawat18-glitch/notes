# BANKER-CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIP, RIGHTS & GENERAL DUTIES

The banker-customer relationship forms the foundational contract in commercial banking, governed by the Banking Regulation Act 1949, the Indian Contract Act 1872, the Negotiable Instruments Act 1881, and settled common law principles. The legal character of this relationship is dynamic, transforming according to the operational nature of each specific transaction.

---

## § 1.1 Unit 01: Banker-Customer Relationship

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 01)**  
> **Core Proposition:** A bank is legally defined under Section 5(b) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 as an institution accepting deposits of money from the public for lending or investment, repayable on demand or otherwise, and withdrawable by cheque, draft, order, or otherwise. Section 7 makes the use of words 'bank', 'banker', or 'banking' mandatory for banking entities.

### 1. Master Matrix of Transactional Legal Relationships

| Banking Transaction / Activity | Bank Status | Customer Status | Primary Governing Statute / Canon |

### 2. Legal Nuance: Specific-Purpose Funds & Termination of Trust Status

A deposit of money in a bank generally creates a pure debtor-creditor relationship. The money becomes the absolute property of the bank, and the banker is free to deploy it as it deems fit. However, when money is remitted or delivered with an **explicit, designated instruction for a specific unexecuted purpose** (e.g., to retire a documentary bill or purchase specific securities):
• The banker holds such funds as a **Trustee** for the customer until the designated mandate is executed.
• If the bank suspends payment or enters liquidation before executing the mandate, the customer is entitled to full refund as a beneficiary, ranking above general unsecured creditors.
• **Critical Exception:** Once the mandate is executed or if the customer permits the proceeds to be credited to a general account, the fiduciary relationship immediately terminates and reverts to a debtor-creditor relationship.

## § 1.2 Unit 07: Banker's Special Relationship, Rights & Duties

### 1. Right of General Lien (Section 171, Indian Contract Act, 1872)

A banker's lien is an **implied pledge** conferring the right to retain goods and securities belonging to a debtor until the general balance of accounts is discharged:
• **Scope:** Applies to all securities, commercial paper, and bills delivered to the bank in the ordinary course of business as a banker.
• **Implied Right of Sale:** Unlike ordinary bailees who only hold a possessory retaining lien, a banker has the statutory right to sell the retained securities upon giving reasonable notice to the borrower.
• **Exclusions from General Lien:**
  1. Safe custody articles and locked boxes (deposited for safe keeping, not for credit).
  2. Money deposited for a specific unfulfilled purpose known to the bank.
  3. Securities left in the bank inadvertently or by mistake.
  4. Shares deposited with the bank with a mandate to sell through a broker.
  5. Title deeds deposited for equitable mortgage (governed by the mortgage contract, not lien).

### 2. Right of Set-Off

The right of set-off empowers a banker to combine two or more accounts belonging to the same customer to arrive at a net debit or credit position.
• **Mandatory Conditions for Lawful Set-off:**
  - Both debts must be **mutual**, certain, and for an **ascertained sum of money**.
  - Both debts must be **due and payable immediately** (cannot set off a time deposit not yet matured against a demand debt unless contractually stipulated).
  - Debts must be in the **same right and same capacity**.
• **Capacity Invariants (Deadly Exam Traps):**
  - Sole Proprietor account and Individual Personal account: **Set-off Permitted** (proprietorship has no separate legal entity).
  - Individual account and Partnership account: **No Set-off** without express authorization.
  - Joint account (A & B) and Individual account (A alone): **No Set-off** of joint funds against individual liability.
  - Trust account and Trustee personal account: **Strictly Prohibited**.

### 3. Clayton's Rule (Sections 59 to 61, Indian Contract Act, 1872)

Established in *Devaynes v. Noble (Clayton's Case, 1816)*, Clayton's rule governs the statutory appropriation of payments in running open accounts (Cash Credit / Overdraft):
• **Statutory Principles of Appropriation:**
  - **Section 59:** Debtor has the first right to indicate to which specific debt a payment should be applied.
  - **Section 60:** If debtor fails to indicate, the creditor (bank) has the discretion to apply it to any lawful debt, including a time-barred debt (but not a disputed debt).
  - **Section 61 (Clayton's Rule):** If neither party appropriates, the law applies the payment in chronological order: **the earliest credit discharges the earliest debit**.
• **Operational Hazard in Banking:** In a running cash credit account, if a guarantor dies, retires, or a partner withdraws, the bank must **immediately freeze (break) the existing account** and open a new running account for subsequent operations. If the bank fails to break the account, subsequent credits by the surviving borrower automatically extinguish the pre-death guaranteed debits under Clayton's Rule, while new debits constitute fresh unsecured advances, releasing the estate of the deceased guarantor!

### 4. Banker's Duty of Secrecy: Tournier Doctrine & Statutory Exceptions

The banker's implied duty of confidentiality established in *Tournier v. National Provincial Bank (1924)* (codified for public sector banks in Section 13 of Banking Companies Acquisition Acts 1970/1980 and Section 44 of SBI Act 1955) permits disclosure only under four strictly demarcated exceptions:
1. **Under Compulsion of Law:**
   - Income Tax Act, 1961: Notice under Section 131, Section 133(6) (requisition of information), or Section 226(3) (garnishee notice).
   - Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS): Section 94 (*earlier Section 91 of CrPC*) — summons to produce account documents.
   - Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002: Section 12 (mandatory CTR/STR reporting to FIU-IND).
   - Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891 / Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023: Certified copies pursuant to court orders.
   - Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA): Enforcement Directorate statutory requisitions.
2. **Duty to the Public to Disclose:** Disclosures to prevent treason, state crimes, money laundering networks, or public danger (*Tournier* doctrine).
3. **In the Interests of the Bank:** Recovery suits before DRT or Civil Court, Section 13(2) SARFAESI notices, asserting banker's lien/set-off, or defending against litigation by the borrower.
4. **Express or Implied Consent of Customer:** Banker's status references provided with explicit customer authorization; mandatory sharing with Credit Information Companies (CICs: CIBIL, Equifax, Experian, CRIF) under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005.

## § 1.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. A partnership firm has an overdrawn cash credit account guaranteed by Mr. X. Mr. X dies on 15 March. The bank receives notice of death on 16 March but continues transactions in the same running account. Over the next month, the firm deposits ₹15 Lakh and withdraws ₹14 Lakh. In a recovery suit against Mr. X's legal heirs, what is the legal position?**  
A. The legal heirs are liable for the full balance because the guarantee is irrevocable  
B. Under Clayton's Rule, the deposits of ₹15 Lakh extinguished the guaranteed pre-death liability, releasing the estate of Mr. X  
C. The bank can exercise general lien on Mr. X's personal assets regardless of account operation  
D. Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act makes the legal heirs criminally liable  

**Q2. Under Section 171 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, in which of the following instances is a commercial bank legally entitled to exercise its Right of General Lien?**  
A. On gold ornaments deposited in a sealed box marked 'Safe Custody'  
B. On a fixed deposit receipt handed over to the bank inadvertently by a customer after renewal  
C. On commercial export bills lodged by a customer for collection in the ordinary course of banking  
D. On equity shares sent to the bank with a specific mandate to sell through a stockbroker  

**Q3. A customer maintains two operative accounts with a bank branch: (i) an individual Savings account in credit of ₹2,50,000, and (ii) a joint Current account in the names of the customer and his spouse in debit of ₹1,80,000. Can the bank exercise its right of set-off without a specific agreement?**  
A. Yes, because both accounts are within the same bank branch  
B. Yes, because the credit balance exceeds the debit balance  
C. No, because the accounts are held in different legal capacities (individual vs joint)  
D. No, because savings accounts are statutorily exempt from set-off  

---

## § 1.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under Clayton's Rule (Section 61, Indian Contract Act), in an unbroken running account, payments in are applied to the earliest debits. The ₹15 Lakh deposited post-death extinguished the pre-death guaranteed balance. The ₹14 Lakh withdrawn created a new debit for which the deceased guarantor's estate is not liable. The bank should have immediately frozen the account upon notice of death.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** A banker's general lien applies strictly to securities and commercial paper delivered in the ordinary course of business as a banker (e.g., bills for collection). Safe custody articles (A), inadvertent deliveries (B), and shares with a specific sale mandate (D) are held in special capacities (bailee/trustee) and are exempt from general lien.  
• **Q3 — Answer: C.** A fundamental prerequisite for the lawful exercise of set-off is that debts must be due in the *same right and same capacity*. An individual credit cannot be set off against a joint liability without the joint holders' express written consent.
