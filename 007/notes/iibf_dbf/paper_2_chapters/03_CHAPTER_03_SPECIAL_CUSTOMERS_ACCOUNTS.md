# OPERATIONS IN ACCOUNTS OF SPECIAL CATEGORIES OF CUSTOMERS

Commercial banks handle varied customer profiles possessing varying contractual capacities under the Indian Contract Act 1872, Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act 1956, and the Indian Majority Act 1875. Banking operations for minors, visually impaired individuals, illiterate persons, joint survivorships, and partnerships necessitate strict procedural and statutory safeguards.

## § 3.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

Operations in Accounts of Special Customers

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Legal Capacity to Contract (Section 11, Indian Contract Act)**
> Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject (18 years, or 21 years if court guardian appointed), of sound mind, and not disqualified by law.

## 1. Master Rules for Special Categories of Individual Customers

| Customer Type | Legal Framework / Capacity | Cheque Book / Overdraft Rules | Operational Guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minor (Under 10 Years) | Account opened and operated by natural guardian (Father/Mother) or Court Guardian | No overdraft; No personal liability on minor | Guardian signs on behalf of minor. Balance belongs to minor. |
| Minor (10+ Years, Literate) | Permitted by RBI to open and operate self-managed savings/FD account | Cheque book permitted; Overdraft strictly prohibited | Cannot be declared insolvent; Cannot ratify debts upon attaining majority. |
| Illiterate Person | Competent to contract; signs via Left Thumb Impression (LTI) for male / Right Thumb Impression (RTI) for female | Cheque book generally not issued unless with crossed order; cash withdrawal in person only | Thumb impression must be attested by independent witness known to bank. |
| Blind Person | Full legal capacity; equal rights to banking facilities under Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 | Cheque book, ATM/Debit Card, NetBanking fully permissible with talking ATM aids | Witness required during account opening; no discrimination permitted. |
| Purdahnashin Woman | Strict seclusion; transaction treated with heightened caution by courts | Cheque book permitted; withdrawals via authorized agent or witnessed mandate | Bank must ensure independent legal/financial understanding without undue influence. |
| Insolvent Person | Disqualified from contracting; property vests in Official Assignee / Receiver | Account must be immediately frozen upon receipt of Adjudication Order | Any credit balance must be handed over to the Official Receiver. |

## 2. Joint Account Survivorship Mandates

• **1. Either or Survivor (E or S):** Either party can operate during joint lifetime. Upon death of one, the survivor operates the account without requiring legal representation from deceased's heirs.
• **2. Former or Survivor (F or S):** Only the *Former* (first account holder) can operate during lifetime. The *Survivor* can operate only after the death of the Former upon producing death certificate.
• **3. Anyone or Survivors (A or S):** Used for 3 or more holders. Any single holder can operate. On death of one, remaining survivors continue operations.
• **4. Jointly Operated Accounts:** All holders must sign every withdrawal/cheque. On death of one holder, the balance is payable to the survivor(s) **AND** the legal heirs of the deceased joint holder jointly.

## 3. Non-Corporate Entities: Partnership & HUF

| Parameter | Partnership Firm (Partnership Act 1932) | Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governing Law | Indian Partnership Act, 1932 | Hindu Law (Mitakshara / Dayabhaga) & Hindu Succession Act |
| Creation / Membership | Created by Agreement / Contract between partners | Created by status of birth in the Hindu coparcenary |
| Management Head | All partners have implied authority to bind the firm | Exclusively managed by the **Karta** (eldest coparcener) |
| Liability | Unlimited joint and several liability across all partners | Karta has unlimited liability; Coparceners liable only to extent of their share in joint family property |
| Female Membership | Women can be full partners | Daughters are coparceners by birth post 2005 Amendment |
| Dissolution on Death | Death of partner dissolves firm unless partnership deed specifies otherwise | Death of Karta does not dissolve HUF; next senior coparcener becomes Karta |

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on Special Customers:
1. **Stopping Payment in Joint Accounts:** In an 'Either or Survivor' account, **ANY** of the joint holders can instruct the bank to stop payment of a cheque drawn by the other holder. But revocation of stop-payment requires consent of **ALL** holders.
2. **Minor as Partner Trap:** Under Section 30 of Indian Partnership Act, a minor CANNOT be a full partner, but can be admitted only to the **benefits of an existing partnership** with consent of all partners. Minor's personal property is not liable.
3. **Delegation by Karta:** A Karta cannot appoint an outsider as agent through Power of Attorney to manage HUF business unless strictly authorized by all adult coparceners.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** Under RBI guidelines, a literate minor who has attained which age is legally permitted to open and operate a savings bank account independently in their own name?
• (A) 7 years
• (B) 10 years
• (C) 14 years
• (D) 16 years

**Q2:** In a joint deposit account operated under the survivorship mandate "Former or Survivor", who is authorized to withdraw funds during the lifetime of both depositors?
• (A) Either depositor can withdraw at will
• (B) Only the first named accountholder (Former) during their lifetime
• (C) Only the second named accountholder (Survivor)
• (D) Both depositors must sign jointly

**Q3:** When an illiterate customer opens an account, how must their cheques or withdrawal slips be executed in the presence of bank officials?
• (A) Digital thumb impression with zero witnesses
• (B) Left hand thumb impression (LTI) for males / Right hand thumb impression (RTI) for females, verified by an independent witness
• (C) Verbal confirmation over telephone banking
• (D) Written authorization by an advocate

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(B) 10 years** — Minors who have attained the age of 10 years can open and operate savings accounts independently, subject to bank prudential turnover caps.

2. **(B) Only the first named accountholder (Former) during their lifetime** — Under "Former or Survivor", only the Former can operate the account while alive; the Survivor can operate/claim funds only upon the Former’s death.

3. **(B) Left hand thumb impression (LTI) for males / Right hand thumb impression (RTI) for females, verified by an independent witness** — Established banking practice requires physical thumb impression attested by an independent witness known to the bank.

## § 3.2 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

<details>
<summary>What is the legal effect of a minor partner in a Partnership firm under Section 30 of the Indian Partnership Act 1932?</summary>
A minor cannot be a full partner because a minor’s agreement is void ab initio (Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose). However, with the consent of all partners, a minor may be admitted to the benefits of partnership. The minor’s share in the firm’s property/profits is liable for partnership debts, but the minor is not personally liable. Upon attaining majority, the minor has 6 months to elect whether to become a full partner.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Explain the legal status of an account held by a Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) and the doctrine of Karta borrowing for Legal Necessity.</summary>
An HUF account is operated exclusively by the Karta (the senior coparcener). The Karta has implied authority to bind the family estate for debts incurred for "Legal Necessity" (Patni/ancestral business upkeep, taxes, family welfare). Coparceners are liable only to the extent of their undivided share in the joint family property, whereas the Karta is personally liable for borrowings.
</details>
