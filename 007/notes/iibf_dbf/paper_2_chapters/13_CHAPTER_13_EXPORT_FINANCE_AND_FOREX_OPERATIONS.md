# EXPORT FINANCE, PRE/POST-SHIPMENT CREDIT & ECGC POLICIES

Export credit fuels international trade through rupee and foreign currency credit lines subsidized by RBI interest equalization schemes. Covering Pre-Shipment Packing Credit and Post-Shipment discounting (bills under collection/LCs), risk mitigation relies upon Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India (ECGC) insurance umbrellas.

## § 13.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

Export Finance & Foreign Exchange Operations for Banks

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Export Credit Architecture in Indian Banking**
> Under RBI Master Directions on Export Credit, banks provide financial assistance to exporters at concessional interest rates across two distinct operational stages.

## 1. Master Matrix: Pre-Shipment vs Post-Shipment Export Credit

| Parameter | Pre-Shipment Credit (Packing Credit / EPC) | Post-Shipment Export Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligibility Basis | Valid export order or confirmed irrevocable Letter of Credit (LC) from overseas buyer | Shipping documents (Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, Customs Shipping Bill, Commercial Invoice) |
| Permissible Tenor | Normally up to **180 days**; extendable by bank up to **270 days**; beyond 270 days on commercial rates | Up to **9 months (270 days)** from date of shipment for realization under FEMA regulations |
| Liquidation / Repayment | Liquidated out of export proceeds, negotiation of export bills, or conversion into Post-Shipment Credit | Liquidated strictly upon realization of export proceeds in foreign exchange via banking channels |
| Concessional Interest Cap | Eligible under Interest Equalization Scheme (IES) for MSME manufacturer exporters | Eligible for concessional subvention / equalization up to regulatory realization limits |

## 2. Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (ECGC) Covers

ECGC provides credit insurance covers to banks and direct exporters to mitigate risks associated with cross-border trade:

• **1. Packing Credit Guarantee (WTPCG):** Protects banks against losses due to insolvency or default of the exporter in repaying pre-shipment advances.
• **2. Post-Shipment Export Credit Guarantee (WTPSG):** Protects banks against non-realization of export bills discounted/purchased.
• **3. Standard Policy to Exporters:** Protects exporters against **Commercial Risks** (insolvency of buyer, protracted default, refusal to accept goods) and **Political Risks** (war, civil strife, import restrictions, exchange transfer blockage by foreign government).
• **Risks NOT Covered by ECGC:** Quality disputes, buyer's non-payment due to exporter's fault, foreign exchange fluctuations.

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on Export Finance:
1. **Liquidation of Packing Credit:** Packing credit **CANNOT be repaid with local cash/funds**. It MUST be liquidated from export proceeds or by converting into post-shipment bills.
2. **Export Proceeds Realization Deadline:** Under FEMA 1999 regulations, export proceeds must be realized within **9 months** from the date of shipment (15 months for warehouses established abroad).
3. **Gold Card Scheme:** Valid for **3 years** (renewable), issued to exporters with good track record of 3 years, offering 20% standby limits and faster processing within 25 days for fresh limits.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** What is the primary purpose of Pre-Shipment Export Credit (Packing Credit)?
• (A) Financing the customs duties on imported luxury consumer goods
• (B) Financing the procurement of raw materials, processing, manufacturing, and packing of export goods prior to shipment
• (C) Financing freight and insurance after cargo reaches the foreign port
• (D) Liquidating the importer's overdraft limit

**Q2:** Under RBI Export Credit guidelines, what is the normal maximum period for which Pre-Shipment Packing Credit in Rupees is granted without interest penalties?
• (A) 90 days
• (B) 180 days (extendable up to 270/360 days on merits)
• (C) 30 days
• (D) 3 years

**Q3:** What is the primary role of the Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India (ECGC)?
• (A) Providing direct working capital rupee term loans to exporters
• (B) Issuing insurance covers to Indian exporters and commercial banks against commercial and political risks of overseas buyers
• (C) Regulating foreign exchange rates under FEMA
• (D) Handling customs cargo clearance at seaports

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(B) Financing the procurement of raw materials, processing, manufacturing, and packing of export goods prior to shipment** — Packing credit funds the production cycle before export dispatch against a valid Letter of Credit or confirmed export order.

2. **(B) 180 days (extendable up to 270/360 days on merits)** — Standard concessional export credit period is up to 180 days, extendable by banks based on manufacturing lead times.

3. **(B) Issuing insurance covers to Indian exporters and commercial banks against commercial and political risks of overseas buyers** — ECGC protects exporters against buyer insolvency, protracted default, and political risks (war, foreign exchange transfer restrictions).

<details>
<summary>Distinguish between Whole Turnover Packing Credit (WTPC) and Individual Packing Credit Guarantee issued by ECGC.</summary>
Individual Packing Credit Guarantee covers a specific exporter’s credit facility; the bank selects accounts to insure and pays a higher premium. Whole Turnover Packing Credit Guarantee (WTPCG) covers the entire export packing credit portfolio of the bank across all eligible exporters; premium rates are lower, and the bank must submit regular turnover declarations and cannot cherry-pick risks.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the "Gold Card Scheme" for exporters introduced by the RBI?</summary>
A simplified credit scheme for creditworthy exporters with a track record of good performance: (1) In-principle revolving limit sanctioned for 3 years; (2) Automatic 20% standby ad hoc limit for sudden export orders; (3) Concessional interest rates; (4) Faster appraisal within 25 days for fresh limits and 15 days for renewals.
</details>
