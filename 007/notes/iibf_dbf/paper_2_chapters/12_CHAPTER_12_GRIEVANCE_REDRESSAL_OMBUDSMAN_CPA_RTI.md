# GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL, INTEGRATED OMBUDSMAN, CPA & RTI

The customer protection architecture in banking operates across a tiered statutory pyramid: internal grievance machinery, the Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021 (RB-IOS), consumer dispute tribunals under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, and transparency obligations under the Right to Information Act, 2005.

---

## § 12.1 Unit 19: The Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 19)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Launched on 12 November 2021, RB-IOS integrates the earlier Banking Ombudsman Scheme (2006), Ombudsman for NBFCs (2018), and Ombudsman for Digital Transactions (2019) into a single unified 'One Nation One Ombudsman' framework.

### 1. Master Operational Architecture of RB-IOS, 2021

| Operational Dimension | Statutory Requirement & Parameter | Key Legal / Procedural Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Covered Regulated Entities (REs)** | All Commercial Banks, RRBs, Scheduled UCBs, Non-Scheduled UCBs (deposits $\ge$ ₹50 Cr), NBFCs (assets $\ge$ ₹100 Cr with customer interface), and System Participants. | Excludes Housing Finance Companies (HFCs under NHB) and Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS). |
| **Prerequisite for Filing Complaint** | Complainant must **first lodge complaint with the bank**. | Complainant can approach Ombudsman only if: (i) Bank rejects complaint, (ii) Complainant is unsatisfied, or (iii) **Bank fails to reply within 30 days**. |
| **Limitation Window** | Within **1 year** from receipt of rejection/reply from bank; or within **1 year and 30 days** of filing if bank gave no reply. | Absolute outer ceiling: Within **2 years** of the cause of action. |
| **Centralized Receipt & Processing** | Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (**CRPC**) established at **RBI, Chandigarh**. | Physical complaints sent to CRPC Chandigarh; digital complaints via CMS portal. |
| **Financial Compensation Limits** | • **Direct Loss:** Up to **₹20 Lakh** (₹2,000,000) for consequential loss.<br>• **Mental Agony / Harassment:** Additional compensation up to **₹1 Lakh** (₹100,000) for loss of time and harassment. | Separate from credit card dispute reversals. Award binding on bank unless appealed. |
| **Appellate Authority** | **Executive Director in charge of Consumer Education and Protection Department (CEPD), RBI**. | Appeal must be filed within **30 days** of receipt of award/decision. |

## § 12.2 Unit 20: The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (CPA 2019)

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 replaced the 1986 Act, expanding consumer rights to digital commerce and direct banking services:
• **Banking as a Service:** Section 2(42) explicitly defines 'service' to include **banking, financing, insurance, transport, and processing of energy**.
• **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA):** Established under Section 10 to regulate false advertisements, unfair trade practices, and consumer rights violations as an enforcement watchdog.
• **Three-Tier Adjudication Pecuniary Jurisdiction (Revised Thresholds):**
  1. **District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission:** Entertains complaints where value of goods/services paid as consideration **does not exceed ₹50 Lakh**.
  2. **State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission:** Consideration value **exceeds ₹50 Lakh but does not exceed ₹2 Crore**.
  3. **National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC):** Consideration value **exceeds ₹2 Crore**.
• **Product Liability & Mediation:** Chapter V establishes statutory **Mediation Cells** attached to Consumer Commissions for fast consensual settlement without appeal.

## § 12.3 Unit 21: The Right to Information Act, 2005 (RTI Act)

• **Applicability to Banks:** Applies to all **Public Authorities** (all Public Sector Banks, Regional Rural Banks, RBI, and government-owned financial institutions). Private sector commercial banks are **NOT public authorities** under Section 2(h) of the RTI Act (though information accessible to RBI can be requisitioned through RBI subject to statutory exemptions).
• **Statutory Timelines for Disposal:**
  - Standard Request: Central Public Information Officer (CPIO) must reply within **30 days** of receipt.
  - Life or Liberty: If information concerns the **life or liberty of a person**, information must be provided within **48 hours**!
  - Third-party information: 40 days (notice to third party within 5 days).
• **Section 8 Exemptions (Non-Disclosure):**
  - Section 8(1)(d): Commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property.
  - Section 8(1)(e): Information available to a person in his **fiduciary capacity** (protects customer account balances and personal credit records).
  - Section 8(1)(j): Personal information having no relationship to public activity or interest.
• **Overriding Effect (Section 22):** RTI Act overrides the Official Secrets Act 1923 and any inconsistent law.

## § 12.4 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. Under the Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021 (RB-IOS), what is the maximum financial compensation that the Ombudsman can award for direct consequential loss suffered by a complainant?**  
A. ₹10 Lakh  
B. ₹20 Lakh  
C. ₹50 Lakh  
D. ₹1 Crore  

**Q2. Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, which judicial forum has original pecuniary jurisdiction to entertain a consumer complaint where the value of banking consideration paid does not exceed ₹50 Lakh?**  
A. District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission  
B. State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission  
C. National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission  
D. Lok Adalat  

**Q3. Under the Right to Information Act, 2005, what is the mandatory statutory timeframe within which a Central Public Information Officer (CPIO) of a Public Sector Bank must provide information if the request concerns the life or liberty of an individual?**  
A. Within 24 hours  
B. Within 48 hours  
C. Within 7 days  
D. Within 30 days

## § 12.5 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under RB-IOS 2021, the Ombudsman has the statutory authority to award compensation up to **₹20 Lakh** for actual direct consequential loss suffered by the complainant, plus up to **₹1 Lakh** for loss of complainant's time, expenses, and harassment.  
• **Q2 — Answer: A.** Under the revised pecuniary thresholds of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, the **District Commission** has jurisdiction for consideration values up to **₹50 Lakh**. The State Commission handles values between ₹50 Lakh and ₹2 Crore, and the National Commission handles values exceeding ₹2 Crore.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** While standard RTI requests have a disposal timeframe of 30 days, Section 7(1) of the RTI Act mandates that if the information sought concerns the **life or liberty of a person**, the CPIO must provide it within **48 hours** of receiving the application.
