# CREDIT APPRAISAL, ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGIES & WORKING CAPITAL MATH

Credit appraisal is the rigorous financial, technical, and managerial evaluation of a borrower's creditworthiness and repayment capability. The operational methodology varies between working capital financing (evaluated on operating cash cycles and current asset flows) and term loan assessment (evaluated on long-term project viability, debt-service capacity, and capital gearing).

## § 14.1 Unit 23: Credit Appraisal Techniques & Financial Ratios

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 23)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Credit evaluation integrates qualitative parameters (the 5 Cs of credit: Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, and Conditions) with quantitative balance sheet and cash flow analytics. Working capital math relies on the Nayak Committee Turnover Method and Tandon Committee MPBF formulas.

### 1. Working Capital Assessment Methodologies

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 WORKING CAPITAL ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGIES                    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. TURNOVER METHOD (NAYAK COMMITTEE - Limits up to ₹5 Crore):               │
│    • Total Working Capital Requirement (WCR) = 25% of Projected Turnover.  │
│    • Minimum Promoter Margin Contribution   =  5% of Projected Turnover.   │
│    • Maximum Permissible Bank Finance (MPBF) = 20% of Projected Turnover.  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. TANDON COMMITTEE MPBF FORMULAS (Limits > ₹5 Crore):                      │
│    • Working Capital Gap (WCG) = Current Assets (CA) - Current Liab (CL)    │
│      (excluding bank borrowings).                                           │
│    • METHOD I:                                                              │
│      MPBF = 0.75 × (CA - CL) = 75% of Working Capital Gap.                  │
│      Borrower margin contribution = 25% of WCG from long-term funds.       │
│      Results in minimum Current Ratio of 1.17 : 1.                          │
│    • METHOD II:                                                             │
│      MPBF = (0.75 × CA) - CL.                                               │
│      Borrower margin contribution = 25% of TOTAL Current Assets.            │
│      Results in minimum Current Ratio of 1.33 : 1.                          │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. CASH BUDGET METHOD:                                                      │
│    • Mandatory for seasonal industries (sugar, tea, construction, software)│
│    • Sanctioned limit varies across monthly projected cash inflows/outflows.│
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. Core Financial Ratios & Benchmark Thresholds

| Financial Ratio | Formula / Mathematical Definition | Appraisal Significance & Benchmark Guidelines |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Current Ratio (CR)** | $$\frac{\text{Current Assets (CA)}}{\text{Current Liabilities (CL)}}$$ | Measures short-term liquidity. Standard benchmark under Tandon Method II is **$\ge$ 1.33 : 1**. |
| **Quick Ratio (Acid Test)** | $$\frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventories}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$ | Measures immediate liquidity without relying on inventory sales. Ideal benchmark is **$\ge$ 1.0 : 1**. |
| **Debt-Equity Ratio (DER)** | $$\frac{\text{Total Long-Term Debt}}{\text{Tangible Net Worth (TNW)}}$$ | Evaluates financial leverage and solvency. Typical commercial benchmark is **$\le$ 2.0 : 1** (higher for infra). |
| **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)** | $$\frac{\text{Net Profit} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Term Loan Interest}}{\text{Term Loan Principal Repayment} + \text{Interest}}$$ | **Term Loan Appraisal Metric:** Measures ability to service debt from cash profits. **Common bank policy benchmark is 1.50 to 2.00** (*Note: Bank/policy dependent; not an RBI statutory requirement*). |

## § 14.2 Solved Appraisal Math & Numerical Drills

### Scenario 1: Nayak Committee Turnover Method
• **Problem:** An enterprise has an audited annual turnover of ₹8 Crore and projects a turnover of ₹10 Crore for the next financial year. What is the working capital requirement, margin, and maximum bank finance under the Nayak Committee method?
• **Solution:**
  1. $\text{Projected Turnover} = \text{₹10.00 Crore}$
  2. $\text{Total Working Capital Requirement (25% of turnover)} = 0.25 \times 10 = \text{₹2.50 Crore}$
  3. $\text{Borrower Margin (5% of turnover)} = 0.05 \times 10 = \text{₹0.50 Crore}$
  4. $\text{Maximum Permissible Bank Finance (20% of turnover)} = 0.20 \times 10 = \text{₹2.00 Crore}$

### Scenario 2: Tandon Committee Method II Computation
• **Problem:** A borrower presents the following financial estimates: Current Assets = ₹400 Lakh; Current Liabilities (other than bank borrowing) = ₹100 Lakh. Calculate MPBF under Tandon Method I and Method II.
• **Solution:**
  1. $\text{Working Capital Gap (WCG)} = CA - CL = 400 - 100 = \text{₹300 Lakh}$
  2. **Method I MPBF:** $0.75 \times (CA - CL) = 0.75 \times 300 = \text{₹225 Lakh}$
  3. **Method II MPBF:** $(0.75 \times CA) - CL = (0.75 \times 400) - 100 = 300 - 100 = \text{₹200 Lakh}$

## § 14.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. Under the Nayak Committee recommendations for financing working capital to MSE units with aggregate limits up to ₹5 Crore, what is the minimum percentage of projected annual turnover that a commercial bank is required to provide as fund-based working capital finance?**  
A. 15%  
B. 20%  
C. 25%  
D. 75%  

**Q2. Under Tandon Committee Method II of lending, what is the minimum Current Ratio that a borrowing enterprise must structurally achieve?**  
A. 1.00 : 1  
B. 1.17 : 1  
C. 1.33 : 1  
D. 2.00 : 1  

**Q3. In term loan evaluation, what is the Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR), and how is a ratio between 1.50 and 2.00 interpreted by lending bankers?**  
A. It is a mandatory statutory ratio prescribed under Section 24 of the BR Act  
B. It measures operational profitability against total current liabilities  
C. It is a widely accepted internal bank policy benchmark indicating comfortable cash accruals to service term loan debt obligations  
D. It represents the proportion of equity capital held by promoters

## § 14.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under the Nayak Committee Turnover Method, the total working capital requirement is estimated at 25% of projected turnover, with the **bank financing at least 20% of turnover** and the borrower contributing at least 5% as margin from long-term equity.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Under Tandon Method II, because the borrower must fund 25% of *total Current Assets* from long-term funds, the resulting Current Ratio mathematically equals: $\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{CA}{0.75 \times CA} = \frac{1}{0.75} \approx \mathbf{1.33 : 1}$. (Method I yields 1.17:1).  
• **Q3 — Answer: C.** DSCR measures the coverage of debt obligations (principal installment + interest) from cash accruals (PAT + depreciation + interest). A DSCR of **1.50 to 2.00** is a standard commercial policy benchmark indicating financial safety, though it is determined by individual bank credit policy rather than an RBI statutory floor.
