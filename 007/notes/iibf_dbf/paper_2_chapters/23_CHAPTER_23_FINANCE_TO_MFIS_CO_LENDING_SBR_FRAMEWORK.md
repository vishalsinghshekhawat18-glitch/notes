# FINANCE TO MFIS, CO-LENDING & SCALE-BASED REGULATION

The non-banking financial sector complements commercial banking by expanding financial inclusion to unbanked frontiers. Regulated under the RBI's Harmonized Regulatory Framework for Microfinance (2022 Directions), the Co-Lending Model (CLM), and the Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) architecture, banks and NBFCs collaborate through structured risk-sharing arrangements while subject to tiered regulatory oversight.

---

## § 23.1 Unit 38: Microfinance Regulatory Architecture (2022 Baseline)

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 38)**  
> **Core Proposition:** In April 2022, the Reserve Bank unified microfinance regulations across all lending entities (commercial banks, small finance banks, NBFC-MFIs, cooperative banks), replacing legacy criteria with a client-centric definition.

### 1. Master Microfinance Regulatory Directives (2022 Framework)

| Regulatory Parameter | Statutory Directive & Rule | Examination Invariant |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Household Income Ceiling** | A microfinance loan is defined as a **collateral-free loan** to a household having an **annual household income up to ₹3,00,000 (₹3 Lakh)**. | Applies across rural, semi-urban, and urban areas uniformly. Household = husband, wife, and unmarried children. |
| **Cap on Debt Repayment (FOIR)** | Total monthly debt repayment obligations of the household (across all lenders) **CANNOT exceed 50% of monthly household income**. | Assessed through Credit Information Companies (CICs) prior to sanction. |
| **Collateral Security** | Strictly **COLLATERAL-FREE**. | Lenders cannot demand physical, financial, or third-party collateral/guarantees. |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | **Strictly ZERO Prepayment Penalty**. | Borrowers can prepay at any time without fee or foreclosure charges. |
| **Margin Cap Elimination** | Earlier margin caps (10% / 12%) were **completely abolished**. | Replaced by a Board-approved, risk-based transparent pricing policy. |
| **Qualifying Assets for NBFC-MFIs** | Minimum **60% of total assets** (net of intangible assets) must consist of microfinance loans *(RBI circular dated 6 June 2025)*. | Reduced from the earlier 75% requirement (and historical 85% rule) to foster portfolio diversification and financial resilience. |

## § 23.2 Co-Lending Model (CLM) & Scale-Based Regulation (SBR)

### 1. Co-Lending Model (CLM) Framework

The RBI Co-Lending Model enables commercial banks and registered NBFCs (including Housing Finance Companies) to co-originate priority sector loans:
• **Mandatory Risk-Sharing Ratio:**
  - The NBFC **MUST retain a minimum of 20% share** of the individual loans on its own balance sheet.
  - The bank takes a **maximum of 80% share** of the loan.
• **Blended Interest Rate:** Borrower is charged a single blended interest rate reflecting the proportionate weighted average of the bank's and NBFC's interest rates.
• **Customer Interface:** The NBFC acts as the single point of interface for customer onboarding, documentation, loan servicing, and collections.
• **Escrow Account:** Loan disbursements and borrower repayments are routed strictly through a designated common escrow bank account.

### 2. Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) Framework for NBFCs

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE FOUR-TIER SCALE-BASED REGULATION (SBR)                  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. BASE LAYER (BL):                                                         │
│    • Non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size < ₹1,000 Crore.               │
│    • NBFC-Peer to Peer (P2P), NBFC-Account Aggregator (AA), NOF ₹10 Crore.  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. MIDDLE LAYER (ML):                                                       │
│    • ALL Deposit-taking NBFCs (NBFC-D), regardless of asset size.           │
│    • Non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size $\ge$ ₹1,000 Crore.           │
│    • Standalone Primary Dealers (SPDs), Core Investment Companies (CICs),   │
│      Infrastructure Debt Funds (IDFs), and Housing Finance Companies (HFCs).│
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. UPPER LAYER (UL):                                                        │
│    • NBFCs specifically identified by RBI based on systemic significance.   │
│    • MANDATORY INVARIANT: The TOP TEN (10) eligible NBFCs by asset size    │
│      shall ALWAYS reside in the Upper Layer!                                │
│    • Subject to bank-like capital requirements: CET1 ratio of at least 9%.  │
│    • Mandatory listing on stock exchanges within three (3) years.           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. TOP LAYER (TL):                                                          │
│    • Empty by default. Populated only if RBI determines that an Upper Layer │
│      NBFC exhibits extreme systemic risk / contagion potential.             │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## § 23.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. Under the Reserve Bank of India's Harmonized Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans (2022 Directions), what is the maximum annual household income ceiling prescribed for a borrower to qualify for a collateral-free microfinance loan?**  
A. ₹1,25,000 for rural / ₹2,00,000 for urban  
B. ₹2,00,000 across all areas  
C. ₹3,00,000 across all areas (rural, semi-urban, and urban)  
D. ₹5,00,000 for salaried households  

**Q2. Under the RBI Co-Lending Model (CLM) between scheduled commercial banks and NBFCs for priority sector lending, what is the mandatory minimum credit risk share that the partner NBFC must retain on its own balance sheet?**  
A. Minimum 5%  
B. Minimum 10%  
C. Minimum 20%  
D. Exactly 50%  

**Q3. Under the RBI Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) framework for Non-Banking Financial Companies, which of the following is a mandatory statutory rule governing the Upper Layer (NBFC-UL)?**  
A. Only government-owned NBFCs can be placed in the Upper Layer  
B. The Top 10 eligible NBFCs by asset size shall always be included in the Upper Layer  
C. Upper Layer NBFCs are exempt from Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital requirements  
D. Deposit-taking NBFCs are prohibited from entering the Upper Layer  

---

## § 23.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** Under the April 2022 RBI Microfinance Directions, the distinction between rural and urban limits was abolished. A microfinance loan is defined as a collateral-free loan to a household having an **annual income up to ₹3,00,000** across all geographic areas.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Under the Co-Lending Model (CLM), the partner NBFC must retain a **minimum of 20% share** of individual loans on its books, ensuring skin in the game, while the bank can fund up to 80%.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** Under RBI SBR guidelines, based on a comprehensive scoring methodology evaluating size, interconnectedness, and complexity, the **top 10 eligible NBFCs in terms of asset size are always mandated to be placed in the Upper Layer (NBFC-UL)**.
