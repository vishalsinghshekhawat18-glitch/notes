# OPERATIONAL DEPOSIT ACCOUNTS, INOPERATIVE ACCOUNTS & ATTACHMENT ORDERS

Deposit accounts represent the primary liabilities of a commercial bank, requiring meticulous operational discipline in interest calculation, tax deduction at source (TDS), handling inoperative accounts, and executing court-ordered attachments including Garnishee Orders and Income Tax Notices.

---

## § 5.1 Unit 05: Operational Aspects of Deposit Accounts

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 05)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Inoperative accounts and unclaimed deposits are subject to statutory transfer to the Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund under Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Attachment orders served by Civil Courts and Tax Authorities impose strict legal duties on bankers.

### 1. Inoperative Accounts & Unclaimed Deposits (DEA Fund Architecture)

• **Inoperative / Dormant Account Definition:**
  - An account is classified as **inoperative (dormant)** if there are **no customer-induced transactions for a continuous period of 2 years**.
  - Customer-induced transactions include cash deposit/withdrawal, cheque clearance, ATM/POS debit, Internet/Mobile banking transfer, and standing instructions.
  - Bank-induced transactions (interest credit, annual service charges debit) **DO NOT prevent an account from becoming inoperative**.
• **RBI Operational Safeguards on Inoperative Accounts:**
  - Banks **CANNOT levy any penal charges** for non-maintenance of minimum balance on inoperative accounts.
  - Savings account interest **must continue to be credited** regularly, regardless of inoperative status.
  - Reactivation of an inoperative account must be done **completely free of cost** upon fresh customer KYC verification.
• **Unclaimed Deposits & The DEA Fund (Section 26A, BR Act, 1949):**
  - Any deposit account remaining inoperative or unclaimed for **10 years or more** (or term deposits unclaimed for 10 years after maturity date) must be transferred to the **Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund** maintained by the Reserve Bank of India.
  - Balances must be transferred along with accrued interest by the end of the following month.
  - **UDGAM Portal (Unclaimed Deposits - Gateway to Access inforMation):** Centralized web portal launched by RBI allowing public to search for unclaimed deposits across multiple banks in one centralized window.
  - Depositors retain the statutory right to claim refund of their funds with interest from their bank at any time, which the bank settles and reclaims from the DEA Fund.

## § 5.2 Court Orders: Garnishee Orders vs. Income Tax Attachments

### 1. Master Comparative Framework

| Dimension | Garnishee Order (CPC Order XXI Rule 46) | Income Tax Attachment (IT Act Sec 226(3)) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Issuing Authority** | Competent Civil Court execution of money decree. | Tax Recovery Officer (TRO) / Assessing Officer. |
| **Procedural Stages** | Two-stage: Order Nisi (interim) & Order Absolute. | Single administrative attachment notice. |
| **Scope of Attachment** | Strictly debts **"due or accruing due"** at moment of service. Does NOT attach future credits. | Attaches money due **AND "money that may become due"** subsequently until tax debt is satisfied. |
| **Joint Account Operation** | Order against A alone **CANNOT attach joint account (A & B)**. | Attaches proportionate share of debtor unless proven otherwise. |
| **Sovereign Priority** | Subordinate to government tax attachments. | Priority as Crown Debt; takes precedence over civil Garnishee, **BUT cannot override prior pledge/lien**. |

### 2. Legal Scope: Debts "Due or Accruing Due"

• A Garnishee Order attaches only debts belonging to the judgment debtor that are either **due immediately** (payable on demand, e.g., current/savings balances) or **accruing due** (an existing obligation payable on a future date, e.g., Fixed Deposit maturing in 6 months).
• **Items NOT Attached by Garnishee Order:**
  - Cheques deposited by customer but not yet cleared/credited.
  - Money held by bank in trust or as a bailee.
  - Overdraft limits or unutilized credit limits (an undrawn loan limit is not a debt owed by bank to customer).
  - Subsequent credits received after the exact moment of service of the Order Nisi.

## § 5.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. An operative savings bank account has had zero customer-induced transactions since 1 January 2014. What is the mandatory statutory treatment for this deposit balance under Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949?**  
A. The entire balance is forfeited and appropriated as bank fee income  
B. The balance must be transferred to the Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund of the RBI after 10 years  
C. The bank must compulsorily credit the funds to the Prime Minister National Relief Fund  
D. The bank must file a civil suit before the Debt Recovery Tribunal  

**Q2. A bank branch is served with a Court Garnishee Order Nisi on 10 April at 11:30 AM against Mr. Sharma for ₹3,00,000. At that exact time, his savings account balance is ₹1,20,000. At 2:00 PM on the same day, Mr. Sharma deposits a cash sum of ₹2,00,000. How much money is legally attached under the Garnishee Order?**  
A. The full decreed amount of ₹3,00,000  
B. Exactly ₹1,20,000 (balance existing at the moment of service)  
C. ₹3,20,000 (total cumulative balance at end of day)  
D. Zero, because the funds were insufficient to meet the decree  

**Q3. Under Section 226(3) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, what is a key legal difference between an Income Tax Attachment Notice and a Civil Court Garnishee Order?**  
A. Income Tax Attachment cannot attach fixed deposits  
B. Income Tax Attachment attaches not only debts due but also money that may become due subsequently  
C. Garnishee Orders take absolute precedence over Income Tax attachments under all circumstances  
D. Income Tax notices require confirmation by the High Court before execution

## § 5.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, any deposit account remaining inoperative or unclaimed for **10 years or more** must be transferred to the **DEA Fund** maintained by RBI. Depositors can still reclaim their money anytime through the bank.  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** A Garnishee Order attaches strictly the debts *due or accruing due* at the **exact moment the order is served** on the bank. Credits received subsequent to service (the ₹2,00,000 deposited at 2:00 PM) are not attached by the order; the court must issue a fresh or supplementary order to attach subsequent deposits.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** Unlike a Garnishee Order which attaches only existing debts at the moment of service, an Income Tax Notice under Section 226(3) has a wider continuing scope: it attaches money due *and any money that may become due subsequently*, operating continuously until the tax liability is fully satisfied.
