# PAYING & COLLECTING BANKS & ANCILLARY BANKING SERVICES

The statutory protection afforded to paying and collecting bankers forms the operational foundation of cheque clearance under the Negotiable Instruments Act 1881. Ancillary banking encompasses demand drafts, electronic fund transfer networks (NEFT and RTGS), and agency services performed on behalf of governments and central institutions.

---

## § 10.1 Units 13 & 14: Statutory Protection to Paying & Collecting Bankers

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Units 13 & 14)**  
> **Core Proposition:** A paying banker has a statutory duty under Section 31 to honour cheques if drawn properly against sufficient funds. Protection is available under Sections 10, 85, 85A, and 89. Collecting bankers enjoy statutory protection under Section 131 if collecting crossed cheques in good faith and without negligence.

### 1. Protection to the Paying Banker

| Statutory Section | Instrument Type Covered | Conditions Required for Statutory Protection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 85(1)** | Order Cheques | Payment made in **due course** (Section 10); endorsement purports to be made by or under authority of payee. Bank not required to verify authenticity of payee endorsement. |
| **Section 85(2)** | Bearer Cheques | Payable to bearer; protected if paid in due course to the bearer, **notwithstanding any endorsement on the back**. *"Once a bearer cheque, always a bearer cheque."* |
| **Section 85A** | Bank Drafts | Draft drawn by one branch on another; protected if endorsement purports to be by or under authority of payee. |
| **Section 89** | Materially Altered Cheques | Protected if payment is made in good faith and without negligence, **provided the alteration is NOT apparent** on visual inspection. |
| **Section 128** | Crossed Cheques | Paying banker paying a crossed cheque in due course (to a bank for general crossing, or designated bank for special crossing) is deemed to have paid the true owner. |

> **Critical Legal Trap — Forgery of Drawer's Signature:**  
> The protection under Section 85 applies **ONLY to forged endorsements**. If the **drawer's signature is forged**, the mandate is void *ab initio*. A bank paying a cheque with a forged drawer signature has zero statutory protection and cannot debit the customer's account, regardless of how skillful the forgery was (*Bihta Cooperative Bank v. Bank of Bihar*).

### 2. Protection to the Collecting Banker (Section 131)

A collecting banker is an agent for collection and is liable under common law for conversion if collecting a stolen instrument. Section 131 grants statutory immunity provided four non-negotiable conditions are satisfied:
1. The cheque must be **crossed generally or specially** before it comes into the hands of the bank (bank cannot cross an uncrossed cheque to claim Section 131 protection).
2. The bank must collect the cheque as an **agent for a customer** (not as a holder for value).
3. The collection must be executed in **good faith**.
4. The collection must be executed **WITHOUT NEGLIGENCE**.
• **What Constitutes Negligence by Collecting Banker:**
  - Opening an account without proper KYC or address verification.
  - Collecting an 'Account Payee' crossed cheque for an account other than the named payee.
  - Crediting cheques drawn in favor of a company or partnership into the personal account of a director or partner without inquiry.
  - Failing to notice clear discrepancies in endorsements or amounts.

## § 10.2 Unit 15: Ancillary Banking Services & Electronic Payment Rails

### 1. Demand Drafts & Banker's Cheques

• **Demand Draft (DD):** An order drawn by one office of a bank upon another office of the same bank for payment of a specified sum to order.
  - Governed by Section 85A NI Act.
  - Validity is **3 months** from date of issue.
  - **Section 31, RBI Act Restriction:** Demand Drafts **CANNOT be issued payable to bearer on demand**. (Only RBI and Central Government can issue bearer demand instruments).
  - Drafts for **₹50,000 and above** cannot be issued against cash; must be funded through account debit.
• **Banker's Cheque (Pay Order):** Drawn on the issuing branch itself; payable locally within the clearing zone.

### 2. Electronic Remittance Systems: NEFT vs. RTGS

| Operational Feature | NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) | RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Settlement Architecture** | Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) in half-hourly batches (48 batches daily). | Real Time continuous gross settlement of each transaction individually. |
| **Operating Hours** | **24 x 7 x 365 days** (Round the clock). | **24 x 7 x 365 days** (Round the clock). |
| **Minimum Amount** | **No Minimum limit** (even ₹1 can be sent). | **MINIMUM ₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh)**. |
| **Maximum Amount** | No upper ceiling. | No upper ceiling. |
| **Indo-Nepal Scheme** | Supported up to ₹2 Lakh per transaction. | Not applicable. |
| **Beneficiary Credit TAT** | Next batch settlement (normally within 2 hours). | Real-time, within 30 minutes of settlement. |

## § 10.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. A commercial bank pays an order cheque presented at the counter where the drawer's signature was skillfully forged by an employee. The forgery could not be detected even under ultraviolet inspection. Can the bank claim statutory protection under Section 85(1) of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881?**  
A. Yes, because the payment was made in good faith and without negligence  
B. Yes, because ultraviolet inspection established due diligence  
C. No, Section 85 protects only against forged endorsements; a forged drawer signature makes the cheque void ab initio, and the bank cannot debit the customer  
D. No, unless the customer failed to maintain checkbook custody  

**Q2. Under Section 131 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, which of the following conditions is MANDATORY for a collecting banker to claim statutory protection against conversion?**  
A. The cheque must have been crossed prior to being received by the collecting bank  
B. The bank must have purchased the cheque as a holder for value  
C. The cheque must be drawn on a nationalized public sector bank  
D. The collecting bank must obtain an indemnity bond from the drawer  

**Q3. Under Reserve Bank of India directives, what is the statutory minimum transaction amount required to initiate an interbank fund transfer via Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS)?**  
A. ₹50,000  
B. ₹1,00,000  
C. ₹2,00,000  
D. ₹5,00,000

## § 10.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: C.** A paying banker's protection under Section 85(1) extends strictly to forged **endorsements**. A forged **drawer's signature** is a legal nullity—the bank has no mandate to pay. The bank cannot debit the customer's account under any circumstances, regardless of the skillfulness of the forgery.  
• **Q2 — Answer: A.** Section 131 protection applies strictly if the cheque was **already crossed generally or specially before** it reached the collecting banker's hands. If an open (uncrossed) cheque is deposited, crossing it after receipt does not confer Section 131 protection.  
• **Q3 — Answer: C.** The statutory minimum transaction threshold for RTGS is **₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh)** with no upper ceiling. Transactions below ₹2 Lakh must be routed through NEFT or IMPS.
