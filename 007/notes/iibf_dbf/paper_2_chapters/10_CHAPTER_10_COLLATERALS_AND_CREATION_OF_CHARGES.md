# COLLATERALS & CHARGES: PLEDGE, HYPOTHECATION, LIEN & MORTGAGES

Securing bank advances requires legally enforceable charges over tangible and intangible assets. Governed by the Indian Contract Act 1872, Transfer of Property Act 1882, and SARFAESI Act 2002, the five primary security charges—Pledge, Hypothecation, Mortgage, Lien, and Assignment—possess distinct possession, ownership, and enforcement attributes.

## § 10.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

Types of Collaterals & Modes of Creating Charges

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Charge on Securities**
> A charge is a legal right created by a borrower in favor of a lending bank over assets (movable, immovable, or actionable claims) to secure the repayment of financial advances.

## 1. Master Comparison Matrix of the 5 Primary Security Charges

| Charge Type | Nature of Asset | Physical Possession | Ownership Title | Governing Statute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pledge | Movable goods, Gold, Goods in warehouse | With the **Bank (Pawnee)** | With Borrower (Pawnor) | Section 172, Indian Contract Act 1872 |
| Hypothecation | Movable assets (Stock, Machinery, Vehicles) | With the **Borrower (Hypothecator)** | With Borrower | Section 2(1)(n), SARFAESI Act 2002 |
| Mortgage | Immovable property (Land, Buildings) | Constructive / Physical / Symbolic | With Mortgagor (transfers interest) | Section 58, Transfer of Property Act 1882 |
| Assignment | Actionable claims (LIC policies, Book debts, Supply bills) | Documentary assignment transferred to bank | Transferred to Bank (Assignee) | Section 130, Transfer of Property Act 1882 |
| Lien (General) | Securities, Commercial paper, Goods | With the **Bank** (in ordinary course of business) | With Customer | Section 171, Indian Contract Act 1872 |

## 2. The 6 Types of Mortgages (Section 58, Transfer of Property Act 1882)

| Mortgage Type | Section in TP Act | Creation Method & Formalities | Bank's Remedy / Sale Power without Court |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equitable Mortgage (Deposit of Title Deeds) | Section 58(f) | Delivery of original title deeds to the bank with intent to create security in notified towns. No stamp duty/registration needed in most states. | Enforceable via SARFAESI Act 2002 without court intervention; or via civil court decree for sale. |
| Simple Mortgage | Section 58(b) | Registered mortgage deed without delivery of possession. Mortgagor binds himself personally to pay the mortgage money. | Bank has no right to sell property without obtaining a Civil Court decree. |
| Mortgage by Conditional Sale | Section 58(c) | Ostensible sale of property with condition that sale becomes void on repayment, or becomes absolute on default. | Remedy is **Foreclosure** (court order debarring mortgagor from redeeming), NOT sale. |
| Usufructuary Mortgage | Section 58(d) | Delivery of possession to mortgagee who receives rents and profits in lieu of interest/principal. | Mortgagee cannot sue for sale or foreclosure; recovers debt strictly from property usufruct (rents/yields). |
| English Mortgage | Section 58(e) | Absolute transfer of property to mortgagee with agreement to re-transfer upon repayment on a fixed date. | Mortgagee has power to sell property without court intervention under Section 69 TP Act in specific conditions. |
| Anomalous Mortgage | Section 58(g) | A mortgage which is not any of the above 5 types (e.g. combination of simple and usufructuary mortgage). | Terms governed strictly by the custom and express terms in the mortgage deed. |

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on Collaterals & Charges:
1. **Pledge vs Hypothecation Possession:** In a Pledge, possession is **ALWAYS with the Bank**. In Hypothecation, possession is **ALWAYS with the Borrower**.
2. **Equitable Mortgage Requirements:** 3 essential requisites for a valid Equitable Mortgage under Sec 58(f): (a) a debt, (b) deposit of **ORIGINAL title deeds**, and (c) execution in a town notified by the State Government.
3. **Foreclosure Remedy:** The remedy of **Foreclosure** is available ONLY in *Mortgage by Conditional Sale* and certain *Anomalous Mortgages*. It is **NEVER** available in Simple or Equitable mortgages.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** Under Section 172 of the Indian Contract Act 1872, what constitutes the fundamental legal ingredient of a "Pledge"?
• (A) Transfer of ownership of goods to the bank without possession
• (B) Bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt with physical or constructive delivery of possession
• (C) Registration of charge with CERSAI without delivery
• (D) Assignment of actionable claims

**Q2:** Which type of mortgage under Section 58 of the Transfer of Property Act 1882 is created in notified towns by merely delivering title deeds of immovable property with intent to create a security?
• (A) Simple Mortgage
• (B) English Mortgage
• (C) Equitable Mortgage (Mortgage by Deposit of Title Deeds)
• (D) Usufructuary Mortgage

**Q3:** Under the SARFAESI Act 2002, which security charge over movable assets is defined as a charge without delivery of possession?
• (A) Pledge
• (B) Hypothecation
• (C) Banker’s Lien
• (D) Set-off

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(B) Bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt with physical or constructive delivery of possession** — Delivery of possession is the essential test of a pledge. Ownership remains with the pledgor, while special property and possession pass to the pledgee.

2. **(C) Equitable Mortgage (Mortgage by Deposit of Title Deeds)** — Section 58(f) T.P. Act allows mortgage by deposit of title deeds in specified towns without requiring registered mortgage deeds, saving stamp duty.

3. **(B) Hypothecation** — Defined in Section 2(1)(n) of SARFAESI Act as a charge created on movable property without delivery of possession of the property to the creditor.

<details>
<summary>What are the essential legal requirements for creating a valid Equitable Mortgage by deposit of title deeds under Section 58(f) of the T.P. Act?</summary>
Three indispensable requirements: (1) Existence of a debt; (2) Deposit of original documents of title to immovable property; (3) Intention that the deeds shall be security for the debt. Must be effected in towns specifically notified by the State Government.
