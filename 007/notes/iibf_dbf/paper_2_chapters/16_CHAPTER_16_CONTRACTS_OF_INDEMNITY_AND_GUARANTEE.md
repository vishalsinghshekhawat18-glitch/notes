# CONTRACTS OF INDEMNITY, GUARANTEE & BANK GUARANTEES

Contracts of indemnity and guarantee are core risk-mitigation structures in commercial banking. Governed by the Indian Contract Act 1872, they range from bilateral indemnity agreements to tripartite bank guarantees, deferred payment guarantees (DPGs), and performance bonds. Proper legal separation between validity period, claim invocation period, and the Law of Limitation is vital for assessing contingent liabilities.

---

## § 16.1 Units 30 & 31: Contracts of Indemnity vs. Guarantee

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Units 30 & 31)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Section 124 of the Indian Contract Act defines a Contract of Indemnity as a contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor or any other person. Section 126 defines a Contract of Guarantee as a contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default.

### 1. Master Comparative Framework

| Structural Feature | Contract of Indemnity (Section 124) | Contract of Guarantee (Section 126) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number of Parties** | **Two Parties:** Indemnifier (Promisor) and Indemnity Holder (Promisee). | **Three Parties:** Principal Debtor (Borrower), Creditor (Bank), Surety (Guarantor). |
| **Number of Contracts** | **Only One Contract** between Indemnifier and Indemnity Holder. | **Three Contracts:** (i) Creditor & Debtor, (ii) Creditor & Surety, (iii) Debtor & Surety (implied indemnity). |
| **Nature of Liability** | **Primary and Independent** liability; arises upon occurrence of designated loss. | **Secondary and Collateral** liability; arises only upon default of Principal Debtor. |
| **Co-Extensive Rule** | Not applicable. | **Co-extensive under Section 128:** Surety is liable to the same extent as principal debtor unless contract limits it. |
| **Right to Sue Third Parties** | Indemnifier cannot sue third parties in his own name without assignment. | Surety, upon paying the debt, **steps into the shoes of the creditor** (Right of Subrogation under Section 140). |

### 2. Discharge & Rights of a Surety

• **Continuing Guarantee (Section 129):** A guarantee extending to a series of transactions (e.g., Cash Credit limits).
  - **Revocation by Notice (Sec 130):** Surety can revoke future transactions at any time by giving notice to the creditor.
  - **Revocation by Death (Sec 131):** Death of surety revokes the guarantee regarding *future transactions* in the absence of a contract to the contrary (past transactions remain binding on the estate).
• **Discharge of Surety (Sections 133–139):**
  - **Variance in Terms (Sec 133):** Any material variance made in loan terms without surety's consent discharges the surety as to transactions subsequent to the variance.
  - **Release of Principal Debtor (Sec 134):** Discharges surety.
  - **Composition or Extension of Time (Sec 135):** If creditor contracts to give time to or not sue the principal debtor without surety's consent, surety is discharged.
  - **Impairment of Remedy (Sec 139):** If creditor does any act inconsistent with surety's rights or omits to preserve securities, surety is discharged to the extent of the value of securities lost.

## § 16.2 Unit 31b & 33: Bank Guarantees & Section 28 Claim Period Doctrine

> **P0 Audit Fix — Stop-Ship Regulatory Clearance (C-04)**  
> Disentangling Bank Guarantee Validity, Claim Period, and Statutory Limitation:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE THREE TIERS OF BANK GUARANTEE LIFECYCLE                 │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. VALIDITY PERIOD (EXPIRY DATE):                                           │
│    • The period during which the guaranteed performance must be completed   │

├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. CLAIM INVOCATION PERIOD:                                                 │
│    • The agreed grace period following the validity date within which the   │

│    • Exception 3 to Section 28 of the Indian Contract Act: Permits banks to │

│ 3. STATUTORY LIMITATION PERIOD (LIMITATION ACT, 1963):                      │
│    • The timeframe within which a beneficiary can file a legal suit in court│
│      against the bank after a timely claim is wrongfully rejected:          │
│      - For Private Commercial Beneficiaries: THREE (3) YEARS.               │
│      - For Government / Sovereign Departments: THIRTY (30) YEARS.           │

```

### 3. Classification of Bank Guarantees

• **Financial Guarantee:** Direct financial undertaking that customer will meet monetary obligations (e.g., advance payment guarantee, bid bond, tax duty payment).
• **Performance Guarantee:** Undertaking that customer will successfully execute an engineering or commercial contract. Invoked if work is defective or delayed.
• **Deferred Payment Guarantee (DPG):** Undertaking issued on behalf of an importer/purchaser of capital goods guaranteeing payment of term installments spread over several years.

## § 16.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. Under Section 128 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, what is the exact nature of the liability of a personal surety (guarantor) vis-à-vis the principal debtor?**  
A. The surety's liability is purely secondary and conditional upon the creditor first exhausting all legal remedies against the principal debtor  
B. The surety's liability is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor, meaning the creditor can proceed directly against the surety upon default without suing the debtor first  
C. The surety is liable only for 50% of the outstanding debt  
D. The surety's liability terminates automatically after one year from execution  

**Q2. Under Exception 3 to Section 28 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, what is the minimum claim invocation period that a bank can legally stipulate in a bank guarantee to validly extinguish the beneficiary's right to lodge a demand?**  
A. Minimum 30 days  
B. Minimum 6 months  
C. Not less than one (1) year  
D. Exactly 3 years  

**Q3. If a creditor bank grants a binding formal extension of repayment time to a borrowing company without the knowledge or written consent of the continuing guarantor, what is the legal consequence on the guarantor's liability under Section 135 of the Indian Contract Act?**  
A. The guarantor's liability is doubled as penalty  
B. The guarantor is completely discharged from liability  
C. The guarantor remains bound under the doctrine of severability  
D. The guarantor can be sued only after the extended period expires

## § 16.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under Section 128, the liability of the surety is **co-extensive** with that of the principal debtor unless contractually limited. The creditor bank is under no legal obligation to exhaust remedies or obtain an execution against the borrower before proceeding against the guarantor (*Bank of Bihar v. Damodar Prasad*).  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Exception 3 to Section 28 of the Indian Contract Act permits bank guarantees to contain an extinguishment clause, provided the period for lodging a claim is **not less than one (1) year** from the date of occurrence of the specified event/expiry.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** Under Section 135, an agreement between the creditor and the principal debtor to give time or compound the debt without the surety's consent **discharges the surety**, because it impairs the surety's eventual remedy against the debtor.
