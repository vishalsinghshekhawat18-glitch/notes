# LETTERS OF CREDIT (LC), UCPDC 600 & BILL FINANCE

Documentary Letters of Credit (LCs) and commercial bill discounting represent the lifeblood of international and domestic trade finance. Governed globally by the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCPDC 600) and domestically by the Negotiable Instruments Act 1881, these instruments substitute a commercial buyer's credit risk with the irrevocable payment commitment of an issuing bank.

---

## § 17.1 Unit 32: Letters of Credit & UCPDC 600 Rules

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 32)**  
> **Core Proposition:** A Letter of Credit is an irrevocable commitment by an Issuing Bank to pay the Beneficiary against presentation of strictly conforming documents within the prescribed validity period. Under Article 4 of UCPDC 600, banks deal with documents and not with goods, services, or performance.

### 1. Key Parties to a Letter of Credit

| Party | Legal Definition & Operational Function | Primary Liability / Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Applicant (Buyer / Importer)** | The party on whose request the credit is issued. | Obligated to reimburse issuing bank upon presentation of conforming documents. |
| **Issuing Bank (Opening Bank)** | The bank that issues the credit at request of applicant. | **Primary, independent liability** to pay beneficiary upon compliant presentation. |
| **Beneficiary (Seller / Exporter)** | The party in whose favor the credit is issued. | Entitled to receive payment upon presenting strictly conforming documents. |
| **Advising Bank** | Bank in exporter's country authenticating and forwarding the LC. | Verifies apparent authenticity of credit; assumes **zero payment commitment**. |
| **Confirming Bank** | Bank adding its own irrevocable guarantee to the LC at request of issuing bank. | Assumes **same primary liability as issuing bank**; pays even if issuing bank fails. |
| **Nominated / Negotiating Bank** | Bank authorized by issuing bank to pay, accept drafts, or negotiate documents. | Examines documents; advances funds to beneficiary under authorization. |

### 2. Specialized Types of Letters of Credit

• **Revocable vs. Irrevocable:** Under Article 3 of UCPDC 600, **all credits are deemed to be IRREVOCABLE** even if there is no indication to that effect. (Revocable LCs are obsolete).
• **Confirmed Credit:** Gives beneficiary double security (Issuing Bank + Confirming Bank).
• **Transferable Credit:** Can be made available by the beneficiary (first beneficiary) to one or more second beneficiaries (suppliers) in whole or in part (permitted only if expressly marked 'Transferable').
• **Back-to-Back LC:** Second separate credit opened by beneficiary's bank on the security of the original master credit.
• **Standby Letter of Credit (SBLC):** Governed by UCPDC 600 or ISP98. Operates like a bank guarantee; drawn upon only in the event of default/non-performance by the applicant.
• **Red Clause LC:** Contains a special clause (historically in red ink) authorizing the nominated bank to make **pre-shipment clean advances** to the beneficiary for purchasing raw materials before presenting export shipping documents.
• **Green Clause LC:** Permits pre-shipment advances plus **storage and warehousing charges** at the port of shipment against warehouse receipts.

### 3. Key Articles of UCPDC 600

• **Article 14(b) — The 5 Banking Days Rule:** The issuing bank, confirming bank, and nominated bank shall each have a **maximum of five (5) banking days** following the day of presentation to determine if a presentation is compliant.
• **Doctrine of Strict Compliance:** Documents presented must strictly conform to the terms of the credit and UCPDC standards. If invoices describe goods differently than the credit, the bank must reject (*Equitable Trust Co. v. Dawson Partners*).
• **Article 34 — Disclaimer on Documents:** Banks assume no liability or responsibility for the form, sufficiency, accuracy, genuineness, falsification, or legal effect of any document, or for the condition, quantity, or existence of the goods represented thereby.

## § 17.2 Unit 34: Laws Relating to Bill Finance

• **Classification of Commercial Bills:**
  - **Demand Bills:** Payable on demand/sight without grace days. Documents handed over strictly on payment (**Documents against Payment - D/P**).
  - **Usance Bills:** Payable after a specified tenor (e.g., 60 or 90 days sight) plus 3 days of grace. Documents handed over upon acceptance of the bill by the drawee (**Documents against Acceptance - D/A**).
• **Bank's Remedies in Bill Discounting:** When a bank discounts a bill, it becomes a **Holder in Due Course (HIDC)**. If the drawee dishonours the bill, the bank has the legal right to recover the full amount with interest from the drawer (its customer) and all prior endorsers.

## § 17.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. Under Article 14(b) of the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCPDC 600), what is the maximum timeframe allowed to an issuing bank or confirming bank to examine presented documents and determine whether to accept or refuse them?**  
A. 48 hours from receipt  
B. Maximum five (5) banking days following the day of presentation  
C. Seven (7) calendar days  
D. Thirty (30) days  

**Q2. Under UCPDC 600, if a documentary credit does not explicitly state whether it is revocable or irrevocable, how is the credit legally classified?**  
A. It is legally classified as Revocable  
B. It is deemed to be Irrevocable under Article 3  
C. It is void for uncertainty  
D. It is treated as an ordinary commercial guarantee  

**Q3. Which specialized Letter of Credit allows an exporter to receive an advance of funds from the negotiating bank prior to shipment of goods specifically to cover raw material procurement and warehousing/storage charges at the port?**  
A. Red Clause Letter of Credit  
B. Green Clause Letter of Credit  
C. Transferable Letter of Credit  
D. Revolving Letter of Credit  

---

## § 17.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Article 14(b) of UCPDC 600 explicitly provides that the issuing bank, confirming bank, and nominated bank shall each have a **maximum of five banking days** following the day of presentation to examine documents and communicate acceptance or notice of refusal.  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** Under Article 3 of UCPDC 600, a credit is **irrevocable even if there is no indication to that effect** on the document. The concept of a revocable credit has been entirely removed from standard international trade practice.  
• **Q3 — Answer: B.** A **Green Clause LC** permits pre-shipment advances for manufacturing plus payment for **warehousing, storage, and insurance** at the port of shipment against warehouse receipts. (A Red Clause LC provides pre-shipment advances for purchase/manufacture only, without warehousing cover).
