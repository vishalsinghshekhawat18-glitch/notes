# MANDATES, POWER OF ATTORNEY & COURT ORDERS (GARNISHEE / ATTACHMENT)

Bankers frequently encounter delegated authorities (Mandates and Powers of Attorney) and judicial interventions impounding customer balances. Executing Garnishee Orders issued under Order XXI Rule 46 of the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC) 1908 and Attachment Orders issued by tax authorities requires strict adherence to legal priorities and claim settlement protocols.

## § 5.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

Mandate, Power of Attorney & Legal Orders (Garnishee vs Attachment)

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Third-Party Operational Authorities & Court Orders**
> Banks frequently receive third-party operational authorizations or statutory attachment orders that freeze or direct the payment of customer funds.

## 1. Deep Comparison: Garnishee Order vs Attachment Order

| Parameter | Garnishee Order (Order 21 Rule 46 CPC) | Income Tax Attachment Order (Sec 226(3) IT Act) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issuing Authority | Civil Court in execution of decree | Income Tax Officer / Tax Recovery Officer (TRO) |
| Two-Stage Process | Yes: (1) **Order Nisi** (Freeze/Show-cause) followed by (2) **Order Absolute** (Payment direction) | No: Single direct order directing immediate remittance to Govt treasury |
| Application to Future Credits |  **No.** Attaches only funds existing *at the exact moment and date of service* of the order. |  **Yes.** Attaches current credit balance **AND all subsequent future credits** until the full tax demand is cleared. |
| Joint Accounts Rule | Cannot attach joint account (A & B) for individual debt of A unless specified in order | Can attach proportionate share of joint holder (50% presumed unless specified) |
| Priority of Orders | Subordinate to Income Tax Attachment Orders | Enjoys **Statutory Priority** (Crown Debt) over Civil Court Garnishee Orders |
| Effect on Un-cleared Cheques | Does not attach cheques deposited but not yet cleared/credited | Attaches proceeds as soon as they clear into the account |
| Right of Set-off by Bank | Bank can exercise prior right of set-off before calculating attachable balance | Bank can exercise set-off for dues already due and payable before order date |

## 2. Deceased Account Claim Settlement Framework

Under RBI Master Directions on Customer Service, banks follow structured protocols to settle claims of deceased depositors without causing undue hardship to survivors:

• **1. Accounts with Valid Nomination / Survivorship Mandate:** Claim settled in favor of the nominee or surviving joint holder within **15 days** of receipt of death certificate and KYC proof, without requiring legal representation (Probate, Letters of Administration, or Succession Certificate).
• **2. Accounts without Nomination / Survivorship (Below Threshold Limit):** Banks settle claims based on simple Indemnity Bond, Letter of Disclaimer from other legal heirs, and KYC proof up to board-approved threshold limits (typically ₹1 Lakh to ₹5 Lakhs).
• **3. Accounts with Substantial Balances & Disputes:** Legal representation (Succession Certificate under Indian Succession Act 1925) is mandatory.
• **4. Safe Deposit Lockers of Deceased:** Nominee is allowed to open the locker and inventory the contents in the presence of 2 independent witnesses; nominee receives contents as custodian for legal heirs.

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on Legal Orders:
1. **Future Deposits in Garnishee Order:** A deposit made in the afternoon *after* a Garnishee Order Nisi was served in the morning is **NOT** attached. The bank must open a separate new account for subsequent transactions.
2. **IT Order Priority:** If both a Garnishee Order and an IT Attachment Order arrive, the **Income Tax Attachment Order takes absolute precedence**.
3. **Deceased Account with Mandate / PoA:** A Mandate or Power of Attorney terminates **immediately** on the death, insanity, or insolvency of the principal. Any cheque signed by a PoA holder presented after the principal's death must be returned with remark 'Drawer Deceased'.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** A Garnishee Order Nisi served upon a bank attaches which of the following funds?
• (A) Debts due or accruing due at the exact moment of service of the order
• (B) Deposits credited to the account on subsequent days after service
• (C) Safe custody packets containing gold ornaments
• (D) Shares held in the customer's demat account

**Q2:** How does an Income Tax Attachment Order issued under Section 226(3) of the Income Tax Act 1961 fundamentally differ from a civil court Garnishee Order?
• (A) IT Attachment Order cannot attach fixed deposits
• (B) IT Attachment Order attaches both present debts and subsequent credits coming into the account after service
• (C) Garnishee orders carry higher legal priority than tax attachment orders
• (D) IT Attachment Order applies only to joint accounts

**Q3:** Under RBI guidelines on settlement of claims in deceased accounts without nomination or legal representation, up to what maximum threshold are banks encouraged to fix simplified claims procedures against indemnity?
• (A) ₹50,000
• (B) ₹1,00,000
• (C) ₹5,00,000 (or board-approved limits)
• (D) ₹10,00,000

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(A) Debts due or accruing due at the exact moment of service of the order** — A Garnishee Order attaches only funds existing as a debt due or accruing due at the time of service; it does not attach subsequent deposits.

2. **(B) IT Attachment Order attaches both present debts and subsequent credits coming into the account after service** — Unlike a Garnishee Order, an IT Attachment Order under Section 226(3) is continuous and attaches subsequent deposits until the tax liability is fully satisfied.

3. **(C) ₹5,00,000 (or board-approved limits)** — RBI mandates banks to formulate board-approved threshold limits (commonly ₹1 Lakh to ₹5 Lakh) for fast settlement of deceased claims without demanding succession certificates.

## § 5.2 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

<details>
<summary>Distinguish between Garnishee Order Nisi and Garnishee Order Absolute.</summary>
Garnishee Order Nisi is an interim order directing the bank (garnishee) to freeze the judgment debtor’s funds and appear in court to show cause why the funds should not be paid to the judgment creditor. Garnishee Order Absolute is the final judicial decree issued after the hearing, directing the bank to directly pay the attached sum to the court or judgment creditor.
</details>

<details>
<summary>State the rule governing applicability of a Garnishee Order against a Joint Account where only one accountholder is the judgment debtor.</summary>
A Garnishee Order issued against an individual judgment debtor CANNOT attach a joint account held by the debtor along with other innocent joint holders, because the debt is joint whereas the liability is individual (unless the order explicitly names all joint holders or the joint mandate establishes individual ownership of funds).
</details>
