# NEGOTIABLE INSTRUMENTS ACT 1881 & MODERN CTS CLEARING

The Negotiable Instruments Act 1881 governs cheques, promissory notes, and bills of exchange. Its statutory provisions protect holders in due course, define crossing and material alteration, impose criminal liability for cheque dishonour under Section 138, and interface with modern Cheque Truncation System (CTS) continuous clearing networks.

---

## § 9.1 Unit 12: Statutory Framework of Negotiable Instruments

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Unit 12)**  
> **Core Proposition:** A negotiable instrument is a transferable document entitling the holder to an unconditional sum of money. Section 13 defines it as a promissory note, bill of exchange, or cheque payable either to order or to bearer.

### 1. The Triad of Negotiable Instruments

| Statutory Feature | Promissory Note (Section 4) | Bill of Exchange (Section 5) | Cheque (Section 6) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number of Parties** | 2 (Maker and Payee) | 3 (Drawer, Drawee, Payee) | 3 (Drawer, Drawee Bank, Payee) |
| **Nature of Undertaking** | **Unconditional Promise** to pay. | **Unconditional Order** to pay. | **Unconditional Order** on a bank. |
| **Drawee Entity** | Maker himself is debtor. | Any person or corporate body. | Must **ALWAYS be a specified Bank**. |
| **Payable on Demand / Usance** | Can be on demand or usance. | Can be on demand or usance. | **ALWAYS payable on demand only**. |
| **Acceptance Required** | No acceptance required. | Must be accepted by Drawee. | No acceptance required. |
| **Grace Days** | 3 days grace for usance notes. | 3 days grace for usance bills. | **ZERO grace days**. |
| **Stamping** | Required under Stamp Act. | Required under Stamp Act. | **Statutorily EXEMPT from stamp duty**. |

### 2. Holder (Section 8) vs. Holder in Due Course (Section 9)

• **Holder (Sec 8):** Any person entitled in his own name to the possession of the instrument and to receive or recover the amount due thereon.
• **Holder in Due Course (HIDC - Sec 9):** A person who for **valuable consideration** became the possessor/payee/endorsee of an instrument:
  1. **Before maturity**.
  2. In **good faith** and without sufficient cause to believe that any defect existed in the title of the person from whom he derived it.
• **Privileges of HIDC (Section 36, 42, 43, 58):**
  - Defect in prior title does not affect HIDC: *"A holder in due course cleanses the instrument of all prior defects."*
  - Estoppel against denying original validity of instrument.
  - Can recover against all prior parties until the instrument is discharged.

### 3. Crossing & Material Alteration (Sections 123–131, 87)

• **General Crossing (Sec 123):** Two parallel transverse lines across the face, with or without "and Company". Mandates payment **only through a bank**.
• **Special Crossing (Sec 124):** Name of a banker added across the face. Payable **only through the designated bank**.
• **Not Negotiable Crossing (Sec 130):** The person taking it **shall not have and cannot give a better title than that which the person from whom he took it had**. Transferability remains, but negotiability (title cleansing) is destroyed!
• **Material Alteration (Sec 87):** Any alteration that changes the legal character, date, sum payable, time, place of payment, or rate of interest. **Renders the instrument void** unless made with the consent of all parties.

### 4. Criminal Liability for Dishonour: Section 138 Framework

• **Operative Conditions:**
  1. Cheque presented within validity period (3 months from date of issue).
  2. Dishonoured due to **insufficiency of funds** or exceeds arrangement.
  3. Payee makes a demand for payment by giving a **written notice within 30 days** of receiving information/memo of dishonour from the bank.
  4. Drawer fails to make payment within **15 days** of receipt of notice.
  5. Complaint must be filed within **1 month** before the Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate First Class.
• **Statutory Penalties:** Imprisonment for a term which may extend to **two (2) years**, or with fine which may extend to **twice the amount of the cheque**, or with both.

## § 9.2 Unit 06a: Cheque Truncation System (CTS) & Continuous Clearing

> **P0 Audit Fix — Stop-Ship Regulatory Clearance (C-05)**  
> The Cheque Truncation System (CTS) substitutes the physical movement of cheques with digital images and MICR data.

• **Transition to Continuous Clearing:**
  - Historically, CTS-2010 operated on fixed multi-hour batch clearing windows across three national grids (Northern, Western, Southern).
  - Under current RBI directions, CTS has transitioned towards **continuous clearing with on-realisation settlement**, shortening the clearing turnaround time from days to a continuous intra-day processing cycle.
• **Positive Pay System (PPS):**
  - Operational safeguard introduced by RBI for high-value cheques:
  - Account holder electronically re-confirms key cheque details (date, payee name, amount) before issuance.
  - Recommended for cheques **$\ge$ ₹50,000**; mandatory under bank discretion for cheques **$\ge$ ₹5 Lakh**.
  - Cheques not registered under PPS are excluded from dispute resolution mechanisms.

## § 9.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. An instrument bears two parallel transverse lines across its face with the specific words 'Not Negotiable' written between them. What is the precise legal consequence of this crossing under Section 130 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881?**  
A. The cheque cannot be transferred to any other person and must be credited to payee only  
B. The cheque remains transferable, but no transferee can acquire a better title than that possessed by the transferor  
C. The cheque becomes void ab initio  
D. The paying bank is relieved of all statutory duties under Section 85  

**Q2. Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, what is the statutory time window within which a payee must send a formal legal demand notice to the drawer after receiving the bank memo of dishonour?**  
A. Within 7 days  
B. Within 15 days  
C. Within 30 days  
D. Within 60 days  

**Q3. Which of the following alterations on a crossed cheque is legally recognized as a 'Material Alteration' that renders the instrument void under Section 87 if done without the drawer's consent?**  
A. Converting a blank endorsement into a special endorsement  
B. Converting a General Crossing into a Special Crossing  
C. Altering the date from 10 March to 10 April to extend validity  
D. Writing 'Account Payee' within a general crossing  

---

## § 9.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under Section 130 of the NI Act, a 'Not Negotiable' crossing does **not restrict transferability**; it restricts negotiability. The holder cannot acquire a title better than the transferor's title. If the cheque was stolen or obtained by fraud, no subsequent holder can claim the privileged status of a Holder in Due Course.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** Under Section 138(b), the payee must give a written notice demanding payment within **30 days** of receiving information/memo of dishonour from the bank. The drawer is then given 15 days to pay before a complaint can be filed.  
• **Q3 — Answer: C.** Altering the date, amount, payee name, or time/place of payment changes the fundamental legal liability of the parties and is a **Material Alteration** under Section 87, voiding the instrument. Converting general crossing to special crossing or writing Account Payee are expressly authorized by statute and do not invalidate the cheque.
