# FOREIGN EXCHANGE REMITTANCES, NRI ACCOUNTS & FEMA ARCHITECTURE

Foreign exchange operations in India are regulated under the Foreign Exchange Management Act 1999 (FEMA), which replaced the restrictive FERA regime. Indian foreign exchange management focuses on facilitating external trade and payments while promoting orderly forex market development. Banking operations encompass inward and outward remittances under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), specialized NRI account structures, and resident foreign currency mechanisms.

---

## § 7.1 Unit 08 & 10: FEMA Framework & Remittance Facilities

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Units 08 & 10)**  
> **Core Proposition:** FEMA Section 5 mandates full convertibility on Current Account transactions (trade in goods/services, travel, education), subject to reasonable restrictions under Current Account Rules 2000. Capital Account transactions (Section 6) are prohibited unless expressly permitted by RBI.

### 1. The Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)

• **Eligibility:** Resident Individuals (including minors, with guardian approval). Not available to Corporates, Partnership Firms, HUFs, or Trusts.
• **Permissible Limit:** Up to **USD 250,000 per financial year (April – March)** for any permitted current or capital account transaction.
• **Permitted Purposes:**
  - Private visits abroad (all countries except Nepal and Bhutan).
  - Gift or donation to a person residing outside India.
  - Going abroad for employment, emigration, maintenance of close relatives.
  - Business travel, medical treatment, studies abroad.
  - Opening foreign currency accounts abroad, purchasing property or shares abroad.
• **Prohibited Remittances:** Trading in foreign exchange abroad, purchase of lottery tickets, remittance for sweepstakes or banned magazines, margins for overseas derivatives trading.

### 2. Resident Foreign Currency Accounts

| Account Type | Eligible Depositor | Key Permitted Inflows | Operational Currency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exchange Earner's Foreign Currency (EEFC)** | 100% Export-oriented units, service exporters, software firms | Up to **100% of foreign exchange earnings** from exports/services. | Fully convertible foreign currency; **Non-interest bearing current account**. |
| **Resident Foreign Currency (RFC)** | NRIs returning permanently to India for settlement | Balance in NRE/FCNR accounts, overseas assets acquired when non-resident. | Foreign currency; interest-bearing savings, current, or term deposit. |
| **RFC (Domestic) Account** | Resident individuals | Foreign exchange acquired while traveling abroad, gifts from overseas relatives. | Current account; non-interest bearing. |

## § 7.2 Unit 09: Operational Aspects of NRI Accounts

### 1. Master Comparative Framework

| Feature / Dimension | NRE (Non-Resident External) | NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) | FCNR(B) (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) | SNRR (Special Non-Resident Rupee) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Currency of Denomination** | Indian Rupee (INR) | Indian Rupee (INR) | Foreign Currency (USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, CAD, AUD) | Indian Rupee (INR) |
| **Permitted Inflows** | Inward remittances from abroad in convertible forex. | Domestic earnings (rent, dividends, pension, local sale proceeds). | Inward remittances in designated foreign currency. | Legitimate business inflows for external trade contracts. |
| **Repatriability** | **100% Freely and fully repatriable** abroad without limit. | Capped at **USD 1 Million per FY** for bona fide purposes. | **100% Freely and fully repatriable** abroad. | Fully repatriable (net of applicable taxes). |
| **Taxation in India** | **100% TAX-FREE** (Exempt under Income-tax Act §10(4)(ii)).* | **TAXABLE** (Subject to 30% TDS + cess under Income Tax Act). | **100% TAX-FREE** (Exempt under Income-tax Act §10(15)(iv)(fa)).* | Taxable under domestic law. |
| **Permissible Account Nature** | Savings, Current, Recurring, Fixed Deposit. | Savings, Current, Recurring, Fixed Deposit. | **TERM DEPOSIT ONLY** (Maturity 1 to 5 years). | Non-interest bearing current account only. |
| **Exchange Rate Risk** | **Depositor bears risk** (conversion from forex to INR). | Depositor bears risk. | **ZERO exchange risk to depositor** (Bank bears exchange risk). | Depositor bears risk. |

*\*Note on 2026 Statutory Tax Transition: The legacy Income-tax Act, 1961 was repealed effective 1 April 2026 upon the commencement of the Income-tax Act, 2025. While banking examinations test the historical statutory sections (Section 10(4)(ii) for NRE and Section 10(15)(iv)(fa) for FCNR(B)), these tax-exempt benefits continue seamlessly mapped under the corresponding schedules of the Income-tax Act, 2025.*

### 2. Immovable Property Acquisition by NRIs/OCIs

• An NRI or Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) has general permission to acquire any **residential or commercial immovable property** in India.
• **Prohibition on Purchase:** Under FEMA (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, NRIs and OCIs **cannot acquire agricultural land, plantation property, or farm houses by purchase** under the general permission framework (acquisition is permitted only by way of inheritance from a resident individual).

## § 7.3 High-Yield Examination Drill

**Q1. Under the Reserve Bank of India's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), what is the maximum annual foreign exchange remittance limit permitted for a resident individual per financial year?**  
A. USD 100,000  
B. USD 250,000  
C. USD 500,000  
D. USD 1,000,000  

**Q2. An Indian citizen residing in Dubai wishes to open a fixed deposit in India where she earns interest without incurring any Indian income tax, and is completely free from foreign currency exchange fluctuations. Which account should she open?**  
A. Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) Account  
B. Non-Resident External (NRE) Savings Account  
C. Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) [FCNR(B)] Account  
D. Special Non-Resident Rupee (SNRR) Account  

**Q3. Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, which of the following categories of immovable property in India is an NRI or OCI prohibited from acquiring by purchase under the general permission framework?**  
A. Commercial office space in Mumbai  
B. Residential apartment in New Delhi  
C. Agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouse  
D. Ready-to-move industrial factory warehouse  

---

## § 7.4 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), resident individuals can freely remit up to **USD 250,000 per financial year** (April to March) for permissible current or capital account transactions.  
• **Q2 — Answer: C.** An **FCNR(B)** account is maintained in designated foreign currencies (USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, etc.) as a term deposit (1 to 5 years). The interest earned is completely exempt from Indian income tax (Income-tax Act §10(15)(iv)(fa), mapped into Income-tax Act, 2025 schedules effective 1 April 2026), and because the principal and interest are returned in foreign currency, the depositor bears **zero foreign exchange risk**.  
• **Q3 — Answer: C.** Under FEMA (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, NRIs and OCIs have general permission to purchase residential and commercial properties in India. However, they **cannot acquire agricultural land, plantation property, or farm houses by purchase** under the general permission framework (acquisition is permitted only by way of inheritance from a resident).
