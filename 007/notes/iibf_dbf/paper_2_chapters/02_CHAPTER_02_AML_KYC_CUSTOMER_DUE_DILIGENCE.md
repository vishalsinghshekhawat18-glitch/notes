# AML / KYC NORMS, RISK CATEGORIZATION & DUE DILIGENCE (CDD)

Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2002 (PMLA) and RBI Master Directions on KYC mandate institutional frameworks to prevent banks from being used intentionally or unintentionally as conduits for money laundering and terrorist financing. Customer Due Diligence (CDD) operationalizes risk-based categorizations, verified through statutory Officially Valid Documents (OVDs).

## § 2.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

AML / KYC Norms & Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: 4 Structural Pillars of KYC Policy**
> Under RBI Master Direction on KYC (updated under PMLA 2002 and PML Rules 2005), every regulated entity must establish a Board-approved KYC framework built upon 4 mandatory pillars.

## 1. Master KYC Risk Categorization & Periodic Updation Schedule

| Risk Category | Customer Profile Examples | Mandatory Periodic Re-KYC Cycle | Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low Risk | Salaried individuals with clear source of income, Govt departments, PSUs, Regulated financial institutions | Once every 10 Years | Standard CDD; self-declaration allowed if no change in address/identity |
| Medium Risk | Blind persons, Stock brokers, Non-resident customers (NRIs), High net-worth individuals without PEP links | Once every 8 Years | Periodic review of transaction volumes vs declared income |
| High Risk | Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs), Non-Face-to-Face accounts, Trusts/Charities, Bullion dealers, Cross-border correspondents | Once every 2 Years | Mandatory Senior Management approval for account opening and source of funds verification |

## 2. Officially Valid Documents (OVD) & Central KYC (CKYCR)

Under PML Rule 2(1)(d), only the following **6 documents** qualify as proof of identity and address (OVD):

• **1. Passport**.
• **2. Driving Licence (DL)**.
• **3. Proof of possession of Aadhaar Number** (with Aadhaar number masked/redacted unless voluntarily provided for DBT).
• **4. Voter's Identity Card** issued by the Election Commission of India.
• **5. Job Card issued by NREGA** signed by an officer of the State Government.
• **6. Letter issued by the National Population Register (NPR)** containing details of name and address.
• **Central KYC Registry (CKYCR):** Managed by CERSAI under Section 12 of PMLA. Regulated entities must upload KYC data of individual customers within 10 days of account opening to generate a unique 14-digit **KYC Identifier (KIN)**.

## 3. Regulatory Reporting Thresholds to FIU-IND

| Report Type | Statutory Trigger Threshold | Submission Frequency / Deadline | Special Rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash Transaction Report (CTR) | All individual or integrally connected cash transactions > ₹10 Lakhs in a calendar month | By 15th of the succeeding month | Both deposits and withdrawals in cash are aggregated |
| Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) | Any transaction irrespective of amount giving rise to reasonable suspicion of illicit proceeds/terror financing | Within 7 working days of arriving at conclusion of suspicion | Strict 'Tipping-off' prohibition under Section 12 PMLA (Bank cannot inform customer) |
| Cross Border Wire Transfer Report (CWTR) | All cross-border wire transfers > ₹5 Lakhs or foreign currency equivalent | By 15th of the succeeding month | Both inward and outward wire transfers monitored |
| Non-Profit Organization Transaction Report (NTR) | All receipts by Non-Profit Organizations (NPOs) > ₹10 Lakhs | By 15th of the succeeding month | Applies to registered NPOs/NGOs |

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on AML / KYC:
1. **PAN is NOT an OVD for Address:** PAN card serves only as proof of identity (and financial transaction verification), NOT as proof of address since it does not print address details.
2. **STR Threshold Trap:** STR has **NO monetary threshold**. A transaction of even ₹5,000 can trigger an STR if it has no economic rationale or fits money laundering typologies.
3. **Re-KYC Frequency Trap:** High Risk = **2 Years**, Medium Risk = **8 Years**, Low Risk = **10 Years**. Low risk accounts do not require physical branch visits if self-declaration confirms unchanged details.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** Under RBI Master Directions on KYC, what is the mandatory frequency for periodic KYC updation for High-Risk, Medium-Risk, and Low-Risk customers respectively?
• (A) Every 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years
• (B) Every 2 years, 8 years, and 10 years
• (C) Every 3 years, 5 years, and 7 years
• (D) Every 6 months, 2 years, and 5 years

**Q2:** Which of the following documents is NOT recognized as an Officially Valid Document (OVD) for individual proof of identity under PMLA Rules?
• (A) Passport and Driving Licence
• (B) Voter’s Identity Card issued by Election Commission
• (C) PAN Card / e-PAN Card
• (D) Letter issued by National Population Register (NPR)

**Q3:** What is the regulatory filing timeline for submitting a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) to the Financial Intelligence Unit - India (FIU-IND)?
• (A) By 15th of the succeeding calendar month
• (B) Within 7 working days of arriving at a conclusion of suspicion
• (C) Within 24 hours of transaction execution
• (D) Within 30 calendar days from the date of quarterly audit

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(B) Every 2 years, 8 years, and 10 years** — High-Risk accounts require re-KYC at least once every 2 years; Medium-Risk every 8 years; Low-Risk every 10 years.

2. **(C) PAN Card / e-PAN Card** — PAN is an officially required document for financial transaction taxation, but under revised PMLA definitions, an OVD for individual CDD must contain both identity and address proof (Passport, DL, Voter ID, Aadhaar, MNREGA card, NPR letter). PAN alone does not verify address.

3. **(B) Within 7 working days of arriving at a conclusion of suspicion** — Cash Transaction Reports (CTR) are filed by the 15th of the succeeding month; STRs must be furnished within 7 working days of the Principal Officer concluding the transaction is suspicious.

## § 2.2 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

<details>
<summary>State the three classical stages of Money Laundering and describe the operational mechanics of each stage.</summary>
1. Placement: Introducing illicit cash into the formal financial system (smurfing/structuring deposits below reporting caps). 2. Layering: Concealing the criminal source through complex chains of financial transactions, wire transfers, offshore shell companies, and fictitious trade invoices. 3. Integration: Re-injecting laundered funds into the legitimate economy through luxury assets, real estate, or lawful corporate investments.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What constitutes a "Small Account" under RBI KYC Directions, and what are its operational transaction ceilings?</summary>
A Small Account is opened by an individual lacking officially valid documents, valid for 12 months (extendable to another 12 months upon proof of applying for an OVD). Ceilings: (1) Total credits in a financial year ≤ ₹1,00,000; (2) Total withdrawals and transfers in a calendar month ≤ ₹10,000; (3) Balance at any point in time ≤ ₹50,000; (4) Zero foreign remittances permitted.
</details>
