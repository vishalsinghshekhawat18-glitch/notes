# BANKING ETHICS, CUSTOMER RIGHTS CHARTER & GOVERNANCE

Banking functions as a high-trust fiduciary institution requiring ethical leadership, fair business practices, and sound corporate governance. Institutionalized via the RBI Charter of Customer Rights (2015), the Banking Ombudsman Scheme (Integrated Ombudsman 2021), and the Companies Act 2013, ethical banking prevents predatory misselling and insider malfeasance.

## § 20.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

Ethics, Business Values & Corporate Governance in Banks

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Ethics vs Law vs Values in Financial Institutions**
> Banking is founded upon public trust. While law defines the minimum acceptable boundary of legal behavior, ethics demands doing what is morally right beyond legal technicalities.

## 1. The 5 Pillars of RBI's Charter of Customer Rights (2015)

| Customer Right | Statutory Objective | Operational Application in Bank Branches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Right to Fair Treatment | Customers cannot be discriminated against on grounds of gender, age, religion, caste, or physical disability | Equal access to all banking facilities, transparent loan pricing, and uniform service terms. |
| 2. Right to Transparency, Fair & Honest Dealing | Contracts, fee schedules, interest rates, and penalties must be transparent, clear, and easily understandable | Key Fact Statement (KFS) provided for loans; no hidden charges or unfair lock-in clauses. |
| 3. Right to Suitability | Products offered to customers must match their financial needs, risk appetite, and capacity | Strict prohibition on **Mis-selling** of insurance, mutual funds, or derivative products to naive/senior citizen depositors. |
| 4. Right to Privacy | Customer's personal and financial information must remain strictly confidential | Data cannot be shared with telemarketers or third parties without explicit affirmative consent. |
| 5. Right to Grievance Redressal & Compensation | Right to prompt dispute resolution and fair compensation for bank's service deficiencies | Time-bound resolution by Principal Nodal Officer; escalation to **RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021**. |

## 2. Corporate Governance, Whistleblower Policy & Chinese Walls

• **1. Basel Corporate Governance Guidelines for Banks:** Mandates strong board oversight, clear risk appetite statements, independent Risk Management and Audit Committees, and separation of Chairman and CEO roles.
• **2. Whistleblower Policy (Section 177, Companies Act 2013):** Every listed company and scheduled bank must establish a vigil mechanism allowing employees and directors to report unethical behavior, actual or suspected fraud, or violation of the bank's code of conduct with **adequate safeguards against victimisation**.
• **3. Chinese Wall Doctrine:** An ethical and physical barrier established within financial institutions to prevent the flow of sensitive, non-public information between different departments (e.g. between Investment Banking/M&A advisory and Treasury/Stock Broking units) to eliminate **Insider Trading** and conflicts of interest.
• **4. Prevention of Mis-Selling:** Mandatory cooling-off / free-look period of **15 to 30 days** in retail insurance and investment products allowing customers to cancel policies without penalty.

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on Banking Ethics & Governance:
1. **RBI Charter of Customer Rights Count:** The RBI Charter contains exactly **5 Core Rights** (Fair Treatment, Transparency, Suitability, Privacy, and Grievance Redressal).
2. **Mis-Selling and Right to Suitability:** Selling a high-risk ULIP or equity fund to an illiterate rural depositor seeking a fixed deposit is a direct statutory violation of the **Right to Suitability**.
3. **Chinese Wall Principle:** The Chinese Wall specifically exists to prevent **conflict of interest and insider trading** between investment advisory/M&A and trading/treasury divisions.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** Which of the following is NOT one of the 5 core pillars of the RBI Charter of Customer Rights (2015)?
• (A) Right to Fair Treatment
• (B) Right to Transparent, Fair and Honest Dealing
• (C) Right to Unconditional Loan Approval
• (D) Right to Suitability and Privacy

**Q2:** Under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (2021), what is the maximum compensation an Ombudsman can award for mental agony and harassment to a complainant?
• (A) Up to ₹50,000
• (B) Up to ₹1,00,000
• (C) Up to ₹5,00,000
• (D) Up to ₹20,00,000

**Q3:** What is the purpose of establishing "Chinese Walls" inside universal banking institutions?
• (A) Physically segregating branches from currency chests
• (B) Ethical information barriers preventing sensitive price-sensitive data from flowing between commercial lending and treasury/investment advisory arms
• (C) Erecting border surveillance infrastructure
• (D) Enforcing strict dress codes for customer executives

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(C) Right to Unconditional Loan Approval** — The 5 pillars are: (1) Right to Fair Treatment; (2) Right to Transparency, Fair and Honest Dealing; (3) Right to Suitability; (4) Right to Privacy; (5) Right to Grievance Redress and Compensation.

2. **(B) Up to ₹1,00,000** — Ombudsman can award up to ₹20 Lakh for consequential financial loss and an additional compensation up to ₹1 Lakh for mental agony and loss of customer time.

3. **(B) Ethical information barriers preventing sensitive price-sensitive data from flowing between commercial lending and treasury/investment advisory arms** — Chinese Walls eliminate conflicts of interest and prevent insider trading.
