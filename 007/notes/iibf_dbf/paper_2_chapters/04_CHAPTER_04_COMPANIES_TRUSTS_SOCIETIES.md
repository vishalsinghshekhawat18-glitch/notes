# ACCOUNTS OF COMPANIES, TRUSTS, CLUBS & CHARGE REGISTRATION

Corporate banking involves legal personalities distinct from their shareholders (Salomon v. Salomon & Co Ltd). Account operations, borrowing authorizations, and security creations are governed by the Companies Act 2013, Indian Trusts Act 1882, and Societies Registration Act 1860, requiring scrutiny of constitutional documents (MoA, AoA, Trust Deeds) and statutory charge registrations.

## § 4.1 Comprehensive Operational & Legal Analysis

Operations in Accounts of Companies, Trusts, Clubs & Societies

> **Key Concept — Pivotal Concept: Corporate Legal Personality & Constitution**
> A company registered under the Companies Act 2013 is an artificial legal person with perpetual succession and common seal (optional). Its operational perimeter is strictly governed by its charter documents.

## 2. Company Borrowing Powers & Registration of Charges (CERSAI & RoC)

When lending to companies, banks must ensure borrowing is within the limits authorized under Section 180(1)(c) of the Companies Act 2013 and register charges timely:

• **Section 180(1)(c) Borrowing Limits:** Board of Directors cannot borrow money exceeding the aggregate of paid-up share capital, free reserves, and securities premium without prior approval of shareholders by special resolution.
• **Registration of Charges with RoC (Section 77, Companies Act 2013):** Every charge created by a company on its assets (mortgage, hypothecation, pledge) must be registered with the Registrar of Companies (RoC) within **30 days** of creation (extendable up to 60 days on additional fees). Unregistered charges are **VOID** against the liquidator and creditors.
• **CERSAI Registration:** All equitable mortgages and security interests must also be registered on the CERSAI portal within 30 days under Chapter IV-A of the SARFAESI Act 2002.

## 3. Legal Doctrines Governing Corporate Banking

• **Doctrine of Ultra Vires:** Any contract entered into by a company beyond the powers given in its MoA is Ultra Vires (beyond powers) and void ab initio. It cannot be ratified even by 100% shareholder consent.
• **Doctrine of Constructive Notice:** Third parties dealing with a company are presumed to have read and understood its public documents (MoA and AoA registered with RoC).
• **Doctrine of Indoor Management (Turquand's Rule):** Outsiders dealing with a company are entitled to assume that internal procedures and approvals required by AoA have been properly complied with.

> **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> Top Exam Traps on Companies and Trusts:
1. **Trustee Delegation Trap:** A trustee **CANNOT delegate** duties or discretionary powers to co-trustees or outsiders unless the Trust Deed expressly permits it (*Delegatus non potest delegare*).
2. **Registration of Charges Deadline:** Standard registration period with RoC is **30 days**. An unregistered charge makes the bank an **unsecured creditor** in the event of company liquidation.
3. **Director's Personal Cheque into Company Account:** A bank should never credit a cheque drawn in favor of a company to the personal account of a director without verified authority; doing so forfeits statutory protection under Section 131 NI Act.

### Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills (IIBF Pattern)

**Q1:** Under Section 77 of the Companies Act 2013, within what initial statutory timeline must a company register a charge created on its assets with the Registrar of Companies (RoC)?
• (A) 15 days
• (B) 30 days
• (C) 60 days
• (D) 90 days

**Q2:** What is the legal doctrine that protects innocent third-party outsiders dealing in good faith with a company from internal operational irregularities?
• (A) Doctrine of Ultra Vires
• (B) Doctrine of Constructive Notice
• (C) Doctrine of Indoor Management (Turquand’s Rule)
• (D) Doctrine of Subrogation

**Q3:** Can a trustee delegate their fiduciary authority or duties to a co-trustee or third party under the Indian Trusts Act 1882?
• (A) Yes, delegation is an inherent right of any fiduciary
• (B) No, the maxim "delegatus non potest delegare" applies, unless expressly permitted by the trust deed or regular necessity
• (C) Yes, with verbal consent of the local civil judge
• (D) Only if the beneficiary is a minor

#### Solutions & Detailed Explanations

1. **(B) 30 days** — Charges must be registered with RoC within 30 days of creation. The RoC can permit registration within an additional 30 days (total 60 days) on payment of additional fees.

2. **(C) Doctrine of Indoor Management (Turquand’s Rule)** — Established in Royal British Bank v. Turquand; outsiders dealing with a company are bound to know its public documents (MoA/AoA), but can presume internal regularity has been observed.

3. **(B) No, the maxim "delegatus non potest delegare" applies, unless expressly permitted by the trust deed or regular necessity** — Section 47 of the Indian Trusts Act prohibits delegation of fiduciary duties unless authorized by the trust instrument.

## § 4.2 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

<details>
<summary>Differentiate between the Doctrine of Constructive Notice and the Doctrine of Indoor Management in corporate banking.</summary>
Constructive Notice is an equitable doctrine favoring the company: the Memorandum and Articles of Association are public documents registered with RoC; every person dealing with the company is presumed to have read and understood them. Conversely, Indoor Management (Turquand’s Rule) favors the outside creditor/banker: while outsiders must observe public limits in MoA/AoA, they are entitled to assume internal procedures and resolutions have been properly complied with.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the consequences of failing to register a company charge with the Registrar of Companies (RoC) under Section 77?</summary>
If a charge is not registered within the permitted statutory period: (1) The charge is VOID against the liquidator and any other creditor of the company in winding-up; (2) The secured creditor loses priority and is relegated to an unsecured creditor status; (3) The underlying debt remains valid and immediately payable; (4) The company and defaulting officers face statutory penalties under Section 86.
</details>
