# COLLATERALS, CHARGES, MORTGAGES & DOCUMENTATION

Securing advances through legally enforceable charges is the primary safeguard against credit default. Indian jurisprudence recognizes five distinct modes of charging securities: Pledge, Hypothecation, Mortgage, Lien, and Assignment, each anchored in specific statutory enactments governing possession, ownership, and power of sale.

---

## § 15.1 Units 25 & 26: The Five Canonical Modes of Charging Securities

> **Curriculum Alignment — Official IIBF Paper 2 Benchmark (Units 25 & 26)**  
> **Core Proposition:** Creation of security establishes a property right in favor of the lender. The nature of this right—whether possessory (Pledge), non-possessory equitable (Hypothecation), proprietary conveyance (English Mortgage), or title deposit (Equitable Mortgage)—determines the bank's enforcement remedies.

### 1. Master Comparative Matrix of Charging Modes

| Mode of Charge | Governing Statute & Section | Possession of Asset | Ownership of Asset | Primary Asset Covered | Bank's Statutory Remedy on Default |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pledge** | Section 172, Indian Contract Act, 1872 | Transferred to **Bank (Pledgee)** (Actual or Constructive). | Remains with **Borrower (Pledgor)**. | Movable goods, gold ornaments, warehouse receipts, shares. | Right to sell under Section 176 upon giving **reasonable notice** to pledgor; no court intervention needed. |
| **Hypothecation** | Section 2(n), SARFAESI Act, 2002 | Remains with **Borrower (Hypothecator)**. | Remains with **Borrower**. | Movable stocks, inventory, vehicles, plant & machinery. | Enforce SARFAESI Section 13(4) or take possession with borrower consent / court receiver. |
| **Mortgage** | Section 58, Transfer of Property Act, 1882 | Depends on mortgage type (Possession in Usufructuary; with mortgagor in Simple/Equitable). | Interest in immovable property transferred to **Bank (Mortgagee)**. | Immovable property (Land, factory, residential building). | Foreclosure or judicial sale; private sale under SARFAESI Act without court order. |
| **Lien** | Section 171, Indian Contract Act, 1872 | With **Bank (Lien holder)** in ordinary course of business. | Remains with **Borrower**. | Bills, securities, commercial paper, deposit receipts. | General lien gives implied power of sale upon reasonable notice. |
| **Assignment** | Section 130, Transfer of Property Act, 1882 | Actionable claim transferred via written instrument. | Title to actionable claim transfers to **Bank (Assignee)**. | Actionable claims (Life Insurance Policies, Book Debts, Supply Bills). | Direct right to sue debtor/insurer and recover funds in bank's own name. |

## § 15.2 The Six Canonical Types of Mortgages (Section 58, TP Act 1882)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE SIX TYPES OF MORTGAGE (SECTION 58, TP ACT)              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. SIMPLE MORTGAGE (Sec 58b):                                               │
│    • No possession delivered; mortgagor binds himself personally to repay.  │
│    • In default, mortgagee has right to cause property to be sold via COURT.│
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. MORTGAGE BY CONDITIONAL SALE (Sec 58c):                                  │
│    • Ostensible sale on condition: becomes absolute sale on default, or     │
│      void upon repayment. Condition must be in the SAME deed.               │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. USUFRUCTUARY MORTGAGE (Sec 58d):                                         │
│    • Possession delivered to mortgagee; mortgagee receives rents and profits│
│      and appropriates them towards interest and principal. No personal debt.│
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. ENGLISH MORTGAGE (Sec 58e):                                              │
│    • Mortgagor binds himself personally to repay on a certain date, and     │
│      transfers the property ABSOLUTELY to mortgagee, with proviso for       │
│      re-transfer upon repayment.                                            │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. MORTGAGE BY DEPOSIT OF TITLE DEEDS (EQUITABLE MORTGAGE) (Sec 58f):        │
│    • Most popular in banking; created in NOTIFIED TOWNS by delivering       │
│      documents of title to immovable property with INTENT to create security│
│      thereon. No mandatory written deed; low stamp duty.                    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 6. ANOMALOUS MORTGAGE (Sec 58g):                                            │
│    • A mortgage which is not any of the above five categories; governed by  │
│      the custom and contractual terms of the instrument.                    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## § 15.3 Unit 27: Documentation, Stamping & Limitation Periods

• **Essential Loan Documents:**
  - **Demand Promissory Note (DPN):** Unconditional promise to pay on demand.
  - **Loan / Hypothecation Agreement:** Detailed covenants, negative liens, acceleration clauses.
  - **Guarantee Deed:** Enforces co-extensive liability on sureties.
• **Indian Stamp Act, 1899 Compliance:**
  - Documents must be stamped **before or at the time of execution**.
  - An unstamped or improperly stamped document is **inadmissible in evidence** in court (Section 35). Defect can be cured by paying penalty up to **10 times the duty**, EXCEPT for promissory notes and bills of exchange which **cannot be validated even on penalty**!
• **Statutory Limitation Periods (Limitation Act, 1963):**
  - Suit on a Demand Promissory Note (DPN): **3 years** from date of execution.
  - Suit for money lent under personal loan/overdraft: **3 years** from date of loan.
  - Suit against a personal Guarantor: **3 years** from date of demand/default.
  - Mortgage Suit for Sale of Immovable Property: **12 years** from date money becomes due.
  - Acknowledgment of Debt (Section 18, Limitation Act): Valid written acknowledgment signed by borrower **before expiry of limitation period** starts a fresh limitation period of 3 years.

## § 15.5 Answer Key & Detailed Explanatory Rationale

• **Q1 — Answer: B.** Under Section 172 of the Indian Contract Act, a **Pledge** is defined as the bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt. Possession transfers to the bank (pledgee), but ownership remains with the borrower (pledgor). In hypothecation, possession remains with the borrower.  
• **Q2 — Answer: B.** An Equitable Mortgage requires three statutory elements under Section 58(f): (i) an existing debt, (ii) delivery of original documents of title to immovable property, and (iii) the delivery must take place within a **notified town** with the clear **intent to create security**.  
• **Q3 — Answer: C.** Under Section 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, while most documents can be validated for courtroom admission by paying deficient duty plus a penalty of up to 10 times, **Promissory Notes and Bills of Exchange are absolute statutory exceptions**—they cannot be validated under any circumstances if unstamped at execution.
