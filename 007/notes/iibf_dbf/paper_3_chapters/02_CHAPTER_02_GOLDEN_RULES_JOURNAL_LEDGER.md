## 1. Why This Matters in Banking Operations
Every loan disbursement, deposit credit, clearing clearing settlement, and provisioning charge in banking is fundamentally executed through double-entry debits and credits. Mastery of transaction analysis, subsidiary books, cash book balancing, and the boundary between capital and revenue outlays ensures operational accuracy and prevents misclassification errors that could distort a bank's financial statements or credit appraisals.

## 2. Core Framework: Double-Entry & The 3 Golden Rules
First formulated systematically by the Franciscan friar and mathematician **Luca Pacioli in 1494** (*Summa de Arithmetica*), double-entry bookkeeping enforces that every economic transaction exerts a dual effect of equal monetary magnitude across accounts.

### Master Account Classification & Golden Rules

## 3. Subsidiary Books & The Cash Book Architecture
In high-volume environments like commercial banks and trading corporations, recording every transaction in a single journal is unfeasible. Transactions are subdivided into specialized **Subsidiary Books**:

1. **Cash Book (Single, Double, Triple Column):**
   - *Single Column:* Cash transactions only.
   - *Double Column:* Cash and Discount columns, or Cash and Bank columns.
   - *Triple Column:* Cash, Bank, and Discount columns.
   - *Petty Cash Book:* Maintained under the **Imprest System** where the petty cashier is reimbursed the exact expenses incurred during the period to restore the original float.
   - *Dual Character:* The Cash Book is both a **Book of Original Entry** (Journal) and a **Principal Book** (Ledger) because cash and bank balances are balanced directly within it without separate ledger accounts.
2. **Purchases Book & Sales Book:** Record strictly credit purchases and credit sales of trade inventory (cash purchases/sales go directly to the Cash Book; capital asset purchases on credit go to Journal Proper).
3. **Returns Books:** Purchases Returns (Return Outward) and Sales Returns (Return Inward) Books.
4. **Journal Proper (Residual Book):** Records opening entries, closing entries, transfer entries, rectification entries, and purchase of fixed assets on credit.

## 4. Capital vs. Revenue Expenditures & Receipts

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                CAPITAL vs REVENUE EXPENDITURE COMPARISON                           |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Parameter             | Capital Expenditure                   | Revenue Expenditure                |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| Economic Purpose      | Acquires or enhances long-term assets | Maintains existing operating assets|
| Benefit Period        | Extends over multiple financial years | Consumed within current fiscal year|
| Earning Capacity      | Increases revenue-generating capacity | Maintains existing earning capacity|
| Accounting Treatment  | Capitalized on Balance Sheet (Asset)  | Expensed in Profit & Loss Account  |
| Examples              | • Purchase of core banking software   | • Annual AMC for banking software  |
|                       | • Installation cost of ATM machine    | • Electricity bill for ATM kiosk   |
|                       | • Legal fees for acquiring property   | • Legal expenses defending a suit  |
+-----------------------+---------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
```

## 6. Worked Example: Step-by-Step Journalizing
**Scenario:** Record the following transactions for a commercial branch:
1. Paid ₹25,000 wages for installing a new automated cash deposit machine.
2. Received ₹40,000 dividend directly into the bank account from an equity investment.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
- Transaction 1: Installation wages enhance the capital asset and bring it to working condition. It must be capitalized to Machinery A/c, NOT expensed to Wages A/c.
  - *Journal Entry:*
    $$\text{Debit: ATM Machinery Account (Real A/c - Comes In) \quad ₹25,000}$$
    $$\text{Credit: Bank Account (Personal A/c - Giver) \quad ₹25,000}$$
    *(Being installation wages capitalized to asset cost)*
- Transaction 2: Bank receives the economic value; Dividend is a nominal gain.
  - *Journal Entry:*
    $$\text{Debit: Bank Account (Personal A/c - Receiver) \quad ₹40,000}$$
    $$\text{Credit: Dividend Income Account (Nominal A/c - Gain) \quad ₹40,000}$$
    *(Being dividend directly credited to bank account)*

> [!CAUTION]
> **Examiner Trap Alert:**
> 1. **Outstanding Rent A/c** is a **Representative Personal Account**, NOT a Nominal Account.
> 2. **Bank Account** is a **Personal Account** (an artificial juridical person), NOT a Real Account.
> 3. Wages paid for erecting plant or installing machinery are **Capital Expenditure**; charging them to Wages Account is an **Error of Principle**.
> 4. Cash Book serves as BOTH a journal and a ledger; a separate Cash Account is never opened in the general ledger when a Cash Book is maintained.

## 7. Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills

**Q1.** An expenditure of ₹50,000 incurred on the overhaul of a second-hand transport vehicle immediately after purchase to make it roadworthy is:
- (A) Revenue Expenditure
- (B) Capital Expenditure
- (C) Deferred Revenue Expenditure
- (D) Operating Expenditure

**Q2.** The Petty Cash Book is maintained on which standardized accounting system?
- (A) Accrual System
- (B) Single-Entry Float System
- (C) Imprest System
- (D) Dual Balance System

**Q3.** In a triple-column cash book, the discount columns are:
- (A) Balanced at the end of every week
- (B) Totalled and posted to Discount Allowed and Discount Received Accounts
- (C) Transferred directly to the Suspense Account
- (D) Reconciled with the Bank Passbook

#### Solutions & Explanations
* Q1 Correct Answer: (B) Capital Expenditure. All outlays incurred to bring a second-hand asset into operating condition are capitalized as part of the asset's acquisition cost.
* Q2 Correct Answer: (C) Imprest System. The head cashier replenishes the exact total spent by the petty cashier at periodic intervals, maintaining a constant opening float.
* Q3 Correct Answer: (B) Totalled and posted to Discount Allowed and Discount Received Accounts. The debit discount column total represents Discount Allowed (expense), and the credit column total represents Discount Received (gain); discount columns are totalled, NOT balanced against each other.

## 8. Active Recall & Self-Diagnostic Prompts

<details>
<summary>Why are discount columns in a three-column cash book not balanced against each other?</summary>

Because they represent two entirely distinct economic events: Debit discount is Discount Allowed to debtors (a nominal expense), while Credit discount is Discount Received from creditors (a nominal gain). They are posted separately to their respective ledger accounts.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Classify: Goodwill, Prepaid Salaries, and Proprietor's Drawings.</summary>

Goodwill: Real Account (intangible asset); Prepaid Salaries: Representative Personal Account; Proprietor's Drawings: Personal Account.
</details>

## 9. Last-Minute Revision Box
- Golden Rules: Personal (Dr Receiver / Cr Giver), Real (Dr Comes in / Cr Goes out), Nominal (Dr Expenses & Losses / Cr Incomes & Gains).
- Luca Pacioli (1494): Originator of double-entry treatise.
- Imprest System: Constant float maintained for petty cash.
- Capital vs Revenue: Any cost necessary to make an asset operational is Capital Expenditure.
- Discount Columns in Cash Book: Never balanced; totals posted separately to Nominal Accounts.
