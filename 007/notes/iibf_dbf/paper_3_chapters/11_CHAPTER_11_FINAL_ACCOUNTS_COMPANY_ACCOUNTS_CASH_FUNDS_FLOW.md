## 1. Why This Matters in Banking Operations
Before a bank can sanction credit facilities, term loans, or working capital lines to corporate borrowers, credit underwriters must dissect the borrower's audited annual financial statements. Understanding how a Trial Balance transitions into Trading, Profit & Loss, and Balance Sheet statements, mastering company share capital structures, and distinguishing accounting profit from actual cash liquidity via Cash Flow and Funds Flow Statements are paramount skills for modern bank credit appraisal and risk management.

## 2. From Trial Balance to Final Accounts: The Core Adjustments
Final financial statements synthesize periodic operating results into three statements:
1. **Trading Account:** Measures gross operating performance ($\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Net Sales} - \text{Cost of Goods Sold}$).
2. **Profit & Loss Account:** Measures net operating profitability ($\text{Net Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} + \text{Other Income} - \text{Operating Expenses}$).
3. **Balance Sheet:** A statement of financial position showing assets, liabilities, and owners' equity at a specific date.

### Year-End Adjustment Protocol
Transactions occurring at year-end that are not yet recorded in the Trial Balance exert a **dual effect** on final accounts:

| Year-End Adjustment Item | First Entry (Profit & Loss / Trading) | Second Entry (Balance Sheet) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Closing Stock** | Credited to Trading Account | Disclosed under Current Assets |
| **Outstanding Expenses** | Added to the relevant Expense in P&L | Disclosed under Current Liabilities |
| **Prepaid Expenses** | Deducted from the relevant Expense in P&L | Disclosed under Current Assets |
| **Accrued Income (Earned but not received)**| Added to the relevant Income in P&L | Disclosed under Current Assets |
| **Income Received in Advance (Unearned)** | Deducted from the relevant Income in P&L | Disclosed under Current Liabilities |
| **Provision for Doubtful Debts** | Debited to Profit & Loss Account | Deducted from Sundry Debtors in Assets |
| **Depreciation on Fixed Assets** | Debited to Profit & Loss Account | Deducted from the specific Fixed Asset |

## 3. Company Accounts: Share Capital Architecture
Under the **Companies Act 2013**, corporate capital structure is organized into legal tiers:
- **Authorized Capital:** The maximum nominal capital a company is legally authorized to issue under its Memorandum of Association.
- **Issued Capital:** The portion of authorized capital offered to the public for subscription.
- **Subscribed Capital:** The portion of issued capital applied for by investors.
- **Called-up Capital:** The portion of subscribed capital demanded by directors from shareholders.
- **Paid-up Capital:** The actual monetary amount received from shareholders ($\text{Called-up Capital} - \text{Calls in Arrears}$).

### Key Legal Rules for Share Issue
1. **Issue at Premium (Section 52):** Securities premium can strictly be utilized for: (i) issuing fully paid bonus shares, (ii) writing off preliminary expenses, (iii) writing off share issue expenses/commission, (iv) premium on redemption of preference shares/debentures, or (v) buy-back of shares under Section 68.
2. **Prohibition on Issue at Discount (Section 53):** Companies are **prohibited from issuing shares at a discount**, except for "Sweat Equity Shares" issued under Section 54.
3. **Forfeiture of Shares:** When a shareholder defaults on allotment or call payments, shares may be forfeited after proper legal notice. Forfeited shares may subsequently be reissued at a discount, provided the discount **does not exceed the amount already forfeited on those shares**.

- **Direct vs. Indirect Method:** The *Direct Method* reports major classes of gross cash receipts and payments. The *Indirect Method* begins with Net Profit before Tax and reconciles it by adding back non-cash expenses (depreciation) and adjusting for changes in working capital.

## 5. Funds Flow Statement: Movement in Working Capital
While a Cash Flow Statement tracks pure cash and cash equivalents, a **Funds Flow Statement** evaluates changes in broader net working capital ($\text{NWC} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}$):
- **Schedule of Changes in Working Capital:** Computes the net increase or decrease in NWC between two balance sheet dates.
- **Sources of Funds (Inflows of Working Capital):** Funds from Operations (adjusted net profit), issue of shares/debentures, long-term bank borrowings, sale of fixed assets.
- **Applications of Funds (Outflows of Working Capital):** Funds lost in operations, redemption of preference shares/debentures, repayment of long-term term loans, purchase of fixed assets, dividend and tax payments.

## 6. Master Comparative Framework: Profit vs. Cash Flow vs. Funds Flow

| Dimension | Accounting Profit (P&L) | Cash Flow Statement | Funds Flow Statement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Basis** | **Accrual Basis** (earned/incurred) | **Cash Basis** (actual receipt/payout) | **Working Capital Basis** (Net CA - CL) |
| **Treatment of Depreciation**| Debited as an operating expense | **Added back** (non-cash charge) | **Added back** to compute Funds from Operations |
| **Primary Liquidity Insight** | Shows operational profitability | Discloses exact cash solvency & liquidity | Discloses long-term financial policy & capital allocation |
| **Governing Standard** | Companies Act Schedule III / BR Act | **AS 3 / Ind AS 7** (Mandatory) | Management accounting tool (Non-mandatory) |

## 7. Worked Numerical: Cash Flow from Operating Activities
**Scenario:** A company reports Net Profit before Tax of ₹5,00,000 for the year. The following adjustments are noted:
- Depreciation charged during the year: ₹1,20,000.
- Profit on sale of machinery: ₹30,000.
- Increase in Sundry Debtors (Current Asset): ₹80,000.
- Increase in Sundry Creditors (Current Liability): ₹50,000.
- Income tax paid during the year: ₹1,10,000.

**Solution Step-by-Step (Indirect Method):**
1. $\text{Net Profit before Tax} = \text{₹5,00,000}$
2. Add: Non-cash expense (Depreciation) $= + \text{₹1,20,000}$
3. Less: Non-operating gain (Profit on sale of machinery) $= - \text{₹30,000}$
   - *Operating Profit before Working Capital Changes* $= \text{₹5,90,000}$
4. Adjust for Working Capital Movements:
   - Less: Increase in Debtors (cash tied up in receivables) $= - \text{₹80,000}$
   - Add: Increase in Creditors (cash preserved by supplier credit) $= + \text{₹50,000}$
   - *Cash Generated from Operations* $= \text{₹5,60,000}$
5. Less: Income Tax Paid $= - \text{₹1,10,000}$
6. **Net Cash Flow from Operating Activities** $= \mathbf{\text{₹4,50,000}}$.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Examiner Trap Alert:**
> 1. Under Section 53 of the Companies Act 2013, companies are **strictly prohibited from issuing shares at a discount**, except for sweat equity shares.
> 2. Depreciation is **added back** in cash flow calculations because it is a non-cash expense that reduced accounting profit without any physical cash outflow.
> 3. An increase in a Current Asset represents a **Cash Outflow (−)**; an increase in a Current Liability represents a **Cash Inflow (+)**.
> 4. Dividends paid are classified under **Financing Activities** in the Cash Flow Statement.

## 8. Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills

**Q1.** Under AS 3 / Ind AS 7, where is the repayment of a long-term bank term loan classified in the Cash Flow Statement?
- (A) Operating Activities
- (B) Investing Activities
- (C) Financing Activities
- (D) Non-cash Reconciliation Schedule

**Q2.** Under Section 53 of the Companies Act 2013, can a company issue ordinary equity shares at a discount?
- (A) Yes, with approval of the Board of Directors
- (B) Yes, up to a maximum discount of 10%
- (C) No, issue of shares at a discount is void except for sweat equity shares
- (D) Yes, if sanctioned by the Registrar of Companies

**Q3.** In preparing a Cash Flow Statement under the indirect method, an increase in Trade Receivables (Debtors) during the fiscal year is:
- (A) Added to Net Profit
- (B) Deducted from Net Profit
- (C) Disclosed under Financing Activities
- (D) Ignored as a non-operating item

#### Solutions & Explanations
* Q1 Correct Answer: (C) Financing Activities. Transactions altering the equity or borrowing structure of a company are financing cash flows.
* Q2 Correct Answer: (C) No, issue of shares at a discount is void except for sweat equity shares under Section 54.
* Q3 Correct Answer: (B) Deducted from Net Profit. An increase in debtors indicates that revenues recognized on an accrual basis have not yet been collected in cash, requiring a deduction to determine cash flow.

## 9. Active Recall & Self-Diagnostic Prompts

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between Cash Flow and Funds Flow regarding working capital?</summary>

Cash Flow evaluates movements purely in cash and bank balances. Funds Flow evaluates broader changes in Net Working Capital (Current Assets minus Current Liabilities), where movements between non-cash current assets and liabilities do not affect the net total.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Why is profit on sale of fixed assets deducted from net profit in operating cash flow calculations?</summary>

Because the gain was credited to the Profit & Loss statement, but the entire cash realized from the asset sale (cost + profit) is classified as an Investing Activity. To prevent double counting and isolate pure operating cash flow, the gain must be deducted from net profit.
</details>

## 10. Last-Minute Revision Box

- Share Capital: Authorized $\ge$ Issued $\ge$ Subscribed $\ge$ Called-up $\ge$ Paid-up.
