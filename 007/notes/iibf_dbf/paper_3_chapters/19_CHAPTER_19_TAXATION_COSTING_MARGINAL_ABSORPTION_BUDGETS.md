## 1. PART A: TAXATION IN BANKING OPERATIONS (DIRECT & INDIRECT)

Commercial banks occupy a critical dual role in national tax governance: they act as corporate taxpayers liable for corporate taxes and Goods and Services Tax (GST), and simultaneously serve as statutory collection and reporting agents for the Government through Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) and Tax Collected at Source (TCS).

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                         TAXATION ARCHITECTURE IN COMMERCIAL BANKING                                |
+-------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
|               DIRECT TAXATION                   |               INDIRECT TAXATION                  |
|    (Income-tax Act 1961 / 2025 Transition)      |             (CGST / SGST / IGST 2017)            |
+-------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
| • TDS on Term Deposit Interest (§194A)          | • 18% Standard GST on fee-based services         |
| • TDS on Cash Withdrawals (§194N)               | • Pure interest on loans/deposits EXEMPT         |
| • PAN Default Withholding: 20% (§206AA)         | • Special Bank ITC Option: 50% monthly (§17(4))  |
| • Exemption Declarations: Form 15G / 15H        | • Reverse Charge Mechanism on specified services |
+-------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1.1 TDS on Bank Term Deposits (Section 194A)
Under Section 194A of direct tax statutes (governed under the historical Income-tax Act 1961 transitioning into the Income-tax Act, 2025 framework effective 1 April 2026), banking companies, cooperative banks, and post offices must deduct income tax at source on interest paid or credited on time deposits (fixed deposits and recurring deposits).

| Depositor Category | Annual Interest Threshold | Standard TDS Rate (Valid PAN) | Higher TDS Rate without PAN (§206AA) | Applicable Exemption Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General Individuals / Entities** | **₹40,000** per financial year per bank | **10%** | **20%** | **Form 15G** (Age < 60 yrs, total income below basic exemption) |
| **Senior Citizens (Age 60+ Years)** | **₹50,000** per financial year (§80TTB) | **10%** | **20%** | **Form 15H** (Age ≥ 60 yrs, final tax on total income is NIL) |

#### Operational Mandates:
1. **Branch vs Bank-Wide Aggregation:** Following Core Banking Solution (CBS) deployment, interest is aggregated across **all branches of the bank under the same PAN / Customer ID (CIF)**.
2. **Timing of Deduction:** TDS is deducted at the **point of credit** (interest accrual or quarterly compounding) or **payment**, whichever occurs earlier.
3. **Savings Bank Interest:** Interest on savings bank accounts is **completely exempt from TDS** under Section 194A. Under Section 80TTA, individuals receive a deduction up to ₹10,000 on savings interest; Senior citizens under Section 80TTB receive a deduction up to ₹50,000 across both savings and term deposits.
4. **Remittance Deadlines:** Monthly TDS deducted must be deposited to the Central Government by the **7th of the following month** (except for March deductions, which must be deposited by **30 April**).
5. **Certificates & Statements:** Quarterly TDS returns are filed in **Form 26Q**; certificates are issued in **Form 16A**; details are reflected in the taxpayer's **Form 26AS** and Annual Information Statement (AIS). Under the 2025/2026 digital compliance portal, centralized single-window verification (Form 121 transition) is enabled.

### 1.2 TDS on High-Value Cash Withdrawals (Section 194N)
Introduced to discourage cash-driven shadow transactions and enforce digital audit trails:
- **Standard Account Holders (Regular ITR Filers):** TDS is deducted at **2%** on aggregate cash withdrawals exceeding **₹1 Crore** in a financial year from one or more accounts maintained with the bank.
- **Non-Filers (Persons who have not filed ITR for the preceding 3 assessment years):**
  - **2% TDS** on cash withdrawals between **₹20 Lakhs and ₹1 Crore**.
  - **5% TDS** on cash withdrawals exceeding **₹1 Crore**.
- **Exempt Entities:** Central/State Governments, other banks, post offices, banking correspondents (BCs), and agricultural produce market committee (APMC) commission agents.

### 1.3 Goods and Services Tax (GST) in Banking Operations
Under the CGST Act, 2017:
1. **Taxable Fee-Based Services (18% GST):** Processing fees, documentation charges, locker rent, folio charges, credit card annual fees, ATM transactions beyond the free monthly cap, NEFT/RTGS outward charges for corporate customers, and demand draft commission attract **18% standard GST** (9% CGST + 9% SGST for intra-state; 18% IGST for inter-state).
2. **Exempt Banking Services:** Interest or discount earned on loans, advances, or deposits is strictly **exempt from GST**.
3. **Special Input Tax Credit (ITC) Option for Banks (Section 17(4), CGST Act 2017):**
   - Because banks generate both taxable services (fee income) and exempt supplies (interest income), normal input tax apportionment under Section 17(2) requires cumbersome proportional calculations.
   - Section 17(4) grants banking companies and financial institutions an irrevocable annual statutory option: **avail a flat 50% of the eligible Input Tax Credit every month on inputs, capital goods, and input services**, while the remaining 50% lapses.
   - The 50% restriction does not apply to GST paid on supplies between separate branch registrations of the same banking entity (distinct persons under the same PAN).

## 2. PART B: COSTING FUNDAMENTALS & CLASSIFICATION OF COSTS

Cost Accounting is the process of accounting for cost from the point at which expenditure is incurred or committed to the establishment of its ultimate relationship with cost centers and cost units.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    TOTAL COST (COST SHEET) STRUCTURE                               |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses = PRIME COST                                    |
|       + Factory / Works Overheads                   = WORKS COST / FACTORY COST                    |
|       + Office & Administrative Overheads           = COST OF PRODUCTION                           |
|       + Selling & Distribution Overheads            = TOTAL COST / COST OF SALES                   |
|       + Profit Margin                               = SELLING PRICE / REVENUE                      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1 Classification of Costs
1. **By Nature or Element:**
   - **Material Cost:** Cost of tangible substances used in manufacturing.
   - **Labour Cost:** Remuneration paid to employees.
   - **Expenses:** Costs other than materials and labour (e.g., patent royalties, machinery hiring).
2. **By Traceability / Relation to Cost Object:**
   - **Direct Costs:** Costs directly identifiable with a specific cost unit or product (Direct Material, Direct Labour, Direct Chargeable Expenses).
   - **Indirect Costs (Overheads):** Costs that cannot be identified with a single cost unit and must be apportioned (Factory Overheads, Office Overheads, Selling Overheads).
3. **By Behavior in Relation to Activity Volume:**
   - **Fixed Costs:** Remain constant in total amount over a relevant range of output, regardless of volume fluctuations (e.g., factory rent, manager salary, annual insurance). **Fixed cost per unit decreases as output increases**.
   - **Variable Costs:** Vary directly in total in proportion to changes in volume of output (e.g., raw material, direct wages). **Variable cost per unit remains constant**.
   - **Semi-Variable / Mixed Costs:** Contain both fixed and variable elements (e.g., telephone bill with fixed line rental plus per-call charges; electricity with minimum demand charge plus per-unit tariff).
4. **By Decision-Making Relevance:**
   - **Sunk Costs:** Historical costs already incurred that cannot be altered by current or future decisions (e.g., past R&D expense). Completely excluded from decision-making.
   - **Opportunity Costs:** The benefit foregone by choosing one alternative over the next best alternative.
   - **Differential / Incremental Costs:** The change in total cost resulting from a change in the level or nature of activity.
   - **Marginal Cost:** The variable cost of producing one additional unit.

### 2.2 Cost Centers and Cost Units
- **Cost Center:** A location, person, or item of equipment (or group of these) for which costs may be ascertained and used for cost control.
  - *Production Cost Center:* Directly engaged in manufacturing (e.g., Assembly line, Weaving department).
  - *Service Cost Center:* Provides support to production departments (e.g., Maintenance shop, Boiler house, Canteen).
  - *Personal Cost Center:* Refers to a person or group (e.g., Plant Manager).
  - *Impersonal Cost Center:* Refers to a location or equipment (e.g., Machine Shop A).
- **Cost Unit:** A quantitative unit of product, service, or time in terms of which costs are ascertained or expressed:
  - *Cement:* Per metric tonne (MT).
  - *Textiles:* Per meter.
  - *Electricity:* Per kilowatt-hour (kWh).
  - *Passenger Transport:* Per passenger-kilometer.
  - *Hospitality:* Per room-day / patient-day.
  - *Banking:* Per transaction processed / per account maintained.

## 3. PART C: METHODS OF COSTING ACROSS INDUSTRIES

The method of costing chosen depends on the nature of the product, production methods, and industrial structure.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    METHODS OF COSTING TAXONOMY                                     |
+--------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| METHOD                   | APPLICATION / EXAMPLES                                                  |
+--------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Unit / Single Costing    | Standard identical products: Mining, Quarries, Cement, Breweries        |
| Job Costing              | Distinct customer orders: Printing presses, Tailoring, Repair workshops |
| Batch Costing            | Groups of identical items: Pharmaceuticals, Machine parts, Readymade    |
| Contract Costing         | Long-duration civil projects: Bridges, Highways, Ship construction      |
| Process Costing          | Continuous sequential stages: Chemicals, Petroleum refining, Sugar, Oil |
| Operating / Service      | Undertakings rendering services: Transport, Hospitals, Hotels, Banks    |
| Multiple / Composite     | Complex assembled goods: Automobiles, Aircraft, Computer hardware       |
+--------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 3.1 Batch Costing & Economic Batch Quantity (EBQ)
In Batch Costing, a batch of identical units is treated as a single cost unit. Cost per unit is computed by dividing total batch cost by the number of units in the batch.
- **Economic Batch Quantity (EBQ):** The batch size that minimizes total inventory holding costs and machine setup/order costs:

$$\text{EBQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times D \times S}{C}}$$

Where:
- $D$ = Annual demand (units)
- $S$ = Setup / tooling cost per batch
- $C$ = Annual inventory carrying cost per unit

### 3.2 Contract Costing (Construction & Civil Works)
Contract costing applies to large, long-term construction works executed at site for an external client.
1. **Architect's / Surveyor's Certificate:** Certifies the value of work completed to date (**Value of Work Certified**).
2. **Work Uncertified:** Work completed since the last inspection but not yet formally certified by the architect, valued at actual cost.
3. **Retention Money:** A percentage of the certified work withheld by the contractee as security against defect liability:

$$\text{Cash Received} = \text{Work Certified} \times (1 - \text{Retention \%})$$

4. **Escalation Clause:** A contractual provision permitting upward revision of contract price if prices of raw materials or labour wage rates rise beyond specified limits during execution.
5. **Profit on Incomplete Contracts (Traditional / Exam Benchmark Rules):**
   - **Completion < 25%:** No profit taken to Profit & Loss Account; entire notional profit transferred to Work-in-Progress reserve.
   - **Completion 25% to < 50%:**
     
     $$\text{Profit Transferred to P&L} = \frac{1}{3} \times \text{Notional Profit} \times \frac{\text{Cash Received}}{\text{Work Certified}}$$

   - **Completion 50% to 90%:**
     
     $$\text{Profit Transferred to P&L} = \frac{2}{3} \times \text{Notional Profit} \times \frac{\text{Cash Received}}{\text{Work Certified}}$$

   - **Near Completion (> 90%):** Estimated total profit is computed, and proportional profit is recognized based on work certified or cost incurred.

### 3.3 Process Costing, Losses, and Equivalent Units
Process costing is applied where production flows through a continuous series of distinct, sequential processes (e.g., chemical plants, oil refineries, textiles).
1. **Normal Loss:** Unavoidable loss inherent in the manufacturing process (due to evaporation, shrinkage, chemical reaction).
   - Normal loss is treated as part of the cost of good production.
   - Scrap value realized from normal loss is **credited to the respective Process Account**, thereby reducing the total process cost.
2. **Abnormal Loss:** Controllable loss arising from plant breakdown, sub-standard materials, or operator negligence exceeding expected normal loss.
   - Valued at the full unit cost of good production.
   - Transferred to the **Costing Profit & Loss Account**; never loaded onto good units.
3. **Abnormal Gain / Effect:** When actual output exceeds expected output (actual loss < normal loss).
   - Valued identically to good output and credited to Costing Profit & Loss Account.
4. **Equivalent Production Units:** The expression of partially completed work-in-progress (WIP) in terms of fully completed equivalent units:

$$\text{Equivalent Units} = \text{Uncompleted Units} \times \text{Degree of Completion (\%age)}$$

### 3.4 Joint Products and By-Products
- **Joint Products:** Two or more products of significant economic value emerging simultaneously from a single raw material process (e.g., Gasoline, Diesel, Kerosene from crude oil).
  - Apportionment methods: Physical Units Method, Sales Value at Split-off Point, Net Realizable Value (NRV).
- **By-Products:** Products of minor commercial value emerging incidentally during the manufacture of the main product (e.g., Molasses in sugar refining; Bagasse in sugarcane processing).
  - Net realizable value of by-products is commonly credited to the main product's manufacturing account.

### 3.5 Operating / Service Costing
Applies to service providers where no tangible product is manufactured:
- **Composite Cost Units:**
  - Transport: $\text{Passenger-Kilometers} = \text{Passengers} \times \text{Distance (km)}$
  - Goods Freight: $\text{Tonne-Kilometers} = \text{Tonnes Carried} \times \text{Distance (km)}$
  - Hotel: $\text{Room-Days} = \text{Rooms Occupied} \times \text{Days}$
  - Hospital: $\text{Patient-Days} = \text{Beds Occupied} \times \text{Days}$

## 4. PART D: MARGINAL COSTING & MANAGERIAL DECISION MAKING

Marginal costing is a specialized technique where **only variable costs are charged to cost of sales**, and fixed costs are written off against contribution in the period incurred.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  THE MARGINAL COSTING PARADIGM                                     |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Sales Revenue                                                                                     |
|  LESS: Variable Costs (Direct Material + Direct Labour + Variable Overheads)                       |
|  EQUALS: CONTRIBUTION                                                                              |
|  LESS: Fixed Costs (Period Costs)                                                                  |
|  EQUALS: NET OPERATING PROFIT                                                                      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 4.1 Fundamental Equations of Marginal Costing
1. **Contribution Equation:**

$$C = S - V = F + P$$

Where $S$ = Sales, $V$ = Variable Cost, $F$ = Fixed Cost, $P$ = Profit, $C$ = Contribution.

2. **Profit-Volume (P/V) Ratio:** The ratio of contribution to sales, measuring profitability per rupee of turnover:

$$\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Sales}} = \frac{S - V}{S} = 1 - \frac{V}{S}$$

$$\text{P/V Ratio across Two Periods} = \frac{\text{Change in Profit (or Contribution)}}{\text{Change in Sales}} \times 100$$

3. **Break-Even Point (BEP):** The level of activity at which total revenue equals total cost (Zero Profit, Zero Loss):

$$\text{BEP (in physical units)} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Cost}}{\text{Contribution per unit}} = \frac{F}{s - v}$$

$$\text{BEP (in monetary value)} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Cost}}{\text{P/V Ratio}} = \frac{F}{\text{P/V}}$$

4. **Cash Break-Even Point:** Measures the sales volume required to cover only cash fixed outlays (excluding non-cash items such as depreciation):

$$\text{Cash BEP (units)} = \frac{\text{Cash Fixed Costs}}{\text{Contribution per unit}} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost} - \text{Depreciation}}{\text{Contribution per unit}}$$

5. **Margin of Safety (MoS):** The excess of actual or budgeted sales over break-even sales:

$$\text{MoS (value)} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{Break-Even Sales}$$

$$\text{MoS (value)} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}}$$

$$\text{MoS \%} = \frac{\text{Actual Sales} - \text{Break-Even Sales}}{\text{Actual Sales}} \times 100 = \frac{\text{MoS}}{\text{Actual Sales}} \times 100$$

$$\text{Total Sales} = \text{Break-Even Sales} + \text{Margin of Safety}$$

$$\text{Profit} = \text{Margin of Safety} \times \text{P/V Ratio}$$

### 4.2 Managerial Decisions Guided by Marginal Costing
1. **Make or Buy Decisions:** An item should be manufactured internally if the **marginal cost of production is less than the external purchase price**, provided spare capacity exists and fixed costs are unaffected.
2. **Accepting an Export / Special Order:** An order below prevailing domestic price can be accepted if:
   - Selling price exceeds marginal cost ($P > V$).
   - Regular domestic market prices are not disrupted.
   - Surplus production capacity exists without displacing existing profitable sales.
3. **Key / Limiting Factor (Principal Budget Factor):** When resources (raw materials, skilled labour hours, machine time) are constrained, products must be ranked and manufactured according to **Contribution per unit of Limiting Factor**:

$$\text{Ranking Metric} = \frac{\text{Contribution per unit}}{\text{Limiting Factor required per unit}}$$

4. **Shutdown Point:** The sales volume below which it is more economical to suspend operations temporarily rather than continue:

$$\text{Shutdown Point (units)} = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Cost} - \text{Unavoidable / Shutdown Costs}}{\text{Contribution per unit}} = \frac{\text{Avoidable Fixed Costs}}{\text{Contribution per unit}}$$

## 5. PART E: ABSORPTION COSTING & PROFIT RECONCILIATION

### 5.1 Absorption Costing (Total Costing)
Under Absorption Costing, both fixed and variable manufacturing costs are charged to products. Units of inventory absorb a portion of fixed manufacturing overhead.

- **Overhead Absorption Rate (OAR):**

$$\text{OAR} = \frac{\text{Budgeted Overhead}}{\text{Budgeted Base (Labour Hours, Machine Hours, or Units)}}$$

- **Overheads Absorbed:**

$$\text{Absorbed Overheads} = \text{Actual Base} \times \text{Pre-determined OAR}$$

- **Under / Over-Absorption:**
  - $\text{Under-Absorption} = \text{Actual Overheads} > \text{Absorbed Overheads}$ (Debited to Costing P&L).
  - $\text{Over-Absorption} = \text{Absorbed Overheads} > \text{Actual Overheads}$ (Credited to Costing P&L).

### 5.2 Profit Reconciliation: Marginal vs Absorption Costing

| Production vs Sales Volume | Relationship Between Opening & Closing Inventory | Profit Comparison | Underlying Accounting Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Production > Sales** | $\text{Closing Stock} > \text{Opening Stock}$ | **Absorption Profit > Marginal Profit** | Fixed overhead is capitalized inside closing inventory under absorption costing and carried forward to next period. |
| **Sales > Production** | $\text{Opening Stock} > \text{Closing Stock}$ | **Marginal Profit > Absorption Profit** | Previously capitalized fixed overheads in opening inventory are released and charged to the current period under absorption. |
| **Production = Sales** | $\text{Closing Stock} = \text{Opening Stock}$ | **Absorption Profit = Marginal Profit** | No inventory volume change; fixed overheads charged equal period fixed cost under both methods. |

$$\text{Difference in Profit} = (\text{Closing Inventory Units} - \text{Opening Inventory Units}) \times \text{Fixed Overhead Absorption Rate per unit}$$

## 6. PART F: STANDARD COSTING & VARIANCE ANALYSIS

Standard Costing is a control system that establishes pre-determined standard costs against which actual performance is measured. The differences are analyzed as **variances**.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                   STANDARD COST VARIANCE TAXONOMY                                  |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  TOTAL MATERIAL COST VARIANCE (MCV)                                                                |
|       = Material Price Variance (MPV) + Material Usage Variance (MUV)                              |
|         [MUV splits into: Material Mix Variance (MMV) + Material Yield Variance (MYV)]             |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  TOTAL LABOUR COST VARIANCE (LCV)                                                                  |
|       = Labour Rate Variance (LRV) + Labour Efficiency Variance (LEV) + Idle Time Variance (ITV)   |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 6.1 Direct Material Variances
1. **Material Cost Variance (MCV):** Difference between standard cost of standard quantity and actual cost of actual quantity:

$$\text{MCV} = (\text{SQ} \times \text{SP}) - (\text{AQ} \times \text{AP})$$

2. **Material Price Variance (MPV):** Arises from paying a different price than standard, evaluated on **actual quantity purchased/used**:

$$\text{MPV} = \text{AQ} \times (\text{SP} - \text{AP})$$

3. **Material Usage / Quantity Variance (MUV):** Arises from consuming more or less quantity than standard, valued at **standard price**:

$$\text{MUV} = \text{SP} \times (\text{SQ} - \text{AQ})$$

**Reconciliation Identity:**

$$\text{MCV} = \text{MPV} + \text{MUV}$$

### 6.2 Direct Labour Variances
1. **Labour Cost Variance (LCV):**

$$\text{LCV} = (\text{SH} \times \text{SR}) - (\text{AH} \times \text{AR})$$

2. **Labour Rate Variance (LRV):** Evaluated on **actual hours paid**:

$$\text{LRV} = \text{AH} \times (\text{SR} - \text{AR})$$

3. **Labour Efficiency Variance (LEV):** Evaluated at **standard wage rate**:

$$\text{LEV} = \text{SR} \times (\text{SH} - \text{AH})$$

4. **Idle Time Variance (ITV):** Arises due to abnormal idle time (power failure, strikes); **always Adverse (A)**:

$$\text{ITV} = \text{Abnormal Idle Hours} \times \text{Standard Rate}$$

**Reconciliation Identity:**

$$\text{LCV} = \text{LRV} + \text{LEV} + \text{ITV}$$

### 6.3 Overhead Variances
- **Variable Overhead Variance:**
  - $\text{VO Expenditure Variance} = (\text{Actual Hours} \times \text{Standard Rate}) - \text{Actual VO Cost}$
  - $\text{VO Efficiency Variance} = \text{Standard Rate} \times (\text{Standard Hours} - \text{Actual Hours})$
- **Fixed Overhead Variance:**
  - $\text{FO Expenditure Variance} = \text{Budgeted Fixed Overhead} - \text{Actual Fixed Overhead}$
  - $\text{FO Volume Variance} = (\text{Standard Hours for Actual Output} - \text{Budgeted Hours}) \times \text{Standard FO Rate}$

## 7. PART G: BUDGETARY CONTROL & CASH BUDGETS

A budget is a financial or quantitative statement prepared prior to a defined period of time to pursue a given objective. Budgetary control is the system of continuous comparison of actuals with budgets.

### 7.1 Classification of Budgets
1. **Fixed Budget:** Designed to remain unchanged irrespective of the volume of output or turnover achieved. Effective only in highly predictable environments.
2. **Flexible Budget:** Recognizes the difference between fixed, semi-variable, and variable costs, and is designed to change in relation to the level of activity actually attained. Vital for meaningful performance evaluation.
3. **Master Budget:** The consolidated summary budget incorporating all functional budgets (Sales, Production, Material, Cash, Capital Expenditure) culminating in a budgeted Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Account.
4. **Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB):**
   - Pioneered by **Peter Pyhrr** at Texas Instruments.
   - Does not take previous year's figures as a base (unlike incremental budgeting).
   - Starts from a "zero base" for every new budget period.
   - Every program, project, or expense must be justified afresh through structured **Decision Packages**.
   - Decision packages are evaluated and ranked based on cost-benefit analysis before allocating funds.

### 7.2 Cash Budget Formulation in Commercial Enterprises & Banks
The Cash Budget forecasts expected cash inflows and cash outflows over specified operational intervals (monthly or quarterly).

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    CASH BUDGET FORMAT ARCHITECTURE                                 |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Opening Cash / Bank Balance                                                                       |
|  ADD: Operational Cash Receipts (Cash Sales, Debtors Collections, Interest/Dividends Received)     |
|  ADD: Capital Receipts (Issue of shares/debentures, Sale of fixed assets)                          |
|  TOTAL CASH AVAILABLE (A)                                                                          |
|                                                                                                    |
|  LESS: Operational Cash Outflows (Cash Purchases, Creditors Payments, Wages, Operating Expenses)   |
|  LESS: Capital Outflows (Purchase of Fixed Assets, Debt Redemption, Taxes Paid, Dividends Paid)    |
|  TOTAL CASH DISBURSEMENTS (B)                                                                      |
|                                                                                                    |
|  CLOSING CASH BALANCE = (A) - (B)                                                                  |
|  FINANCING ARRANGEMENT (Overdraft drawn / short-term surplus invested)                             |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Golden Rule of Cash Budgets:**
> **Depreciation is a non-cash expense and is STRICTLY EXCLUDED from Cash Budgets**. Only actual cash receipts (e.g., realization from debtors) and cash disbursements (e.g., payments to creditors, purchase of assets, tax payments) enter the budget.

## Practice Questions & Solved Numerical Drills

#### Question 1 (Marginal Costing & Break-Even Computation)
A manufacturing division sells its product at ₹50 per unit. Variable cost is ₹30 per unit, and annual fixed overheads are ₹6,00,000. For the year:
1. Compute the P/V Ratio, Break-Even Point in units, and Break-Even Sales value.
2. Compute the sales value required to earn a target profit of ₹2,00,000.
3. If actual sales are ₹20,00,000, compute the Margin of Safety in value and percentage.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Contribution per unit:** $C = S - V = ₹50 - ₹30 = ₹20$.
   - $\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{C}{S} \times 100 = \frac{20}{50} \times 100 = 40\%$.
   - $\text{BEP (units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{C} = \frac{6,00,000}{20} = 30,000 \text{ units}$.
   - $\text{BEP (value)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{P/V Ratio}} = \frac{6,00,000}{0.40} = ₹15,00,000$.
2. **Required Sales for Target Profit:**
   - $\text{Required Sales} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost} + \text{Target Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}} = \frac{6,00,000 + 2,00,000}{0.40} = \frac{8,00,000}{0.40} = ₹20,00,000$.
3. **Margin of Safety (MoS):**
   - $\text{MoS (value)} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{BEP Sales} = ₹20,00,000 - ₹15,00,000 = ₹5,00,000$.
   - $\text{MoS \%} = \frac{\text{MoS}}{\text{Actual Sales}} \times 100 = \frac{5,00,000}{20,00,000} \times 100 = 25\%$.
   - *Verification:* $\text{Profit} = \text{MoS} \times \text{P/V Ratio} = ₹5,00,000 \times 40\% = ₹2,00,000$. Matches exactly.

#### Question 2 (Standard Costing Variance Analysis)
Standard material for 1 unit of product is 5 kg at ₹20 per kg. During March, 1,000 units were produced using 5,200 kg of raw material purchased at ₹22 per kg.
Compute:
1. Material Cost Variance (MCV)
2. Material Price Variance (MPV)
3. Material Usage Variance (MUV)
Verify that $MCV = MPV + MUV$.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
- $\text{Standard Quantity (SQ)} = 1,000 \times 5 = 5,000 \text{ kg}$.
- $\text{Standard Price (SP)} = ₹20 \text{ per kg}$.
- $\text{Actual Quantity (AQ)} = 5,200 \text{ kg}$.
- $\text{Actual Price (AP)} = ₹22 \text{ per kg}$.
1. **Material Cost Variance (MCV):**
   - $\text{MCV} = (\text{SQ} \times \text{SP}) - (\text{AQ} \times \text{AP}) = (5,000 \times 20) - (5,200 \times 22) = 1,00,000 - 1,14,400 = -₹14,400 = \mathbf{14,400 \text{ (Adverse)}}$.
2. **Material Price Variance (MPV):**
   - $\text{MPV} = \text{AQ} \times (\text{SP} - \text{AP}) = 5,200 \times (20 - 22) = 5,200 \times (-2) = -₹10,400 = \mathbf{10,400 \text{ (Adverse)}}$.
3. **Material Usage Variance (MUV):**
   - $\text{MUV} = \text{SP} \times (\text{SQ} - \text{AQ}) = 20 \times (5,000 - 5,200) = 20 \times (-200) = -₹4,000 = \mathbf{4,000 \text{ (Adverse)}}$.
4. **Verification:**
   - $\text{MPV} + \text{MUV} = 10,400 \text{ (A)} + 4,000 \text{ (A)} = 14,400 \text{ (A)} = \text{MCV}$. Verified.

## Active Recall & Self-Diagnostic Prompts

<details>
<summary>Prompt 1: Why does Absorption Costing show higher profit than Marginal Costing when production exceeds sales?</summary>

When production exceeds sales, closing stock exceeds opening stock. Under Absorption Costing, a portion of fixed manufacturing overhead is absorbed into each unit of closing stock and carried forward as an asset on the balance sheet rather than written off in the current period. Under Marginal Costing, all fixed overhead is written off immediately against the current period's contribution. Hence, absorption costing reflects higher net profit by the exact value of fixed overhead capitalized inside closing inventory.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Prompt 2: What is the exact distinction between Section 194A TDS on Senior Citizens versus General Citizens?</summary>

General individuals are subject to 10% TDS when annual bank interest on term deposits exceeds ₹40,000 per bank. For Senior Citizens (age 60 years and above), Section 194A read with Section 80TTB establishes a higher statutory threshold of ₹50,000 per bank per financial year.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Prompt 3: How is the Margin of Safety calculated, and what does it signify to a credit analyst?</summary>

$\text{Margin of Safety} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{Break-Even Sales} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}}$. It signifies the cushion or buffer by which sales turnover can drop before the business begins making operating losses. A high MoS indicates low business risk and strong resilience against downturns.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Prompt 4: Why is depreciation omitted from a Cash Budget but included in a Capital Budgeting CFAT calculation?</summary>

In a Cash Budget, only actual cash movements are recorded; depreciation is a non-cash accounting allocation of past capital cost, so no cash leaves the bank account. In Capital Budgeting, operating cash flow after tax ($\text{CFAT} = \text{PAT} + \text{Depreciation}$) includes depreciation add-back because depreciation provides an operational tax shield ($\text{Depreciation} \times t$), reducing tax outflows without consuming actual cash.
</details>
