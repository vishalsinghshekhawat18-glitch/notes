## 1. The Ten Agro-Climatic Zones of Rajasthan (ICAR Classification)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) and the Department of Agriculture classify Rajasthan into **Ten Distinct Agro-Climatic Zones** based on rainfall, soil taxonomy, temperature extremes, and cropping patterns.

```
                      THE TEN AGRO-CLIMATIC ZONES OF RAJASTHAN
  ZONE I: ARID NORTH-WESTERN & TRANSITIONAL:
  ├── Zone I-A:  Arid Western Plain (Barmer, Jodhpur).
  ├── Zone I-B:  Irrigated North-Western Plain (Sri Ganganagar, Hanumangarh).
  └── Zone I-C:  Hyper-Arid Partially Irrigated Zone (Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Churu) ──► LARGEST ZONE!
  
  ZONE II: TRANSITIONAL PLAINS (INLAND & LUNI):
  ├── Zone II-A: Internal Drainage Dry Zone (Nagaur, Sikar, Jhunjhunu).
  └── Zone II-B: Transitional Plain of Luni Basin (Jalore, Pali, Sirohi).
  
  ZONE III: EASTERN SEMI-ARID & FLOOD-PRONE:
  ├── Zone III-A: Semi-Arid Eastern Plain (Jaipur, Ajmer, Tonk, Dausa).
  └── Zone III-B: Flood Prone Eastern Plain (Alwar, Bharatpur, Dholpur, Karauli, Sawai Madhopur).
  
  ZONE IV & V: SOUTHERN SUB-HUMID & SOUTH-EASTERN HUMID:
  ├── Zone IV-A: Sub-Humid Southern Plain & Aravalli Hills (Bhilwara, Rajsamand, Chittorgarh, Udaipur).
  ├── Zone IV-B: Humid Southern Plain (Dungarpur, Banswara, Pratapgarh) ──► SMALLEST ZONE!
  └── Zone V:    Humid South-Eastern Plain (Kota, Baran, Bundi, Jhalawar: Hadoti).
```

| Zone Code | Official Agro-Climatic Nomenclature | Districts Encompassed | Rainfall Range & Soil Type | Dominant Crops & Diagnostic Trait |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zone I-A** | Arid Western Plain | Barmer, Jodhpur | $20\text{–}37\text{ cm}$; Desert sandy soils (Aridisols) | Bajra, Moth, Til, Guar, Cumin (Jeera), Isabgol. Extreme summer heat and droughts. |
| **Zone I-B** | Irrigated North-Western Plain | Sri Ganganagar, Hanumangarh | $10\text{–}30\text{ cm}$; Alluvial and desert alluvium | **Canal-irrigated granary** (IGNP & Gang Canal): Cotton (Narma), Wheat, Mustard, Kinnow. |
| **Zone I-C** | **Hyper-Arid Partially Irrigated** | **Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Churu** | **$< 10\text{–}20\text{ cm}$ (Driest)**; Sandy dune soils | **LARGEST AGRO-CLIMATIC ZONE IN RAJASTHAN BY AREA**. Bajra, Moth, Chana, Groundnut under IGNP. |
| **Zone II-A** | Internal Drainage Dry Zone | Sikar, Jhunjhunu, Nagaur | $30\text{–}50\text{ cm}$; Sandy loam, calcic | Pulses (Moong, Moth), Bajra, Mustard. Shekhawati inland drainage. |
| **Zone II-B** | Transitional Plain of Luni Basin | Jalore, Pali, parts of Sirohi | $30\text{–}50\text{ cm}$; Red-brown loams | Cumin (Jeera), Isabgol, Castor (*Arandi*), Wheat, Mustard. Godwar tract. |
| **Zone III-A** | Semi-Arid Eastern Plain | Jaipur, Ajmer, Tonk, Dausa | $50\text{–}70\text{ cm}$; Loamy sand / alluvium | Bajra, Mustard, Barley, Wheat, Gram. Drained by Banas and Banganga. |
| **Zone III-B** | Flood Prone Eastern Plain | Alwar, Bharatpur, Dholpur, Karauli, Sawai Madhopur | $50\text{–}80\text{ cm}$; Deep alluvial soils | **Mustard capital of India** (Bharatpur), Wheat, Bajra, Sugarcane. Chambal-Banganga basin. |
| **Zone IV-A** | Sub-Humid Southern Plain | Bhilwara, Rajsamand, Chittorgarh, parts of Udaipur | $50\text{–}90\text{ cm}$; Red loamy and mixed black soils | Maize (*Makka* - staple crop of Mewar), Sorghum (Jowar), Wheat, Gram, Cotton. |
| **Zone IV-B** | **Humid Southern Plain** | **Dungarpur, Banswara, Pratapgarh** | $70\text{–}100\text{ cm}$; Deep red loamy soils | **SMALLEST AGRO-CLIMATIC ZONE IN RAJASTHAN BY AREA**. Maize, Rice, Cotton, Soybean, Sugarcane. |
| **Zone V** | **Humid South-Eastern Plain** | **Kota, Baran, Bundi, Jhalawar (Hadoti)** | **$80\text{–}120\text{ cm}$ (Wettest)**; Deep **Black Vertisols** | **Soybean (highest in state)**, Coriander (*Dhaniya* in Baran), Oranges (Jhalawar: "Nagpur of Rajasthan"), Opium. |

## 2. Modern USDA Soil Taxonomy in Rajasthan

The Department of Agriculture and ICAR classify Rajasthan's soils into five master USDA soil orders:

```
                      THE FIVE USDA SOIL ORDERS IN RAJASTHAN
  1. ARIDISOLS: Western & Central Rajasthan (Churu, Sikar, Jhunjhunu, Nagaur, Jodhpur, Pali, Jalore).
     └── Aridic moisture regime; light-colored ochric epipedon, caliche layers.
  2. ENTISOLS: Entire Western desert (Jaisalmer, Barmer, Bikaner) & riverine alluvium.
     └── Torripsamments (Sand dunes, wind-blown sands); raw mineral substrate, zero horizons.
  3. ALFISOLS: Eastern & South-Eastern Plains (Jaipur, Alwar, Bharatpur, Sawai Madhopur, Tonk).
     └── Mature profile; clay illuviation (argillic horizon); high base saturation (>35%); highly fertile.
  4. INCEPTISOLS: Aravalli foothills & Southern Plateau (Sirohi, Pali, Udaipur, Rajsamand, Chittorgarh).
     └── Embryonic development; shallow, red loamy and mountain soils.
  5. VERTISOLS: Hadoti Plateau (Kota, Baran, Bundi, Jhalawar).
     └── Heavy black soils; >30% montmorillonite clay; deep shrinkage cracks; self-plowing.
```

## 3. Forest Resources of Rajasthan: ISFR & Statutory Classification

### 1. Statutory Forest Classification (Rajasthan Forest Act, 1953)
The Total Recorded Forest Area (RFA) in Rajasthan is **$32,863\text{ km}^2$** (~**$9.60\%$ of the state's total geographical area**), partitioned legally into three classes:

```
                      STATUTORY CLASSIFICATION (RFA)
  1. PROTECTED FORESTS (Surakshit Van) ──► ~56.49% (LARGEST LEGAL CATEGORY)
     └── Regulated biotic activity permitted; local communities can graze cattle and collect dry wood under permit.
  2. RESERVED FORESTS (Aarakshit Van)   ──► ~37.05% (STRICTEST CATEGORY)
     └── Total state monopoly; grazing, timber felling, and private entry completely prohibited.
  3. UNCLASSED FORESTS (Avargikrit Van) ──► ~ 6.46% (SMALLEST CATEGORY)
     └── Undemarcated wasteland forests; tree felling and grazing permitted subject to state government royalties.
```

### 3. Natural Forest Types of Rajasthan (Champion & Seth)
1. **Tropical Dry Deciduous (Dhok / Dhokra) Forests**:
   * **The undisputed dominant tree of Rajasthan**: **Dhok (*Anogeissus pendula*) covers over $58\%$ of the total forest area**.
   * Found across Alwar, Bharatpur, Sawai Madhopur, Kota, Bundi, Chittorgarh, Udaipur. Excellent for high-calorific firewood and charcoal.
2. **Tropical Thorn Forests**:
   * Covers ~$6\text{–}7\%$ of forest area across arid and semi-arid tracts (Jodhpur, Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Barmer, Nagaur).
   * Dominated by **Khejri (*Prosopis cineraria*)**, Babool, Khair, Ber, Rohida, and thorny shrubs.
3. **Sub-Tropical Mountain Evergreen Forests**:
   * Covers **less than $0.5\%$ of forest area**; confined exclusively to the high-elevation slopes of **Mount Abu ($>1,200\text{ m}$ elevation)** in Sirohi district.
   * Dominant species: Wild Mango (*Mangifera indica*), Jamun, Bamboo, and the rare endemic medicinal herb **$Dicliptera\ abuensis$** (*Ambervat*).

## 4. Protected Area Network of Rajasthan: National Parks, Tiger Reserves & Sanctuaries

```
                      PROTECTED AREA NETWORK OF RAJASTHAN
   ├── 1. National Parks: THREE (Ranthambore, Keoladeo Ghana, Mukundra Hills).
   ├── 2. Tiger Reserves: FIVE (Ranthambore, Sariska, Mukundra Hills, Ramgarh Vishdhari, Dholpur-Karauli).
   ├── 3. Ramsar Wetland Sites: TWO (Keoladeo Ghana NP & Sambhar Salt Lake).
   ├── 4. Wildlife Sanctuaries: 27 Designated State Sanctuaries.
   └── 5. Conservation Reserves: Over 30 Designated (Sundhamata, Jawai Bandh, Mansamata, Beed Fatehpur, etc.).
```

---

### 1. The Three National Parks of Rajasthan
1. **Ranthambore National Park (Sawai Madhopur)**:
   * Declared Wildlife Sanctuary in 1955; designated **National Park on November 1, 1980 ($282\text{ km}^2$)**; India's premier tiger reserve under **Project Tiger (1973)**.
   * Nestled at the confluence of the Aravalli and Vindhyan mountain ranges; dotted with lakes (Padam Talao, Rajbagh, Malik Talao) and the 10th-century **Ranthambore Fort**; famous for daytime sightings of Royal Bengal Tigers (*Machhli* the legendary tigress).
2. **Keoladeo Ghana National Park (Bharatpur)**:
   * Declared National Park in **1981 ($28.73\text{ km}^2$)**; inscribed as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985**; designated a **Ramsar Wetland Site in 1990**.
   * An artificial, man-made wetland created by Maharaja Suraj Mal using the Ajan Dam on the Banganga and Gambhir rivers. World-renowned as an international **winter bird sanctuary hosting over 370 migratory avian species**, historically the winter home of the critically endangered **Siberian Crane (*Grus leucogeranus*)**.
3. **Mukundra Hills (Darrah) National Park (Kota & Jhalawar)**:
   * Formally notified as National Park in **2012 ($199.55\text{ km}^2$)**; encompasses Darrah, Chambal, and Jaswant Sagar wildlife sanctuaries; named after the scholar-poet **Kavi Mukund Rao**.
