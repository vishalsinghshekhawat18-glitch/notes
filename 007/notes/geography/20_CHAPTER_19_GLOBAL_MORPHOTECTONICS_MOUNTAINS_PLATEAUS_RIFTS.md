## 1. Global Orogenic Systems: World Mountain Belts

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Mountains are elevated portions of the Earth's lithosphere rising prominently above surrounding topography, classified into four distinct morphotectonic genetic categories: **Fold Mountains**, **Block Mountains (Horsts)**, **Volcanic Mountains**, and **Residual (Dissected) Mountains**.
> 
> The grandest mountain belts on Earth are **Young Fold Mountains** generated during the Cenozoic (Tertiary) Alpine Orogeny along active convergent plate boundaries through intense crustal shortening, buckling, and thrust-nappe faulting.

```
                    CHRONOLOGICAL ORIDGENIC EPOCHS
  1. PRE-CAMBRIAN OROGENIES (> 540 Ma):
     └── Ancient crystalline cratonic roots; severely eroded (Laurentian, Baltic, Fennoscandian, Dharwar).
  2. CALEDONIAN OROGENY (~400 Ma / Silurian-Devonian):
     └── Scottish Highlands, Scandinavian Mountains, Grampians, older Appalachian roots.
  3. HERCYNIAN / VARISCAN OROGENY (~250–300 Ma / Carboniferous-Permian):
     └── Ural Mountains, Harz Mountains, Vosges, Black Forest, Tien Shan, Altai, Pennines.
  4. ALPINE-HIMALAYAN & CIRCUM-PACIFIC OROGENY (~30–65 Ma / Cenozoic):
     └── Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas, Caucasus, Pyrenees, Zagros, Karakoram.
```

## 2. World Plateaus: Intermontane, Volcanic & Continental Shields

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> A **Plateau** (or Tableland) is an extensive elevated tract of relatively level land that rises abruptly above surrounding terrain on at least one side.
> 
> Plateaus are classified into four morphogenetic categories based on tectonic setting:
> 1. **Intermontane Plateaus**: Plateaus completely surrounded and hemmed in by fold mountain ranges; the highest and most complex plateaus on Earth.
> 2. **Piedmont Plateaus**: Plateaus situated at the foot of mountain ranges, bounded on the other side by low plains or oceans.
> 3. **Volcanic / Lava Plateaus**: Constructed by successive horizontal sheets of fluid, low-viscosity basaltic lava erupted through fissure eruptions.
> 4. **Continental Shields / Relict Plateaus**: Ancient, stable, crystalline Precambrian masses that form the geological cores of modern continents.

```
GLOBAL PLATEAU INVENTORY
  ├── 1. Intermontane Plateaus (Highest & Vastest):
  │      ├── Tibetan Plateau: "Roof of the World" (Mean elev > 4,500 m; ~2.5 million km²).
  │      ├── Bolivian Altiplano: Hemmed by Cordillera Occidental & Oriental (Host to Lake Titicaca at 3,812 m).
  │      ├── Colorado Plateau: Hemmed by Rockies; dissected by Colorado River (Grand Canyon).
  │      ├── Anatolian Plateau: Hemmed by Pontic & Taurus mountains in Turkey.
  │      └── Iranian Plateau: Hemmed by Elburz & Zagros mountains.
  │
  ├── 2. Volcanic Lava Plateaus (Flood Basalt Provinces):
  │      ├── Deccan Traps (India): Upper Cretaceous basaltic lava flows (~500,000 km²).
  │      ├── Columbia-Snake River Plateau (USA): Miocene basalt flows dissected by Columbia river.
  │      ├── Ethiopian Highlands: East African volcanic shield; source of Blue Nile.
  │      └── Paraná Basin (Brazil): Immense Cretaceous basalt plateau.
  │
  └── 3. Continental Shields & Relict Plateaus:
         ├── Canadian (Laurentian) Shield: Exposed Precambrian granite/gneiss; vast lakes and mineral wealth.
         ├── Guiana Highlands: Ancient sandstone tablelands (*Tepuis*); hosts Angel Falls.
         ├── Brazilian Shield (Mato Grosso / Minas Gerais): Iron ore deposits.
         ├── Patagonian Plateau: Arid, cold basaltic-gravel plateau in rain shadow of southern Andes.
         └── Kimberley Plateau: Ancient rugged sandstone/basalt plateau in NW Australia.
```

## 3. World Rift Valleys & Structural Basins

### The East African Great Rift Valley (EARS)
* **Tectonic Architecture**: The most spectacular active continental rift system on Earth, stretching over **$6,400\text{ km}$** from the **Afar Triple Junction** south through Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, to Mozambique.
* **The Afar Triple Junction**: A junction of three tectonic spreading rifts meeting at angles of ~120°:
  1. The **Red Sea Rift** (separating Africa and Arabia).
  2. The **Gulf of Aden Rift** (extending to Carlsberg Ridge).
  3. The **East African Rift System (Main Ethiopian Rift)**.
* **The Two Western & Eastern Branches**:
  * **Eastern Rift (Gregory Rift)**: Passes through Ethiopia and Kenya; dotted with shallow, highly alkaline soda lakes (Lake Natron, Lake Bogoria, Lake Nakuru) and active volcanoes (Mount Kilimanjaro, Mount Kenya, Ol Doinyo Lengai).
  * **Western Rift (Albertine Rift)**: Bordered by massive fault scarps; hosts the deepest freshwater graben lakes on the African continent:
    * **Lake Tanganyika**: Second deepest lake in the world ($1,470\text{ m}$ deep); second largest by volume.
    * **Lake Malawi (Nyasa)**: $706\text{ m}$ deep; extraordinarily rich in endemic cichlid fish species.
    * **Lake Albert, Lake Edward, Lake Kivu**.
  * **Lake Victoria Paradox**: Lake Victoria is the largest lake in Africa by surface area (~$68,800\text{ km}^2$), but it is **NOT located inside a rift valley graben**; it sits in a shallow structural sag or downwarp between the Eastern and Western rift branches (max depth only ~$80\text{ meters}$).
