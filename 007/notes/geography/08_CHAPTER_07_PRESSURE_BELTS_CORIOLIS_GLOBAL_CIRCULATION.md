## 1. Atmospheric Pressure & The Forces Governing Winds

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Wind is the horizontal movement of air generated by unequal atmospheric heating across latitudes. Air does not simply blow straight from high pressure to low pressure; its trajectory is governed by a delicate vector equilibrium between four fundamental physical forces: the **Pressure Gradient Force (PGF)**, the **Coriolis Force**, the **Centrifugal Force**, and **Surface Friction**.

* **Standard Sea-Level Atmospheric Pressure**: The weight exerted by a vertical column of air resting on a unit area:

$$\text{Standard Sea-Level Pressure} = 1,013.25\text{ mb (hPa)} = 760\text{ mm of Hg} = 29.92\text{ inches of Hg} = 101.325\text{ kPa}$$

* **Isobars**: Lines drawn on a weather map connecting geographic points of equal atmospheric pressure reduced to mean sea level. The spacing between isobars indicates the pressure gradient:
  * Closely spaced isobars = **Steep Pressure Gradient** $\implies$ High wind velocities.
  * Widely spaced isobars = **Gentle Pressure Gradient** $\implies$ Light breezes or calm conditions.

```
                  THE FOUR FORCES GOVERNING AIR TRAJECTORY
  1. PRESSURE GRADIENT FORCE (PGF)
     └── Acts perpendicular (at 90°) to isobars, directed from HIGH to LOW pressure.
  2. CORIOLIS FORCE (Fc)
     └── Apparent deflecting force caused by Earth's rotation; acts perpendicular to wind velocity.
  3. FRICTIONAL FORCE (Ff)
     └── Surface drag retarding wind velocity in lowest 1–2 km; diminishes Coriolis deflection.
  4. CENTRIFUGAL FORCE (Fce)
     └── Outward radial force generated whenever air flows along curved cyclonic or anticyclonic paths.
```

---

### The Physics of the Coriolis Force

Formulated mathematically by French engineer **Gaspard-Gustave de Coriolis** in 1835:

$$F_c = 2 \cdot m \cdot v \cdot \Omega \cdot \sin \phi$$

*Where $m$ is mass of air parcel, $v$ is wind velocity, $\Omega$ is Earth's angular rotational velocity ($7.292 \times 10^{-5}\text{ rad/s}$), and $\phi$ is latitude.*

```
                   CORIOLIS FORCE LATITUDINAL GRADIENT
                     POLES (90°): sin 90° = 1.0  ──► MAXIMUM CORIOLIS FORCE
                          │
                          ▼
                     MID-LATITUDES (45°): sin 45° = 0.707
                          │
                          ▼
                     EQUATOR (0°): sin 0° = 0.0  ──► ZERO CORIOLIS FORCE
```

#### Core Physical Properties of the Coriolis Force
1. **Deflection Direction (Ferrel's Law, 1856)**: In the **Northern Hemisphere**, moving air deflects to its **RIGHT**; in the **Southern Hemisphere**, moving air deflects to its **LEFT**.
2. **Dependence on Latitude ($\sin \phi$)**: The Coriolis force is **ZERO at the Equator ($\phi = 0^\circ$)** and increases steadily toward the poles, reaching its **MAXIMUM at the Poles ($\phi = 90^\circ$)**.
3. **Speed vs. Direction**: The Coriolis force **NEVER changes wind speed**; it acts at right angles to the direction of motion, altering only the **direction** of air movement.
4. **Why Tropical Cyclones Cannot Form at the Equator**: Tropical cyclones require the Coriolis force to impart rotational spin around a central low. Because $F_c = 0$ at the Equator, converging air flows directly into the low and fills it up without spinning; hence, tropical cyclones **never originate within 0° to 5° of the Equator**.

### Upper-Air Winds: The Geostrophic Balance

In the upper troposphere (above 1 to 2 km altitude), the Earth's surface friction drops to zero. 

> ⚙️ **Causal Mechanism & Physical Dynamics: The Geostrophic Wind**
> When air begins moving under the influence of the Pressure Gradient Force (PGF) from high to low pressure, the Coriolis force deflects it to the right (in NH). 
> As wind velocity increases, the Coriolis force grows stronger until it becomes **exactly equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the Pressure Gradient Force**:
> 
> $$\text{PGF} = F_c \implies -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial P}{\partial n} = 2 v \Omega \sin \phi$$
> 
> At this dynamic equilibrium, the air parcel ceases deflecting toward the low pressure. Instead, it blows **strictly PARALLEL to straight isobars**. This is the **Geostrophic Wind**.

```
                THE GEOSTROPHIC EQUILIBRIUM (Northern Hemisphere)
                       LOW PRESSURE (996 mb)
                                ▲
                                │ Pressure Gradient Force (PGF)
                                │
    Geostrophic Wind ───────────● (Air Parcel) ──────────► Blows Parallel to Isobars
    (Flows Eastward)            │
                                │ Coriolis Force (Fc)
                                ▼
                       HIGH PRESSURE (1004 mb)
```

### Surface Winds: Frictional Cross-Isobar Flow
In the lowest 1,000 meters of the atmosphere (the planetary boundary layer), surface friction retards wind speed ($v$). 
* Because Coriolis force depends on speed ($F_c \propto v$), a reduction in wind speed weakens the Coriolis force.
* As a result, the Coriolis force can no longer fully balance the Pressure Gradient Force.
* The dominant PGF pulls the wind across isobars at an angle toward the low pressure:
  * Over smooth oceans, surface winds cross isobars at **10° to 20°**.
  * Over rough, mountainous continental land, winds cross isobars at **30° to 45°**.

## 2. The Seven Planetary Pressure Belts

The global distribution of surface atmospheric pressure is organized into **seven alternating latitudinal belts**, classified by their origin into **Thermally Induced** and **Dynamically Induced** belts.

```
THE SEVEN PLANETARY PRESSURE BELTS
  ├── 1. Equatorial Low Pressure Belt (5°N to 5°S) ───────────► THERMALLY INDUCED
  │      └── Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) / Doldrums; calm winds, violent vertical convection.
  │
  ├── 2. Subtropical High Pressure Belts (30° to 35° N & S) ────► DYNAMICALLY INDUCED
  │      └── "Horse Latitudes"; descending dry limbs of Hadley & Ferrel cells; calm, cloudless, anticyclonic.
  │
  ├── 3. Sub-Polar Low Pressure Belts (60° to 65° N & S) ─────► DYNAMICALLY INDUCED
  │      └── Convergence of warm westerlies and cold polar easterlies; Polar Front cyclogenesis.
  │
  └── 4. Polar High Pressure Belts (85° to 90° N & S) ────────► THERMALLY INDUCED
         └── Extreme radiational cold and descending air; permanent ice cap anticyclones.
```

```
                                  90°N [POLAR HIGH] (Thermal)
                                   ▲   \ Polar Easterlies
                                   │    ▼
                                  60°N [SUB-POLAR LOW] (Dynamic / Polar Front)
                                   ▲   /
                                   │  /  Prevailing Westerlies
                                   │ /
                                  30°N [SUBTROPICAL HIGH] (Dynamic / Horse Latitudes)
                                   │ \
                                   │  ▼  North-East Trade Winds
                                   ▼   \
                   0° [EQUATORIAL LOW / ITCZ / DOLDRUMS] (Thermal)
                                   ▲   /
                                   │  ▲  South-East Trade Winds
                                   │ /
                                  30°S [SUBTROPICAL HIGH] (Dynamic / Horse Latitudes)
                                   │ \
                                   │  \  Prevailing Westerlies (Roaring Forties)
                                   │   ▼
                                  60°S [SUB-POLAR LOW] (Dynamic)
                                   ▲   / Polar Easterlies
                                   │  ▲
                                  90°S [POLAR HIGH] (Thermal)
```

## 3. Tri-Cellular Meridional Circulation Architecture

The atmospheric circulation does not operate as a single equator-to-pole circulation cell (as George Hadley initially postulated in 1735). The rapid rotation of the Earth and the Coriolis force break global circulation into **three distinct meridional circulation cells** in each hemisphere:

```
TRI-CELLULAR CIRCULATION ENGINE
  ├── 1. HADLEY CELL (0° to 30° Lat): Thermally Direct Tropical Cell
  │      └── Air rises at Equator (Thermal Low) ➔ Flows poleward in upper troposphere ➔ 
  │          Sinks at 30° (Subtropical High) ➔ Returns to Equator as Surface Trade Winds.
  │
  ├── 2. FERREL CELL (30° to 60° Lat): Thermally Indirect Mid-Latitude Cell
  │      └── Driven like a mechanical gear between the Hadley and Polar cells. 
  │          Air sinks at 30° (Subtropical High) ➔ Moves poleward as Surface Westerlies ➔ 
  │          Forced upward at 60° (Sub-Polar Low) ➔ Returns equatorward aloft.
  │
  └── 3. POLAR CELL (60° to 90° Lat): Thermally Direct High-Latitude Cell
         └── Air sinks at Poles (Polar High) ➔ Moves equatorward as Surface Polar Easterlies ➔ 
             Rises at 60° (Sub-Polar Low) ➔ Returns poleward aloft.
```

```
                          TRI-CELLULAR CROSS-SECTION (Northern Hemisphere)
    Altitude
       ▲
       │       [Upper Tropopause]
 15 km │  ◄──────── Upper Divergence ────────┐
       │                                     │
       │  ▲ Rising Warm Air                  ▼ Sinking Air (Dry)
       │  │                                  │
  0 km └──┴────────── North-East Trades ─────┴────────── Surface Westerlies ─────►
          0° (Equator)                      30°N (Subtropics)                   60°N
          ◄────────── HADLEY CELL ──────────►◄────────── FERREL CELL ──────────►
```

## 4. Planetary Wind Systems

Planetary (or Prevailing) winds blow continuously year-round in fixed directions across the globe, driven by the planetary pressure belts.

### 1. The Trade Winds (Tropical Easterlies)
* Blow from the **Subtropical Highs (30°–35°)** toward the **Equatorial Low (0°–5°)**.
* Deflected by Coriolis force:
  * **North-East Trade Winds** in the Northern Hemisphere.
  * **South-East Trade Winds** in the Southern Hemisphere.
* Characterized by exceptional directional consistency and steady speeds (traditionally used by commercial merchant sailing vessels, hence "Trade" from Middle English *tred* = path/track).
* **Rainfall Distribution**: Trade winds gather moisture over tropical oceans and deposit heavy orographic rainfall on the **Eastern coasts of continents** (e.g. northeast Brazil, eastern Madagascar, Coromandel Coast). By the time they reach the **Western margins of continents**, they are desiccated, blowing offshore; this creates the world's hot tropical coastal deserts (Atacama, Namib, Western Australian).

### 2. The Prevailing Westerlies (Mid-Latitude Winds)
* Blow from the **Subtropical Highs (30°–35°)** toward the **Sub-Polar Lows (60°–65°)**.
* Deflected by Coriolis force:
  * **South-Westerlies** in the Northern Hemisphere.
  * **North-Westerlies** in the Southern Hemisphere.
* Bring maritime moisture and frontal rains to the **Western margins of mid-latitude continents** (e.g. British Isles, Western Europe, British Columbia, Southern Chile).
* **The Violent Westerlies of the Southern Hemisphere**:
  * In the Northern Hemisphere, vast continental landmasses with mountain barriers break and disrupt the flow of westerlies.
  * In the **Southern Hemisphere**, between 40°S and 65°S, there is an almost unbroken expanse of continuous ocean with zero continental friction. The westerlies accelerate into permanent, roaring atmospheric tempests, named by sailors according to their latitude:
    * **The Roaring Forties** (40°S to 50°S)
    * **The Furious Fifties** (50°S to 60°S)
    * **The Shrieking / Screaming Sixties** (60°S to 65°S)

### 3. The Polar Easterlies
* Blow from the **Polar Highs (90°)** toward the **Sub-Polar Lows (60°–65°)**.
* Extremely cold, dry, dense winds blowing from the **North-East in NH** and **South-East in SH**.
* Collide violently with the warm, moist westerlies along the sub-polar low, creating the unstable frontal boundary known as the **Polar Front**.

## 5. Seasonal Migration of Planetary Belts & Global Climatic Impacts

Because the Earth's rotational axis is tilted at **23.5°**, the overhead noon sun migrates between the Tropic of Cancer ($23.5^\circ\text{N}$ on June 21) and the Tropic of Capricorn ($23.5^\circ\text{S}$ on December 22).

> ⚙️ **The Thermal Equator Shift**
> As the zone of maximum heating (the Thermal Equator) shifts seasonally, all planetary pressure belts and their associated planetary wind systems **migrate northward by 5° to 10° in July (Northern Summer)** and **southward by 5° to 10° in January (Northern Winter)**.

```
                   SEASONAL MIGRATION OF THE ITCZ & PRESSURE BELTS
   JULY (Northern Summer)          MARCH / SEPT (Equinox)         JANUARY (Northern Winter)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   ITCZ shifts to 15°–20°N         ITCZ centered on Equator (0°)   ITCZ shifts to 10°–15°S
   (Over Ganga Plains / Thar)      Symmetrical wind belts          (Over Northern Australia)
```

### Major Climatic Consequences of Belt Migration
1. **The Mediterranean Climate ($Cs$)**:
   * Situated on the western margins of continents between 30° and 45° latitude (California, Mediterranean basin, central Chile, Cape Town, SW Australia).
   * **In Summer**: Subtropical High pressure belts shift poleward. These regions fall under the dry, sinking, rainless offshore Trade Winds $\implies$ **Hot, bone-dry summers**.
   * **In Winter**: Pressure belts shift equatorward. These regions fall under the moist, onshore rain-bearing Westerlies $\implies$ **Mild, wet, rainy winters**.
2. **The Indian Monsoon Reversal**:
   * In June–July, the ITCZ migrates far north over the heated Indo-Gangetic Plains (forming the **Monsoon Trough**).
   * The South-East Trade Winds of the Southern Hemisphere cross the Equator. Upon crossing into the Northern Hemisphere, the Coriolis force deflects them to their **RIGHT**, transforming them into the moisture-laden **South-West Monsoon** of India.

## 6. Upper Tropospheric Jet Streams & Rossby Waves

Discovered by military aviators during World War II when B-29 bombers flying over Japan encountered headwinds exceeding 300 km/h while remaining nearly stationary over ground targets.

> 🧠 **Key Concept: Jet Streams**
> Jet streams are narrow, meandering, high-velocity atmospheric rivers of geostrophic wind embedded within the upper troposphere (between 9 and 14 km altitude). 
> 
> They flow from **West to East** in both hemispheres, with core velocities ranging from **150 to over 400 km/h**, measuring several hundred kilometers wide and a few kilometers thick.

```
                           THE GLOBAL JET STREAM SYSTEM
  Altitude
     ▲
14 km│                              POLAR FRONT JET (PFJ)
     │                     (Meanders between 40°–60°; 9–12 km altitude;
     │                      steepest thermal contrast; drives weather fronts)
     │                                     ▲
     │  SUBTROPICAL WESTERLY JET (STJ)     │
     │  (Centered at ~30°N & S; 12–14 km;  │
     │   formed by conservation of angular │
     │   momentum at Hadley cell edge)     │
 0 km└───┴─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────► Latitude
         0° (Equator)                      30°                               60°
```

### The Four Major Planetary Jet Streams

| Jet Stream Type | Latitude Domain | Altitude & Velocity | Thermal Genesis & Dynamics | Climatological Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Polar Front Jet Stream (PFJ)** | **40° to 60° N & S** (Highly variable) | Altitude: **9 to 11 km**.<br>Velocity: **150 to 450 km/h** (strongest in winter). | Generated along the steep horizontal thermal gradient where cold polar air meets warm subtropical air at the Polar Front. | Steers mid-latitude temperate cyclones; controls severe cold polar outbreaks and heat domes. |
| **Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream (STJ)** | **25° to 35° N & S** (More persistent) | Altitude: **12 to 14 km**.<br>Velocity: **200 to 350 km/h**. | Formed at the poleward boundary of the Hadley Cell by the **Conservation of Angular Momentum** of air moving poleward from the equator. | Bifurcates around the Tibetan Plateau in winter; the southern branch steers **Western Disturbances** into northwest India, bringing winter rains for Rabi wheat crops. |
| **Tropical Easterly Jet Stream (TEJ)** | **8° to 15°N** *(Northern Summer only)* | Altitude: **14 to 16 km**.<br>Velocity: **100 to 180 km/h**. | Seasonal jet formed during summer by the intense thermal heating of the **Tibetan Plateau** (acting as an elevated heat engine). Upper air diverges outflow to form a strong easterly jet over India and Africa. | **The Master Anchor of the Indian Monsoon**: Its upper-level divergence over the Arabian Sea drives low-level monsoon air inflow; sudden arrival heralds the "Burst of the Monsoon". |
| **Polar Night Jet Stream** | **60° to 70° N & S** *(Winter only)* | Upper stratosphere / mesosphere ($>30\text{ km}$). | Deep polar winter darkness generates intense radiational cooling, forming a ferocious circumpolar vortex. | Confines the Antarctic cold pool, enabling Polar Stratospheric Clouds and catalytic ozone hole depletion. |

### Rossby Waves & The Jet Stream Lifecycle

The circumpolar flow of the upper westerlies does not follow straight latitudinal paths. Under the influence of continental barriers (e.g. Rockies, Himalayas) and thermal gradients, the jet stream undulates into gigantic planetary meanders termed **Rossby Waves** (formulated by Carl-Gustaf Rossby in 1939).

```
THE ROSSBY WAVE INDEX CYCLE (Period: 4 to 6 Weeks)
  ├── Stage 1: Zonal Flow (High Index)
  │      └── Jet stream is straight and flat; cold air confined strictly to poles; warm air to tropics.
  ├── Stage 2: Wave Amplification
  │      └── Small ripples develop; jet stream begins undulating into ridges and troughs.
  ├── Stage 3: Deep Meridional Meandering (Low Index)
  │      └── Massive loops: Warm air plunges far north into Ridges; freezing polar air dives south into Troughs.
  └── Stage 4: Cut-Off Lows & Highs (Blocking Pattern)
         └── Extreme loops pinch off: Cold air pools stranded in subtropics ("Cut-off Low"); 
             Warm high-pressure domes stranded in north ("Blocking Anticyclones" / Heat Domes).
```

```
                   MEANDERING OF A ROSSBY WAVE
            [POLAR AIR] ──►                [POLAR AIR]
               \                               /
                \  RIDGE (Warm Air Pushed N)  /
                 \       ▲                   /
                  \     / \                 /
                   \   /   \               /
                    ▼ /     ▼             ▼
                    TROUGH (Cold Air Dives South)
```

* **Upper-Air Divergence & Surface Weather**:
  * **East of an Upper Trough (Ahead of Trough)**: Isobars diverge aloft. Air is evacuated faster than it can be replaced, creating **Upper-Level Divergence**. This draws air upward from the surface, initiating **surface cyclogenesis and storm formation**.
  * **West of an Upper Trough (Behind Trough)**: Air converges aloft (**Upper-Level Convergence**), sinking toward the ground and building **surface anticyclones and clear, stable weather**.

## 8. The 15 Deadliest Exam Traps in Pressure & Wind Dynamics

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 1: Coriolis Force at the Equator**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Coriolis force is strongest at the Equator because rotational linear velocity is highest there."
> *Correction*: **False**. While the Earth's linear rotational speed is highest at the Equator (~1,670 km/h), the **Coriolis force is governed by the sine of latitude ($2v\Omega \sin \phi$)**. Since $\sin(0^\circ) = 0$, the Coriolis force is **ZERO at the Equator** and maximum at the Poles ($\sin 90^\circ = 1$).

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 2: Cyclone Genesis on the Equator**
> *Trap Statement*: "The warmest equatorial ocean waters breed the world's most ferocious tropical cyclones."
> *Correction*: **False**. Tropical cyclones **CANNOT form between 0° and 5° latitude**. Without the Coriolis force ($F_c = 0$), air flows directly down the pressure gradient and fills the central low pressure before a rotating vortex can be organized.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 3: Subtropical High Pressure Origin**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Subtropical High Pressure belt is formed by extreme thermal freezing of the atmosphere."
> *Correction*: **False**. The Subtropical High Pressure belt (30°–35°) is **Dynamically Induced**, NOT thermally induced. It is caused by the upper-air **subsidence of cooling air** descending from the Hadley and Ferrel cells.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 4: Direction of Geostrophic Wind**
> *Trap Statement*: "Geostrophic winds blow directly from high pressure to low pressure perpendicular to isobars."
> *Correction*: **False**. In geostrophic flow, the Pressure Gradient Force is exactly balanced by the Coriolis Force. The resulting wind blows **strictly PARALLEL to isobars**, not across them.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 5: Roaring Forties Location**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Roaring Forties are violent winds blowing across the Rocky Mountains of North America at 40°N."
> *Correction*: **False**. The Roaring Forties blow between **40°S and 50°S in the SOUTHERN Hemisphere**, where the unbroken expanse of ocean allows westerlies to accelerate without friction from continental landmasses.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 6: Doldrums Wind Conditions**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Doldrums are notorious among sailors for continuous, violent horizontal gale-force winds."
> *Correction*: **False**. The Doldrums (Equatorial Trough) is a **Zone of Calms**. Horizontal air movement is almost completely absent; air moves primarily vertically in strong convective updrafts.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 7: Tropical Easterly Jet Seasonality**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Tropical Easterly Jet Stream flows year-round over the Indian Ocean."
> *Correction*: **False**. The Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ) is an exclusively **Summer seasonal jet (June to September)**. It is formed by the intense summer heating of the Tibetan Plateau and disappears completely during the winter.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 8: Ferrel's Law Deflection in Southern Hemisphere**
> *Trap Statement*: "According to Ferrel's Law, wind in the Southern Hemisphere deflects to its right."
> *Correction*: **False**. Wind deflects to the **RIGHT in the Northern Hemisphere**, and to the **LEFT in the Southern Hemisphere**.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 9: Mediterranean Rainfall Season**
> *Trap Statement*: "Mediterranean regions receive heavy rains in summer due to the poleward shift of Trade Winds."
> *Correction*: **False**. Mediterranean climates experience **bone-dry summers** (under subtropical high pressure) and receive their rains in **WINTER** when the equatorward shift of pressure belts brings them under the moist **Westerlies**.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 10: Horse Latitudes Name Origin**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Horse Latitudes were named because wild horses gallop across the plains there."
> *Correction*: **False**. Spanish sailing ships carrying horses were becalmed for weeks in the dead, sinking air of the subtropical highs. To conserve drinking water for the crew, sailors were forced to throw their horses into the sea.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 11: Trade Wind Desiccation of Deserts**
> *Trap Statement*: "Trade winds cause desertification on the eastern margins of tropical continents."
> *Correction*: **False**. Trade winds blow from the east; they bring heavy rainfall to the **Eastern margins** of continents and cause aridity and deserts on the **Western margins** (e.g. Atacama, Sahara, Kalahari) where they blow offshore.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 12: Western Disturbances Driver**
> *Trap Statement*: "Western Disturbances are steered into India by the southwest summer monsoon."
> *Correction*: **False**. Western Disturbances are shallow temperate depressions steered into northwest India during **Winter** by the southern branch of the **Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream (STJ)** flowing south of the Himalayas.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 13: Jet Stream Flow Direction**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Polar Front Jet stream blows from east to west across Europe."
> *Correction*: **False**. Both the Polar Front Jet and Subtropical Jet are **Westerly jets**—they blow from **West to East** in both the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 14: Sub-Polar Low Pressure Genesis**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Sub-Polar Low Pressure belts at 60°–65° are thermally induced by warm ocean currents."
> *Correction*: **False**. Sub-polar lows are **Dynamically Induced** by the convergence of contrasting air masses (Polar Front) and the centrifugal flinging of air away from the rotating globe.

> ⚠️ **Test-Maker Trap 15: Coriolis Force Effect on Velocity**
> *Trap Statement*: "The Coriolis force accelerates gentle winds into high-speed hurricanes."
> *Correction*: **False**. The Coriolis force **CANNOT change the speed of wind**; it acts at a strict 90° angle to motion, altering only the direction of flow. Wind speed is accelerated solely by the **Pressure Gradient Force**.
