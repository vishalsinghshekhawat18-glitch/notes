## 1. Derwent Whittlesey's World Agricultural Typology (1936)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Agricultural typology is the systematic spatial classification of agricultural landscapes based on operational inputs, crop-livestock combinations, intensity, and societal disposition of produce.
> 
> In 1936, American geographer **Derwent Whittlesey** published the definitive, universally accepted classification of world agriculture in the *Annals of the Association of American Geographers*, demarcating the globe into **13 Distinct Agricultural Realms** based on five strict diagnostic functional criteria.

```
                   WHITTLESEY'S FIVE DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA
  1. Crop and Livestock Association: What specific combinations of plants and animals are raised?
  2. Methods & Technology Applied: Hand tools, animal draught power, or mechanized chemical inputs?
  3. Intensity of Land Use: Inputs of labor, capital, and fertilizers per unit area of cultivated land.
  4. Disposal of Products: Subsistence consumption by the farm household vs. Commercial market sale.
  5. Structural Ensemble: Housing patterns, barns, field boundaries, and tenure institutions.
```

## 2. Johann Heinrich von Thünen's Agricultural Location Model (1826)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Johann Heinrich von Thünen (German economist and landowner) established the first spatial economic model in his seminal treatise *Der isolierte Staat* (The Isolated State, 1826).
> 
> Von Thünen demonstrated that the spatial arrangement of agricultural land uses around a central market town is governed strictly by **Locational Rent (Economic Rent)**—which is determined by the trade-off between **crop market value, production cost, and transportation cost per unit distance**.

```
                   VON THÜNEN'S ECONOMIC RENT FORMULA
                              R = Y(p - c) - YFm
   Where:
     R = Locational Rent / Economic Rent per unit of land.
     Y = Crop yield per unit of land.
     p = Market selling price per unit of crop at the central town.
     c = Production cost per unit of crop (seed, labor, fertilizers).
     F = Freight / Transportation rate per unit of distance.
     m = Radial distance from the farm to the central market town.
```

* **Core Axiom**: As distance from the market ($m$) increases, transport costs ($YFm$) escalate linearly, driving economic rent ($R$) toward zero. Crops that are **heavy, bulky, highly perishable, or yield high market returns per hectare** must locate closest to the central market.
