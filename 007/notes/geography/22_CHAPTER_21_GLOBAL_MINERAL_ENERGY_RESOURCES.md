## 1. Global Geometallurgy: Mineral Classifications & Crustal Genesis

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Minerals are naturally occurring, inorganic crystalline solid substances possessing a definite chemical composition and characteristic atomic structure.
> 
> The global spatial distribution of metallic ores is strictly governed by ancient **Precambrian shields and cratons** (hosting banded iron formations, gold, and nickel) and **active/fossil convergent plate boundaries** (hosting porphyry copper, lead, zinc, and tin deposits).

```
METALLURGICAL RESOURCE CLASSIFICATION
  ├── 1. Metallic Minerals:
  │      ├── Ferrous (Iron-Bearing): Iron Ore, Manganese, Chromite, Nickel, Cobalt.
  │      ├── Non-Ferrous: Copper, Bauxite (Aluminum), Lead, Zinc, Tin.
  │      └── Precious Metals: Gold, Silver, Platinum Group Metals (PGMs).
  │
  ├── 2. Non-Metallic Minerals: Limestone, Dolomite, Mica, Gypsum, Rock Phosphate, Potash.
  │
  ├── 3. Conventional Fossil Fuels: Coal, Petroleum (Crude Oil), Natural Gas.
  │
  └── 4. Critical Energy Transition Minerals: Lithium, Cobalt, Rare Earth Elements (REEs), Graphite, Nickel.
```

## 2. Ferrous Metallic Resources: Iron Ore & Manganese

### 1. Iron Ore: The Backbone of Industrial Civilization
Iron ore exists in nature in four primary mineralogical oxide and carbonate states:

```
IRON ORE MINERALOGICAL HIERARCHY
  ├── 1. Magnetite (Fe3O4): 72.4% Fe ──► Black, magnetic; hardest; highest grade (Kursk, Kiruna, Kudremukh).
  ├── 2. Hematite (Fe2O3):  69.9% Fe ──► Reddish; world's most widely mined ore (Pilbara, Carajás, Bailadila).
  ├── 3. Limonite (2Fe2O3·3H2O): ~59.8% Fe ──► Brown/Yellow hydrated iron oxide; bog iron ore.
  └── 4. Siderite (FeCO3):   48.2% Fe ──► Grey/Brown iron carbonate; lowest industrial grade.
```

### 2. Manganese: The Indispensable Steel Scavenger
* **Industrial Role**: Over **$90\%$ of global manganese** is consumed in the steel manufacturing industry. It acts as a deoxidizer and desulfurizer, and imparts hardness, toughness, and abrasion resistance (*Ferro-manganese alloys*). Approximately **$6\text{ to }10\text{ kg}$ of manganese is consumed per metric ton of crude steel produced**.
* **Global Distribution**:
  1. **South Africa (World #1)**: Dominates the global market, producing over **$35\text{–}40\%$ of world output** from the colossal **Kalahari Manganese Field** in Northern Cape.
  2. **Gabon**: Moanda mine (high-grade manganese ore).
  3. **Australia**: Groote Eylandt (Northern Territory) and Woodie Woodie (WA).
  4. **China & India**: India's reserves concentrated in Madhya Pradesh (Balaghat - Bharveli mine, deepest underground manganese mine in Asia), Maharashtra (Bhandara), and Odisha.

## 4. Conventional Hydrocarbons & Coal

### 1. Coal: Carbon Ranks & Global Coal Basins

```
COAL RANK EVOLUTION (COALIFICATION PROCESS)
  Plant Debris ──► Peat (50% Carbon) ──► Lignite (60–70% C) ──► Bituminous (75–86% C) ──► Anthracite (90–95% C)
  [Increasing Geological Pressure, Temperature, Depth, Fixed Carbon & Calorific Heat Value ──────────────►]
```

* **The Four Ranks of Coal**:
  1. **Anthracite**: Highest rank; **$90\text{ to }95\%$ fixed carbon**; hard, shiny black; smokeless flame, highest calorific value ($>30\text{ MJ/kg}$); scarce (~$1\%$ of world reserves).
  2. **Bituminous Coal**: **$75\text{ to }86\%$ fixed carbon**; divided into coking coal (metallurgical) and non-coking coal (thermal power); world's most abundant commercial coal.
  3. **Lignite ("Brown Coal")**: **$60\text{ to }70\%$ carbon**; high moisture content ($30–50\%$); low calorific value; prone to spontaneous combustion; used locally for pit-head power generation.
  4. **Peat**: Earliest precursor stage; spongy, high moisture ($>70\%$), low heat output.

### 2. Petroleum (Crude Oil) & Natural Gas Geography

```
PETROLEUM ACCUMULATION TRAPPING PREREQUISITES
  1. Rich Organic Source Rock: Marine plankton/algae buried in anaerobic shale ("oil window": 60°–120°C).
  2. Porous & Permeable Reservoir Rock: Sandstone or fractured limestone allowing fluid storage and migration.
  3. Impermeable Cap Rock (Seal): Thick evaporite salt sheets or dense shale preventing hydrocarbon escape.
  4. Geological Trap: Structural anticline, fault trap, or stratigraphic salt dome.
```

## 5. Critical Minerals & The Energy Transition Geopolitics

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The transition from a fossil-fuel-based energy system to clean energy technologies (Electric Vehicles, Lithium-Ion Batteries, Wind Turbines, Photovoltaics, Defense Electronics) is fundamentally **a transition from a fuel-intensive to a mineral-intensive system**.
> 
> Critical minerals are characterized by extreme geographical concentration of mining and processing, high supply-chain vulnerability, and lack of viable short-term substitutes.

```
                   THE CLEAN ENERGY CRITICAL MINERAL NEXUS
  ├── Lithium:             Lithium-Ion Battery cathodes & electrolytes (EVs, grid storage).
  ├── Cobalt:              NMC battery cathodes (Prevents thermal runaway, enhances stability).
  ├── Nickel (Class 1):    High-energy-density battery chemistries (NMC, NCA).
  ├── Rare Earths (REEs):  Neodymium (Nd) & Dysprosium (Dy) in permanent magnets (EV motors, wind turbines).
  └── Graphite:            Anode material for virtually 100% of commercial lithium-ion batteries.
```

---

### The Three Critical Geopolitical Bottlenecks

```
THE THREE GEOPOLITICAL ENERGY TRANSITION BOTTLENECKS
  ├── 1. The Lithium Triangle (South America) ──► Bolivia, Chile, Argentina (Salar Brines).
  ├── 2. The Cobalt Monopolization (Central Africa) ──► Democratic Republic of Congo (>70% of world supply).
  └── 3. The Chinese REE & Refining Chokepoint ──► China controls 70% of mining & >90% of processing.
```
