## 1. 🏛️ BANKING FOUNDATIONS, BALANCE-SHEET MECHANICS & CODES

### 1.1 What a Bank Does: The Balance-Sheet Model [FOUNDATION]

A bank is a specialized financial intermediary. It accepts deposits from savers, raises wholesale borrowings, maintains liquid statutory reserves, invests in marketable securities, and originates credit advances. Its economic role is to perform **maturity transformation** (borrowing short-term to lend long-term), **liquidity transformation** (converting liquid deposits into illiquid loans), and **risk transformation** (absorbing diversified credit risk).

#### Master Bank Balance Sheet Architecture
| Assets (Uses of Funds — What the World Owes the Bank) | Liabilities & Equity (Sources of Funds — What the Bank Owes) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cash & Balances with RBI**: CRR cash deposits, vault cash, interbank balances | **Customer Deposits**: Demand deposits (Current, Savings) & Time deposits (FD, RD) |
| **Investments**: Approved G-Secs (SLR), T-Bills, corporate bonds, equity shares | **Borrowings**: Liquidity adjustment borrowings from RBI (Repo/MSF), interbank loans |
| **Loans & Advances**: Retail credit, MSME loans, corporate credit, agri advances | **Other Liabilities & Provisions**: Accrued interest, tax provisions, unpaid expenses |
| **Fixed & Other Assets**: Bank premises, digital IT infrastructure, deferred tax assets | **Capital & Reserves (Net Worth)**: Common equity (CET1), reserves, retained earnings |

$$\mathbf{Total\ Assets = Total\ Liabilities + Regulatory\ Equity\ (Net\ Worth)}$$

> 💡 **Core Insight:** A customer deposit is a **liability of the bank** because the bank is contractually obligated to repay the depositor. A loan is an **asset of the bank** because the borrower is contractually indebted to the bank. Interest paid on deposits is an operational funding cost; interest and processing fees earned on loans represent primary operating revenue.

---

### 1.2 Liquidity, Solvency, and Profitability: The Three Cardinal Pillars [FOUNDATION]

* **Liquidity**: The capacity to meet cash-flow and withdrawal obligations promptly as they fall due. A bank can be fully solvent (assets far exceeding liabilities) but become instantly **illiquid** if depositors trigger a sudden run and its assets cannot be converted into cash without catastrophic fire-sale losses.
* **Solvency**: The structural condition where the fair economic value of a bank's assets exceeds its total liabilities to external creditors, underpinned by sufficient loss-absorbing capital. Persistent loan defaults and credit impairments destroy equity capital, eventually rendering the bank **insolvent**.
* **Profitability**: The ability to generate net positive earnings after funding costs, operational expenses, loan-loss provisions, and taxes. A bank can be liquid and solvent today, yet suffer chronic unprofitability that progressively erodes capital over time.

### 1.3 Deposit Categories & CASA Dynamics [FOUNDATION]

| Deposit Category | Statutory Nature | Interest Earning Mechanics | Primary Use & Exam Distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Current Account** | Pure Demand Liability | Zero interest under standard domestic banking rules | Designed for commercial entities, traders, and businesses requiring frequent daily transaction turnover |
| **Savings Account** | Demand Liability | Compounded interest calculated on **daily end-of-day balances** | Designed for individuals, households, and eligible charitable trusts; combines savings yield with payment access |
| **Fixed / Term Deposit** | Time Liability | Fixed contractual interest; premature withdrawal subject to penal interest | Funds placed for a fixed tenor (7 days to 10 years); higher interest yielding longer-term funding stability |
| **Recurring Deposit** | Time Liability | Regular monthly fixed installments over a defined horizon | Disciplined savings vehicle building a lumpsum maturity corpus |
| **CASA Ratio** | Funding Metric | $$\mathbf{CASA\ Ratio = \frac{Current\ Deposits + Savings\ Deposits}{Total\ Deposits} \times 100}$$ | A high CASA ratio provides low-cost operational funding, lowering the bank's blended Cost of Funds (CoF) |

### 1.4 How a Bank Earns Money: NII & Net Interest Margin (NIM) [FOUNDATION]

A commercial bank's income statement is governed by two core components:
1. **Net Interest Income (NII)**:
   $$\mathbf{NII = Interest\ Income\ (from\ loans\ \&\ investments) - Interest\ Expense\ (paid\ on\ deposits\ \&\ borrowings)}$$
2. **Net Interest Margin (NIM)**:
   $$\mathbf{NIM = \frac{Net\ Interest\ Income\ (NII)}{Average\ Earning\ Assets} \times 100}$$
3. **Operating Profit & Net Profit**:
   $$\mathbf{Operating\ Profit = NII + Non\text{-}Interest\ Income\ (fees,\ commissions,\ treasury\ gains) - Operating\ Expenses\ (opex)}$$
   $$\mathbf{Net\ Profit\ (PAT) = Operating\ Profit - Loan\ Loss\ Provisions\ (credit\ costs) - Corporate\ Taxes}$$

### 1.5 Credit Creation Mechanics: Safe Conceptual Model [FOUNDATION]

When a commercial bank sanctions a credit facility and disburses loan proceeds, it does not physically hand over bags of currency; instead, it opens a credit ledger entry and **credits the borrower's deposit account**. In doing so, the commercial banking system **creates fresh deposits**. 

Credit creation is not infinite or mechanical:
- The textbook **Money Multiplier ($1 / \text{CRR}$)** is an illustrative theoretical abstraction.
- In reality, commercial bank lending is strictly bounded by **capital adequacy requirements (CRAR)**, **regulatory liquidity mandates (LCR, NSFR, SLR)**, **creditworthy borrower demand**, **internal risk appetite**, and **profitability constraints**. Banks do not lend simply because they hold excess reserve balances.

### 1.6 Master Banking Ratios Matrix [FOUNDATION]

| Regulatory / Financial Ratio | Mathematical Formula & Meaning | What It Does NOT Tell You on Its Own |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Net Interest Margin (NIM)** | $\frac{\text{NII}}{\text{Average Earning Assets}} \times 100$ (spread efficiency) | Does not reflect underlying credit risk, borrower default rates, or provision costs |
| **CASA Ratio** | $\frac{\text{CASA Deposits}}{\text{Total Deposits}} \times 100$ (low-cost deposit share) | Does not measure overall balance sheet asset quality, solvency, or net bottom-line profit |
| **Gross NPA Ratio** | $\frac{\text{Gross NPAs}}{\text{Gross Advances}} \times 100$ (total delinquent loans) | Does not account for loan-loss provisions already built up to absorb potential write-offs |
| **Net NPA Ratio** | $\frac{\text{Gross NPAs} - \text{Cumulative Provisions}}{\text{Net Advances}} \times 100$ (uncovered bad debt) | Cannot be compared across banks without verifying individual provisioning coverage policies |
| **CRAR / Capital Adequacy** | $\frac{\text{Eligible Capital (Tier 1 + Tier 2)}}{\text{Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA)}} \times 100$ | Is NOT capital divided by total unweighted assets; weights vary heavily by asset risk |
| **Return on Assets (ROA)** | $\frac{\text{Net Profit after Tax}}{\text{Average Total Assets}} \times 100$ (asset efficiency) | Can be inflated by high-risk volatile treasury trading gains or one-off asset sales |
| **Return on Equity (ROE)** | $\frac{\text{Net Profit after Tax}}{\text{Average Shareholder Equity}} \times 100$ (equity return) | High ROE can merely reflect dangerous financial leverage (holding very thin equity capital) |
| **Cost-to-Income Ratio** | $\frac{\text{Operating Expenses (excluding provisions)}}{\text{Total Operating Income (NII + Other Income)}} \times 100$ | Lower is not always better if the bank is dangerously under-investing in cybersecurity or risk controls |

| **LEI** *(Legal Entity Identifier)* | **20** Alphanumeric | Global ISO 17442 standard identifier for entities participating in financial transactions | **Dual Mandatory Regulatory Thresholds:**<br>1. **Credit Exposure $\ge$ ₹5 Crore**: Non-individual borrowers across SCBs, AIFIs, NBFCs (barred from credit renewal/enhancement if missing)<br>2. **Single Remittance $\ge$ ₹50 Crore**: Cross-border capital/current transactions under FEMA |
| **RTGS UTR** *(Unique Transaction Reference)* | **22** Alphanumeric | `4 (Bank IFSC)` + `1 ('R' for RTGS)` + `1 (Channel: 'C' Customer, 'I' Inter-bank, 'P' Participant)` + `8 (Date: YYYYMMDD)` + `8 (Sequential Running No: nnnnnnnn)` | Real-Time Gross Settlement system governed by RBI RTGS System Regulations |

| **Card-on-File Tokenisation (CoFT)** | Token Identifier | Substitutes a unique algorithmic token for actual 16-digit card PAN | Merchants and payment aggregators strictly barred from storing actual card numbers; managed via bank portal |

> ⚠️ **EXAM ANGLE:**
> - **The RTGS vs NEFT Length Trap**: RTGS UTR is **22 characters**; NEFT UTR is **16 characters** (examiners routinely swap these two numbers).
> - **RTGS Anatomy Trap**: Format is: **4 IFSC + 1 System (R) + 1 Channel (C) + 8 Date (YYYYMMDD) + 8 Sequence = 22** (Notice: 8-digit running sequence, not 9; 5th is 'R', 6th is Channel).
> - **LEI Dual-Threshold Trap**: Borrower credit exposure threshold is **$\ge$ ₹5 Crore**; cross-border FEMA remittance threshold is **$\ge$ ₹50 Crore per transaction**.
> - **IFSC 5th Character**: The 5th character is always numeric **0 (zero)**, NEVER the alphabetical letter 'O'.
> - **MICR Sequence**: MICR 9-digit sequence is **City Code (3) $\to$ Bank Code (3) $\to$ Branch Code (3)** (City matches first 3 digits of PIN).

## 4. ⚖️ RBI GOVERNANCE, MONETARY POLICY & LIQUIDITY AGGREGATES

### 4.1 RBI Central Board of Directors [FOUNDATION]

The governance and general superintendence of the Reserve Bank of India are vested in the Central Board of Directors under **Section 8 of the RBI Act, 1934**.
- **Statutory Composition (Maximum 21 Members)**:
  - **Governor** and up to **4 Deputy Governors** (appointed under Section 8(1)(a)).
  - **4 Directors** nominated from the 4 Local Boards (Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, New Delhi).
  - **2 Government Officials** (typically Economic Affairs Secretary and Financial Services Secretary from MoF).
  - **10 Non-Official Directors** nominated by the Government of India representing diverse economic fields.
- **Tenure**: Official directors (Governor/DGs) hold office for terms up to 5 years and are eligible for reappointment.

### 4.2 Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) Framework [FOUNDATION + RULE-SENSITIVE]

The MPC is a statutory committee constituted under **Sections 45-ZB to 45-ZG of the RBI Act, 1934** (amended via Finance Act, 2016).

| Parameter | Statutory Rule & Legal Mandate |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Total Membership** | **6 Members** |
| **RBI Members (3)** | • **Governor** (ex-officio Chairperson)<br>• **Deputy Governor** in charge of Monetary Policy<br>• **One Officer** of the RBI nominated by the Central Board |
| **External Members (3)** | Appointed by the Central Government on the recommendations of a Cabinet Search-cum-Selection Committee |
| **External Member Tenure** | Fixed **4-year term**; **STRICTLY NOT eligible for reappointment** |
| **Meeting Quorum** | Minimum **4 members**, with at least the Governor or Deputy Governor in attendance |
| **Voting Mechanics** | Each member has one vote; the **Governor holds a second / casting vote** in the event of an equality of votes (tie) |
| **Publication of Minutes** | Minutes, member rationale, and individual voting records must be published on the **14th day** after the meeting under Section 45ZL |

### 4.3 Monetary Policy Objective: Flexible Inflation Targeting [FOUNDATION]

Under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, the primary objective of monetary policy is to **maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth**.
- **Nominal Anchor**: **Consumer Price Index (CPI - Combined)**, NEVER WPI.
- **Inflation Target**: **4.00%** with a statutory tolerance band of **±2.00%** (lower tolerance limit: **2.00%**; upper tolerance limit: **6.00%**).
- **Failure to Meet Target**: Defined as average headline CPI inflation remaining outside the 2%–6% band for **three consecutive quarters**. If failed, RBI must submit a statutory report to the Central Government explaining causes, remedial actions, and an estimated time-frame for convergence.

### 4.4 Policy Rate & Liquidity Corridor Tools [RULE-SENSITIVE]

```
Corridor Ceiling:  Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) Rate / Bank Rate  [Repo + 25 bps]
                                     ▲
Policy Anchor:                 Repo Rate                              [Operating Benchmark]
                                     ▼
Corridor Floor:    Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) Rate               [Repo - 25 bps]
```

| Operational Tool | Economic Mechanism | Transmission Mechanics & Governing Rules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Policy Repo Rate** | Key policy benchmark signal | Rate at which RBI lends short-term liquidity to banks against eligible government collateral (G-Secs) |
| **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)** | Liquidity absorption floor | Uncollateralized facility allowing banks to park overnight liquidity with RBI; absorbs surplus cash without pledging G-Secs |
| **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)** | Liquidity injection ceiling | Emergency penal overnight borrowing window for banks against approved G-Secs dipping into their SLR buffer up to permitted limit |
| **Bank Rate** | Statutory benchmark | Long-term reference rate under Section 49 of RBI Act; penal rate linked to discounting of bills and banking penalties |
| **Open Market Operations (OMO)** | Outright liquidity tool | Outright purchase (injects durable cash) or sale (absorbs durable cash) of government securities by RBI |
| **VRR / VRRR Auctions** | Fine-tuning operations | Variable Rate Repo (injects liquidity) and Variable Rate Reverse Repo (absorbs liquidity) for non-standard tenors |

#### Master Comparison: Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) vs Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)
| Dimension | Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) | Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Statute** | **Section 42(1), RBI Act 1934** | **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act 1949** |
| **Maintenance Medium** | Strictly **Cash Balances** | **Approved Liquid Assets**: Cash, Gold, unencumbered approved G-Secs / T-Bills |
| **Custody Location** | Deposited and maintained **with RBI** | Maintained by the bank **on its own balance sheet** |
| **Interest Return** | **0% Interest** (RBI pays zero interest on CRR) | Bank earns interest/coupons and yield on underlying G-Secs and gold holdings |
| **Statutory Floor & Ceiling** | 2006 Amendment removed earlier 3% floor and 20% ceiling; RBI has complete discretion | Statutory ceiling capped at **40%** (2007 amendment removed earlier 25% floor) |
| **Operational Benchmark** | Policy variable | Capped at statutory ceiling; operational rate maintained at **18.00%** |

### 4.5 Sovereign Cash Management & Public Debt Instruments [RULE-SENSITIVE]

* **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)**:
  - Clean, short-term advances extended by RBI to Central and State Governments under **Section 17(5) of RBI Act, 1934** to bridge temporary cash-flow mismatches between expenditure and receipts.
  - Repayable within a maximum of **3 months (90 days)**.
  - Overdraft (OD) facility is permitted beyond WMA limits for specified operational days.
* **Treasury Bills (T-Bills)**:
  - Short-term sovereign debt instruments issued by the Government of India, issued at a discount and redeemed at par (face value).
  - Issued in three standard tenors: **91-day**, **182-day**, and **364-day**.
  - Minimum investment: **₹10,000** and in multiples thereof; auctioned weekly on Wednesdays.
* **Cash Management Bills (CMBs)**:
  - Ultra short-term discounted sovereign instruments issued to meet temporary cash mismatches; maturities are strictly **less than 91 days**.
* **Dated Government Securities (G-Secs) & Bond Yield Mechanics**:
  - Long-term fixed or floating-rate sovereign bonds with maturities from 2 years up to 40+ years.
  - **Inverse Price-Yield Relationship**: When market interest rates/yields rise, the market price of existing fixed-coupon bonds **falls**; when yields decline, bond prices **rise**. Longer duration bonds experience greater price volatility for a given yield shift.

### 4.6 Monetary & Liquidity Aggregates: Classical vs Modern [FOUNDATION]

#### 1. Classical Monetary Aggregates (M0 to M4)
* **Reserve Money ($M_0$)** *(Monetary Base / High-Powered Money)*:
  $$\mathbf{M_0 = Currency\ in\ Circulation\ (CIC) + Bankers'\ Deposits\ with\ RBI + 'Other'\ Deposits\ with\ RBI}$$
* **Narrow Money ($M_1$)** *(Most Liquid Commercial Money)*:
  $$\mathbf{M_1 = Currency\ with\ the\ Public + Demand\ Deposits\ with\ Banking\ System\ (CASA) + 'Other'\ Deposits\ with\ RBI}$$
  *(Note: Currency with the Public = Currency in Circulation minus Cash in hand with banks)*
* **Intermediate Money ($M_2$)**:
  $$\mathbf{M_2 = M_1 + Savings\ Deposits\ with\ Post\ Office\ Savings\ Banks}$$
* **Broad Money ($M_3$)** *(Standard Policy Aggregate tracked by RBI)*:
  $$\mathbf{M_3 = M_1 + Time\ Deposits\ with\ the\ Banking\ System\ (Fixed\ \&\ Recurring\ Deposits)}$$
* **Comprehensive Money ($M_4$)**:
  $$\mathbf{M_4 = M_3 + Total\ Post\ Office\ Deposits\ (EXCLUDING\ National\ Savings\ Certificates\ /\ NSC)}$$

#### 2. Modern New Monetary & Liquidity Aggregates (Dr. Y.V. Reddy Committee, 1998)
* **$NM_1$**: Currency with the Public + Demand Deposits with Banking System + 'Other' Deposits with RBI.
* **$NM_2$**: $NM_1$ + Short-Term Time Deposits of residents (contractual maturity up to 1 year).
* **$NM_3$**: $NM_2$ + Long-Term Time Deposits of residents (maturity $> 1$ year) + Call/Term Borrowings from Financial Institutions.
* **$L_1$**: $NM_3$ + All Deposits with Post Office Savings Banks (**EXCLUDING NSC**).
* **$L_2$**: $L_1$ + Term Deposits with Term Lending / Refinance Institutions (NABARD, EXIM Bank, SIDBI, NHB) + Term Borrowings by FIs + CDs issued by FIs.
* **$L_3$**: $L_2$ + Public Deposits accepted by Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs).
* **Money Multiplier ($m$)**:
  $$\mathbf{m = \frac{Broad\ Money\ (M_3)}{Reserve\ Money\ (M_0)}}$$

> ⚠️ **EXAM ANGLE:**
> - **M0 vs M1 Currency Component**: $M_0$ contains **Currency in Circulation**; $M_1$ contains **Currency with the Public** (excludes commercial bank vault cash).
> - **The Post Office NSC Trap**: National Savings Certificates (NSC) are **strictly excluded** from both $M_4$ and $L_1$ calculations.
> - **MPC Minutes Timeline**: Statutory deadline for minutes publication is the **14th day** following the MPC meeting.

### 5.2 ATM Free Limits, Interchange Fees & Failed Transaction Turnaround (SLA) [RULE-SENSITIVE]

* **Free Monthly ATM Transaction Quota**:
  - **Own-Bank ATMs**: Minimum **5 free transactions per month** (financial + non-financial).
  - **Other-Bank ATMs (6 Designated Metros)**: Minimum **3 free transactions per month** (*Metros: Mumbai, New Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata, Bengaluru, Hyderabad*).
  - **Other-Bank ATMs (Non-Metro Centers)**: Minimum **5 free transactions per month**.
* **Interchange Fee Caps (between banks)**: Financial transaction cap: **₹17**; Non-financial transaction cap: **₹6**.
* **Customer Charge Ceiling**: Maximum customer fee after exhausting free limits: **₹21** plus applicable taxes.
* **Failed Transaction Reversal SLA & Statutory Compensation**:
  - When an account is debited but cash is not dispensed at an ATM, the bank must auto-reverse the transaction within **T+5 calendar days** ($T = \text{Transaction Date}$).
  - For any delay beyond $T+5$ calendar days, the bank is statutorily mandated to pay compensation of **₹100 per day of delay** directly to the customer's account without requiring a customer grievance.

### 5.4 DICGC Deposit Insurance & 90-Day Resolution Breakdown [RULE-SENSITIVE]

The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) is a **wholly owned subsidiary of the RBI**, governed under the **DICGC Act, 1961** (Authorized capital: ₹50 Crore).
- **Insurance Coverage Limit**: Up to **₹5,00,000 (₹5 Lakh)** per depositor per insured bank in the **same right and same capacity**, covering both **principal and interest combined**.
- **Cross-Branch Aggregation Rule**: Deposits across all branches of the *same insured bank* are aggregated together. Having accounts in 5 different branches of SBI provides a single composite cover of ₹5 Lakh, not 5 × ₹5 Lakh. Different legal capacities (e.g., individual savings vs partner in a firm) qualify for separate ₹5 Lakh limits.
- **Excluded Deposits**: Deposits of foreign governments, central/state governments, interbank deposits, and state land development banks.

#### The Statutory 90-Day Moratorium Resolution Timeline (Section 18A)
```
Day 0: RBI Direction / Moratorium
  │
  ├─► Stage 1 (Days 1–45):   Insured Distressed Bank compiles & submits depositor claim list [45 Days]
  │
  ├─► Stage 2 (Days 46–75):  DICGC audits, processes & verifies claim ledgers               [30 Days]
  │
  └─► Stage 3 (Days 76–90):  DICGC disburses funds directly to depositor bank accounts     [15 Days]
                             ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                             Total Mandatory Statutory Resolution Limit = Exactly 90 Days
```

> ⚠️ **EXAM ANGLE:**
> - **Ombudsman Compensation Slabs**: Direct financial loss ceiling is **₹30 Lakh**; mental anguish ceiling is **₹3 Lakh** (Composite max: **₹33 Lakh** under RB-IOS 2026).
> - **Ombudsman Appeal Window**: **30 days** against an *Award* only (cannot appeal against a complaint rejection).
> - **Failed ATM Reversal SLA**: **T+5 calendar days**; delay compensation is **₹100 per day**.
> - **DICGC 90-Day Formula**: Bank submits list in **45 days** + DICGC verifies in **30 days** + Disbursement within **15 days** = **90 days total**.

## 6. 🛡️ BASEL III CAPITAL FRAMEWORK, D-SIBs & STRESSED ASSETS (NPAs)

### 6.1 Basel III Capital Adequacy Framework [RULE-SENSITIVE]

Bank capital functions as an equity cushion absorbing credit, market, and operational losses to prevent depositor loss and systemic panic. Basel capital adequacy is evaluated against **Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA)**, not gross balance-sheet assets.

$$\mathbf{Capital\ Adequacy\ Ratio\ (CRAR)\ [CAR] = \frac{Eligible\ Regulatory\ Capital\ (Tier\ 1 + Tier\ 2)}{Risk\text{-}Weighted\ Assets\ (RWA)} \times 100}$$

#### Basel III Global Standards vs. Mandated RBI Floors for Indian Commercial Banks
| Capital Metric / Ratio | Basel III Global Accord Baseline | Mandated RBI Floor for Indian SCBs | Structural Function & Quality |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)** | **4.50%** | **5.50%** | Highest loss-absorbing going-concern capital (equity shares, retained earnings) |
| **Additional Tier 1 (AT1)** | **1.50%** | **1.50%** | Perpetual non-cumulative debt instruments with loss-absorption write-down features |
| **Total Tier 1 Capital** | **6.00%** | **7.00%** | Composite going-concern capital (CET1 + AT1) |
| **Tier 2 Capital** | **2.00%** | **2.00%** | Gone-concern supplementary capital (subordinated debt, eligible revaluation reserves) |
| **Minimum Base Total Capital** | **8.00%** | **9.00%** | Minimum statutory capital baseline before mandatory buffers |
| **Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB)** | **2.50%** | **2.50%** | Maintained strictly in Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital |
| **Total Capital including CCB** | **10.50%** | **11.50%** | Comprehensive regulatory capital requirement before D-SIB surcharges |
| **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)** | $\ge$ **100%** | $\ge$ **100%** | High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) to cover net cash outflows over **30 calendar days** |
| **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)** | $\ge$ **100%** | $\ge$ **100%** | Structural resilience over a **1-year horizon**: $\frac{\text{Available Stable Funding}}{\text{Required Stable Funding}} \ge 100\%$ |
| **Leverage Ratio** | **3.00%** | **4.00% for D-SIBs**<br>**3.50% other banks** | Non-risk-weighted backstop: $\frac{\text{Tier 1 Capital}}{\text{Total Exposure Measure}} \times 100$ |

> 💡 **Critical Examiner Trap Resolved:** The Basel Committee's global baseline for total minimum capital is **8.0%** (10.5% with CCB). The **9.0%** figure is an India-specific prudential requirement established by the RBI, which pushes the total Indian commercial bank requirement to **11.5%** with CCB.

### 6.4 Stressed Asset Recovery Legal Infrastructure [FOUNDATION]

* **SARFAESI Act, 2002**: Empowers eligible secured creditors to enforce security interests over mortgaged collateral out-of-court (issuing 60-day demand notices under Section 13(2) and taking possession under Section 13(4)) without filing an ordinary civil suit. Does NOT apply to agricultural land or loans below ₹1 Lakh.
* **Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs)**: Established under Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 for claims of ₹20 Lakh and above across banks and financial institutions.
* **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016**: Creditor-in-control corporate resolution process through Committee of Creditors (CoC) via the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT). Default threshold: ₹1 Crore.
* **Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs)**: Specialized entities registered under Section 3 of SARFAESI Act to purchase stressed debt from banks against Security Receipts (SRs) and pursue bad debt resolution.

> ⚠️ **EXAM ANGLE:**
> - **LCR vs NSFR Horizons**: LCR covers a **30-calendar-day** stress period; NSFR covers a **1-year structural horizon**.
> - **D-SIB Surcharges**: SBI = **Bucket 4 (+0.80%)**; HDFC Bank = **Bucket 2 (+0.40%)**; ICICI Bank = **Bucket 1 (+0.20%)**.
> - **Agricultural NPA Triggers**: Term loan overdue for **2 crop seasons** (short-duration crops) or **1 crop season** (long-duration crops).
> - **Doubtful Asset Provisioning**: Secured portion escalates: **D1 = 25%**, **D2 = 40%**, **D3 = 100%**. Unsecured portion is always **100%**.

## 7. 🌾 DIFFERENTIATED BANKS, UCBs, PRIORITY SECTOR & RURAL INCLUSION

### 7.1 Small Finance Banks (SFBs) vs Payments Banks (PBs) [RULE-SENSITIVE]

Both differentiated bank models originated from the **Committee on Comprehensive Financial Services for Small Businesses and Low Income Households (chaired by Dr. Nachiket Mor, 2014)**, licensed under Section 22 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

| Statutory Parameter | Small Finance Banks (SFBs) | Payments Banks (PBs) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Regulatory Committee** | Usha Thorat Committee (Evaluation) | Nachiket Mor Committee |
| **Minimum Paid-Up Capital** | **₹200 Crore** (₹100 Crore for UCBs transitioning) | **₹100 Crore** |
| **Lending & Credit Operations** | **Fully permitted** to advance loans and extend credit | **STRICTLY PROHIBITED** from lending or extending credit |
| **Credit Cards Issuance** | Permitted to issue Credit Cards | **STRICTLY PROHIBITED** from issuing credit cards *(Debit/ATM cards permitted)* |
| **Customer Deposit Ceiling** | **No ceiling** (unlimited demand and time deposits) | Capped at **₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh)** per individual customer at end of day |
| **Deposit Types Accepted** | Demand Deposits (CASA) + Time Deposits (FD/RD) | **Demand Deposits ONLY** (Savings & Current; zero FDs/RDs) |
| **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** | **60% of ANBC** *(effective FY 2025–26 under PSL Directions, 2025)* | **Not Applicable** (as lending is prohibited) |
| **Loan Concentration Rule** | At least **50% of loan portfolio must be $\le$ ₹25 Lakh** | Not Applicable |

## 9. ☂️ INSURANCE FOUNDATIONS, 100% FDI & THE BIMA TRINITY

### 9.1 The Seven Fundamental Principles of Insurance [FOUNDATION]

| Principle | Legal Doctrine & Operational Meaning | Exam Nuance & Boundary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Utmost Good Faith** *(Uberrimae Fidei)* | Both insurer and insured must fully disclose all material facts accurately prior to contract formation | Non-disclosure or concealment of material facts renders the insurance contract voidable |
| **2. Insurable Interest** | The insured must possess a legitimate pecuniary or legal relationship to the subject matter, suffering financial loss upon its destruction | Must exist at inception in life insurance; must exist at time of loss in marine insurance; at both times in property insurance |
| **3. Principle of Indemnity** | The insurer promises to restore the insured to the exact financial position enjoyed immediately prior to the loss, preventing commercial profit | Applies to general and property insurance; does **NOT apply to Life Insurance** (human life cannot be monetarily indemnified) |
| **4. Subrogation** | After settling an indemnity claim, the insurer acquires all legal rights and remedies of the insured against liable third parties | Operates strictly as a corollary of indemnity; does NOT apply to life or personal accident insurance |
| **5. Contribution** | Where multiple indemnity policies cover the exact same subject matter, insurers share the loss rateably | Prevents double recovery; does NOT apply to life insurance policies |
| **6. Proximate Cause** *(Causa Proxima)* | The dominant, active, and efficient cause that sets in motion a train of events leading to a loss determines coverage | If the proximate cause is an uninsured/excluded peril, the claim is repudiated |
| **7. Mitigation of Loss** | The insured must take all reasonable and prudent steps to minimize loss or damage during an insured catastrophe | Insured cannot deliberately act negligibly simply because the asset is covered by insurance |

## 10. 📈 CAPITAL MARKETS, SEBI REFORMS & MACRO BASE YEARS

### 10.1 Capital Markets Infrastructure & Securities Instruments [FOUNDATION]

* **Primary Market vs Secondary Market**:
  - **Primary Market**: Issuers raise fresh capital directly from investors through Initial Public Offerings (IPOs), Further Public Offerings (FPOs), or Private Placements. Proceeds flow directly to the issuing company.
  - **Secondary Market**: Investors trade already-issued securities among themselves on stock exchanges (BSE, NSE). Trading transfers ownership; proceeds flow to selling investors, not the corporate issuer.
* **Core Market Intermediaries**:
  - **Depositories (NSDL, CDSL)**: Hold securities in electronic/dematerialized form; regulated by SEBI under Depositories Act, 1996.
  - **Depository Participants (DPs)**: Financial institutions/brokers acting as retail access conduits between investors and depositories.
  - **Clearing Corporations (NSCCL, ICCL)**: Provide multilateral clearing, novation, settlement guarantee, and counterparty risk management.
* **Money Market Debt Instruments**:
  - **Commercial Paper (CP)**: Short-term unsecured promissory note issued by highly-rated corporate entities, primary dealers, and AIFIs to meet working capital requirements (tenors: 7 days to 1 year; minimum ₹5 Lakh).
  - **Certificate of Deposit (CD)**: Negotiable, dematerialized money-market instrument issued by scheduled commercial banks against term deposits (tenors: 7 days to 1 year; minimum ₹1 Lakh).

### 10.2 SEBI Capital Market Reforms & Risk Guardrails [RULE-SENSITIVE]

* **Equity Settlement Cycle Evolution**:
  - India transitioned from $T+2$ to standard **$T+1$ settlement in January 2023**.
  - SEBI introduced an optional **$T+0$ beta rolling settlement** in March 2024 (trading from 9:15 AM to 1:30 PM, settling funds and securities by 4:30 PM same day), expanded in December 2024 towards instant settlement.
* **Mutual Fund Lite (MF Lite)**:
  - Relaxed, light-touch regulatory framework tailored exclusively for **passively managed schemes** (Index Funds and Exchange Traded Funds [ETFs]), reducing compliance overhead, capital requirements, and disclosure friction. Does NOT apply to actively managed mutual funds.
* **Equity Derivatives (F&O) Market Guardrails (Risk Mitigation)**:
  - **Contract Lot Sizing**: Minimum index derivative contract value enhanced from ₹5 Lakh to **₹15 Lakh to ₹20 Lakh** to curb retail over-leverage.
  - **Weekly Expiries Rationalisation**: Capped at **one benchmark index expiry per exchange per week**.
  - **Upfront Option Premium Collection**: Mandatory upfront collection of option buyer premiums.
  - **Intraday Monitoring**: Real-time snapshot monitoring of index position limits to prevent synthetic manipulation.

### 10.4 Fiscal Indicators & Public Finance Terminology [FOUNDATION]

* **Revenue Deficit**: Revenue Expenditure minus Revenue Receipts. Measures borrowing incurred to finance everyday consumption expenditure that creates no productive capital asset.
* **Fiscal Deficit**: Total Expenditure minus Total Non-Debt Receipts. Represents the **gross borrowing requirement of the government**.
* **Primary Deficit**: Fiscal Deficit minus Interest Payments. Indicates the government's borrowing requirement excluding legacy debt-servicing burdens.
* **Direct Tax vs Indirect Tax**: Direct taxes (income tax, corporate tax) are levied directly on income/wealth where impact and incidence fall on the same entity. Indirect taxes (GST, customs) are transaction-based and can be shifted across the supply chain to final consumers.

## 12. ⚡ QUICK REVISION MATRICES, MUST-KNOW PAIRS & NUMERICAL CHEAT-SHEET

### 12.1 22 Must-Know Conceptual Distinction Pairs [FOUNDATION]

| Concept Pair | Core Distinction in One Sentence |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CRR vs SLR** | CRR is cash maintained with the RBI (yielding 0% interest); SLR is eligible liquid assets (G-Secs, cash, gold) maintained by the bank itself. |
| **RBI Act vs BR Act** | RBI Act 1934 governs central bank powers, currency issue, and monetary policy; BR Act 1949 regulates commercial banking operations, licensing, and supervision. |
| **Liquidity vs Solvency** | Liquidity is meeting current cash-flow obligations as they fall due; Solvency is having sufficient asset value and capital to exceed total liabilities. |
| **Deposits vs Advances** | Deposits are liabilities (the bank owes depositors); Loans and advances are assets (borrowers owe the bank). |
| **NEFT vs RTGS** | NEFT operates in half-hourly deferred net settlement batches; RTGS settles transactions individually in real time with a ₹2 Lakh minimum. |
| **UPI vs Bank Account** | UPI is an interoperable messaging and payment routing protocol; a bank account is the underlying deposit liability repository. |
| **SFB vs Payments Bank** | SFBs advance loans and accept unlimited deposits; Payments Banks can accept demand deposits up to ₹2 Lakh but are prohibited from lending. |
| **DICGC vs RBI Ombudsman** | DICGC provides deposit insurance up to ₹5 Lakh in liquidation/moratorium; the Ombudsman resolves operational service deficiency complaints. |
| **LCR vs NSFR** | LCR measures 30-day short-term acute cash outflow resilience; NSFR enforces 1-year structural stable balance sheet funding. |
| **Gross NPA vs Net NPA** | Gross NPA is total defaulted loans; Net NPA is Gross NPA minus accumulated loan-loss provisions. |
| **Nostro vs Vostro** | Nostro is our account with a foreign bank in foreign currency; Vostro is a foreign bank's account with us in Indian Rupees. |
| **Primary vs Secondary Market** | In primary markets, corporate issuers raise fresh capital; in secondary markets, investors trade existing securities among themselves. |
| **ETF vs Index Mutual Fund** | ETFs trade continuously throughout the trading day on stock exchanges; index mutual funds transact at end-of-day NAV. |
| **Insurance Penetration vs Density** | Penetration is insurance premium as a percentage of GDP; Density is insurance premium per capita (in USD). |
| **Indemnity vs Life Insurance** | General insurance indemnifies actual financial loss; Life insurance pays a contractually agreed sum assured upon death or maturity. |
| **Consolidated Fund vs Public Account** | Consolidated Fund holds sovereign revenues and borrowings requiring Parliamentary appropriation; Public Account holds fiduciary public moneys. |
| **Fundamental Right vs Article 300A** | Property is a legal/constitutional right under Article 300A, no longer an enforceable Fundamental Right in Part III. |
| **CPI vs WPI** | CPI includes consumer services and serves as the MPC's inflation target; WPI tracks wholesale goods only and excludes services. |
| **M0 vs M1** | M0 includes currency in circulation; M1 includes currency with the public (which excludes vault cash held by commercial banks). |
| **Commercial Paper vs CD** | CPs are issued by corporate entities and primary dealers; CDs are issued by scheduled commercial banks. |
| **T+1 vs T+0 Settlement** | T+1 settles trades on the next business day; T+0 settles cash and securities on the same trade date. |
| **Statute Assent vs Commencement** | An Act receives Presidential assent on one date, but provisions take legal effect on the notified commencement date. |

### Master Answer Key & Diagnostic Explanations

1. **Deposit = Liability; Loan = Asset**: The bank owes deposited funds back to the customer on demand or maturity; conversely, the borrower is indebted to the bank, generating future principal repayments and interest cash flows.
2. **Liquidity vs Solvency vs Profitability**: Liquidity is having cash/liquid assets to pay obligations due immediately; Solvency is having total asset value exceeding total debt liabilities plus equity cushion; Profitability is generating positive net operating income after costs, provisions, and taxes.
3. **Basel III Total Capital Discrepancy**: Global Basel III minimum total capital is **8.00% of RWA** (10.5% with 2.5% CCB); India's RBI mandates a higher SCB base of **9.00% of RWA**, resulting in an **11.50% total capital requirement** with CCB.
4. **RBI Act Sec 22 vs Sec 24**: Section 22 confers exclusive legal authority upon RBI to issue currency banknotes; Section 24 establishes the maximum banknote denomination ceiling (up to ₹10,000).
5. **CRR vs SLR Governing Laws**: CRR is mandated under **Section 42(1) of RBI Act, 1934**; SLR is mandated under **Section 24 of Banking Regulation Act, 1949**.
6. **Repo Rate vs CRR Transmission**: Repo changes the policy interest rate and borrowing cost of liquidity from the central bank; CRR directly impounds a fixed percentage of commercial bank deposit liabilities as cash with RBI, altering the physical volume of lendable reserves.
7. **MPC Composition & Rules**: 6 members (3 RBI including Governor, 3 Central Govt external nominees). External members serve a **4-year non-renewable term**. Quorum is **4 members**; Governor holds a casting vote in the event of a tie; minutes are published on the **14th day**.
8. **CPI over WPI as Anchor**: CPI measures retail price changes including consumer services and cost-of-living impacts on households; WPI measures wholesale primary/bulk goods and completely excludes services.
9. **LCR vs NSFR**: LCR enforces holding high-quality liquid assets against severe cash outflows over **30 calendar days**; NSFR requires structural balance-sheet matching of available stable funding against required stable funding over a **1-year horizon**.
10. **RTGS vs NEFT UTR**: RTGS UTR is **22 characters** (`4 IFSC + 1 'R' + 1 Channel + 8 Date + 8 Sequence`); NEFT UTR is **16 characters** generated uniquely per batch clearing.
11. **Payment Rail Settlements**: NEFT uses half-hourly Deferred Net Settlement (DNS); RTGS uses continuous Real-Time Gross Settlement; IMPS is an instant 24x7 interbank retail transfer; UPI is an interoperable mobile payment overlay protocol.
12. **LEI Dual Thresholds**: The **₹5 Crore** threshold applies to aggregate borrower credit exposure from commercial banks/FIs; the **₹50 Crore** threshold applies per individual cross-border transaction under FEMA.
13. **NPA Provisions & CRAR**: Loan-loss provisions are deducted directly from gross revenue on the income statement, reducing net profit; lower net profit reduces retained earnings in Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1), directly reducing the numerator of CRAR.
14. **SMA-2 to Substandard Timeline**: SMA-2 is overdue between **61 to 90 days**; once overdue payments cross **90 days**, the loan is formally classified as a Non-Performing Asset (Substandard NPA).
15. **Doubtful Asset Slabs**: Provisions on the secured portion escalate based on time in the doubtful category: **D1 (up to 1 yr) = 25%**, **D2 (1–3 yrs) = 40%**, **D3 (> 3 yrs) = 100%**. Unsecured exposure requires **100% provision** immediately.
16. **Payments Bank Restrictions**: Licensed under Section 22 of BR Act 1949 with a specific mandate barring lending; customer deposits are capped at **₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh)** at end-of-day.
17. **DICGC Multi-Branch Cover**: Deposits across all branches of the *same bank* are pooled together; the customer receives a **single composite cover up to ₹5 Lakh**, not ₹5 Lakh per branch.
18. **DICGC 90-Day Breakdown (Sec 18A)**: Bank submits depositor claim lists within **45 days** $\to$ DICGC verifies claims within **30 days** $\to$ DICGC disburses funds within **15 days** = **Exactly 90 days total**.
19. **RB-IOS 2026 Provisions**: Direct financial loss compensation up to **₹30 Lakh**; mental anguish compensation up to **₹3 Lakh** (Max: ₹33 Lakh); complaint filing window is **90 days** from response expiry; appeal window against an Award is **30 days**.
20. **Indemnity Principle in Life Insurance**: Indemnity restores an insured to their exact pre-loss financial state; human life has no monetary equivalent, so life insurance pays a contractually agreed sum assured, not an indemnification.
21. **Insurance 100% FDI**: Enacted via the **Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025** (assented 20 Dec 2025; commenced **5 February 2026**).
22. **Bima Trinity Roles**: Bima **Sugam** is the universal digital electronic exchange portal; Bima **Vistar** is the bundled composite micro-insurance product; Bima **Vahak** is the women-led Gram Panchayat distribution force.
23. **Active Macro Base Years**: GDP = **2022–23**; CPI-Combined = **2024 = 100** (358 items); IIP = **2022–23 = 100**; Core Industries = **2022–23** (9 sectors with Iron Ore).
24. **GST Council Voting (Art 279A)**: Central Government holds **1/3rd (33.33%)** voting weight; State Governments collectively hold **2/3rd (66.67%)** voting weight; decisions require a **3/4th (75%) weighted majority** of members present and voting.
25. **Right to Property Transition**: Omitted from Fundamental Rights (Art 19(1)(f) and Art 31) via the **44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978** and re-established as a **Constitutional / Legal Right under Article 300A in Part XII**.
