📰 [APR-010] **RBI — Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) Mandatory for Digital Payments**

🪝 Hook — A single OTP will no longer cut it: from April 1, 2026, every UPI, card, and wallet transaction needs a second layer of verification.

- **Mandatory Two-Factor Scope**: 2FA made compulsory across UPI, debit/credit cards, and prepaid mobile wallets — standalone SMS OTP is legally insufficient.
- **Secondary Factor Choices**: Requires PIN, biometric fingerprint, hardware token, password, or facial recognition as the dynamic secondary authentication layer.
- **Institutional Liability Shift**: Regulated banks and payment platforms held financially liable to compensate customers for unauthorized fraud losses arising from system vulnerabilities.
- **Staggered Compliance Timeline**: Effective **April 1, 2026**; full enforcement across all merchant terminals and cross-border transactions mandated by **October 2026**.

🎯 Exam Angle → 🔥 HIGH — Effective date (Apr 1, 2026) vs full implementation date (Oct 2026) — classic date-confusion trap.

📰 [APR-011] **RBI — Digital Payments Fraud Safeguards (Discussion Paper)**

🪝 Hook — RBI's proposed fix for UPI fraud: force a one-hour "cooling-off" pause on big transfers, borrowing straight from the "golden hour" principle in crime prevention.

- **1-hour cooling-off period** proposed for A2A transfers above ₹10,000 (no chargeback option currently exists)
- **Data-Driven Fraud Threshold**: Threshold chosen because ₹10,000+ transfers = ~45% of fraud cases by volume, ~98.5% by value
- **High-Value Secondary Verification**: Transactions above ₹50,000 → additional verification (trusted-individual approval)
- **Mule account** annual credit cap proposed at **₹25 lakh**
- **New user controls**: channel on/off toggle, custom limits, emergency "kill switch"

💼 Interview Q → *What is the "golden hour" principle referenced by RBI?* → The idea that the period immediately after a fraudulent transaction is most critical to prevent dissipation of funds — hence the proposed 1-hour delay window.

🎯 Exam Angle → 🔥 HIGH — ₹10,000 (cooling-off threshold) vs ₹50,000 (extra verification) vs ₹25 lakh (mule account cap) — three-number trap cluster.

📰 [APR-013] **RBI — NBFC Regulatory Overhaul Cluster** *(merged: 3-layer proposal, NBFC-UL threshold change, branch authorisation, TReDS)*

🪝 Hook — RBI is rewriting the NBFC rulebook on three fronts at once: how NBFCs are layered, how the "upper layer" is defined, and how easily they can open branches.

- **A.** Proposed new **3-layer** framework (Upper/Middle/Lower) to replace existing 4-layer Scale-Based Regulation (Base/Middle/Upper/Top, Oct 2021)
- **B.** Proposed replacing NBFC-UL identification methodology with a flat **₹1 lakh crore asset threshold** (vs. current Top-10 + parametric scoring: 70% quantitative, 30% qualitative); to be reviewed every 5 years. Also proposed removing the carve-out that shielded govt-owned NBFCs (NABARD, Exim Bank, SIDBI) from UL classification — aligning to "ownership-neutral regulation"
- **C.** NBFC Branch Authorisation, 2026 — NBFCs can open branches without prior RBI approval based on Net Owned Funds (NOF) & credit rating thresholds (₹50 crore NOF / AA rating benchmark)
- **D.** Proposed dropping due-diligence requirement for MSMEs onboarding **TReDS** (Trade Receivables Discounting System)

⏰ Status flag → All four are draft/proposed as of April 2026 — check for finalisation before exam.

🎯 Exam Angle → 🔥 HIGH — ₹1 lakh crore NBFC-UL threshold is the standout figure across this cluster.
