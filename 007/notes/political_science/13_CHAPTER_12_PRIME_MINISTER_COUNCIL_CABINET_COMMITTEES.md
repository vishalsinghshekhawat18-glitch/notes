## 1. The Real Executive Architecture (*De Facto* Governance)

In the Indian constitutional system, while the President is the nominal, constitutional executive head (*de jure*), the **Prime Minister is the real executive authority (*de facto*)**. In the immortal words of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar in the Constituent Assembly:
> *"If any functionary under our Indian Constitution is to be compared with the US President, he is the Prime Minister and not the President of the Union."*

## 2. The Prime Minister of India: Appointment, Powers & Dynamics

### A. Appointment & Qualifications (Article 75(1))
- **Article 75(1)**: *"The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President and the other Ministers shall be appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister."*
- **Parliamentary Convention**: The President appoints the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha.
- **Presidential Discretion**: When no single party secures an absolute majority in the Lok Sabha, the President exercises situational discretion to appoint a Prime Minister.
  - The President conventionally invites the leader of the **single largest party** or the leader of a **pre-poll coalition** and directs him to seek a **vote of confidence** in the Lok Sabha within a specified timeframe (usually one month).
  - *Historical Precedent*: In 1979, President N. Sanjiva Reddy appointed Charan Singh after the fall of the Morarji Desai Janata government.
- **Eligibility & House Affiliation**:
  - The Prime Minister may be a member of either the **Lok Sabha** or the **Rajya Sabha**.
  - *Distinguished Rajya Sabha Prime Ministers*:
    1. Indira Gandhi (1966)
    2. H.D. Deve Gowda (1996)
    3. I.K. Gujral (1997)
    4. Dr. Manmohan Singh (2004, 2009)
  - **Six-Month Leeway (Article 75(5))**: A person who is not a member of either House can be appointed Prime Minister (or Minister), but ceases to be so upon the expiration of **six consecutive months** unless he is elected or nominated to either House (*confirmed in S.P. Anand v. H.D. Deve Gowda, 1996*).

### C. Article 78: The Duties of the Prime Minister
Article 78 defines the duties of the Prime Minister in relation to the President:
1. **Article 78(a)**: To communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation.
2. **Article 78(b)**: To furnish such information relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation as the President may call for.
3. **Article 78(c)**: If the President so requires, to submit for the consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a minister but which has not been considered by the Council (*enforces collective deliberation*).

## 3. The Union Council of Ministers: Constitutional Architecture

### A. Core Articles Overview (Articles 74 & 75)
- **Article 74(1)**: Mandates a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President, who **shall act in accordance with such advice** (subject to the 44th Amendment one-time reconsideration proviso).
- **Article 74(2)**: Bars judicial inquiry: *"The question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the President shall not be inquired into in any court."*
  - *Distinction*: In *S.R. Bommai (1994)*, the Supreme Court held that while the advice itself is shielded under Article 74(2), the **material on the basis of which the advice was tendered** is not exempt from judicial scrutiny!
- **Article 75(2)**: Ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President** (*Individual Responsibility*).
- **Article 75(3)**: The Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha)** (*Collective Responsibility*).

---

### B. The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act (2003): Structural Reforms
The 91st Amendment Act, 2003 introduced two transformative restrictions into Article 75:
1. **Size Ceiling (Article 75(1A))**:
   $$\text{Total Number of Ministers (including PM)} \le 15\% \times \text{Total Strength of the Lok Sabha}$$
   *(For Lok Sabha strength of 543, the Union Council of Ministers cannot exceed 81 ministers).*
2. **Anti-Defection Disqualification for Ministerial Office (Article 75(1B))**:
   A member of either House of Parliament belonging to any political party who is disqualified on the ground of defection under the **Tenth Schedule** shall also be disqualified from being appointed as a Minister for the duration of the period commencing from the date of disqualification until the expiration of his legislative term, or until he contests election again and is re-elected, whichever is earlier.

### D. The Doctrine of Individual Responsibility (Article 75(2))
- Ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President**.
- In practical constitutional reality, this "pleasure" is exercised **strictly on the advice of the Prime Minister**.
- If a minister's conduct or performance is unsatisfactory, or if a minister loses the confidence of the Prime Minister, the PM can ask the minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him.

### E. Legal Responsibility: British vs. Indian Divergence
- **British System**: Every official order issued by the monarch must be countersigned by a minister, who is **legally responsible** in a court of law for the legality of that order ("The King can do no wrong").
- **Indian System**: There is **NO provision for the legal responsibility of a minister**.
  1. Orders of the President are authenticated by civil servants (Secretaries) under the *Authentication Rules, 1958*.
  2. Courts are barred by **Article 74(2)** from inquiring into what advice was tendered by ministers to the President.

> [!NOTE]
> **Kitchen Cabinet / Inner Cabinet**:
> An informal, extra-constitutional body consisting of the Prime Minister and 2 to 4 influential colleagues or personal advisors. It acts as an ultra-concentrated decision-making circle.
> - *Advantages*: Fast decision-making; high secrecy on sensitive strategic matters.
> - *Disadvantages*: Reduces the constitutional Cabinet to a rubber stamp; bypasses parliamentary accountability.

## 5. Cabinet Committees: The Policy Processing Engine

### A. Nature, Origin & Characteristics
1. **Extra-Constitutional Bodies**: They are **not mentioned anywhere in the Constitution**. They are created by the Prime Minister under the **Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961**.
2. **Two Types**:
   - **Standing Committees**: Permanent in nature (e.g., ACC, CCS, CCEA).
   - **Ad-Hoc Committees**: Temporary bodies created for specific crises or policy reviews, disbanded once their task is complete.
3. **Membership**:
   - Usually comprise 3 to 8 ministers.
   - Dominated by Cabinet Ministers, though Ministers of State can be members or special invitees.
4. **Chairmanship**:
   - The **Prime Minister** chairs almost all crucial Cabinet Committees.
   - *Exceptions*: The **Cabinet Committee on Accommodation** and the **Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs** are conventionally chaired by senior ministers (such as the Home Minister or Defence Minister).

## 6. The Cabinet Secretariat & The Cabinet Secretary

### A. The Cabinet Secretariat
- Operates directly under the **Prime Minister of India**.
- Functions as the administrative nerve centre coordinating inter-ministerial policy.
- Entrusted with the administration of:
  1. *The Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961*.
  2. *The Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961*.
- Prepares Cabinet agendas, circulates classified memoranda, records minutes of meetings, and monitors the implementation of Cabinet decisions by line ministries.

### B. The Cabinet Secretary: The Administrative Apex
- **Senior-most civil servant of the Republic of India**.
- Head of the civil services and ex-officio Chairman of the **Civil Services Board**.
- Acts as the chief advisor to the Prime Minister on administrative governance.
- Ranks **No. 11** in the *Table of Precedence* of India (above Attorney General and Service Chiefs).

## 8. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. What is the precise ceiling imposed by the 91st Constitutional Amendment Act 2003 on the size 
    of the Union Council of Ministers?
A1. The total number of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, in the Council of Ministers shall 
    not exceed 15% of the total strength of the House of the People (Lok Sabha) under Article 75(1A).

Q2. What occurs to the Council of Ministers if the sitting Prime Minister dies in office or resigns?
A2. The entire Council of Ministers automatically dissolves. The death or resignation of the PM 
    creates an institutional vacuum that collapses the ministry, whereas the death or resignation 
    of any other minister merely creates a casual ministerial vacancy.

Q3. Can an individual who is not a member of either House of Parliament be appointed Prime Minister? 
    Cite the constitutional provision and judicial confirmation.
A3. Yes, under Article 75(5), any person can be appointed Prime Minister or Minister for up to 6 
    consecutive months without being an MP. This was upheld by the Supreme Court in S.P. Anand v. 
    H.D. Deve Gowda (1996).

Q4. In which article was the word "Cabinet" first introduced into the Constitution of India, and by 
    which amendment?
A4. The word "Cabinet" was introduced into Article 352 by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978. 
    It did not exist in the original 1950 Constitution text.

Q5. Which two Standing Cabinet Committees are NOT conventionally chaired by the Prime Minister?
A5. (1) The Cabinet Committee on Accommodation, and (2) The Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs 
    (both are conventionally chaired by senior ministers like the Home Minister or Defence Minister).
```
