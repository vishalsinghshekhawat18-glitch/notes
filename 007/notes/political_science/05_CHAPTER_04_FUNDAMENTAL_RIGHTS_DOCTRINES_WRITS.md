## 4.1 Epistemic Foundations: The Magna Carta of India

Part III of the Constitution (Articles 12 to 35) is rightfully described as the **Magna Carta of India**. Inspired by the American **Bill of Rights**, it establishes a comprehensive, enforceable charter of civil liberties designed to promote the ideal of **Political Democracy**.

Fundamental Rights function as a **negative obligation on the State** (preventing executive tyranny and legislative overreach) and a **positive guarantee to the individual** (securing human dignity, liberty, and equality).

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       THE CORE CHARACTERISTICS OF PART III RIGHTS                           │
├─────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Justiciability   │ Directly enforceable in courts under Articles 32 (SC) and 226 (HC).   │
│ 2. Defended by Apex │ The Supreme Court is the designated guarantor and defender of FRs.    │
│ 3. Not Absolute     │ Subject to "Reasonable Restrictions" striking a balance between       │
│                     │ individual liberty and collective social order.                       │
│ 4. Not Sacrosanct   │ Can be curtailed by Parliament via Article 368, provided the          │
│                     │ **Basic Structure** of the Constitution is not destroyed.             │
│ 5. Emergency Limits │ Suspended during National Emergency (Art 352), EXCEPT Articles 20 & 21│
│                     │ (which can NEVER be suspended under any circumstance—44th Amend 1978).│
└─────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 4.2 Preliminary Thresholds: Articles 12 and 13

### 1. Article 12: The Definition of "State"
Fundamental Rights are enforceable primarily against the arbitrary actions of the **State**, not against private individuals (with specific exceptions like Articles 15(2), 17, 23, and 24). Article 12 defines the "State" for the purpose of Part III:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                            THE FOUR COMPONENT TIERS OF ARTICLE 12                           │
├───────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Central Executive & Legislature│ Government of India and Parliament of India.            │
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. State Executive & Legislature  │ Government of each State and State Legislatures.        │
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Local Authorities              │ Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards, Improvement│
│                                   │ Trusts, Port Trusts (within India or under GoI control).│
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Other Authorities              │ Statutory and non-statutory bodies functioning as       │
│                                   │ instrumentalities of the State (LIC, ONGC, SAIL, GAIL,  │
│                                   │ CSIR, NTA, Airport Authority of India).                 │
└───────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **The Decisive Instrumentality Test (*Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib, 1981*)**: A corporation or agency is an instrumentality of the State under Article 12 if:
  1. Entire share capital is held by the government.
  2. Financial assistance of the state meets almost the entire expenditure of the corporation.
  3. Corporation enjoys a monopoly status conferred or protected by the state.
  4. Deep and pervasive state control exists.
  5. Functions performed are of public importance and closely related to governmental functions.
* **Is the Judiciary a "State" under Article 12?**
  * When performing **administrative functions** (e.g., recruitments, appointments): **YES, it is a State**.
  * When performing **judicial functions** (adjudicating cases): **NO, it is not a State**. A judicial order of a competent court cannot be challenged as a violation of Fundamental Rights under Article 32 (the remedy lies in an appeal or review, as held in *Naresh Sridhar Mirajkar v. State of Maharashtra, 1967*).

---

### 2. Article 13: Laws Inconsistent with Fundamental Rights
Article 13 provides the constitutional backbone for **Judicial Review**, declaring all laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights to be **void**.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                           THE DOCTRINAL TRINITY OF ARTICLE 13                               │
├─────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Doctrine of      │ Pre-constitutional laws (enacted prior to 26 Jan 1950) that clash with│
│    Eclipse (Art 13(1))│ FRs do not die completely; they are overshadowed ("eclipsed") and   │
│                     │ remain dormant. If a constitutional amendment removes the clash, the  │
│                     │ law becomes active again without fresh enactment (*Bhikaji 1955*).    │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Doctrine of      │ If a statute contains valid and invalid provisions, courts will strike│
│    Severability     │ down ONLY the unconstitutional clauses, provided they can be severed  │
│    (Art 13(1) & (2))│ from the rest without dismantling the entire legislative purpose.     │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Doctrine of      │ A citizen CANNOT waive their Fundamental Rights. Because FRs are an   │
│    Non-Waiver       │ obligation imposed on the State for the public good, no citizen can   │
│                     │ voluntarily surrender them (*Basheshar Nath v. CIT, 1959*).           │
└─────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Definition of "Law" under Article 13(3)**:
  * Permanent laws enacted by Parliament or State Legislatures.
  * Temporary laws (Ordinances issued by President [Art 123] or Governor [Art 213]).
  * Delegated legislation (Statutory rules, regulations, orders, notifications, bye-laws).
  * Non-legislative sources of law (Custom or usage having the force of law).
* **Is a Constitutional Amendment Act a "Law" under Article 13?**
  * In *Golaknath (1967)*, the SC held that an amendment under Article 368 is an ordinary "law" under Article 13, and Parliament cannot abridge FRs.
  * The **24th Constitutional Amendment Act (1971)** inserted **Article 13(4)** and **Article 368(3)**, declaring that nothing in Article 13 applies to an amendment made under Article 368.
  * In *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)*, the Supreme Court upheld Article 13(4), ruling that a constitutional amendment is **constituent law, not ordinary law**. However, if an amendment abrogates a Fundamental Right that forms part of the **Basic Structure**, the court will invalidate it.

## 4.3 Group I: Right to Equality (Articles 14 to 18)

│ Article 16  │ Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment                       │
│ Article 17  │ Abolition of Untouchability                                                   │
│ Article 18  │ Abolition of Titles                                                           │
└─────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Article 14: Equality Before Law & Equal Protection of the Laws
*"The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India."*
* Applies to **all persons** (citizens, foreign nationals, statutory corporations, companies).
* **The Dual Conceptual Dichotomy**:
  * **"Equality Before Law" (British Origin)**: Negative concept signifying the absence of any special privilege in favor of any individual; all individuals are subject to the ordinary law of the land.
  * **"Equal Protection of the Laws" (American Origin)**: Positive concept signifying the equality of treatment under equal circumstances; like should be treated alike without discrimination.
* **The Doctrine of Reasonable Classification**: Article 14 forbids *class legislation*, but permits *reasonable classification* of persons, objects, and transactions by the legislature. To be valid, a classification must pass two tests:
  1. **Intelligible Differentia**: The classification must be founded on an intelligible differentia which distinguishes persons/things grouped together from others left out.
  2. **Rational Nexus**: The differentia must have a rational relation to the object sought to be achieved by the statute.
* **The New Dynamic Concept (*E.P. Royappa v. State of TN, 1974*)**: Justice P.N. Bhagwati propounded that equality is a dynamic concept with many aspects and cannot be confined within traditional limits. **Equality is antithetic to Arbitrariness**: *"Where an act is arbitrary, it is implicit in it that it is unequal both according to political logic and constitutional law and therefore violative of Article 14."*
* **Exceptions to Article 14**:
  * **Article 361**: The President and State Governors enjoy immunity from criminal proceedings, arrest, and imprisonment during their term of office.
  * **Article 105 & 194**: MPs and MLAs enjoy immunity for anything said or any vote cast in Parliament or State Assemblies.
  * **Article 31C**: Laws enacted to give effect to DPSPs under Article 39(b) and 39(c) cannot be challenged under Article 14 (*"Where Article 31C comes in, Article 14 goes out"*).
  * Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors, and diplomats enjoy diplomatic immunity from civil and criminal jurisdiction.

---

### 2. Article 15: Prohibition of Discrimination
*"The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth or any of them."*
* **The Word "Only"**: Discrimination based on religion/sex **combined with other factors** (e.g., physical capacity, economic criteria) is not prohibited.
* **Horizontal Enforcement (Article 15(2))**: No citizen shall, on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth, be subjected to restriction regarding:
  * Access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, and places of public entertainment.
  * Use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, and places of public resort maintained wholly or partly out of State funds.  
  *(Enforceable against both the State and private individuals).*
* **Constitutional Exceptions (Affirmative Action)**:
  * **Article 15(3)**: State can make special provisions for **women and children** (e.g., reservation of seats for women in local bodies, free education for children).
  * **Article 15(4)**: Added by the **1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951** (overruling *State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan*). Enables the State to make special provisions for the advancement of any **socially and educationally backward classes of citizens (SEBCs)** or for the **SCs and STs**.
  * **Article 15(5)**: Added by the **93rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2005**. Empowers the State to provide reservations for SEBCs, SCs, and STs in **admissions to educational institutions** (including private aided or unaided institutions, *except minority educational institutions covered under Article 30(1)*).
  * **Article 15(6)**: Added by the **103rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019**. Empowers the State to provide up to **10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)** of citizens (other than SEBCs/SCs/STs) in educational admissions. Upheld as constitutionally valid in *Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)*.

---

### 3. Article 16: Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment
Guarantees equality of opportunity for all citizens in matters relating to employment or appointment to any office under the State.
* Prohibits discrimination on **seven specific grounds**: Religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence. *(Note: "Descent" and "Residence" are added here, which are not in Article 15).*
* **Exceptions to Article 16**:
  * **Article 16(3)**: Parliament can prescribe **residence** within a State/UT as a qualification for certain appointments (currently applied only in parts of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana under Article 371-D). State legislatures cannot prescribe residence; only Parliament has this power.
  * **Article 16(4)**: Empowers the State to make **reservations in appointments or posts in favor of any backward class of citizens** which, in the opinion of the State, is **not adequately represented** in the services under the State.
  * **Article 16(4-A)**: Added by the **77th Amendment Act, 1995** (and modified by the **85th Amendment, 2001**). Empowers the State to provide **reservation in promotions with consequential seniority** for SCs and STs.
  * **Article 16(4-B)**: Added by the **81st Amendment Act, 2000**. The "Carry Forward Rule"—backlog vacancies of SCs/STs are treated as a separate class and are not counted towards the 50% reservation ceiling of the current year.
  * **Article 16(5)**: Incumbent of an office connected with a religious institution can be required to profess that particular religion.
  * **Article 16(6)**: 103rd Amendment 2019—up to 10% reservation in public appointments for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS).

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE INDRA SAWHNEY (MANDAL CASE, 1992) JURISPRUDENTIAL CODE                  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. 27% OBC Quota Upheld: Backward classes can be identified primarily on caste basis.       │
│ 2. Creamy Layer Exclusion: Advanced sections among OBCs must be excluded from quota benefits.│
│ 3. 50% Ceiling Rule: Total reservation shall not exceed 50% except in extraordinary cases.  │
│ 4. No Reservation in Promotions: Article 16(4) applies only to initial appointments.        │
│    (Overridden by 77th Amendment 1995 inserting Art 16(4-A) for SCs and STs).               │
│ 5. Efficiency Guardrail: Reservations must be balanced with administrative efficiency       │
│    under Article 335.                                                                       │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4. Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability
* Untouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. The enforcement of any disability arising out of untouchability is an offence punishable in accordance with law.
* **Key Attributes**:
  * An **absolute right**; no exceptions or reasonable restrictions exist.
  * The word *"Untouchability"* is **neither defined in the Constitution nor in any Act of Parliament**. The Mysore High Court (*Hadibandhu Behera*) held that it refers not to physical touch in a literal sense, but to the historical social practice developed in the Hindu caste system.
  * To enforce Article 17, Parliament enacted the *Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955*, comprehensively overhauled and renamed in 1976 as the **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** (and strengthened via the *SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act, 1989*).
  * A person convicted of an untouchability offence is **disqualified from contesting elections** to Parliament or State Legislatures under Section 8 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

---

### 5. Article 18: Abolition of Titles
* Prohibits the State from conferring any title, except **military or academic distinctions**.
* Prohibits an Indian citizen from accepting any title from any foreign State.
* Foreigners holding an office of profit/trust under the State cannot accept any title, present, or emolument from a foreign State without the **consent of the President**.
* **National Awards Controversy (*Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India, 1996*)**:
  * The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of the National Awards (**Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri**).
  * The Court ruled that these awards denote **state recognition of merit and excellence**, and are not "titles" prohibited under Article 18.
  * **Strict Proviso**: The recipients **cannot use them as prefixes or suffixes to their names**. If used as prefixes or suffixes in stationery, invitations, or public books, the award is liable to be forfeited.

## 4.4 Group II: Right to Freedom (Articles 19 to 22)

### 1. Article 19: Protection of Six Fundamental Freedoms
Article 19 guarantees six basic democratic freedoms to **citizens only** (not available to foreigners or legal companies).

* **Implicit Rights Embedded in Article 19(1)(a) by Judicial Interpretation**:
  * Freedom of the Press (*Indian Express Newspapers 1985*).
  * Right to Information (*RTI Act 2005* rooted in 19(1)(a)).
  * Right to silence / not to speak (*Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala, 1986*—National Anthem case).
  * Right to fly the National Flag (*Naveen Jindal 2004*).
  * Right against phone tapping (*PUCL 1997*).
  * Right to Internet access (*Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India, 2020*—freedom of speech and trade via the Internet enjoys constitutional protection under Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g)).

### 2. Article 20: Protection in Respect of Conviction for Offences
Article 20 provides three absolute constitutional shields to an accused person (citizen or foreigner):

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                            ARTICLE 20 THREEFOLD CONSTITUTIONAL SHIELD                       │
├─────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. No Ex-Post Facto │ No person shall be convicted of any offence except for violation of   │
│    Law (Art 20(1))  │ a law in force at the time of commission of the act, nor subjected to │
│                     │ a penalty greater than that prescribed at the time.                   │
│                     │ **CRITICAL LIMITATION**: Applies **ONLY to criminal laws**, NOT to   │
│                     │ civil laws or tax laws (tax liabilities can be imposed retroactively).│
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. No Double        │ No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more  │
│    Jeopardy         │ than once.                                                            │
│    (Art 20(2))      │ **CRITICAL LIMITATION**: Available **ONLY in proceedings before a     │
│                     │ court of law or judicial tribunal**. Does not bar departmental or     │
│                     │ administrative inquiries and dismissals.                              │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. No Self-         │ No person accused of an offence shall be compelled to be a witness   │
│    Incrimination    │ against himself.                                                      │
│    (Art 20(3))      │ **CRITICAL LIMITATION**: Applies only to accused in criminal cases.   │
│                     │ Does NOT protect against compulsory giving of thumb impressions,      │

* **The Narco-Analysis Landmark (*Selvi v. State of Karnataka, 2010*)**: The Supreme Court held that involuntary administration of polygraph (lie-detector) tests, narco-analysis, and brain mapping constitutes cruel, inhuman treatment violating **Article 20(3)** and the right to privacy under **Article 21**.

### 3. Article 21: Protection of Life and Personal Liberty
*"No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law."*

#### The Historic Paradigm Shift: Gopalan to Maneka Gandhi

#### The Expanding Horizons of Article 21 (Chapter 92 of Laxmikanth 8e Synthesis):
By interpreting "Life" not as mere animal existence but as life with human dignity, the Supreme Court has read dozens of fundamental rights into Article 21:
1. **Right to Privacy**: Declared an intrinsic part of the right to life and liberty by a unanimous 9-judge Constitution bench in *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India (2017)*.
2. **Right to Livelihood**: *Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985)* (Pavement dwellers case).
3. **Right to Clean Environment, Water & Air**: *M.C. Mehta* series; *Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991)*; recently expanded in 2024 (*MK Ranjitsinh*) to include the **Right to be free from adverse impacts of climate change**.
4. **Right to Die with Dignity (Passive Euthanasia & Living Wills)**: *Common Cause v. Union of India (2018)*.
5. **Decriminalization of Consensual Same-Sex Relations**: *Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)* (Reading down Section 377 IPC to protect sexual autonomy and constitutional morality).
6. **Decriminalization of Adultery**: *Joseph Shine v. Union of India (2018)* (Striking down Section 497 IPC as a paternalistic violation of women's dignity).
7. **Right to Speedy Trial**: *Hussainara Khatoon (1979)* (Under-trial prisoners).
8. **Right to Free Legal Aid**: *M.H. Hoskot v. State of Maharashtra (1978)*.
9. **Right against Solitary Confinement**: *Sunil Batra v. Delhi Administration (1980)*.
10. **Right against Handcuffing**: *Prem Shankar Shukla v. Delhi Administration (1980)*.

### 4. Article 21-A: Right to Education
* Inserted by the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002**.
* Mandates that the State shall provide **free and compulsory education to all children aged 6 to 14 years** in such manner as the State may determine.
* Parliament enacted the *Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009*, requiring 25% reservation for disadvantaged sections in private unaided schools.
* *(Note: Education for children below 6 years is placed under Directive Principles in **Article 45**).*

### 5. Article 22: Protection Against Arrest and Detention
Article 22 grants procedural protections against arrest, bifurcated into two regimes:
* **Punitive Detention**: To punish a person for an offence committed by them after trial and conviction in a court.
* **Preventive Detention**: Detention of a person without trial and conviction, based on reasonable suspicion to prevent them from committing an offence in the future.

│                                   │ 3. Right to be produced before nearest magistrate within│
│                                   │    **24 hours** (excluding journey time).               │
│                                   │ 4. Right to release after 24 hours unless magistrate    │
│                                   │    authorizes further detention.                        │
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Non-Applicability Exception       │ Punitive protections are **DENIED to Enemy Aliens** and │
│                                   │ persons arrested under **Preventive Detention Laws**.   │

* **Statutory Preventive Detention Acts**: *TADA (1985)*, *POTA (2002)*, *National Security Act (NSA 1980)*, *COFEPOSA (1974)*, and the *Unlawful Activities Prevention Act (UAPA 1967)*.

## 4.5 Group III: Right Against Exploitation (Articles 23 & 24)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        RIGHT AGAINST EXPLOITATION MAPPING                                   │
├─────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Article 23  │ Prohibition of traffic in human beings, begar, and other forced labor.        │
│ Article 24  │ Prohibition of employment of children in factories, mines, hazardous work.   │
└─────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Article 23 Deconstructed**:
  * Prohibits human trafficking, *begar* (involuntary labor without remuneration), and bonded labor. Applies against **both the State and private persons**.
  * **Constitutional Exception**: The State can impose **compulsory service for public purposes** (e.g., military conscription, disaster work), provided it makes no discrimination on grounds *only* of religion, race, caste, or class.
* **Article 24 Deconstructed**:
  * Forbids employment of children below the age of **14 years** in any factory, mine, or other hazardous activities (e.g., construction, railways).
  * Does not prohibit employment in harmless/non-hazardous work. However, the *Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016* amended this to prohibit employment of children below 14 in **all commercial occupations and processes**, allowing work only in family enterprises after school hours.

## 4.6 Group IV: Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25 to 28)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        RIGHT TO FREEDOM OF RELIGION MAPPING                                 │
├─────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Article 25  │ Freedom of conscience, free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.│
│ Article 26  │ Freedom of religious denominations to manage their religious affairs.         │
│ Article 27  │ Freedom from payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion.       │
│ Article 28  │ Freedom from attendance at religious instruction in educational institutions. │
└─────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Article 25 (Individual Right)**:
  * Freedom of conscience, profession (declaring faith openly), practice (performing rituals), and **propagation** (spreading tenets of faith).
  * **Propagation $\neq$ Conversion**: Article 25 does **NOT include the right to forcibly convert** another person (*Rev. Stanislaus v. State of MP, 1977*).
  * Subject to **Public Order, Morality, Health**, and other provisions of Part III.
  * **Explanation II**: The reference to Hindus in Article 25 includes **Sikhs, Jains, and Buddhists**. The wearing and carrying of *kirpans* is included in the profession of the Sikh religion.
* **Article 26 (Collective Denominational Right)**:
  * Grants religious denominations the right to establish and maintain institutions, manage internal religious affairs, and own/acquire and administer property in accordance with law.
  * **The Essential Religious Practices (ERP) Test**: The Supreme Court protects only those practices that are foundational and integral to the religion (e.g., *Sabarimala Case [Indian Young Lawyers Association 2018]* struck down exclusion of women aged 10–50 as violating Article 25).
* **Article 27 (Tax vs. Fee)**:
  * Prohibits the State from spending public tax money to promote any specific religion.
  * **Crucial Exception**: The State can levy a **FEE** on pilgrims to provide secular services (security, sanitation, crowd management). It cannot levy a **TAX**.
* **Article 28 (Religious Instruction in Schools)**:

## 4.7 Group V: Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29 & 30)

* **Article 29: Protection of Interests of Minorities**:
  * **Article 29(1)**: Any section of citizens residing in India having a distinct language, script, or culture has the right to conserve the same. **Not restricted to minorities**—protects any section (majority or minority).
  * **Article 29(2)**: No citizen shall be denied admission into any educational institution maintained by the State or receiving aid out of State funds on grounds only of religion, race, caste, language.
* **Article 30: Right of Minorities to Establish Educational Institutions**:
  * Conferred **ONLY upon Religious and Linguistic Minorities** (does not extend to any other minority, such as cultural, sexual, or political minorities).
  * Right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.
  * Minority institutions are exempt from reservations under Article 15(5) and the 25% quota under the RTE Act.

## 4.9 Articles 33, 34, and 35: Exceptional Immunities

* **Article 33**: Empowers **Parliament** (and NOT State Legislatures) to restrict or abrogate the Fundamental Rights of members of the **Armed Forces, Paramilitary Forces, Police Forces, and Intelligence Agencies** to ensure proper discharge of duties and maintenance of discipline.
* **Article 34**: Empowers Parliament to indemnify state officials for acts done during the operation of **Martial Law** (military rule) in any area.
* **Article 35**: Declares that the power to make laws to give effect to certain specified Fundamental Rights vests **exclusively in Parliament, and NOT in State Legislatures** (e.g., Article 16(3) residence requirements, Article 32 writ powers for other courts, Article 33, Article 34, prescribing punishment for untouchability under Article 17 and forced labor under Article 23).

## 4.10 Publication Callout Boxes: Strategic Matrices & Warnings

### [CONSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM: THE ARTICLE 31C SHIELD]
> **Why is Article 31C called the "Great Compromise"?**  
> Under Article 31C (inserted by the 25th Amendment 1971), if Parliament passes a law to implement the Directive Principles contained in **Article 39(b)** (equitable distribution of material resources) or **Article 39(c)** (preventing concentration of wealth), that law **CANNOT be declared void on the ground that it violates Article 14 or Article 19**. In *Kesavananda (1973)*, the Supreme Court upheld this protection, famously observing: *"Where Article 31C comes in, Article 14 goes out."*

---

### [DISTINCTION MATRIX: ARTICLE 32 VS. ARTICLE 226]
| Parameter | Article 32 (Supreme Court) | Article 226 (High Court) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Status** | A guaranteed Fundamental Right. | A constitutional discretionary power. |
| **Grounds of Invocation** | **Infringement of Fundamental Rights only**. | Infringement of FRs **OR any other legal right**. |
| **Discretion to Decline** | **NO.** SC cannot dismiss a valid petition without remedy. | **YES.** HC can decline if alternative efficacious remedy exists. |
| **Territorial Scope** | Pan-India jurisdiction. | State territory or where cause of action arose. |

---

### [EXAM TRAP & WARNING: HIGH-YIELD PITFALLS FOR CANDIDATES]
> ⚠️ **Trap 1: "State Legislatures can make laws prescribing residence requirements under Article 16(3)."**  
> **FALSE**: Under **Article 35**, this power is vested **EXCLUSIVELY in Parliament**, preventing regional fragmentation.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 2: "All Fundamental Rights are suspended during a National Emergency under Article 352."**  
> **FALSE**: Following the 44th Amendment Act 1978, **Articles 20 and 21 CAN NEVER BE SUSPENDED** under any circumstance.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 3: "The writ of Mandamus can be issued against the President of India or State Governors."**  
> **FALSE**: Under Article 361 and writ jurisprudence, Mandamus **CANNOT be issued** against the President of India or State Governors for the exercise of their official duties.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 4: "Article 20(1) protects individuals from retroactive changes in civil and tax laws."**  
> **FALSE**: The protection against Ex-Post Facto laws applies **ONLY to criminal laws**. Parliament can legislate retroactive civil taxes.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 5: "Article 19 is available to foreign nationals and corporate companies."**  
> **FALSE**: Article 19 freedoms are guaranteed **ONLY to natural Indian citizens**.

## 4.11 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

1. **Which test is used by the Supreme Court to determine whether a private entity or corporation qualifies as a "State" under Article 12?**  
   *Answer*: The Instrumentality Test laid down in *Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib (1981)* (deep and pervasive state control, government financial dominance, public functions).

2. **Differentiate between the Doctrine of Eclipse and the Doctrine of Severability.**  
   *Answer*: Eclipse applies to pre-constitutional laws that become dormant when shadowed by FRs and revive if the clash is removed; Severability separates invalid clauses from valid clauses of a statute, striking down only the unconstitutional parts.

3. **What are the two grounds required for a classification to be valid under Article 14?**  
   *Answer*: (1) Intelligible Differentia (clear distinguishing characteristic), and (2) Rational Nexus (logical connection between the differentia and the legislative objective).

4. **Name the two grounds of discrimination prohibited under Article 16 that are NOT mentioned in Article 15.**  
   *Answer*: **"Descent"** and **"Residence"**.

5. **Which Supreme Court case established the 50% ceiling on reservations and banned reservations in promotions?**  
   *Answer*: *Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)* (Mandal Case).

6. **What is the constitutional significance of the *Maneka Gandhi (1978)* ruling regarding Article 21?**  
   *Answer*: It overruled *A.K. Gopalan (1950)*, replaced literal "Procedure Established by Law" with substantive "Due Process of Law" (procedure must be just, fair, and reasonable), and established the "Golden Triangle" of Articles 14, 19, and 21.

7. **Can an accused person be forced to undergo narco-analysis or lie-detector tests? Cite the relevant case.**  
   *Answer*: No. The Supreme Court in *Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010)* held that involuntary narco-analysis violates the protection against self-incrimination under Article 20(3) and the right to privacy under Article 21.

8. **Which writ can be sought by any citizen without fulfilling the strict rule of locus standi?**  
   *Answer*: **Quo-Warranto** (inquiring into the legal authority by which a person occupies a substantive public office).

9. **Can the writ of Prohibition or Certiorari be issued against private individuals or legislative bodies?**  
   *Answer*: No. Prohibition applies strictly to judicial and quasi-judicial bodies; Certiorari applies to judicial, quasi-judicial, and administrative bodies. Neither can be issued to private individuals or legislatures.

10. **Explain the meaning of the phrase "Where Article 31C comes in, Article 14 goes out."**  
    *Answer*: If Parliament passes a law to implement the socio-economic Directive Principles under Article 39(b) or 39(c), that law cannot be challenged or invalidated for violating Article 14 (Equality) or Article 19 (Freedoms).
