## 1. Constitutional Epistemic Anchor & Institutional Design

Part V of the Indian Constitution (*The Union*, Articles 52 to 151) opens in Chapter I (*The Executive*, Articles 52 to 78) with the architecture of the Union Executive. The Union Executive consists of five distinct constitutional organs:
1. **The President of India** (Head of State, First Citizen, Supreme Commander of Defence Forces)
2. **The Vice-President of India** (Second highest constitutional dignitary, Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha)
3. **The Prime Minister** (Head of Government, de facto executive)
4. **The Union Council of Ministers** (Aiding and advising the President, collectively responsible to Lok Sabha)
5. **The Attorney General for India** (Chief legal advisor to the Union Government, Article 76)

### The Constitutional Mandate: *De Jure* vs. *De Facto* Executive
- **Article 52**: *"There shall be a President of India."* (Mandatory and non-derogable; the office can never fall vacant without an immediate statutory successor).
- **Article 53(1)**: The executive power of the Union shall be vested in the President and shall be exercised by him either directly or through officers subordinate to him in accordance with the Constitution.
- **The Bagehotian Duality in India**: In the Westminster framework adopted by the Constituent Assembly, the President is the *constitutional, formal, titular head (de jure)*, whereas the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister wields the *real, political executive power (de facto)* (*Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab, 1974*).

## 2. The President of India: Election Mechanics & Mathematics

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP: State Legislative Councils (Vidhan Parishads)**
> Members of State Legislative Councils (MLCs)—whether elected or nominated—have **zero participation** in presidential elections. Even in states with bicameral legislatures (UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana), only the Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly) members vote.

### B. Mathematical Formulas: Parity & Uniformity (Article 55)
Article 55 ensures two constitutional objectives:
1. **Uniformity** in the scale of representation of different states (inter-state parity).
2. **Parity** between the States as a whole and the Union of India (Union-State parity).

#### 1. Value of the Vote of an MLA
$$\text{Value of Vote of 1 MLA} = \frac{\text{Total Population of the State (1971 Census)}}{\text{Total Number of Elected Members in State Assembly}} \times \frac{1}{1000}$$

*Calculation Rule*: If the remainder after dividing by 1000 is 500 or more, the value of the vote is increased by 1. If less than 500, the remainder is ignored.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The 1971 Census Freeze**: Under the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act 1976 (extended by the 84th Constitutional Amendment Act 2001), "population" for calculating MLA vote values remains strictly anchored to the **1971 Census** until the relevant figures for the first census taken after the year **2026** have been published. This prevents states with successful population control policies from being penalized in presidential weightage.

#### 2. Value of the Vote of an MP
$$\text{Value of Vote of 1 MP} = \frac{\text{Sum Total of Vote Values of all Elected MLAs of All States}}{\text{Total Number of Elected Members of Parliament (LS + RS)}}$$

*Note*: Following the reorganization of Jammu & Kashmir, the total value of an MP's vote was revised to **700** (from the earlier 708) in the 2022 Presidential Election because the J&K Assembly was not in place.

### C. System of Voting: PR-STV & The Electoral Quota
- Voting is conducted in accordance with the **System of Proportional Representation by means of the Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV)** through a **secret ballot**.
- To win, a candidate does not merely require a simple plurality; the candidate must secure an absolute majority of votes polled, termed the **Electoral Quota**.

$$\text{Electoral Quota} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Number of Valid Votes Polled}}{1 + 1} \right) + 1 = \left( \frac{\text{Total Valid Votes}}{2} \right) + 1$$

#### The Counting Mechanism (Alternative Vote / Transfer Algorithm):
1. **First Preference Counting**: Each elector marks preferences (1, 2, 3...) on the ballot. If a candidate secures the quota in round 1, they are declared elected.
2. **Elimination & Value Transfer**: If no candidate reaches the quota in the first round, the candidate with the lowest first-preference votes is eliminated. Their ballot papers are inspected, and their second preferences are transferred to the respective surviving candidates at full value.
3. This process continues iteratively until a candidate crosses the Electoral Quota.
4. *Historical Landmark*: **V.V. Giri (1969)** remains the only President of India elected on the basis of **second-preference vote counting**, defeating N. Sanjiva Reddy. (N. Sanjiva Reddy was later elected unopposed in 1977).

---

### D. Candidacy, Security Deposit & Election Disputes (Article 71)
- Under the *Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952* (amended in 1997):
  - Nomination requires **50 proposers and 50 seconders** from the Electoral College.
  - Security Deposit: **₹15,000** deposited in the Reserve Bank of India. Forfeited if the candidate fails to secure **one-sixth (1/6th)** of the valid votes polled.
- **Article 71 Jurisdictional Monopoly**:
  - All doubts and disputes arising out of the election of a President or Vice-President are inquired into and decided **exclusively by the Supreme Court of India**, whose decision is final.
  - The election **cannot be called into question** on the ground of the existence of any vacancy in the electoral college (e.g., if a state assembly is dissolved or seats are vacant) (*Article 71(4)*, added via 11th Amendment Act 1961).
  - If the election of a person is declared void by the Supreme Court, acts done by him in the exercise of presidential duties prior to the court's decision **remain valid and are not invalidated** (*Article 71(2)*).

> [!CAUTION]
> **EXAM TRAP: The Rarest Majority in the Indian Constitution**
> The majority required under Article 61 is **two-thirds of the TOTAL membership of the House**. It is NOT "two-thirds of members present and voting with absolute majority" (which is Article 368). It is the strictest majority known to Indian Constitutional Law. To date, **no President of India has ever been impeached**.

### D. Vacancy in the Office of President (Article 62)
1. **Expiration of 5-year tenure**: Election must be concluded *prior* to expiration. The outgoing President continues in office until his successor enters, preventing any interregnum (no acting President steps in).
2. **Casual Vacancy (Death, Resignation, Impeachment, Judicial Invalidation)**:
   - Election to fill vacancy must be held **within six months** from the date of occurrence.
   - The newly elected President holds office for a **full term of 5 years** from the date of entry (not merely the unexpired period).
   - **Order of Succession (President Act, 1969)**:
     $$\text{President} \longrightarrow \text{Vice-President} \longrightarrow \text{Chief Justice of India} \longrightarrow \text{Senior-most SC Judge}$$
   - *Historical Instance*: In May 1969, upon President Zakir Husain's death, VP V.V. Giri resigned to contest elections. CJI **Justice M. Hidayatullah** acted as President from July 20, 1969 to August 24, 1969.

## 4. Comprehensive Taxonomy of Presidential Powers

### A. Executive Powers
1. All executive actions of the Government of India are formally expressed to be taken in the name of the President (**Article 77(1)**).
2. Makes rules specifying the manner in which orders and instruments made in his name are authenticated (**Article 77(2)**), and rules for the more convenient transaction of business (**Transaction of Business Rules, Article 77(3)**).
3. **Appointing Authority**:
   - Prime Minister and other Ministers (on PM's advice, **Article 75**).
   - Attorney General for India (pleasure of the President, **Article 76**).
   - Comptroller and Auditor General of India (**Article 148**).
   - Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (**Article 324**).
   - Chairman and Members of UPSC (**Article 316**).
   - Governors of States (pleasure of the President, **Article 155**).
   - Finance Commission Chairman and Members (**Article 280**).
   - Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (**Article 350-B**), National Commissions for SCs (**Art 338**), STs (**Art 338-A**), and BCs (**Art 338-B**).
4. Direct administration of Union Territories through administrators/Lieutenant Governors appointed by him (**Article 239**).
5. Power to declare any area as a Scheduled Area and administer Scheduled and Tribal Areas (**Fifth & Sixth Schedules**).

---

### B. Legislative Powers & The Veto Architecture (Article 111)
1. **Summoning, Proroguing, and Dissolution**: Summons and prorogues both Houses; dissolves the Lok Sabha (**Article 85**). (Maximum gap between two parliamentary sessions cannot exceed 6 months).
2. **Right to Address & Send Messages**: Opening session address following general election and the first session of each year (**Article 87**).
3. **Nomination**: Nominates **12 members** to the Rajya Sabha having special knowledge or practical experience in *Literature, Science, Art, and Social Service* (**Article 80(3)**). *(Note: Nomination of 2 Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha under Art 331 was discontinued by the 104th Amendment Act, 2019)*.
4. **Disqualification of MPs**: Decides on questions of disqualification of members under **Article 103** in consultation with the **Election Commission of India**, whose opinion is binding. (Defection under 10th Schedule is decided by Presiding Officers, not the President).
5. **Prior Recommendation Required for Bills**:
   - Bill altering boundaries or names of states (**Article 3**).
   - Money Bills (**Article 110/117(1)**).
   - Bills involving expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India (**Article 117(3)**).
   - Bills affecting taxation in which States are interested (**Article 274**).
   - Bills imposing restrictions on trade and commerce (**Article 304(b)**).

#### The Veto Powers of the President (Article 111)
When an ordinary bill is presented to the President after being passed by both Houses, the President has three constitutional options under Article 111:
1. He may give his **assent** to the Bill → Bill becomes an Act.
2. He may **withhold his assent** to the Bill → Bill dies (**Absolute Veto**).
3. He may **return the Bill** (if it is not a Money Bill) for reconsideration of the Houses → (**Suspensive Veto**).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Veto Over State Legislation (Article 201)**:
> When the Governor reserves a state bill for the consideration of the President under Article 200, the President can: (1) Assent, (2) Withhold assent, or (3) Direct the Governor to return the bill for reconsideration within 6 months.
> **The Critical Difference**: If the State Legislature passes the bill again (with or without amendment) and sends it back to the President, **the President is NOT bound to give his assent**! He still retains absolute veto over state bills (*Article 201*).

### D. Judicial Powers & The Pardoning Power (Article 72)
Under Article 72, the President has the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment or to suspend, remit or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence:
1. In all cases where punishment or sentence is by a **Court Martial** (Military Court).
2. In all cases where punishment or sentence is for an offence against a law relating to a matter to which the **executive power of the Union extends**.
3. In all cases where the sentence is a **sentence of death**.

#### The 5 Modalities of Executive Clemency:
- **Pardon**: Completely absolves the offender from all sentences, punishments, and disqualifications. It restores the individual to the position as if the crime had never been committed.
- **Commutation**: Substitution of one form of punishment for a **lighter form** (e.g., death sentence commuted to rigorous life imprisonment).
- **Remission**: Reducing the **amount of sentence** without changing its character (e.g., 5 years of rigorous imprisonment reduced to 2 years of rigorous imprisonment).
- **Respite**: Awarding a **lesser sentence** in place of one originally awarded due to some special condition (e.g., pregnancy of a woman offender, severe mental or physical infirmity).
- **Reprieve**: A **temporary stay** on the execution of a sentence (especially death) for a period, enabling the prisoner to seek pardon or commutation.

#### Principles Established by Supreme Court (*Kehar Singh 1989*, *Epuru Sudhakar 2006*):
1. The power is exercised by the President on the **advice of the Union Cabinet**, not at his individual discretion.
2. The President can scrutinize evidence afresh and form an opinion different from that of the court.
3. The power is **subject to limited judicial review**: Courts can strike down presidential clemency if the order was passed without application of mind, is mala fide, founded on extraneous or political considerations, or suffers from arbitrariness.
4. There is no fundamental right to an oral hearing before the President.

### E. Advisory Jurisdiction (Article 143)
Article 143 authorizes the President to consult the Supreme Court:
1. **Clause (1)**: On a question of law or fact of public importance which has arisen or is likely to arise. (Supreme Court **may or may not** tender its opinion; e.g., refused in *Ayodhya Reference, 1993*).
2. **Clause (2)**: On any dispute arising out of any pre-constitution treaty, agreement, covenant, or engagement. (Supreme Court **must** tender its opinion).
- *Constitutional Status*: The opinion delivered by the Supreme Court under Article 143 is purely **advisory and consultative**; it does not bind the President and does not operate as a binding judicial precedent under Article 141.

---

## 5. Constitutional Position & Discretionary Powers

### A. The Evolution of Article 74(1)
- **Original Constitution (1950)**: *"There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President who shall, in the exercise of his functions, act in accordance with such advice."* (Ambiguous whether advice was legally binding).
- **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976 - Emergency)**: Made presidential obedience **strictly mandatory**: *"The President SHALL act in accordance with such advice."*
- **44th Constitutional Amendment Act (1978 - Post-Emergency)**: Injected a constitutional safety brake: Added a proviso enabling the President to **require the Council of Ministers to reconsider such advice, either generally or otherwise**. However, the President **SHALL act in accordance with the advice tendered after such reconsideration**.


### B. Situational Discretion (Reserve Powers of the President)
While the Constitution does not explicitly confer discretionary powers on the President (unlike the Governor under Article 163), the President exercises **situational discretion** under parliamentary conventions:
1. **Appointment of the Prime Minister**: When no single party or pre-poll alliance secures a clear majority in the Lok Sabha, or when the incumbent PM dies in office and there is no recognized successor. (Convention established: invite leader of the largest single party/coalition and ask them to secure a vote of confidence within a reasonable timeframe, e.g., Neelam Sanjiva Reddy inviting Charan Singh in 1979).
2. **Dismissal of the Council of Ministers**: When the ministry loses the confidence of the Lok Sabha (e.g., defeated in a No-Confidence Motion) but refuses to resign.
3. **Dissolution of the Lok Sabha**: When the Council of Ministers has lost its majority and no alternative government can be formed.

## 6. The Vice-President of India: Comprehensive Mechanics (Articles 63–71)

### A. Constitutional Office & Functions (Articles 63 & 64)
- **Article 63**: *"There shall be a Vice-President of India."*
- The office is modeled on the lines of the **American Vice-President**.
- Dual Constitutional Capacity:
  1. **Ex-Officio Chairman of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** (**Article 64**): Presides over the Rajya Sabha, regulates its proceedings, decides points of order, and exercises a **casting vote** in case of a tie (**Article 100**).
  2. **Acting President of India** (**Article 65**): Discharges presidential functions in case of vacancy (death, resignation, removal) for a maximum period of **six months**, until a new President takes charge.
  - *Salary Rule*: The Vice-President receives the salary fixed for the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. When acting as President, he draws the salary and allowances of the President of India and **does not perform the duties or draw the salary of the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**.

---

### C. Qualifications & Removal Procedure (Article 67)
- **Qualifications (Article 66(3))**:
  1. Citizen of India.
  2. Completed **35 years of age**.
  3. Qualified for election as a **member of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**. (President must be qualified for Lok Sabha).
  4. Must not hold any Office of Profit.
- **Oath (Article 69)**: Administered by the **President**, or some person appointed on his behalf.

#### The Removal Mechanism (Article 67(b)):
Unlike the President, the Constitution **does not prescribe any formal grounds** (such as "violation of the constitution") for the removal of the Vice-President.

## 8. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. Explain why the 84th Constitutional Amendment Act 2001 froze the population figures for 
    calculating the value of MLA votes at the 1971 Census until after 2026.
A1. To avoid penalizing states (particularly in South India) that successfully implemented family 
    planning and population control programs. If current population were used, states with higher 
    population growth rates would secure an increased proportion of votes, undermining equity in 
    federal representation.

Q2. What is the fundamental difference between the majority required to impeach the President 
    under Article 61 and the majority required to amend the Constitution under Article 368?
A2. Article 61 requires a majority of NOT LESS THAN TWO-THIRDS OF THE TOTAL MEMBERSHIP of each 
    House (the highest majority under the Constitution). Article 368 requires a majority of the 
    total membership of the House PLUS a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members 
    present and voting.

Q3. Can an ordinance promulgated under Article 123 amend a fundamental right or amend the 
    Constitution?
A3. An ordinance cannot amend the Constitution (Article 368). It operates under the exact same 
    constitutional limitations as ordinary legislation passed by Parliament; therefore, under 
    Article 13(2), an ordinance that takes away or abridges Fundamental Rights is void ab initio.

Q4. Under what circumstances can the President exercise absolute veto over a bill passed by 
    Parliament?
A4. In two conventional circumstances: (1) With respect to Private Members' Bills, and (2) When 
    the Cabinet that piloted a government bill resigns before the President signs it, and the 
    incoming new Cabinet advises the President to withhold assent (e.g., PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954).

Q5. If the Vice-President is removed from office under Article 67(b), what specific majority is 
    mandated in the Rajya Sabha and the Lok Sabha?
A5. In the Rajya Sabha: An Effective Majority ("a majority of all the then members of the Council", 
    which means Total Strength minus Vacancies divided by 2, plus 1). In the Lok Sabha: A Simple 
    Majority (a majority of members present and voting).
```
