## 1. Constitutional Epistemic Anchor: The State Executive

Part VI of the Constitution (*The States*, Articles 152 to 237) governs state-level institutions. Chapter II of Part VI (Articles 153 to 167) structures the **State Executive**, which comprises:
1. **The Governor** (Constitutional Head of State & Agent of the Union)
2. **The Chief Minister** (Head of Government, *de facto* executive)
3. **The State Council of Ministers** (Collectively responsible to the State Legislative Assembly)
4. **The Advocate General for the State** (Article 165)

## 2. The Governor: Appointment, Tenure & Dual Capacity

### A. The Office & Mode of Appointment (Articles 153–155)
- **Article 153**: *"There shall be a Governor for each State."*
  - **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**: Added a crucial proviso facilitating administrative flexibility: *The same person can be appointed as Governor for two or more States* (or a State and an adjacent Union Territory).
- **Mode of Appointment (Article 155)**:
  - The Governor is appointed by the **President of India by warrant under his hand and seal**.
  - **The Canadian Model**: The Constituent Assembly rejected the American model of directly elected state governors. Instead, it adopted the Canadian system where the Governor-General appoints provincial Lieutenant-Governors.
  - *Constituent Assembly Rationale (Dr. B.R. Ambedkar & Pt. Nehru)*:
    1. Direct election would create a catastrophic rivalry between the Chief Minister (backed by popular mandate) and an elected Governor.
    2. An elected Governor would inevitably identify with a political party, destroying the non-partisan detachment required of a constitutional umpire.
    3. The nominated Governor acts as a vital institutional linchpin binding the federal Union together.
  - *Supreme Court Ruling (*Hargovind Pant v. Dr. Raghukul Tilak, 1979*)*: The office of the Governor is **not an employment under the Government of India**. It is an independent constitutional office that is neither subordinate to nor under the control of the Central Government.

### C. Oath of Office (Article 159)
- Administered by the **Chief Justice of the High Court** exercising jurisdiction in relation to the State (in his absence, the senior-most judge of that High Court).
- Phrasing matches the President: to faithfully execute the office and *"preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law"*.

---

### D. Tenure & The "Doctrine of Pleasure" (Article 156)
- **Article 156(1)**: *"The Governor shall hold office during the pleasure of the President."*
- Subject to the pleasure of the President, the Governor holds office for a term of **5 years** from the date on which he enters upon his office.
- Resignation is addressed in writing to the **President of India**.
- **The Constitutional Vacuum**: The Constitution prescribes **no security of tenure, no fixed minimum term, and zero grounds** (like corruption or incapacity) for the removal of a Governor by the President.

#### Landmark Jurisprudence: *B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)*
A 5-judge Constitution Bench laid down definitive constitutional limits on the removal of Governors:
1. The President can remove a Governor at any time under Article 156(1) without assigning reasons and without giving an opportunity to show cause.
2. However, this power **cannot be exercised arbitrarily, capriciously, or for unreasonable grounds**.
3. A change in the central government (a new political party coming to power in New Delhi) is **not a valid ground** for en masse removal of Governors appointed by the previous regime.
4. While the court will presume that presidential pleasure was exercised on valid grounds, if an aggrieved Governor demonstrates that removal was *mala fide* or driven by political vendetta, the court can grant judicial review.

## 3. Comprehensive Taxonomy of Governor's Powers

### A. Executive Powers
1. All executive actions of the State Government are formally taken in his name (**Article 166(1)**).
2. Appoints the **Chief Minister** and other ministers (on the CM's advice, **Article 164(1)**).
3. **Mandatory Tribal Welfare Minister (Article 164(1) Proviso)**:
   - In the states of **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, and Odisha**, there shall be a Minister in charge of tribal welfare (who may in addition be in charge of SC/OBC welfare).
   - *(Note: Bihar was excluded from this requirement by the 94th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2006 following the creation of Jharkhand)*.
4. Appoints the **Advocate General for the State** and determines his remuneration; the Advocate General holds office during the **pleasure of the Governor** (**Article 165**).
5. Appoints the **State Election Commissioner** (**Article 243K**). (Crucial: Although appointed by the Governor, the SEC can only be removed in the like manner and on the like grounds as a **Judge of a High Court**).
6. Appoints the Chairman and Members of the **State Public Service Commission (SPSC)** (**Article 316**).
   - > [!WARNING]
     > **EXAM TRAP: SPSC Removal Asymmetry**
     > The Governor appoints the Chairman and members of the SPSC, but **the Governor CANNOT remove them**! Under **Article 317**, they can be removed **only by the President of India** on the grounds of misbehaviour after an inquiry by the Supreme Court.
7. Acts as the **ex-officio Chancellor** of universities in the state and appoints Vice-Chancellors.

---

### B. Legislative Powers & The Veto Architecture (Articles 200 & 201)
1. Summons, prorogues the State Legislature and dissolves the Legislative Assembly (**Article 174**).
2. Addresses the State Legislature at the commencement of the first session after each general election and the first session of each year (**Article 176**).
3. **Nominations to Legislative Council (Article 171(3)(e) & 171(5))**:
   - In states with a bicameral legislature, the Governor nominates **one-sixth (1/6th)** of the members of the State Legislative Council having special knowledge or practical experience in:
   $$\text{Fields of Nomination: } \mathbf{L}\text{iterature}, \mathbf{S}\text{cience}, \mathbf{A}\text{rt}, \mathbf{C}\text{o-operative Movement}, \text{and } \mathbf{S}\text{ocial Service (LSA-CS)}$$
   - > [!IMPORTANT]
     > **The Co-operative Movement Divergence**:
     > The field of **"Co-operative Movement"** exists exclusively for the Governor's nominations to the State Legislative Council under Article 171(5). It does **NOT** exist for the President's nominations to the Rajya Sabha under Article 80(3)!
4. Nominations of Anglo-Indians to State Assembly under Article 333 was **discontinued** by the **104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019**.
5. Decides questions of disqualification of members of the State Legislature under **Article 192** in consultation with the **Election Commission**, whose opinion is binding.

#### The Veto Options of the Governor (Article 200):
When a bill passed by the State Legislature is presented to the Governor, the Governor has **four constitutional options**:
1. He may give **assent** to the Bill → Bill becomes an Act.
2. He may **withhold assent** to the Bill → Bill falls through.
3. He may **return the Bill** (if not a Money Bill) with a message for reconsideration. If the Bill is passed again by the House(s) with or without amendment, **the Governor MUST give his assent** (cannot withhold or return again).
4. He may **reserve the Bill for the consideration of the President**.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Mandatory Reservation of State Bills**:
> The Governor **is constitutionally bound** to reserve a bill for the consideration of the President if, in the opinion of the Governor, the bill *so derogates from the powers of the High Court as to endanger the position which that Court is designed by the Constitution to fill* (**Article 200 Second Proviso**).

#### Landmark Rulings on Article 200 Delays:
- ***State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to Governor (2023)*** & ***State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor (2024)***:
  1. The phrase *"as soon as possible"* in Article 200 contains significant constitutional content. A Governor cannot sit indefinitely on bills passed by the elected legislature (pocket veto is not permissible for state governors).
  2. If the Governor decides to withhold assent under the first part of Article 200, he **must immediately return the bill to the legislature** under the proviso for reconsideration. The Governor cannot withhold assent and keep the bill locked in Raj Bhavan without returning it.
  3. Once a bill is repassed by the legislature and resubmitted, the Governor **has no discretion left** and is constitutionally mandated to grant assent.

---

### C. The Ordinance-Making Power of the Governor (Article 213)
Article 213 vests in the Governor the power to promulgate Ordinances during the recess of the State Legislature.
- **Conditions**:
  1. Promulgated only when the State Legislative Assembly (or either House in a bicameral legislature) is **not in session**.
  2. The Governor must be satisfied that circumstances exist which render it necessary for him to take **immediate action**.
  3. Co-extensive with the legislative power of the State Legislature (State List & Concurrent List). Subject to Fundamental Rights.
- **Lifespan**:
  - Must be laid before the State Legislature upon reassembly.
  - Ceases to operate at the expiration of **six weeks (42 days)** from the reassembly of the legislature.
  - Maximum lifespan without approval = 6 Months + 6 Weeks = **~7.5 Months**.
- **President's Prior Instructions Required**:
  The Governor **cannot** make an ordinance without instructions from the President if:
  1. A Bill containing the same provisions would under the Constitution have required the previous sanction of the President for introduction.
  2. He would have deemed it necessary to reserve a Bill containing the same provisions for the consideration of the President.
  3. An Act of the State Legislature containing the same provisions would have been invalid unless reserved for and assented to by the President (e.g., overriding a central law under Article 254(2)).

---

### D. Judicial Powers & The Pardoning Power (Article 161)
- Under **Article 161**, the Governor can grant pardons, reprieves, respites, and remissions of punishment or suspend, remit, or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence against any law relating to a matter to which the **executive power of the State extends**.
- *Comparison with Presidential Pardoning Power (Article 72)*:
  1. **Court Martial Sentences**: Governor has **zero power**.
  2. **Death Sentences**: Governor **cannot pardon** a death sentence. (However, the Governor **can** suspend, remit, or commute a death sentence under state laws).
  3. *Judicial Review*: Subject to limited judicial review for arbitrariness or mala fides (*Epuru Sudhakar, 2006*).
- **Consultation in Judicial Appointments**:
  - Consulted by the President when appointing the Chief Justice and Judges of the State High Court (**Article 217(1)**).
  - Directly appoints, posts, and promotes **District Judges** in consultation with the State High Court (**Article 233**).

### B. Constitutional Discretion (Explicit Provisions in the Constitution)
The Governor exercises constitutional discretion independently of the Council of Ministers in:
1. **Reservation of a Bill for Presidential Consideration (Article 200)**.
2. **Recommendation for the Imposition of President's Rule (Article 356)**: Reporting breakdown of constitutional machinery in the state under his hand.
3. **Exercising Functions as Administrator of an Adjoining UT (Article 239(2))**: Operates independently of his state Council of Ministers.
4. **Determining Royalty under the Sixth Schedule (Para 9(2))**: Determining amount payable by the State Government of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, or Mizoram to the Autonomous District Council as license royalties for mineral extraction.
5. **Special Responsibilities under Article 371**:
   - Article 371 (Maharashtra & Gujarat): Establishment of separate development boards for Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra, Kutch.
   - Article 371A (Nagaland): Discretion with respect to law and order so long as internal disturbances continue.
   - Article 371C (Manipur): Administration of the Hill Areas.
   - Article 371F (Sikkim): Responsibility for peace and equitable social and economic advancement.
   - Article 371H (Arunachal Pradesh): Special responsibility for law and order.
   - Article 371J (Karnataka): Establishment of separate development board for Hyderabad-Karnataka region.

---

### C. Situational Discretion (Reserve Powers under Parliamentary Conventions)
1. **Appointment of Chief Minister**: When no single party or pre-poll alliance secures a clear majority in the Assembly, or when the incumbent CM dies in office without a designated successor.
2. **Dismissal of the Council of Ministers**: When the ministry loses the confidence of the Legislative Assembly (e.g., defeated on floor test) and refuses to resign.
3. **Dissolution of the Legislative Assembly**: When the ministry has lost its majority and no alternative coalition can form a stable government.

## 7. The Chief Minister & State Council of Ministers

### A. The Chief Minister (Articles 163, 164 & 167)
- **Article 164(1)**: The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor. Other ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- **Article 167**: Duties of the Chief Minister in relation to the Governor:
  1. Communicate all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to state administration and legislative proposals.
  2. Furnish such information as the Governor may call for.
  3. Submit for consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a minister but not considered by the Council.

---

### B. The 91st Amendment Act 2003: State Council of Ministers Constraints
The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 amended **Article 164** to introduce two vital numerical limits:
1. **Maximum Ceiling (Article 164(1A))**:
   $$\text{Total Ministers (including CM)} \le 15\% \times \text{Total Strength of the State Legislative Assembly}$$
2. **Minimum Floor (Article 164(1A))**:
   $$\text{Total Ministers (including CM)} \ge 12 \text{ Ministers}$$
   *(Note: The minimum floor of 12 ministers applies to States to protect small states like Sikkim and Goa; no such minimum floor exists for the Union Council of Ministers!).*
3. **Anti-Defection Disqualification (Article 164(1B))**:
   An MLA/MLC disqualified under the Tenth Schedule is disqualified from holding ministerial office for the remainder of their term or until re-elected.

```
Q1. Which field of nomination exists for the Governor's nominations to the State Legislative Council 
    under Article 171(5) that does NOT exist for the President's nominations to the Rajya Sabha under 
    Article 80(3)?
A1. "Co-operative Movement". Article 171(5) lists Literature, Science, Art, Co-operative Movement, 
    and Social Service. Article 80(3) lists only Literature, Science, Art, and Social Service.

Q2. What is the fundamental difference regarding numerical ceilings between the Union Council of 
    Ministers (Article 75) and the State Council of Ministers (Article 164) under the 91st Amendment Act?
A2. Both have a 15% maximum ceiling. However, Article 164(1A) mandates a MINIMUM FLOOR of 12 ministers 
    (including CM) for States, whereas Article 75 prescribes NO minimum numerical floor for the Union.

Q3. What did the Supreme Court rule in B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010) regarding the removal 
    of Governors?
A3. The 5-judge Constitution Bench held that while the President need not assign reasons under the 
    Doctrine of Pleasure (Article 156(1)), the removal cannot be arbitrary, capricious, or unreasonable. 
    A change in the ruling party at the Centre is not a valid ground for removing Governors.

Q4. In which four states is the Governor constitutionally required to ensure the appointment of a 
    Minister in charge of Tribal Welfare under the proviso to Article 164(1)?
A4. Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, and Odisha. (Bihar was removed from this list by the 
    94th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2006).

Q5. What is the core ratio established in the Supreme Court's 2023 and 2024 judgments (State of Punjab 
    and State of Tamil Nadu) regarding the Governor's handling of bills under Article 200?
A5. The Governor cannot sit indefinitely on bills ("pocket veto"). If the Governor decides to withhold 
    assent, he must return the bill to the legislature under the proviso for reconsideration. If the 
    legislature repasses the bill, the Governor is constitutionally bound to grant assent.
```
