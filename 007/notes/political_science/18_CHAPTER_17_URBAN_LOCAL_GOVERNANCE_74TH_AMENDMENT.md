## 2. The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992: Architecture

### A. Constitutional Additions
- Inserted **Part IX-A** entitled *"The Municipalities"* containing **Articles 243-P to 243-ZG**.
- Inserted the **Twelfth Schedule** containing **18 functional subjects** devolved to Municipalities under **Article 243-W**:
  1. Urban planning including town planning.
  2. Regulation of land-use and construction of buildings.
  3. Planning for economic and social development.
  4. Roads and bridges.
  5. Water supply for domestic, industrial, and commercial purposes.
  6. Public health, sanitation, conservancy, and solid waste management.
  7. Fire services.
  8. Urban forestry, protection of the environment, and ecological aspects.
  9. Safeguarding the interests of weaker sections (handicapped and mentally disabled).
  10. Slum improvement and upgradation.
  11. Urban poverty alleviation.
  12. Provision of urban amenities and facilities (parks, gardens, playgrounds).
  13. Promotion of cultural, educational, and aesthetic aspects.
  14. Burials, burial grounds, cremations, and electric crematoriums.
  15. Cattle pounds; prevention of cruelty to animals.
  16. Vital statistics including registration of births and deaths.
  17. Public amenities including street lighting, parking lots, bus stops, and public conveniences.
  18. Regulation of slaughterhouses and tanneries.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Industrial Township Exception (Article 243-Q(1) Proviso)**:
> A Municipality may **not be constituted** in an urban area or part thereof if the Governor specifies by public notification that it is an **Industrial Township**, where municipal services are provided by an industrial establishment (e.g., Jamshedpur / TISCO township, BHEL townships).

### C. Composition & Wards Committees (Articles 243-R & 243-S)
- **Direct Election**: All seats in a Municipality shall be filled by persons chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies called **Wards** (**Article 243-R(1)**).
- **Nominated Members**: The State Legislature may provide for the representation of:
  1. Persons having special knowledge or experience in municipal administration (these persons **do NOT have the right to vote in the meetings of the Municipality**).
  2. Members of Lok Sabha and MLAs representing constituencies comprised within the municipal area.
  3. Members of Rajya Sabha and MLCs registered as electors within the municipal area.
  4. Chairpersons of committees.
- **Wards Committees (Article 243-S)**:
  - There shall be constituted **Wards Committees**, consisting of one or more wards, within the territorial area of a Municipality having a **population of THREE LAKHS (300,000) OR MORE**.

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP: The Cantonment Board Exception**
> While all other urban local bodies are established under **State legislation** and fall under Entry 5 of the State List (Seventh Schedule), a **Cantonment Board is created under a CENTRAL ACT** (*Cantonments Act, 2006*) and functions under the administrative control of the **Union Ministry of Defence** (Union List, Entry 3).

## 5. Contemporary Urban Governance: The 3F Deficit & Municipal Reforms

### A. The Structural Crises in Indian Urban Governance
1. **The "3F Deficit" (Funds, Functions, Functionaries)**:
   - *Funds*: Municipal revenues constitute less than 1% of India's GDP (compared to >5% in Brazil and >7% in South Africa). Heavy dependence on state grants-in-aid.
   - *Functions*: States have devolved few of the 18 Twelfth Schedule subjects; key civic domains remain captured by state-controlled parastatals (e.g., Water Supply Boards, Development Authorities).
   - *Functionaries*: Critical shortages of urban planners, environmental engineers, and digital municipal staff.
2. **The "Weak Mayor" Paradigm**:
   - In most Indian municipal corporations, the **Mayor** is a ceremonial figure elected for short terms (1 to 2.5 years) with zero executive authority.
   - Real executive power is monopolized by the **Municipal Commissioner**, an appointed state bureaucrat (IAS) accountable upwards to the State Government, not downwards to the municipal council.

---

### B. Modern Municipal Financing: Municipal Bonds
- Under **SEBI (Issue and Listing of Municipal Debt Securities) Regulations, 2015**:
  - Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) can issue **Municipal Bonds (Muni Bonds)** to fund infrastructure projects.
  - *Eligibility*: ULB must have positive net worth, no default track record, and a minimum investment-grade credit rating.
  - *Pioneering Precedents*:
    - **Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation (1998)**: First in South Asia to issue municipal bonds without state guarantee.
    - **Pune Municipal Corporation (2017)**: Issued bonds for 24x7 water supply project.
    - **Lucknow & Ghaziabad (2020–2021)**: First to list green municipal bonds on the BSE.

## 6. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. Where and when was the first Municipal Corporation established in India?
A1. In Madras in 1688, under a Charter issued by King James II / the British East India Company.

Q2. Who is known as the "Father of Local Self-Government in India" and why?
A2. Lord Ripon. His landmark Resolution of 1882 laid the structural foundation of elective local 
    self-governing bodies in India and is hailed as the "Magna Carta of Local Self-Government".

Q3. What is the population threshold above which the constitution of Wards Committees becomes 
    mandatory under Article 243-S?
A3. A population of THREE LAKHS (300,000) OR MORE within the territorial area of a Municipality.

Q4. What is the fundamental difference in elected composition between a District Planning Committee 
    (Article 243-ZD) and a Metropolitan Planning Committee (Article 243-ZE)?
A4. In a District Planning Committee, FOUR-FIFTHS (4/5th / 80%) of the members must be elected from 
    amongst the elected members of the Zilla Parishad and Municipalities. In a Metropolitan Planning 
    Committee, TWO-THIRDS (2/3rd / 66.7%) of the members must be elected by and from amongst elected 
    municipal councillors and panchayat chairpersons.

Q5. Under which Union Ministry do Cantonment Boards operate, and what makes them unique among urban 
    local bodies in India?
A5. Under the Union Ministry of Defence (governed by the Cantonments Act, 2006). They are unique 
    because they are created by a Central Act under the Union List, whereas all other urban bodies 
    are governed by State legislation under the State List.
```
