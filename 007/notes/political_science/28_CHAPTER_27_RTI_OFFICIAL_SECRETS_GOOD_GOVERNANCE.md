## 1. Constitutional Genesis: The Evolution of the "Right to Know"

In a democratic republic governed by the rule of law, secrecy in public affairs is the exception, and openness is the foundational rule.
- **Constitutional Pedigree**: The Right to Information is not explicitly enumerated in Part III of the Constitution, but was judicialized as an **integral facet of Article 19(1)(a)** (Freedom of Speech and Expression) and **Article 21** (Right to Life and Liberty).
- ***State of U.P. v. Raj Narain (1975)* (Justice K.K. Mathew)**:
  > *"In a government of responsibility like ours, where all the agents of the public must be responsible for their conduct, there can be but few secrets. The people of this country have a right to know every public act, everything that is done in a public way, by their public functionaries."*
- **Grassroots Revolution**: The movement for a statutory right was catalyzed in the 1990s by the **Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS)** led by **Aruna Roy** in rural Rajasthan, demanding access to muster rolls and bills under the battle cry: *"Hamara Paisa, Hamara Hisab"* (Our Money, Our Accounts).

### A. Definition of "Public Authority" (Section 2(h))
A Public Authority means any authority, body, or institution of self-government established or constituted:
1. By or under the **Constitution**.
2. By any other law made by **Parliament**.
3. By any other law made by **State Legislature**.
4. By notification issued or order made by the appropriate Government, including:
   - Any body **owned, controlled, or substantially financed** by government funds.
   - Non-Government Organizations (NGOs) **substantially financed, directly or indirectly**, by funds provided by the appropriate Government.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Public Interest Override (Section 8(2))**:
> Notwithstanding anything in the Official Secrets Act, 1923, nor any of the exemptions permissible under Section 8(1), a public authority **may allow access to information if public interest in disclosure outweighs the harm to the protected interests**.
> **The 20-Year Rule (Section 8(3))**: Any information relating to an event or matter which occurred **twenty (20) years** before the date of the request must be disclosed (excluding clauses (a), (c), and (i)).

### D. Exempted Organizations (Section 24)
- Intelligence and security organizations specified in the **Second Schedule** (IB, RAW, Directorate of Revenue Intelligence, Central Economic Intelligence Bureau, Enforcement Directorate, BSF, CRPF, ITBP, CISF, NSG, Assam Rifles, Special Frontier Force) are **exempted from the Act**.
- > [!WARNING]
  > **EXAM TRAP: The Corruption & Human Rights Exception to Section 24**
  > Information pertaining to **ALLEGATIONS OF CORRUPTION AND HUMAN RIGHTS VIOLATIONS is NOT EXEMPT** from disclosure even in these security agencies!
  > Allegations of human rights violations can be disclosed only with the approval of the Central Information Commission within **45 days**.

## 3. Official Secrets Act, 1923 (OSA) vs. RTI Act, 2005

The **Official Secrets Act, 1923** was enacted by Lord Curzon's administration to consolidate British colonial laws relating to state espionage.
- **Section 3**: Penalizes spying, approaching prohibited military areas, or sketching sensitive fortifications.
- **Section 5 (The Catch-All Draconian Clause)**: Makes the unauthorized communication, retention, or receipt of *any official secret, secret document, or code* a punishable criminal offence. The term *"Official Secret"* is **nowhere defined** in the Act, leaving total discretion to bureaucrats to stamp any routine file "Secret" or "Confidential".

### The Supremacy Clause: Section 22 of the RTI Act, 2005
Section 22 provides an absolute non-obstante clause establishing the supremacy of citizen transparency over colonial secrecy:
$$\text{SECTION 22: The provisions of the RTI Act } \mathbf{SHALL\text{ HAVE EFFECT NOTWITHSTANDING ANYTHING INCONSISTENT}}$$
$$\text{therewith contained in the Official Secrets Act, 1923, or any other law for the time being in force.}$$

## 6. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. Under what constitutional article is the Right to Information primarily grounded as an implied 
    fundamental right?
A1. Under Article 19(1)(a) (Freedom of Speech and Expression), as affirmed in State of U.P. v. Raj 
    Narain (1975) and S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (1981).

Q2. What is the mandatory statutory timeframe under Section 7(1) of the RTI Act for providing information 
    that concerns the "Life or Liberty" of a person?
A2. Exactly FORTY-EIGHT (48) HOURS from the time of receipt of the application.

Q3. Does the Official Secrets Act, 1923 override the Right to Information Act, 2005 in case of conflict?
A3. No. Under Section 22 of the RTI Act, 2005, the provisions of the RTI Act have an explicit OVERRIDING 
    EFFECT notwithstanding anything inconsistent contained in the Official Secrets Act, 1923, or any 
    other law.

Q4. Are intelligence agencies like the Intelligence Bureau (IB) and RAW completely immune from all 
    RTI queries under Section 24?
A4. No. Under the provisos to Section 24(1), information pertaining to ALLEGATIONS OF CORRUPTION AND 
    HUMAN RIGHTS VIOLATIONS is NOT exempt from disclosure.

Q5. What is the maximum statutory penalty that the Central Information Commission can impose on a 
    Public Information Officer (PIO) under Section 20 of the RTI Act?
A5. A fine of ₹250 per day up to a MAXIMUM OF ₹25,000, in addition to recommending disciplinary action 
    under applicable service rules.
```
