## 35.1 The Constitutional Philosophy of Social Justice

## 35.2 Protection Against Caste Discrimination & Atrocities

### 1. Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 (PCR Act)
* **Constitutional Anchor**: Enacted directly under **Article 35(a)(ii)** to give effect to **Article 17** (Abolition of Untouchability).
* **Evolution**: Originally enacted as the *Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955*; comprehensively overhauled and renamed in **1976** as the *Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955*.
* **Definition of Civil Right**: Any right accruing to a person by reason of the abolition of "untouchability" by Article 17 of the Constitution.
* **Core Offenses Criminalized**:
  1. Preventing any person on the ground of untouchability from entering places of public worship or drawing water from public wells/tanks.
  2. Denying access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, or places of public entertainment.
  3. Refusing admission to educational institutions, hospitals, or dispensaries established for the public.
  4. Refusing to sell goods or render professional services to any person on equal terms.
  5. Molesting, injuring, boycotting, or insulting a member of a Scheduled Caste.
* **Statutory Rigor**:
  * Offenses are **cognizable and non-compoundable**.
  * Penalties: Imprisonment from 1 month to 6 months and fine.
  * Presumption of guilt: Under Section 12, where any act constituting an offense is committed in relation to an SC member, the Court **shall presume** that the act was committed on the ground of "untouchability".
  * **Electoral Disqualification**: Conviction under the PCR Act results in **automatic disqualification** from contesting Parliament or State Legislative elections under Section 8(1) of the *Representation of the People Act, 1951*.

### 2. Scheduled Castes & Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 (PoA Act)
Enacted in September 1989 (in force from 30 January 1990) to deliver specialized criminal justice against brutal hate crimes, social boycotts, and humiliation perpetrated against Dalits and Adivasis by non-SC/ST individuals.

```
                          EVOLUTION OF SECTION 18 & 18A (PoA ACT)
                                             │
      ┌──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┐
      ▼                                      ▼                                      ▼
SECTION 18 (ORIGINAL 1989)            SUBHASH K. MAHAJAN (2018)              SECTION 18A (2018 AMENDMENT)
* Complete Bar on Anticipatory        * SC introduced preliminary inquiry    * Parliament restored original bar
  Bail (Sec 438 CrPC does not apply)  * Arrest required prior SSP sanction   * No preliminary inquiry needed
* Aimed to prevent intimidation       * Permitted anticipatory bail if no      * No arrest approval needed
  of vulnerable victims                 prima facie atrocity made out        * Upheld in Prathvi Raj Chauhan (2020)
```

#### A. Distinctive Statutory Features
1. **Perpetrator Requirement**: The offense can be committed **only by a person who is NOT a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe** against a member of an SC or ST.
2. **Catalog of Criminalized Atrocities (Section 3)**:
   * Forcing an SC/ST to drink or eat obnoxious or inedible substances.
   * Dumping excreta, sewage, or carcasses in their premises or neighborhood.
   * Forcibly stripping, parading naked, or tonsuring head or face.
   * Wrongfully dispossessing an SC/ST from their land or interfering with water sources.
   * Compelling an SC/ST to do bonded labor or dedicated manual scavenging.
   * Assaulting or using force against an SC/ST woman with intent to outrage her modesty.
   * Instituting false, malicious, or vexatious criminal proceedings against an SC/ST.
3. **Special Courts & Special Prosecutors (Sections 14 & 15)**: State Governments must designate a Court of Session in each district as a **Special Court**, and appoint an **Exclusive Special Public Prosecutor** (minimum 7 years standing) for expeditious trial within 2 months of chargesheet.
4. **Section 18 — Bar on Anticipatory Bail**: Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) **shall not apply** to any case involving an arrest under the Act.

#### B. The 2015 & 2018 Amendments & Landmark Supreme Court Rulings
* **The 2015 Amendment Act**:
  * Added new categories of atrocities: tonsuring, garlanding with footwear, compelling manual scavenging, dedicating SC/ST women as devadasis.
  * Added **Chapter IVA (Rights of Victims and Witnesses)**: Protection from intimidation, state-funded travel and maintenance, right to copies of police documents, and mandatory interim relief within 7 days.
* **The Judicial Flashpoint & Legislative Reversal (*Section 18A*)**:
  * In *Subhash Kashinath Mahajan v. State of Maharashtra (March 2018)*, a 2-judge bench of the Supreme Court held that the bar under Section 18 was not absolute, permitting anticipatory bail where no prima facie case existed, and mandating a preliminary inquiry before FIR registration and written sanction before arresting a public servant.
  * Following widespread civil unrest, Parliament enacted the **Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Amendment Act, 2018**, inserting **Section 18A**:
    1. Preliminary inquiry is **NOT required** for registration of an FIR.
    2. Investigating officer **does NOT require any prior approval** for arrest.
    3. The bar on Section 438 CrPC applies **notwithstanding any judgment, decree, or order of any court**.
  * In *Prathvi Raj Chauhan v. Union of India (February 2020)*, a 3-judge bench led by Justice Arun Mishra **upheld the constitutional validity of Section 18A**, clarifying that pre-arrest bail can be granted only in rare cases where the complaint discloses no prima facie offense under the Act.

## 35.3 Tribal Self-Determination & Forest Rights Architecture

### Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (FRA)
The FRA was enacted by Parliament to rectify the **historical injustice** suffered by forest-dwelling communities whose customary tenurial rights were unrecognized during colonial and post-independence forest consolidations.

```
                           THE THREE-TIER FRA ADJUDICATION LADDER
                                             │
                                             ▼
                                  GRAM SABHA (STATUTORY APEX)
                      * Initiates & Receives Individual & Community Claims
                      * Passes Formal Resolution on Rights
                      * Forest Rights Committee (FRC) Verifies On-Site
                                             │
                                             ▼
                               SUB-DIVISIONAL LEVEL COMMITTEE (SDLC)
                      * Examines Gram Sabha Resolutions
                      * Prepares Draft Record of Proposed Rights
                                             │
                                             ▼
                                 DISTRICT LEVEL COMMITTEE (DLC)
                      * Chaired by District Collector
                      * Final Authority to Approve & Issue Title Deeds (Patta)
```

1. **Eligibility Thresholds**:
   * **Forest Dwelling Scheduled Tribes (FDST)**: Must be an ST residing in the forest and dependent on forestland for livelihood before **13 December 2005**.
   * **Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFD)**: Must have primarily resided in the forest for at least **three generations (75 years)** prior to 13 December 2005.
2. **Four Typologies of Recognized Rights**:
   * **Individual Title Rights (Section 3(1)(a))**: Right to hold and live in forestland for habitation or self-cultivation up to a maximum ceiling of **4 hectares** (only for land actually under cultivation prior to 13 Dec 2005; no new land allocation).
   * **Community Rights (Section 3(1)(b, c))**: Right of ownership, access to collect, use, and dispose of **Minor Forest Produce (MFP)** (tendu patta, bamboo, mahua, lac, honey); grazing rights; water bodies.
   * **Development Rights (Section 3(2))**: Diversion of forestland for 13 specific public utilities (schools, dispensaries, fair price shops, roads, drinking water pipelines) involving felling of $< 75$ trees per hectare and $< 1$ hectare land.
   * **Right to Protect & Manage (Section 3(1)(i))**: Right to protect, regenerate, or conserve community forest resources (CFR) against external commercial destruction.
3. **Inviolable Role of the Gram Sabha**:
   * The **Gram Sabha is the sole statutory initiating authority** under Section 6.
   * In *Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF (The Niyamgiri Dongria Kondh Case, 2013)*, the Supreme Court held that the Gram Sabha has sovereign statutory power under the FRA to decide whether mining proposals violate their religious, cultural, and community forest rights.

## 35.4 Gender Justice & Protection Against Domestic Violence

### 1. Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA)
* **Philosophical Innovation**: A hybrid enactment combining **civil remedies with criminal sanctions**. Criminal law (*Section 498A IPC*) punished cruelty by husbands/in-laws but left abused women homeless and destitute. PWDVA guarantees emergency civil remedies while maintaining the woman's right to life.
* **Scope & Beneficiaries**: An "aggrieved person" is **any woman** who is, or has been, in a **domestic relationship** with the respondent (husband, male partner in a relationship in the nature of marriage/live-in, in-laws, or family members).
* **Exhaustive Definition of Domestic Violence (Section 3)**:
  1. *Physical Abuse*: Bodily hurt, assault, criminal force.
  2. *Sexual Abuse*: Conduct of a sexual nature that abuses, humiliates, or degrades.
  3. *Verbal and Emotional Abuse*: Insults, ridicule, name-calling, repeated threats to cause physical pain, insults for not having a male child.
  4. *Economic Abuse*: Deprivation of financial resources, disposal of household assets, alienation of *Stridhan*, denial of maintenance.
* **Five Enforceable Statutory Reliefs**:
  * **Protection Orders (Section 18)**: Prohibits the abuser from committing violence, entering the victim’s workplace, communicating with her, or alienating shared assets.
  * **Residence Orders (Section 19)**: Enforces the woman’s **Right to Reside in the Shared Household** (*regardless of whether she has any legal title or proprietary interest*); restrains the abuser from evicting her; can direct the abuser to remove himself from the house.
  * **Monetary Relief (Section 20)**: Reimburses medical expenses, loss of earnings, loss of property, and maintenance.
  * **Custody Orders (Section 21)**: Grants temporary custody of children to the mother.
  * **Compensation Orders (Section 22)**: Damages for injuries, mental torture, and emotional distress.
* **Criminal Teeth (Section 31)**: Breach of a Protection Order by the respondent is a **cognizable and non-bailable criminal offense**, punishable with imprisonment up to 1 year and fine up to ₹20,000.

### 2. Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (POSH Act)
* **Origin**: Formulated to codify the Supreme Court’s historic **Vishaka Guidelines** (*Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan, 1997*), framed under Articles 14, 19, and 21 read with the UN CEDAW Convention.
* **Mandatory Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) (Section 4)**:
  * Every employer employing **10 or more employees** must constitute an ICC at each branch/office.
  * *Composition*:
    1. **Presiding Officer**: Senior woman employee.
    2. **Employee Members**: At least 2 employees committed to the cause of women or possessing legal knowledge.
    3. **External Member**: 1 member from an NGO or association committed to women’s rights.
    4. *Gender Mandate*: At least **one-half (50%) of the total members must be women**.
* **Local Complaints Committee (LCC) (Section 6)**: For workplaces employing $< 10$ workers (unorganized sector, domestic workers, small shops), the District Officer constitutes an LCC in every district.
* **Timelines & Safeguards**:
  * Complaint must be filed within **3 months** of the incident.
  * Inquiry must be completed within **90 days**.
  * Strict confidentiality under Section 16 (*RTI Act does NOT apply* to identity of victim, witnesses, or inquiry details).

## 35.5 Protection of Children Against Sexual Abuse

### Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012
* **Universal Age Anchor**: A "child" is defined as **any person below the age of 18 years**, regardless of sex (completely **gender-neutral** protection).
* **Categorization of Offenses**:
  * Penetrative Sexual Assault (Section 3) and Aggravated Penetrative Sexual Assault (Section 5 — committed by police, armed forces, public servant, doctor, teacher, relative, or causing grievous harm).
  * Sexual Assault (Section 7) and Sexual Harassment (Section 11).
  * Use of child for pornographic purposes (Section 14).
* **Statutory Innovations**:
  * **Mandatory Reporting (Section 19)**: Any person who has knowledge that a POCSO offense has been committed **must report it** to the Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU) or local police. Failure to report is punishable with imprisonment up to 6 months.
  * **Child-Friendly Procedure**: The police officer recording the statement **must not be in police uniform**; child cannot be detained at the police station overnight; identity cannot be disclosed in the media.
  * **Reverse Burden of Proof (Sections 29 & 30)**: Where a person is prosecuted for committing an offense under Sections 3, 5, 7, or 9, the Special Court **shall presume that such person has committed the offense**, unless the contrary is proved.
  * **Fast-Track Adjudication**: Evidence must be recorded within **30 days**, and the trial completed within **one year**.

## 35.6 Protection of Differently-Abled & Senior Citizens

### 1. Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (RPwD Act)
* **Shift to Rights-Based Model**: Replaced the welfare-oriented *Persons with Disabilities Act, 1995* to align with the **UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD)**.
* **Expanded Classification**: Increased the number of recognized disabilities from 7 to **21 categories** (including Acid Attack Victims, Autism Spectrum Disorder, Cerebral Palsy, Dwarfism, Muscular Dystrophy, Parkinson's disease, Thalassemia, Hemophilia, Sickle Cell Disease, Multiple Sclerosis).
* **Affirmative Action**:
  * Reservation in higher educational institutions increased from 3% to **5%**.
  * Reservation in government employment increased from 3% to **4%** (1% each for blindness/low vision, deaf/hard of hearing, locomotor disability, and autism/intellectual disability/multiple disabilities).
* **Enforcement**: Central and State Advisory Boards on Disability, and **Chief Commissioner / State Commissioners for Persons with Disabilities** with powers of a Civil Court.

### 2. Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007
* **Statutory Right**: A senior citizen (aged $\ge 60$) or parent unable to maintain themselves can apply to a **Maintenance Tribunal** (headed by an SDO/SDM).
* **Maintenance Order**: Tribunal can order children/heirs possessing their property to pay monthly maintenance up to **₹10,000 per month**.
* **Protection of Property (Section 23)**: If a senior citizen has transferred property by gift or deed on condition that the transferee provides basic amenities and physical needs, and the transferee fails to do so, the transfer of property is deemed **fraudulent, coercive, or under undue influence**, and can be **declared void** by the Maintenance Tribunal.

## 35.8 Examiner Traps & Warning Vault

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 1: Believing Both SC/ST and Non-SC/ST Persons Can Be Charged Under the PoA Act**
> *Exam Trap*: "If an individual belonging to a Scheduled Caste violently assaults a member of a Scheduled Tribe, they are prosecuted under the SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act, 1989."
> *Correction*: FALSE. Under Section 3(1) and 3(2) of the PoA Act, offenses can be committed **ONLY by a person who is NOT a member of a Scheduled Caste or a Scheduled Tribe**. An intra-SC/ST clash is prosecuted under ordinary criminal law (IPC / BNS), not under the PoA Act.

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 2: Believing the Gram Sabha Can Create New Land Allocations Under FRA**
> *Exam Trap*: "Under the Forest Rights Act, 2006, the Gram Sabha can grant up to 4 hectares of virgin forestland to eligible landless tribal youths."
> *Correction*: FALSE. Section 4(3) of the FRA strictly stipulates that forest rights can be recognized **ONLY for forestland under actual occupation and cultivation prior to 13 December 2005**. The FRA recognizes pre-existing customary tenure; it does **NOT authorize any fresh encroachments, clearing of new forest patches, or land distribution**.

## 35.9 60-Second Memory Skeleton (Rapid Recall)

* **PCR Act (1955/1976)**: Enforces Article 17; civil rights definition; non-compoundable; automatic election disqualification under RPA.
* **SC/ST PoA Act (1989)**: Offenses by non-SC/ST only; Section 18/18A bar on anticipatory bail (upheld in *Prathvi Raj Chauhan*); 2015 Chapter IVA victim rights.
* **FRA (2006)**: FDST (before 13 Dec 2005) & OTFD (3 generations / 75 years); Gram Sabha initiates $\rightarrow$ SDLC $\rightarrow$ DLC approves; 4 ha ceiling on actual occupation; MFP ownership.
* **PWDVA (2005)**: Hybrid civil-criminal; covers domestic relationships; physical, sexual, verbal, economic abuse; Residence Order (shared household); Section 31 breach is criminal.
* **POSH Act (2013)**: Codifies *Vishaka (1997)*; ICC mandatory for $\ge 10$ employees; Presiding Officer woman; 50% women members; 1 NGO member; LCC for $< 10$ workers.
* **POCSO Act (2012)**: Child = $< 18$ years (gender neutral); mandatory reporting (Sec 19); non-uniform police; Section 29/30 reverse burden of proof; 1-year trial.
* **RPwD Act (2016)**: Expands disabilities to 21 categories; 5% education reservation; 4% public employment reservation.
* **Senior Citizens Act (2007)**: Maintenance Tribunal (SDM); ₹10k monthly cap; Section 23 declares property transfer void if elderly neglected.

## 35.10 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

#### Card 1 (Statutory Criminal Jurisprudence)
**Question**: Detail the exact legal effect of Section 18A inserted into the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 by Parliament in 2018, and summarize the Supreme Court's ratio in *Prathvi Raj Chauhan v. Union of India (2020)*.
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
Section 18A nullified the procedural fetters introduced by the Supreme Court in <em>Subhash Kashinath Mahajan (2018)</em> by legislatively mandating that: (1) Preliminary inquiry is <strong>not required</strong> before registration of an FIR against any person; (2) The investigating officer <strong>does not require any prior approval</strong> for the arrest of any person; and (3) The provisions of Section 438 CrPC (anticipatory bail) shall not apply to a case under this Act, <strong>notwithstanding any judgment, decree, or order of any court</strong>.<br/>
In <em>Prathvi Raj Chauhan (2020)</em>, a 3-judge bench upheld Section 18A as constitutionally valid, holding that legislative bar on anticipatory bail is justified to prevent intimidation of vulnerable victims and maintain social deterrence. However, the Court clarified a narrow residual exception: if the complaint, taken on its face value, <strong>fails to make out even a prima facie case</strong> under the PoA Act, the bar under Section 18/18A will not apply, and the High Court or Sessions Court may grant pre-arrest bail to prevent clear abuse of legal process.
</details>

#### Card 2 (Tribunal Adjudication & Forest Democracy)
**Question**: Explain how the Supreme Court utilized the Forest Rights Act, 2006 to protect indigenous religious rights in *Orissa Mining Corporation v. Ministry of Environment & Forests (2013)*.
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
In the historic <em>Niyamgiri case (2013)</em>, the Supreme Court adjudicated a dispute over bauxite mining in the Niyamgiri hills of Odisha, inhabited by the primitive Dongria Kondh tribe. The Court held that under Section 3(1)(i) and Section 6 of the FRA read with Article 25 and 26 of the Constitution, forest-dwelling communities possess not merely economic rights over Minor Forest Produce, but also statutory <strong>rights to protect their cultural, customary, and religious heritage</strong> (the Dongria Kondh revered the Niyamgiri hilltop as the sacred abode of their deity, Niyam Raja). The Supreme Court ruled that environmental clearance could not be granted by the Central Government without the <strong>free, informed consent of the local Gram Sabhas</strong>, and directed that the Gram Sabhas alone had the sovereign power to decide whether mining would infringe their religious and community forest rights. All 12 Gram Sabhas unanimously voted against the mining project, terminating the clearance.
</details>

#### Card 3 (Gender Jurisprudence)
**Question**: What is the legal significance of a "Residence Order" under Section 19 of the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, and how does it protect an abused woman against eviction?
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
Under Section 17 and Section 19 of the PWDVA, every woman in a domestic relationship has a statutory <strong>Right to Reside in the Shared Household</strong>, irrespective of whether she has any proprietary right, title, or beneficial interest in the property. A Magistrate passing a Residence Order under Section 19 can: (1) Restrain the respondent/husband/in-laws from dispossessing or evicting the aggrieved woman from the shared household; (2) Direct the respondent to remove himself from the shared household; (3) Restrain the respondent from alienating, mortgaging, or disposing of the shared household; and (4) Direct the respondent to secure alternative accommodation of the same level for the aggrieved woman and pay rent. In <em>Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja (2020)</em>, the Supreme Court overruled its earlier narrow ruling in <em>S.R. Batra (2007)</em>, affirming that a shared household includes premises owned or rented by the husband's joint family/in-laws where the woman lived in a domestic relationship, giving comprehensive protection against marital homelessness.
</details>
