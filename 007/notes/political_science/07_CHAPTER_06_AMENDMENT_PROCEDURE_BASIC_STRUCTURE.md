## 6.1 The Epistemic Dilemma: Constitutional Preservation vs. Organic Adaptation

A constitution is an organic document that must grow with the evolving aspirations and socioeconomic realities of a developing nation. If a constitution is too **rigid**, it risks being overthrown by violent revolution; if it is too **flexible**, it becomes the plaything of temporary parliamentary majorities.

As Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru declared in the Constituent Assembly:  
> *"While we want this Constitution to be as solid and permanent as we can make it, there is no permanence in constitutions. There should be a certain flexibility. If you make anything rigid and permanent, you stop the nation’s growth, the growth of a living, vital, organic people."*

To balance stability and progress, the framers adopted a **three-fold amendment architecture** under **Article 368 (Part XX)**, blending American rigidity with British flexibility.

## 6.3 The Procedural Mechanics of Article 368

The formal procedure for amending the Constitution under Article 368 unfolds through strict sequential statutory steps:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     STEP-BY-STEP AMENDMENT WORKFLOW UNDER ARTICLE 368                       │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ STEP 1: Introduction in Parliament Only                                                     │
│ An amendment bill can be introduced in **EITHER House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha or         │
│ Rajya Sabha). It **CANNOT be initiated in State Legislatures**.                             │
│                                                                                             │
│ STEP 2: Private Member or Minister (No Presidential Prior Sanction)                         │
│ The bill can be introduced either by a Minister or a Private Member. It **DOES NOT          │
│ REQUIRE the prior recommendation of the President**.                                        │
│                                                                                             │
│ STEP 3: Separate Passage by Special Majority in Both Houses                                 │
│ The bill must be passed in **EACH House separately** by the prescribed Special Majority:    │
│ (a) Absolute majority of total membership (>50% of total seats), AND                        │
│ (b) 2/3rds majority of members present and voting.                                          │
│                                                                                             │
│ STEP 4: ABSOLUTE BAN ON JOINT SITTING (No Article 108)                                      │
│ If there is a deadlock between Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, **THERE IS NO PROVISION FOR A     │
│ JOINT SITTING**. If either House rejects the bill, the amendment dies instantly!            │
│                                                                                             │
│ STEP 5: Federal State Ratification (Where Applicable)                                       │
│ If the bill affects federal provisions, it must be ratified by the legislatures of **at   │
│ least half of the States by a SIMPLE MAJORITY** of members present and voting.             │
│ *(There is no time limit within which States must ratify or reject).*                       │
│                                                                                             │
│ STEP 6: Mandatory Presidential Assent (No Veto Power)                                       │
│ The passed bill is presented to the President. Under the **24th Amendment Act, 1971**,      │
│ the President **MUST GIVE HIS ASSENT**. He can neither withhold assent nor return the bill. │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Critical Procedural Distinctions:
* **"Total Membership" Defined**: Refers to the total number of members comprising the House as determined by the Constitution, **regardless of any vacancies or absentees**.
* **Zero State Initiative**: State legislatures cannot initiate any bill for amending the Constitution. Their sole power is to pass a resolution requesting Parliament to create or abolish a State Legislative Council under **Article 169** (which Parliament may accept or reject).
* **Presidential Veto Liquidated**: In ordinary legislation, the President enjoys Suspensive Veto (returning a bill) or Pocket Veto. For a Constitutional Amendment Bill, **Article 368(2)** explicitly uses the mandatory phrasing: *"the President shall give his assent thereto"*.

## 6.4 The Great Jurisprudential War: From Shankari Prasad to Kesavananda (1951–1973)

The fundamental question that divided Indian political institutions for 22 years was: **Can Parliament use Article 368 to amend, abridge, or destroy Fundamental Rights?**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE 22-YEAR CONSTITUTIONAL BATTLE FOR AMENDING SUPREMACY                    │
├─────────────────────┬──────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Landmark Benchmark  │ Year │ Core Constitutional Determination                              │
├─────────────────────┼──────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Shankari Prasad** │ 1951 │ **Parliament CAN amend FRs**. "Law" under Article 13 refers    │
│                     │      │ only to ordinary rules; Article 368 exercises constituent power.│
├─────────────────────┼──────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Sajjan Singh**    │ 1965 │ Reaffirmed Shankari Prasad. (Justices Hidayatullah and         │
│                     │      │ Mudholkar questioned if FRs could be playthings of a majority).│
├─────────────────────┼──────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **I.C. Golaknath**  │ 1967 │ **Overruled Shankari Prasad (6:5)**. FRs have a transcendental │
│                     │      │ position. Article 368 amendment IS a "law" under Art 13(2).   │
│                     │      │ Parliament **CANNOT abridge or take away any Fundamental Right**│
├─────────────────────┼──────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **24th Amendment**  │ 1971 │ Enacted to neutralize Golaknath. Inserted **Art 13(4)** and    │
│                     │      │ **Art 368(3)**: Nothing in Article 13 applies to Article 368.  │
├─────────────────────┼──────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Kesavananda       │ 1973 │ **BIRTH OF THE BASIC STRUCTURE DOCTRINE (7:6)**.               │
│ Bharati**           │      │ Upheld 24th Amendment; Parliament CAN amend FRs, BUT           │
│                     │      │ **CANNOT alter or destroy the Basic Structure**.               │
└─────────────────────┴──────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### Detailed Jurisprudential Evolution:

#### 1. Shankari Prasad v. Union of India (1951)
* **Challenged**: The **1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951** (which inserted Articles 31A and 31B and the Ninth Schedule to shield agrarian land reform and zamindari abolition laws from Article 14 and 19 challenges).
* **Contention**: The amendment abridged the Fundamental Right to Property (Article 31) and was therefore void under Article 13(2) (*"The State shall not make any law which takes away or abridges the rights conferred by this Part"*).
* **Supreme Court Holding (Unanimous 5-judge bench)**:
  * Distinguished between **Ordinary Law** (enacted under legislative powers) and **Constitutional Law** (enacted under constituent power under Article 368).
  * The word *"Law"* in Article 13(2) includes only ordinary legislative law; it **does not include a Constitutional Amendment Act**.
  * Parliament has plenary constituent power to amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights.

#### 2. Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan (1965)
* **Challenged**: The **17th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1964** (which added 44 state land reform acts to the Ninth Schedule).
* **Holding**: A 5-judge bench reaffirmed the *Shankari Prasad* ratio by a 3:2 majority.
* **The Historic Dissent**: Justice M. Hidayatullah and Justice J.R. Mudholkar doubted whether Fundamental Rights could be stripped away by a temporary two-thirds parliamentary majority. Justice Mudholkar asked whether the Constitution had certain **"basic features"** that could not be amended.

#### 3. I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)
* **Challenged**: The Punjab Security of Land Tenures Act, placed in the Ninth Schedule by the 17th Amendment.
* **Supreme Court Holding (11-Judge Constitution Bench, 6:5 Majority)**:
  * Overruled *Shankari Prasad* and *Sajjan Singh*.
  * Chief Justice K. Subba Rao ruled that Fundamental Rights occupy a **"transcendental and immutable position"**.
  * Article 368 merely contains the *procedure* for amendment; it did not confer substantive *power* to amend (power was derived from residuary legislative powers under Entry 97, List I).
  * An amendment passed under Article 368 is a **"Law" within the meaning of Article 13(2)**.
  * Therefore, Parliament **CANNOT amend Part III so as to abridge or take away any Fundamental Right**.
  * Invoked the **Doctrine of Prospective Overruling**: All previous amendments (1st, 4th, 17th) remained valid, but Parliament was prohibited from abridging FRs in the future.

#### 4. The Executive Backlash: The 24th and 25th Amendments (1971)
Faced with the judicial barrier of *Golaknath*, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi enacted aggressive constitutional amendments:
* **The 24th Amendment Act (1971)**:
  * Modified the marginal heading of Article 368 to read: *"Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution and procedure therefor"*.
  * Inserted **Article 13(4)**: *"Nothing in this article shall apply to any amendment of this Constitution made under article 368."*
  * Inserted **Article 368(3)**: *"Nothing in article 13 shall apply to any amendment made under this article."*
  * Made Presidential assent to amendment bills legally compulsory.
* **The 25th Amendment Act (1971)**:
  * Inserted **Article 31C**, providing that laws implementing DPSPs under Article 39(b) and 39(c) could not be challenged under Articles 14, 19, or 31, and barred judicial review over such declarations.

## 6.5 The Watershed: Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)

Argued over 68 days before the largest bench in Indian history (**13 Judges**), the case of *His Holiness Kesavananda Bharati Sripadagalvaru v. State of Kerala* culminated on **24 April 1973** in a monumental 700-page judgment delivered by a **7:6 majority** (Chief Justice S.M. Sikri).

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE RATIO DECIDENDI OF KESAVANANDA BHARATI (1973)                           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Upheld the 24th Constitutional Amendment Act, formally overruling *Golaknath*.           │
│ 2. Affirmed that Article 368 confers substantive constituent power to amend ANY provision   │
│    of the Constitution, including the Preamble and Fundamental Rights.                      │
│ 3. **THE BASIC STRUCTURE DOCTRINE**: The word "Amendment" in Article 368 implies           │
│    alteration, improvement, or addition; it DOES NOT mean liquidation, abrogation, or      │
│    destruction of the original constitutional foundation.                                   │
│ 4. Parliament CANNOT alter, damage, or emasculate the **"Basic Structure"** or              │
│    **"Essential Features"** of the Constitution.                                            │
│ 5. Upheld the first part of Article 31C (saving laws implementing 39(b) & (c)), but         │
│    **STRUCK DOWN the second part** which barred judicial review, affirming that             │
│    **Judicial Review is an unalterable Basic Feature**.                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 6.8 Publication Callout Boxes: Strategic Matrices & Warnings

### [CONSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM: THE 9TH SCHEDULE IMMUNITY SHIELD BEFORE & AFTER 1973]
> **How *I.R. Coelho (2007)* dismantled the Ninth Schedule Black Hole:**  
> Originally created under Article 31B (1st Amendment 1951), the Ninth Schedule was designed to protect land reform laws from judicial review. However, successive governments abused it to shield non-agrarian, politically contentious laws (e.g., Tamil Nadu 69% reservation law).  
> In *I.R. Coelho (2007)*, a 9-judge bench ruled that **no statutory blanket immunity exists**. Any law inserted into the Ninth Schedule **on or after 24 April 1973** is open to judicial challenge. If it violates the "Golden Triangle" (Articles 14, 19, 21) in a manner that damages the **Basic Structure**, the Supreme Court will invalidate the law!

### [DISTINCTION MATRIX: TYPES OF PARLIAMENTARY MAJORITY]
| Type of Majority | Mathematical Threshold | Constitutional Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Majority** | $> 50\%$ of members **present and voting** in the House. | Ordinary Bills; Money Bills; No-Confidence Motion; Art 2 & 3 state creation; Art 169 Legislative Councils. |
| **Absolute Majority** | $> 50\%$ of **total membership** of the House (e.g., 272 in Lok Sabha). | Used during government formation; never used alone to pass legislation. |
| **Effective Majority** | $> 50\%$ of **effective strength** (Total strength minus vacancies). | Removal of Vice-President in Rajya Sabha (Art 67(b)); Removal of Speaker/Deputy Speaker (Art 94). |
| **Special Majority (Art 368)** | $> 50\%$ of total membership **AND** $\ge 2/3\text{rds}$ of members **present and voting**. | Constitutional Amendment Bills (Art 368); Approval of National Emergency (Art 352); Removal of SC/HC Judges (Art 124(4)). |
| **Super Special (Art 61)** | $\ge 2/3\text{rds}$ of **total membership** of the House (362 in Lok Sabha). | **Impeachment of the President of India** (most stringent majority in the Constitution). |

### [EXAM TRAP & WARNING: HIGH-YIELD PITFALLS FOR CANDIDATES]
> ⚠️ **Trap 1: "The President can return a Constitutional Amendment Bill for reconsideration."**  
> **FALSE**: Under the 24th Amendment Act 1971, the President **HAS NO VETO POWER** over a constitutional amendment bill. Assent is mandatory.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 2: "If the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha disagree on an amendment bill, a joint sitting is convened by the President."**  
> **FALSE**: Article 108 (Joint Sitting) applies **ONLY to ordinary bills and financial bills**. A Constitutional Amendment Bill **CANNOT have a joint sitting**; failure in either House kills the bill.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 3: "State legislatures must ratify federal amendments within six months."**  
> **FALSE**: The Constitution prescribes **NO TIME LIMIT** for state legislatures to ratify or reject an amendment bill.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 4: "The term 'Basic Structure' is explicitly defined in Article 368."**  
> **FALSE**: The term 'Basic Structure' is **NOWHERE mentioned in the text of the Constitution**. It is an entirely judge-made constitutional doctrine born on 24 April 1973 in *Kesavananda Bharati*.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 5: "Laws in the Ninth Schedule are completely immune from judicial review."**  
> **FALSE**: Following *I.R. Coelho (2007)*, all laws inserted into the Ninth Schedule **after 24 April 1973** are subject to judicial review if they violate the Basic Structure.

## 6.9 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

1. **What are the two mathematical thresholds required to pass a Constitutional Amendment Bill under Article 368?**  
   *Answer*: (1) Absolute majority of total membership of each House (>50%), AND (2) Two-thirds majority of members present and voting in each House.

2. **Can a Constitutional Amendment Bill be initiated in a State Legislative Assembly?**  
   *Answer*: No. Under Article 368, an amendment bill can be introduced only in either House of Parliament (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha).

3. **Does the President possess any veto power over a Constitutional Amendment Bill? Cite the relevant amendment.**  
   *Answer*: No. The 24th Constitutional Amendment Act of 1971 made Presidential assent to constitutional amendment bills legally mandatory.

4. **Why is a Joint Sitting of Parliament prohibited for resolving a deadlock over an amendment bill?**  
   *Answer*: Article 368 mandates that the bill must be passed by each House separately by special majority, preserving the equal constituent veto of the Rajya Sabha.

5. **Which Supreme Court judgment first established that Fundamental Rights could NOT be amended by Parliament?**  
   *Answer*: *I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)* (11-judge bench).

6. **What was the date of delivery and bench strength of the *Kesavananda Bharati* ruling?**  
   *Answer*: Delivered on **24 April 1973** by a 13-judge Constitution Bench (decided by a 7:6 majority).

7. **How did the Supreme Court apply the Basic Structure Doctrine in *Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)*?**  
   *Answer*: It struck down Article 329A(4) (39th Amendment), holding that barring judicial review over the Prime Minister's election violated the Basic Structure principles of Democracy, Rule of Law, and Judicial Review.

8. **Explain the significance of the *Waman Rao (1981)* cut-off date.**  
   *Answer*: It established that the Basic Structure Doctrine applies prospectively to all constitutional amendments enacted on or after **24 April 1973**; pre-1973 amendments are shielded.

9. **What was the unanimous ratio of the 9-judge bench in *I.R. Coelho (2007)* regarding Ninth Schedule laws?**  
   *Answer*: Laws added to the Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973 do not enjoy blanket immunity and can be invalidated if they violate the Basic Structure on the touchstone of Articles 14, 19, and 21.

10. **Why was the 99th Constitutional Amendment Act (NJAC) struck down in 2015?**  
    *Answer*: The Supreme Court held that the National Judicial Appointments Commission compromised the **Independence of the Judiciary**, which is an unalterable feature of the Basic Structure.
