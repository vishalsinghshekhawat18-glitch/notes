## 3. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992: Architecture

### A. Constitutional Additions
- Inserted **Part IX** entitled *"The Panchayats"* containing **Articles 243 to 243-O**.
- Inserted the **Eleventh Schedule** containing **29 functional subjects** devolved to Panchayats under **Article 243-G** (agriculture, minor irrigation, animal husbandry, rural housing, drinking water, roads, rural electrification, poverty alleviation, education, health).

---

## 4. Key Institutional Organs under Part IX

### A. The Gram Sabha (Article 243A)
- **Definition (Article 243(b))**: A body consisting of all persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village comprised within the area of the Panchayat at the village level.
- Embodiment of **Direct Democracy** (akin to the *Landsgemeinde* in Switzerland).
- Performs such powers and functions at the village level as the Legislature of a State may by law provide.

---

### B. Three-Tier Structure & Chairperson Election Mechanics

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP: The 20 Lakh Population Exception (Article 243B(2))**
> Panchayats at the intermediate level (Block/Taluka) **may not be constituted** in a State having a population **not exceeding twenty lakhs (2 million)** (e.g., Goa, Sikkim). These small states maintain a 2-tier system (Village + District).

---

### C. Duration & Dissolution Safeguards (Article 243E)
- Fixed term of **5 years** from the date appointed for its first meeting.
- Can be dissolved before the expiration of 5 years in accordance with state law.
- **Mandatory Reconstitution Rule**: Elections to reconstitute a dissolved Panchayat **must be completed before the expiration of six months** from the date of its dissolution.
- **Remainder Term Doctrine (Article 243E(4))**:
  A Panchayat constituted upon the dissolution of a Panchayat before the expiration of its duration shall continue **only for the remainder of the period** for which the dissolved Panchayat would have continued.
  - *Exception*: Where the remainder of the period is **less than six months**, it is not necessary to hold elections for that truncated period.

---

### D. Disqualifications (Article 243F)
- A person is disqualified if so disqualified under any law for the time being in force for the purposes of elections to the State Legislature.
- > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The 21-Year Exception (Article 243F(1)(b))**:
  > No person shall be disqualified on the ground that he is less than 25 years of age, **if he has attained the age of twenty-one (21) years**. (Minimum age to contest MLA election is 25; minimum age to contest Panchayat election is 21).

---

### E. The State Election Commission (Article 243K)
- Vests the superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of all elections to Panchayats.
- Consists of a **State Election Commissioner** appointed by the **Governor**.
- **Removal Safeguard**: The State Election Commissioner **shall not be removed from office except in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of a High Court**, and his conditions of service shall not be varied to his disadvantage after appointment.
- **Bar to Interference by Courts (Article 243-O)**: Courts are barred from interfering in electoral matters, including the validity of any law relating to delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats.

---

### F. The State Finance Commission (Article 243-I)
- The Governor shall, within one year from the commencement of the 73rd Amendment and thereafter at the expiration of **every fifth year**, constitute a Finance Commission to review the financial position of Panchayats.
- Recommends:
  1. The distribution between the State and the Panchayats of the net proceeds of taxes, duties, tolls, and fees.
  2. The determination of taxes, duties, and tolls assignable to Panchayats.
  3. The grants-in-aid to Panchayats from the Consolidated Fund of the State.
  4. Measures needed to improve the financial health of Panchayats.
- **Union Finance Commission Linkage (Article 280(3)(bb))**: The Finance Commission of India makes recommendations on measures needed to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of the Panchayats on the basis of the recommendations of the State Finance Commission.

## 5. PESA Act, 1996: Decentralization in Scheduled Areas

### A. Legislative Rationale & Constitutional Context
- Under **Article 243-M(1)**, the provisions of Part IX do **not** automatically apply to the Fifth Schedule Areas.
- Under **Article 243-M(4)(b)**, Parliament was empowered to extend Part IX to Scheduled Areas by law with such exceptions and modifications as it deemed fit.
- Pursuant to the recommendations of the **Dilip Singh Bhuria Committee (1995)**, Parliament enacted the **Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA)**.
- **Coverage**: Applies to Fifth Schedule Areas across **10 States**:
  $$\text{PESA States (10): } \text{Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh,}$$
  $$\text{Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan}$$

---

### B. Core Objectives & Tribal Self-Governance
1. To extend Part IX to Fifth Schedule areas with modifications acknowledging tribal customary law, social and religious practices, and traditional management practices of community resources.
2. To make the **Gram Sabha the focal centre of governance**, transforming participatory democracy into direct self-rule.
3. To prevent panchayats at higher levels from assuming the powers and authority of panchayats at the lower level or of the Gram Sabha.

---

## 6. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. Which committee first recommended that Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) should be accorded 
    constitutional recognition and protection?
A1. The L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986). It recommended inserting a new chapter in the Constitution 
    to protect and preserve local self-government bodies.

Q2. What is the population threshold below which a State is exempted from constituting Panchayats 
    at the intermediate (block) level under Article 243B(2)?
A2. A population not exceeding twenty lakhs (20 Lakh / 2 Million). States below this threshold can 
    operate a 2-tier system (Village and District).

Q3. What happens to a newly elected Panchayat constituted after the premature dissolution of the 
    previous Panchayat after 2 years of its 5-year tenure?
A3. Under Article 243E(4), it serves only for the REMAINDER of the period (i.e., 3 remaining years), 
    not for a fresh full term of 5 years.

Q4. What is the minimum age required to contest elections to a Panchayati Raj Institution under 
    Article 243F(1)(b)?
A4. Twenty-One (21) years. (Unlike the State Legislative Assembly where the minimum age is 25 years).

Q5. How does PESA 1996 alter the balance of power between the District Administration and the 
    Gram Sabha regarding Minor Forest Produce (MFP) and Minor Minerals?
A5. PESA vests outright OWNERSHIP of Minor Forest Produce in the Gram Sabha and mandates PRIOR 
    RECOMMENDATION of the Gram Sabha before granting prospecting licenses or mining leases for 
    minor minerals in Fifth Schedule areas.
```
