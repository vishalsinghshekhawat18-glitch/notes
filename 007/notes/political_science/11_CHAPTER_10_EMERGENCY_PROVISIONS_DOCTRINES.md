## 10.1 The Epistemic Philosophy: The Unitary Metamorphosis

Part XVIII of the Constitution (Articles 352 to 360) contains extraordinary provisions designed to protect the sovereignty, unity, integrity, and security of India, the democratic political system, and the Constitution.

The unique constitutional mechanism of the Indian emergency framework was articulated by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:  
> *"All federal systems including the American are placed in a tight mould of federalism. It cannot change its form and shape. It can never be unitary. On the other hand, the Draft Constitution of India can be **both unitary as well as federal according to the requirements of time and circumstances**. In normal times, it is framed to work as a federal system. But in times of Emergency, it is so designed that it **transforms itself into an entirely unitary state without a formal amendment of the Constitution**."*

## 10.2 National Emergency (Article 352)

### 1. Grounds of Proclamation
* Under **Article 352(1)**, the President can declare a National Emergency when the security of India or any part thereof is threatened by:
  1. **War** (Formal declaration of hostilities with a foreign state).
  2. **External Aggression** (Unilateral foreign attack without formal declaration of war).  
     *(War or External Aggression = **"External Emergency"**).*
  3. **Armed Rebellion** (Violent internal armed uprising).  
     *(Armed Rebellion = **"Internal Emergency"**).*
* **The 44th Amendment Act (1978) Substitution**:  
  Originally, the third ground was **"Internal Disturbance"**. Because this phrase was vague and was abused in June 1975 to declare an emergency for political survival, the 44th Amendment substituted the precise phrase **"Armed Rebellion"**. An emergency can **no longer be declared on grounds of internal disturbance**.
* **Anticipatory Power**: The President can issue a proclamation even before the actual occurrence of war, aggression, or rebellion if satisfied that there is an **imminent danger** thereof.
* **Territorial Scope**: Under the **42nd Amendment Act (1976)**, the President was empowered to limit the operation of a National Emergency to a **specified part of India** (rather than the whole country).

### 3. Consequences of National Emergency

#### A. Executive Effects (Article 353(a))
* The executive power of the Union extends to giving directions to **any State as to the manner in which its executive power is to be exercised**.
* **Crucial Rule**: State governments are **NOT SUSPENDED OR DISMISSED**. The state executive continues to exist, but is brought under the **complete administrative control of the Union**.

#### B. Legislative Effects (Article 353(b) & Article 250)
* Parliament becomes empowered to make laws on **any subject in the State List (List II)**.
* **Crucial Rule**: The State Legislature is **NOT SUSPENDED OR DISSOLVED**. It continues to function, but its legislative powers become **concurrent and subordinate** to Parliament. In case of conflict, the parliamentary law prevails.
* Laws made by Parliament on the State List cease to have effect **six months after the emergency ceases to operate**.
* **Extension of Lok Sabha Term (Article 83(2))**: Parliament can extend the normal 5-year life of the Lok Sabha by law for **one year at a time**, for any length of time. However, this extension cannot continue beyond **six months** after the emergency has ceased. *(The 5th Lok Sabha term was extended twice during the 1975–77 emergency).*

#### C. Financial Effects (Article 354)
* The President may, by order, modify the constitutional distribution of revenues between the Union and the States (e.g., reduce or suspend transfers and grants under Articles 268–279) during the financial year in which the emergency operates.

#### The Dark Chapter and Its Correction: ADM Jabalpur Overruled
* In *ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla (1976)* [The Habeas Corpus Case], a 4:1 majority of the Supreme Court notoriously held that during an emergency, a citizen had no locus standi to move a writ of Habeas Corpus under Article 21, even against illegal and malafide executive detentions. Only **Justice H.R. Khanna** dissented, paying for it with his supersession for the Chief Justiceship.
* **The 44th Amendment Correction (1978)**: Amended Article 359 to provide that the right to move courts for the enforcement of **Articles 20 (Protection against retrospective criminal laws, double jeopardy, self-incrimination) and 21 (Protection of life and personal liberty) CAN NEVER BE SUSPENDED under any circumstances**.
* **Formal Overruling (*K.S. Puttaswamy, 2017*)**: A 9-judge bench explicitly and unanimously overruled *ADM Jabalpur*, adopting Justice Khanna’s dissent as the immutable law of the land.

## 10.3 President's Rule / State Emergency (Articles 356 & 365)

Article 355 imposes a solemn duty upon the Union:  
> *"It shall be the duty of the Union to **protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance** and to **ensure that the government of every State is carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution**."*

To fulfill this duty, the Constitution provides for the invocation of **President's Rule** (also known as State Emergency or Constitutional Emergency).

### 1. The Two Constitutional Triggers
1. **Article 356**: If the President, on receipt of a report from the **Governor of a State or otherwise**, is satisfied that a situation has arisen in which the government of the State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of the Constitution**.
2. **Article 365**: Where any State has **failed to comply with, or give effect to, any directions given by the Union Executive**, it shall be lawful for the President to hold that the constitutional machinery has broken down.

### 3. Consequences of President's Rule
* The President **dismisses the State Council of Ministers** headed by the Chief Minister.
* The **Governor**, on behalf of the President, carries on the state administration with the help of the Chief Secretary and advisors appointed by the Centre.
* The President either **suspends or dissolves the State Legislative Assembly**.
* Parliament exercises the lawmaking and budgetary powers of the State Legislature.
* **The High Court Exemption**: The President **CANNOT assume the powers vested in the High Court of the State**, nor suspend any constitutional provision relating to High Courts. The constitutional position, powers, and independence of the High Court remain completely intact!

### 4. S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994): The Historic Restraint

Prior to 1994, Article 356 was abused over 100 times by ruling central parties to unseat opposition-ruled state governments. In *S.R. Bommai (1994)*, a 9-judge Constitution bench laid down definitive guidelines restricting arbitrary dismissals:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE S.R. BOMMAI (1994) NINE-JUDGE CONSTITUTIONAL CODE                       │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Judicial Review: Proclamation under Article 356 is subject to **Judicial Review**.       │
│ 2. Floor Test Mandatory: The state government’s majority must be tested **ONLY on the floor │
│    of the Assembly**, not through private headcounts at the Raj Bhavan.                     │
│ 3. Assembly Dissolution Safeguard: The President can **ONLY SUSPEND** the State Assembly    │
│    initially; the Assembly **CANNOT be dissolved until Parliament approves the proclamation**│
│    within two months.                                                                       │
│ 4. Judicial Restoration Power: If the court strikes down the proclamation as unconstitutional│
│    or malafide, the court has the power to **reactivate the dissolved Assembly and restore  │
│    the dismissed Ministry**!                                                                │
│ 5. Secularism Anchor: **Secularism is part of the Basic Structure**. A state government     │
│    acting against secularism renders itself liable to dismissal under Article 356.          │
│ 6. Burden of Proof: The burden lies on the Central Government to prove that relevant        │
│    material existed to justify the breakdown of constitutional machinery.                   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 10.4 Financial Emergency (Article 360)

* **Grounds**: If the President is satisfied that a situation has arisen whereby the **financial stability or credit of India or of any part of its territory is threatened**.
* **Parliamentary Approval**: Must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within **2 months** by an ordinary **Simple Majority**.
* **Indefinite Duration**: Once approved by Parliament, a Financial Emergency **continues indefinitely until it is revoked by the President**. Unlike National Emergency and President's Rule, **there is NO requirement for repeated parliamentary approval every six months**!
* **Consequences of Financial Emergency**:
  1. The Union executive can direct any State to observe specified canons of financial propriety.
  2. The President can direct the **reduction of salaries and allowances of all persons serving the State**, including **Judges of the High Courts**.
  3. The President can require that all **Money Bills and Financial Bills passed by the State Legislature be reserved for his consideration**.
  4. The President can direct the **reduction of salaries and allowances of all persons serving the Union**, including **Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts**.
* **Historical Fact**: A Financial Emergency has **NEVER been declared in India** so far (even during the acute balance of payments crisis of 1991).

## 10.6 Publication Callout Boxes: Strategic Matrices & Warnings

### [CONSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM: THE EXTENSION OF PARLIAMENT DURING EMERGENCY]
> **Can the Lok Sabha extend its own tenure indefinitely?**  
> Under **Article 83(2)**, during a National Emergency, Parliament can extend the normal 5-year duration of the Lok Sabha by law for **one year at a time**, for any number of extensions.  
> **The Constitutional Guardrail**: This extension **CANNOT continue beyond a period of six months after the proclamation of Emergency has ceased to operate**. General elections must be conducted within six months of emergency termination.

---

### [DISTINCTION MATRIX: DISSOLUTION UNDER ARTICLE 356 BEFORE & AFTER BOMMAI]
| Era | Governor's Action upon Recommending Article 356 | Supreme Court Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-Bommai (Pre-1994)** | Governor dissolved Assembly immediately on issuing proclamation. | Assembly dissolved before Parliament could debate, making central overreach irreversible. |
| **Post-Bommai (Post-1994)** | Assembly **can only be kept under SUSPENSION** initially. | Dissolution permitted **ONLY AFTER both Houses of Parliament formally approve** the proclamation within 2 months! |

---

### [EXAM TRAP & WARNING: HIGH-YIELD PITFALLS FOR CANDIDATES]
> ⚠️ **Trap 1: "The President can declare a National Emergency on the verbal advice of the Prime Minister."**  
> **FALSE**: Following the 44th Amendment Act 1978, the President can issue a proclamation under Article 352 **ONLY upon receiving the WRITTEN recommendation of the Union Cabinet** (Prime Minister + other Cabinet Ministers).
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 2: "President's Rule and Financial Emergency suspend Fundamental Rights."**  
> **FALSE**: **Neither President's Rule (Article 356) nor Financial Emergency (Article 360) affects Fundamental Rights in any way**. Fundamental Rights are affected **ONLY during a National Emergency under Article 352**!
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 3: "Under Article 358, Article 19 is automatically suspended during an emergency declared on grounds of Armed Rebellion."**  
> **FALSE**: Under the 44th Amendment, Article 358 operates **ONLY during an External Emergency (War or External Aggression)**. It **DOES NOT operate during an internal emergency caused by Armed Rebellion**.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 4: "President's Rule can be extended indefinitely like a National Emergency."**  
> **FALSE**: President's Rule has a **strict constitutional upper ceiling of THREE YEARS**. Beyond 1 year, it requires a National Emergency and Election Commission certification.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 5: "During President's Rule, the President can assume the powers of the State High Court."**  
> **FALSE**: Article 356(1) explicitly provides that the President **CANNOT assume the powers of the High Court**, nor suspend any constitutional provisions concerning High Courts.

## 10.7 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

1. **Which phrase was replaced by "Armed Rebellion" in Article 352 by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978?**  
   *Answer*: The phrase **"Internal Disturbance"**.

2. **What is the approval window and majority required for a proclamation of National Emergency under Article 352?**  
   *Answer*: Must be approved by both Houses within **1 month** by a **Special Majority** (>50% total seats + 2/3rds present & voting).

3. **Can the right to move courts for enforcement of Articles 20 and 21 be suspended during a National Emergency?**  
   *Answer*: No. Under the 44th Amendment (1978), the enforcement of **Articles 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended** under Article 359 under any circumstance.

4. **Which landmark Supreme Court judgment formally overruled the infamous *ADM Jabalpur (1976)* ruling?**  
   *Answer*: *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India (2017)* (9-judge bench).

5. **Differentiate between the constitutional triggers of Article 356 and Article 365.**  
   *Answer*: Article 356 is triggered by the failure of constitutional machinery in a State (on Governor's report or otherwise); Article 365 is triggered when a State fails to comply with lawful administrative directions issued by the Union.

6. **Under *S.R. Bommai (1994)*, at what stage can the President dissolve a State Legislative Assembly under Article 356?**  
   *Answer*: Only **AFTER both Houses of Parliament have formally approved** the proclamation of President's Rule. Prior to approval, the Assembly can only be suspended.

7. **What two conditions must be fulfilled under the 44th Amendment to extend President's Rule beyond one year?**  
   *Answer*: (1) A National Emergency must be in operation in whole or part of the State, and (2) The Election Commission must certify that general assembly elections cannot be held.

8. **What is the maximum constitutional time limit for the continuation of President's Rule in a State?**  
   *Answer*: A maximum of **3 years**.

9. **Does a Financial Emergency under Article 360 require repeated parliamentary approval every six months?**  
   *Answer*: No. Once approved by Parliament within 2 months, a Financial Emergency continues indefinitely until it is formally revoked by the President.

10. **Can the salaries of Supreme Court and High Court judges be reduced during a Financial Emergency?**  
    *Answer*: Yes. Under Article 360(4)(b), the President can direct the reduction of salaries and allowances of all judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
