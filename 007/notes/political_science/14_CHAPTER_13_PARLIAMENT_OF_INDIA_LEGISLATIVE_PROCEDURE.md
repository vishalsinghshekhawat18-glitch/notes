## 1. Constitutional Epistemic Anchor: The Tripartite Parliament

Under **Article 79** of the Indian Constitution, the Parliament of the Union consists of three indispensable organs:
$$\text{PARLIAMENT OF INDIA} = \text{The President} + \text{Council of States (Rajya Sabha)} + \text{House of the People (Lok Sabha)}$$

### Why the President is an Integral Part of Parliament:
1. No bill passed by both Houses can become law without receiving the **President's assent** (**Article 111**).
2. The President summons, prorogues both Houses, and dissolves the Lok Sabha (**Article 85**).
3. The President delivers the opening address to both Houses assembled together at the commencement of the first session after each general election and the first session of each year (**Article 87**).
4. The President promulgates Ordinances when Parliament is not in session (**Article 123**).

## 2. Bicameral Composition & Representation Dynamics

### A. The Rajya Sabha (Council of States - Article 80)
- **Maximum Strength**: **250** (238 representatives of States and UTs + 12 nominated by the President).
- **Current Strength**: **245** (225 from States, 8 from UTs [Delhi: 3, Puducherry: 1, J&K: 4], and 12 Nominated).
- **Nominated Members**: 12 members nominated by the President having special knowledge or practical experience in:
  $$\text{Fields of Nomination: } \mathbf{L}\text{iterature}, \mathbf{S}\text{cience}, \mathbf{A}\text{rt}, \text{and } \mathbf{S}\text{ocial Service (LSAS)}$$
- **Representation of States**:
  - Seats are allocated on the basis of **population** in the **Fourth Schedule**. (Unlike the US Senate where every state has equal representation of 2 senators regardless of size; e.g., UP has 31 seats, while Goa, Sikkim, and NE states have only 1 seat each).
  - Elected by the **elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (MLAs)** in accordance with the system of **Proportional Representation by means of the Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV)**.
  - *Open Ballot Reform (2003)*: To curb cross-voting and corruption, Parliament amended the RPA 1951 to introduce an **open ballot system** for Rajya Sabha elections (electors must show ballot to authorized party agent). Upheld as constitutional by the Supreme Court in *Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2006)*.
- **Tenure & Continuity**:
  - A **permanent body** not subject to dissolution (**Article 83(1)**).
  - One-third of its members retire every second year.
  - An individual member's term is **six years** (determined by Parliament under the *Representation of the People Act, 1951*, **not** explicitly stated in the Constitution!).

---

### B. The Lok Sabha (House of the People - Article 81)
- **Maximum Strength**: **550** (530 from States + 20 from Union Territories).
  - *Note on Anglo-Indian Seats*: The provision for nominating 2 Anglo-Indian members under Article 331 was **discontinued** by the **104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019**.
- **Current Strength**: **543** elected members (524 from States, 19 from UTs).
- **Electoral Basis**: Direct election from territorial constituencies on the basis of **Universal Adult Suffrage** (**Article 326**; the 61st Amendment Act, 1988 lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 years).
- **Delimitation & Seat Freezing Timeline**:
  - **42nd Amendment Act 1976**: Froze seat allocation to states at the **1971 Census** baseline until the year 2000.
  - **84th Amendment Act 2001**: Extended the freeze on total state-wise seat allocations until the first census post-**2026**.
  - **87th Amendment Act 2003**: Permitted intra-state delimitation (redrawing constituency boundaries) based on the **2001 Census** without altering the total number of seats allocated to any state.
- **Duration of Lok Sabha (Article 83(2))**:
  - 5 years from the date of its first meeting, unless dissolved earlier.
  - Can be extended during a **National Emergency (Article 352)** by Parliament by law for **one year at a time**, up to any length.
  - Cannot continue beyond **six months** after the Emergency has ceased to operate.

---

### C. The 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (*Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*)
- **Historic Reservation Mandate**: Inserts **Article 330A** (Lok Sabha), **Article 332A** (State Assemblies), and amends **Article 239AA** (Delhi Assembly) to reserve **one-third (33%)** of all seats for women.
- Includes horizontal reservation for women belonging to SCs and STs within their reserved quotas.
- **Sunset Clause**: Operates for a period of **15 years** from commencement, extendable by Parliament.
- **Implementation Trigger**: Becomes effective only **after an exercise of delimitation is undertaken** based on the relevant figures of the first census conducted after the Act's commencement.

### Vacation of Seats (Article 101):
1. **Double Membership**:
   - Elected to both Houses: Must intimate within 10 days which house he desires to serve; default = **Rajya Sabha seat becomes vacant**.
   - Sitting member of one House elected to other House: Seat in first House becomes vacant.
   - Elected to two seats in same House: Must choose one; default = **both seats become vacant**.
   - Elected as MP and MLA simultaneously: If he does not resign state seat within 14 days, his **Parliament seat becomes vacant**.
2. **Resignation**: Addressed in writing to Speaker (LS) or Chairman (RS). Under the 33rd Amendment Act 1974, the Presiding Officer can reject resignation if satisfied it is not voluntary or genuine.
3. **Absence**: Absent from all meetings for **60 consecutive days** without permission. (In calculating 60 days, periods of prorogation or adjournment exceeding 4 consecutive days are excluded).

### A. The Money Bill Deep Dive (Articles 109 & 110)
- **Article 110(1)** defines a Money Bill as one containing **ONLY** provisions dealing with:
  1. The imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, or regulation of any tax.
  2. The regulation of borrowing of money or giving of guarantees by the Government of India.
  3. The custody of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund of India, payment of moneys into or withdrawal of moneys from such fund.
  4. The appropriation of moneys out of the Consolidated Fund of India.
  5. The declaring of any expenditure to be expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
  6. The receipt of money on account of the Consolidated Fund or Public Account of India.
  7. Any matter incidental to any of the matters specified in points (1) to (6).

#### What is NOT a Money Bill (Article 110(2)):
A bill is not deemed to be a Money Bill by reason only that it provides for the imposition of fines, fees for licenses or services rendered, or the imposition of any local tax by a local authority.

#### The Special Procedure for Money Bills (Article 109):
1. Can be introduced **only in the Lok Sabha** and **only on the prior recommendation of the President**.
2. Must be certified by the Speaker as a Money Bill (**Article 110(3)**). The Speaker's decision is final and cannot be challenged in Parliament or court.
3. After being passed by Lok Sabha, transmitted to Rajya Sabha.
4. Rajya Sabha has strictly **14 DAYS** to take action:
   - It **cannot reject or amend** a Money Bill.
   - It can only make non-binding recommendations.
   - If not returned within 14 days, the Bill is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form passed by Lok Sabha.
5. Lok Sabha may accept or reject any or all recommendations of Rajya Sabha.
6. The President can either give assent or withhold assent; the President **cannot return a Money Bill for reconsideration** (**Article 111**).

---

### B. Joint Sitting of Both Houses (Article 108)
- Invoked by the President when a non-Money, non-Constitutional Amendment bill faces a **legislative deadlock**:
  1. If the bill is rejected by the other House; or
  2. If the Houses have finally disagreed as to the amendments to be made; or
  3. If more than **six months** elapse from the date of reception by the other House without the bill being passed.
- **Rules of Voting**: Passed by a **Simple Majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting**.
- **Numerical Advantage**: Because Lok Sabha has 543 members compared to Rajya Sabha's 245, the Lok Sabha's will virtually always prevail.
- *Historic Joint Sittings Held in India*:
  1. **Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1961**
  2. **Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1978**
  3. **Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002**

### Charged Expenditure vs. Made Expenditure:
- **Expenditure Charged upon Consolidated Fund of India (Article 112(3))**: Non-votable by Parliament; can only be discussed. Includes salaries and allowances of President, Chairman/Deputy Chairman RS, Speaker/Deputy Speaker LS, Supreme Court judges, CAG, UPSC Chairman/members, and debt charges of the Government of India.
- **Expenditure Made from Consolidated Fund**: Votable by the Lok Sabha in the form of Demands for Grants.

### Cut Motions: The Accountability Weapons
1. **Policy Disapproval Cut**: States that the amount of the demand be **reduced to ₹1**. (Represents disapproval of the underlying government policy; if passed, government must resign).
2. **Economy Cut**: States that the amount of the demand be **reduced by a specified amount** (e.g., reduced by ₹100 crore).
3. **Token Cut**: States that the amount of the demand be **reduced by ₹100**. (Airs a specific grievance within the sphere of government responsibility).

> [!CAUTION]
> **The Guillotine Mechanism**:
> On the last of the days allotted for the discussion and voting on Demands for Grants, the Speaker puts every remaining demand to vote at the appointed hour—whether discussed or not. This closing device is termed the **Guillotine**.

> - Established in 1993 (expanded to 24 committees in 2004).

## 9. Parliamentary Privileges (Article 105)

Parliamentary privileges are special rights, immunities, and exemptions enjoyed by the two Houses of Parliament, their committees, and their members:
1. **Individual Privileges**:
   - **Freedom of Speech in Parliament (Article 105(1))**: No member is liable to any proceedings in any court in respect of anything said or any vote given by him in Parliament or any committee thereof (**Article 105(2)**). This freedom is absolute and exempt from Article 19(2) restrictions, subject only to the constitutional rules of procedure and the ban on discussing the conduct of Supreme Court/High Court judges (**Article 121**).
   - **Immunity from Arrest**: Cannot be arrested in **civil cases** during the continuance of a session and **40 days before and 40 days after** the session. (This immunity does **not** apply to criminal offences or preventive detention!).
   - **Exemption from Jury Service**: Refusal to give evidence or appear as a witness in court when Parliament is in session.
2. **Collective Privileges of the House**:
   - Right to publish reports, debates, and proceedings, and right to prohibit others from publishing them.
   - Right to exclude strangers and hold secret sittings.
   - Right to punish members and outsiders for **breach of privilege or contempt of the House** (reprimand, admonition, or imprisonment, e.g., expulsion upheld in *Raja Ram Pal v. Hon'ble Speaker, 2007*).

> * **Empirical Decline in Parliamentary Sittings (Dr. M.R. Madhavan, *Oxford Handbook of Indian Politics*)**:
>   - Analysis of seven decades of parliamentary proceedings by PRS Legislative Research reveals a systemic contraction in legislative deliberation:
>     - **Annual Sittings**: The 1st Lok Sabha (1952–1957) sat for an average of **135 days per year**. By the 16th Lok Sabha (2014–2019), this plummeted to **66 days**, and the 17th Lok Sabha (2019–2024) averaged just **55 days per year**.
>     - **Bypassing the Committee System**: Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs) were established in 1993 to ensure non-partisan, clause-by-clause scrutiny. In the 15th Lok Sabha (2009–2014), **71% of bills were referred to committees**. In the 16th Lok Sabha, this dropped to **27%**, and in the 17th Lok Sabha, it cratered to **only 16%**. Over 80% of legislation was passed without committee review.
>     - **Budgetary Scrutiny via Guillotine**: Between 2019 and 2024, nearly **75% to 80% of the Union Budget's Demands for Grants were guillotined** (passed without any line-item parliamentary debate).
> * **The De-Federalization of the Rajya Sabha (Prof. Sandeep Shastri)**:
>   - Shastri analyzes the impact of the **Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 2003**, which removed the mandatory requirement that a candidate for Rajya Sabha must be an "ordinary resident" of the State they represent (upheld by the Supreme Court in *Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India, 2006*).
>   - **The Consequence**: The Upper House has increasingly ceased to function as an authentic "Council of States" articulating regional interests. Instead, it has been transformed into a national party-political chamber, frequently utilized by parties to rehabilitate senior leaders who lost direct Lok Sabha elections.

    and the Estimates Committee?
A2. The PAC consists of 22 members (15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha). The Estimates Committee 
    consists of 30 members drawn EXCLUSIVELY from the Lok Sabha; the Rajya Sabha has zero representation 
    on the Estimates Committee.

Q3. What is the maximum time period the Rajya Sabha can detain a Money Bill, and what happens if it 
    takes no action within that timeframe?
A3. Exactly 14 days from receipt. If the Rajya Sabha does not return the Money Bill within 14 days, 
    the Bill is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form originally passed by the Lok Sabha.

Q4. Explain the key legal impact of the 104th Constitutional Amendment Act 2019 on the composition 
    of the Lok Sabha.
A4. It discontinued the provision for nomination of 2 Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha by the 
    President under Article 331, reducing the maximum statutory strength of the Lok Sabha from 552 to 550.

Q5. What is the "Guillotine" in parliamentary financial procedure?
A5. On the concluding day of the time allotted for voting on Demands for Grants, the Speaker puts all 
    undiscussed and remaining demands to vote simultaneously without further debate, bringing the 
    voting process to an abrupt close.
