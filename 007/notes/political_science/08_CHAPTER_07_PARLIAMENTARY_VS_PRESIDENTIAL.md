## 7.2 Features of the Parliamentary System (The Westminster Architecture)

Also known as the **Cabinet Government**, **Responsible Government**, or the **Westminster Model**, the parliamentary system operates on eight interrelated institutional features:

### 1. Dual Executive (Nominal vs. Real Executive)
* **De Jure (Titular / Nominal) Executive**: The **President of India** is the constitutional Head of State. All executive actions are formally taken in his name (Article 77), but he exercises powers strictly on the binding advice of the Council of Ministers (**Article 74(1)**).
* **De Facto (Real) Executive**: The **Prime Minister** is the Head of Government. He exercises actual administrative, executive, and policy authority.
* **The Judicial Seal (*Ram Jawaya Kapur 1955* & *Shamsher Singh 1974*)**: The Supreme Court affirmed that the President or Governor is a constitutional head bound by ministerial advice, and our Constitution embodies the English parliamentary convention that the real power resides in the elected Cabinet.

### 2. Majority Party Rule
* The political party that secures a majority of seats in the **Lok Sabha** forms the government.
* The leader of the majority party is appointed as the Prime Minister by the President under **Article 75(1)**; other ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.
* In the event of a hung parliament, a coalition of parties or the single largest party commanding majority support is invited.

### 3. Collective Responsibility (The Bedrock Principle)
* **Article 75(3)** contains the constitutional anchor of parliamentary democracy:  
  > *"The Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha)."*
* **The Doctrine of Solidarity**: The ministry functions as a joint team. If the Lok Sabha passes a **No-Confidence Motion**, all ministers (including ministers belonging to the Rajya Sabha) must resign simultaneously.
* As Lord Salisbury formulated: *"Ministers who disagree with a cabinet decision must either resign or publicly defend it; they cannot criticize a cabinet decision while retaining ministerial office."*

### 4. Political Homogeneity
* Members of the Council of Ministers usually belong to the same political party, sharing a common ideological and legislative agenda. In coalitions, ministers are bound by a mutually agreed **Common Minimum Programme (CMP)**.

### 5. Double Membership (Executive-Legislature Fusion)
* Every minister must be a member of both the **Legislature** and the **Executive**.
* **The 6-Month Proviso (Article 75(5))**: A person who is not a member of either House of Parliament can be appointed as a minister, but **ceases to be a minister if within a period of six consecutive months they do not obtain membership in either the Lok Sabha or the Rajya Sabha**.

### 6. Leadership of the Prime Minister
* Described as *Primus Inter Pares* (first among equals) and *Inter Stellas Luna Minores* (a moon among lesser stars), the Prime Minister is the keystone of the Cabinet arch.
* He presides over Cabinet meetings, allocates portfolios, resolves inter-ministerial conflicts, and advises the President on ministerial appointments or dismissals.

### 7. Dissolution of the Lower House
* The Prime Minister possesses the constitutional authority to advise the President to dissolve the Lok Sabha before the expiry of its 5-year term, forcing fresh general elections.
* This prerogative serves as a vital executive counter-check against parliamentary irresponsibility.

### 8. Secrecy of Procedure
* Ministers operate under an oath of secrecy administered by the President before assuming office (Third Schedule), legally barring them from divulging cabinet deliberations, confidential state papers, and policy discussions.

## 7.4 Why India Adopted the Parliamentary System: The Four Reasons

In the Constituent Assembly, members debated whether to adopt the American presidential system to ensure political stability. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and the Drafting Committee rejected the presidential model for four decisive historical and institutional reasons:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 THE FOUR DRIVERS OF THE PARLIAMENTARY CHOICE                                │
├───────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Historical Institutional       │ India had developed over 70 years of administrative     │
│    Familiarity                    │ familiarity with parliamentary machinery under the      │
│                                   │ Indian Councils Acts and GoI Acts of 1919 and 1935.     │
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Preference for Responsibility  │ Dr. Ambedkar famously argued that a young, diverse      │
│    Over Stability                 │ nation could not afford an unaccountable executive for  │
│                                   │ 4 years. Daily legislative scrutiny was prioritized.    │
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Prevention of Legislative-     │ The presidential model frequently produces deadlocks    │
│    Executive Friction             │ when the President belongs to one party and Congress    │
│                                   │ to another. India needed quick socio-economic reform.   │
├───────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Pluralistic & Heterogeneous    │ India’s vast ethnic, linguistic, and religious diversity│
│    Representation                 │ could never be represented by a single US-style         │
│                                   │ President. A composite Cabinet accommodates all regions.│
└───────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Dr. Ambedkar's Definitive Formulation (4 November 1948):
> *"A democratic executive must satisfy two conditions: it must be a **stable executive** and it must be a **responsible executive**. Unfortunately, it has not been possible to devise a system which can ensure both in equal degree. You can have a system which gives you more stability but less responsibility (the American Presidential model), or you can have a system which gives you more responsibility but less stability (the British Parliamentary model). The Draft Constitution, in recommending the parliamentary system, has **preferred the system of greater responsibility to the system of greater stability**."*

## 7.6 The Real Nature of Ministerial Advice: The Article 74 Trajectory

One of the most critical legal mechanics of the parliamentary executive is the binding nature of the Council of Ministers' advice upon the President:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    THE EVOLUTION OF PRESIDENTIAL BINDING ADVICE                             │
├─────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Original 1949     │ *"There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the│
│ Formulation**       │ head to aid and advise the President."* (Did not explicitly say       │
│                     │ advice was legally binding; operated on British conventions).         │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **42nd Amendment    │ Made advice **ABSOLUTELY BINDING**: *"The President shall, in the    │
│ (1976)**            │ exercise of his functions, act in accordance with such advice."*      │
├─────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **44th Amendment    │ Introduced the **Reconsideration Proviso**: Empowered the President  │
│ (1978)**            │ to require the Council of Ministers to **reconsider such advice once**.│
│                     │ However, the President **MUST accept the advice after                 │
│                     │ reconsideration**, whether altered or reaffirmed!                     │
└─────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **The Shield of Article 74(2)**: *"The question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the President shall not be inquired into in any court."*  
  *(Protects the confidentiality and internal deliberations of the cabinet from judicial scrutiny, affirmed in S.R. Bommai 1994, though the material on which advice is based remains reviewable).*

## 7.7 Publication Callout Boxes: Strategic Matrices & Warnings

### [CONSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM: THE INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBILITY OF MINISTERS]
> **How does Individual Responsibility (Article 75(2)) interact with Collective Responsibility (Article 75(3))?**  
> Under **Article 75(2)**, ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President**. This embodies the principle of **Individual Responsibility**.  
> In constitutional reality, the "pleasure of the President" means the **pleasure of the Prime Minister**. If the Prime Minister loses confidence in an individual minister, he can ask the minister to resign or advise the President to dismiss him. By dismissing an errant or corrupt minister, the Prime Minister safeguards the collective responsibility of the entire Cabinet before the Lok Sabha.

---

### [DISTINCTION MATRIX: DE JURE VS. DE FACTO EXECUTIVE]
| Parameter | De Jure Executive (President) | De Facto Executive (Prime Minister) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Constitutional Title** | Head of the Indian State (First Citizen). | Head of the Government of India. |
| **Formal Powers** | Supreme Commander of Defence Forces; Promulgates ordinances; Pardons offenses. | Advises military action; Drafts ordinances; Advises clemency decisions. |
| **Operational Reality** | Exercises powers strictly on ministerial advice (Art 74). | Directs the administrative, fiscal, and legislative machinery of the nation. |

### [EXAM TRAP & WARNING: HIGH-YIELD PITFALLS FOR CANDIDATES]
> ⚠️ **Trap 1: "The Prime Minister of India must be a member of the Lok Sabha."**  
> **FALSE**: The PM can be a member of **either House** of Parliament (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha). Indira Gandhi and Manmohan Singh were Rajya Sabha MPs.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 2: "Ministers in India are legally responsible for royal/presidential orders, like in the UK."**  
> **FALSE**: India has **NO system of legal responsibility of ministers**. Indian presidential orders do not require a minister's countersignature, and courts cannot inquire into ministerial advice under Article 74(2).
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 3: "A non-MP cannot be appointed as Prime Minister or Minister."**  
> **FALSE**: A person who is not an MP can be appointed under **Article 75(5)**, but must secure membership in either House within **6 consecutive months**.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 4: "The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to Parliament."**  
> **FALSE**: Under Article 75(3), the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible **EXCLUSIVELY to the Lok Sabha (House of the People)**, NOT to Parliament as a whole!
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 5: "The President can reject ministerial advice after reconsideration under the 44th Amendment."**  
> **FALSE**: The President can send advice back for reconsideration **ONLY ONCE**. If the Cabinet reiterates the advice, the President **MUST accept it**.

## 7.8 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

1. **What is the foundational institutional difference between the Parliamentary model and the Presidential model?**  
   *Answer*: The Parliamentary model is based on the **Fusion of Powers** and continuous executive accountability to the legislature; the Presidential model is based on the **Strict Separation of Powers** and fixed executive stability.

2. **Why did Dr. B.R. Ambedkar argue that the Constituent Assembly preferred the British system over the American system?**  
   *Answer*: The framers preferred a system offering greater **responsibility and continuous accountability** over a system offering greater stability with less accountability.

3. **To which specific legislative body is the Council of Ministers collectively responsible under Article 75(3)?**  
   *Answer*: Exclusively to the **Lok Sabha (House of the People)**.

4. **Can a member of the Rajya Sabha be appointed as Prime Minister of India? Cite historical examples.**  
   *Answer*: Yes. Examples include Indira Gandhi (1966), H.D. Deve Gowda (1996), I.K. Gujral (1997), and Dr. Manmohan Singh (2004, 2009).

5. **Explain the meaning of "Individual Responsibility" under Article 75(2).**  
   *Answer*: Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President (in practice, the Prime Minister), allowing the PM to dismiss an individual minister even if the cabinet retains the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

6. **How did the 42nd and 44th Amendments modify the nature of presidential advice under Article 74(1)?**  
   *Answer*: The 42nd Amendment made ministerial advice strictly binding; the 44th Amendment allowed the President to return advice for reconsideration once, but mandated acceptance if reaffirmed.

7. **Does the Indian Constitution recognize the British principle "The King can do no wrong" via ministerial legal responsibility?**  
   *Answer*: No. India has no system of legal responsibility of ministers; official orders do not require countersignatures, and Article 74(2) bars courts from inquiring into advice tendered.

8. **What is a "Shadow Cabinet" and does it exist in the Indian constitutional system?**  
   *Answer*: A Shadow Cabinet is an official opposition body formed in the UK to scrutinize government portfolios and prepare for office; it does not exist in India.

9. **What happens if a minister fails to get elected to either House of Parliament within six months of appointment?**  
   *Answer*: Under Article 75(5), they immediately cease to be a minister.

10. **In which landmark cases did the Supreme Court affirm that the President is a constitutional head bound by ministerial advice?**  
    *Answer*: *Ram Jawaya Kapur v. State of Punjab (1955)* and *Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)*.
