## 1. The Party System in India: Typology & Structural Dynamics

Political parties are the transmission belts of representative democracy, aggregating interests and structuring legislative power.
- **Constitutional Recognition**: Political parties had **no mention in the original Constitution of 1950**. They received explicit constitutional recognition only in **1985 through the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act** inserting the **Tenth Schedule**.
- **Typology of Party Systems**:
  1. *One-Party Dominant System (1952–1967)*: Rajni Kothari famously characterized this as the **"Congress System"**—a broad umbrella consensus party with competitive factions.
  2. *Bipolar / Multi-Party Fragmentation (1967–1989)*: Emergence of regional parties; decline of national hegemony.
  3. *Era of Coalition Governments (1989–2014)*: Structural necessity of pre-poll and post-poll alliances (NDA, UPA).
  4. *One-Party Dominant Multi-Party System (2014–Present)*: Resurgence of single-party parliamentary majority within a multi-party federal matrix.

## 2. Recognition of Political Parties: National vs. State Parties

Under the *Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968*, the Election Commission of India registers political parties (§29A RPA 1951) and accords them recognized status based on objective electoral performance:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   CRITERIA FOR RECOGNITION AS A NATIONAL PARTY                                   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ A registered political party is recognized as a NATIONAL PARTY if it fulfills ANY ONE:           │
│ 1. Secures at least SIX PERCENT (6%) OF VALID VOTES POLLED in any FOUR OR MORE STATES at a       │
│    general election to the Lok Sabha or State Legislative Assembly, AND in addition wins at least│
│    FOUR (4) SEATS IN THE LOK SABHA from any State or States; OR                                  │
│ 2. Wins at least TWO PERCENT (2%) OF SEATS IN THE LOK SABHA (i.e., 11 Seats in 543) at a general │
│    election, and these members are elected from at least THREE (3) DIFFERENT STATES; OR          │
│ 3. Is recognized as a STATE PARTY in at least FOUR (4) STATES.                                   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   CRITERIA FOR RECOGNITION AS A STATE PARTY                                      │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ A registered political party is recognized as a STATE PARTY if it fulfills ANY ONE:              │
│ 1. Secures ≥ 6% of valid votes polled in the State in an Assembly election + wins at least   │
│    TWO (2) SEATS in the State Legislative Assembly; OR                                           │
│ 2. Secures ≥ 6% of valid votes polled in the State in a Lok Sabha election + wins at least    │
│    ONE (1) SEAT in the Lok Sabha from that State; OR                                             │
│ 3. Wins at least THREE PERCENT (3%) OF TOTAL SEATS in the State Legislative Assembly, or         │
│    THREE (3) SEATS, whichever is more; OR                                                        │
│ 4. Wins at least ONE (1) SEAT in the Lok Sabha for every 25 seats allotted to the State; OR      │
│ 5. Secures at least EIGHT PERCENT (8%) OF TOTAL VALID VOTES POLLED in the State at a general     │
│    election to the Lok Sabha or State Assembly (Liberalized criterion introduced in 2011).       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Statutory Privileges of Recognized Parties:
1. **Exclusive Allotment of Symbol**: Reserved symbol across India (for National parties) or across the State (for State parties).
2. **Star Campaigners**: Entitled to nominate up to **40 star campaigners** whose travel expenses are not added to candidate election expenses (unrecognized parties get only 20).
3. **Public Broadcasting**: Free airtime on state-owned electronic media (Doordarshan & All India Radio) during general elections.
4. **Electoral Rolls**: Entitled to receive two free copies of electoral rolls from the ECI.

## 3. The Anti-Defection Law: The Tenth Schedule Architecture

### A. Genesis: The "Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram" Syndrome
In the late 1960s, rampant horse-trading of legislators destabilized state governments. In 1967, a Haryana MLA named Gaya Lal changed his party allegiance three times in a single day, giving birth to the political phrase *"Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram"*.
To arrest this unprincipled opportunism, Parliament enacted the **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985**, amending Articles 101, 102, 190, and 191, and adding the **Tenth Schedule**.

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP: The Nominated Member 6-Month Rule**
> An independently elected member is disqualified if he joins a political party at **any time** after election.
> A nominated member is disqualified **only if he joins a political party after 6 months**! He has a legal window of 6 months to join any party without disqualification.

---

### C. Exemptions from Disqualification (Paragraph 4 & 5)
1. **The Merger Exemption (Paragraph 4)**:
   A member is not disqualified if his original political party merges with another political party, provided that **NOT LESS THAN TWO-THIRDS (2/3rd) OF THE MEMBERS of the legislative party agree to such merger**.
   - > [!IMPORTANT]
     > **Deletion of the One-Third (1/3rd) Split Rule**:
     > Originally, Paragraph 3 exempted a defection if one-third (1/3rd) of the members split from the party.
     > The **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 DELETED Paragraph 3 entirely**! Today, a "split" is no longer recognized. Only a **two-thirds merger** provides immunity from disqualification.
2. **Presiding Officer Exemption (Paragraph 5)**:
   A person elected as Speaker or Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha / State Assembly, or Chairman / Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha / Legislative Council is exempt from disqualification if they resign from their party on assumption of office and do not rejoin while holding office.

## 4. The Deciding Authority & Landmark Jurisprudence: *Kihoto Hollohan (1992)*

### A. The Authority of the Presiding Officer (Paragraph 6)
- Any question regarding disqualification under the Tenth Schedule is decided **exclusively by the Chairman or Speaker of the House**.
- The decision was originally declared by Paragraph 7 to be final and immune from court jurisdiction.

---

### B. Landmark Ruling: *Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)*
A 5-judge Constitution Bench settled the constitutional contours of the Tenth Schedule:
1. **Paragraph 7 Struck Down**: Paragraph 7 (which excluded judicial review) was struck down because it affected the powers of High Courts (Art 226) and Supreme Court (Arts 136, 32) without state ratification under Article 368(2).
2. **The Speaker as a Tribunal**: While deciding questions under the Tenth Schedule, the Speaker or Chairman functions as a **Tribunal** exercising judicial power.
3. **Availability of Judicial Review**: The decision of the Presiding Officer is **subject to judicial review** on grounds of:
   - *Mala fides* (bad faith)
   - Perversity
   - Violation of the principles of natural justice
   - Patent illegality or lack of jurisdiction.
4. **No Interlocutory Interference**: Courts will **not intervene at an interlocutory stage** (before the Speaker has made a final determination).

## 6. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. Under what conditions is a nominated member of Parliament disqualified under the Tenth Schedule?
A1. Under Paragraph 2(3), a nominated member is disqualified if he joins any political party AFTER 
    THE EXPIRY OF SIX (6) MONTHS from the date on which he takes his seat. If he joins within the 
    first 6 months, he is not disqualified.

Q2. What fundamental change did the 91st Constitutional Amendment Act 2003 introduce into the 
    merger/split provisions of the Tenth Schedule?
A2. It completely DELETED Paragraph 3 (which allowed 1/3rd split exemption) and made a TWO-THIRDS (2/3rd) 
    merger under Paragraph 4 the sole permissible defense against disqualification on grounds of party merger.

Q3. What was the central holding of the 5-judge bench in Kihoto Hollohan (1992) regarding the Speaker's 
    decisions under the Tenth Schedule?
A3. The court held that the Speaker functions as a "Tribunal" and that Paragraph 7 (barring judicial 
    review) was unconstitutional. The Speaker's decision is subject to judicial review under Articles 
    226 and 136 on grounds of mala fides, perversity, or violation of natural justice, but courts 
    will not intervene at an interlocutory stage.

Q4. What is the criteria for a political party to be recognized as a National Party based strictly 
    on Lok Sabha seats?
A4. The party must win at least TWO PERCENT (2%) of the total seats in the Lok Sabha (11 seats in 543) 
    at a general election, and these elected members must come from at least THREE (3) DIFFERENT STATES.

Q5. Can an independent MP join a political party 2 years after his election without losing his seat?
A5. No. Under Paragraph 2(2) of the Tenth Schedule, an independent member stands disqualified if he 
    joins ANY political party at any time after his election.
```
