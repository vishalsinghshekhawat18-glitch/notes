## 1. Constitutional Epistemic Anchor: The High Court Architecture

In India's single integrated judicial pyramid, the **High Court** operates directly below the Supreme Court, serving as the constitutional head of the judicial administration within a State.
- **Historical Origin**: The institution of the High Court originated in 1862 under the *Indian High Courts Act, 1861*, which established High Courts at **Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras**. A fourth High Court was set up at **Allahabad in 1866**.
- **Article 214**: *"There shall be a High Court for each State."*
- **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**: Authorized Parliament to establish a **Common High Court** for two or more States or Union Territories (**Article 231**).
- **Current Count**: There are **25 High Courts** in India.
  - In 2019, separate High Courts were established for **Andhra Pradesh** (at Amaravati) and **Telangana** (at Hyderabad).
  - Only three Union Territories have their own High Courts or common High Courts with states: Delhi has its own High Court (established 1966), Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh share a common High Court, and Puducherry/Chandigarh/Lakshadweep/Andaman fall under neighboring State High Courts.

## 2. Appointment, Qualifications & Tenure of High Court Judges

### A. Strength & Appointment (Articles 216 & 217)
- **Strength Determination**: Unlike the Supreme Court where Parliament fixes the sanctioned strength by law, the Constitution leaves the strength of a High Court flexible:
  $$\text{Strength of a High Court is determined by the } \mathbf{PRESIDENT\text{ OF INDIA}} \text{ based on workload.}$$
- **Mode of Appointment (Article 217(1))**:
  - Appointed by the **President of India**.
  - **Chief Justice of High Court**: Appointed after consultation with the **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** and the **Governor of the State**.
  - **Other High Court Judges**: Appointed after consultation with the CJI, the Governor of the State, and the **Chief Justice of the concerned High Court**.
- **The High Court Collegium Mechanism**:
  1. Proposal initiates from the **High Court Collegium** (Chief Justice of HC + 2 senior-most HC judges).
  2. Forwarded to the Governor/Chief Minister, who transmits it to the Union Law Minister.
  3. The Union Law Minister submits it to the **Supreme Court Collegium** (CJI + 2 senior-most Supreme Court judges).
  4. The CJI consults two Supreme Court colleagues conversant with that particular High Court before tendering recommendations to the President.

---

### B. Qualifications for Appointment (Article 217(2))
A person must be a **Citizen of India**, and must satisfy **ANY ONE** of the following:
1. Has for at least **ten years held a judicial office** in the territory of India; **OR**
2. Has for at least **ten years been an advocate of a High Court** (or of two or more such courts in succession).
- > [!WARNING]
  > **EXAM TRAP: No "Distinguished Jurist" for High Courts**
  > While a distinguished jurist can be appointed as a Supreme Court Judge under Article 124(3)(c), there is **NO provision for appointing a distinguished jurist to a High Court** under Article 217(2)!

---

### C. Oath, Tenure, Removal & Transfer (Articles 217, 219, 222)
- **Oath (Article 219)**: Administered by the **Governor of the State** (or person appointed by him).
- **Tenure**: Retires at the age of **62 years** (raised from 60 to 62 by the 15th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1963). (Supreme Court retirement age is 65).
- **Resignation**: Addressed in writing to the **President of India** (NOT the Governor!).
- **Removal**: Removed by the President in the **same manner and on the same grounds (proved misbehaviour or incapacity) as a Supreme Court Judge** (Article 217(1) Proviso (b) cross-referencing Article 124(4)).
- **Transfer of High Court Judges (Article 222)**:
  - President transfers a judge after consultation with the CJI.
  - In the *Third Judges Case (1998)*, SC ruled that CJI must consult: (1) Collegium of 4 senior-most SC judges, (2) CJ of the High Court from which the judge is being transferred, and (3) CJ of the High Court to which the judge is being transferred.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Supervisory Jurisdiction under Article 227**:
> The High Court has **superintendence over all courts and tribunals** throughout the territories in relation to which it exercises jurisdiction.
> - Covers both **administrative and judicial superintendence**.
> - Can be exercised *suo motu* (not dependent on appeal).
> - **The Sole Exclusion (Article 227(4))**: Does not apply to courts or tribunals constituted by or under any law relating to the **Armed Forces (Courts Martial)**.

### Appointments in the Subordinate Judiciary:
1. **District Judges (Article 233)**:
   - Appointed, posted, and promoted by the **Governor of the State in consultation with the High Court**.
   - *Eligibility for Direct Recruitment*: A person who has been an advocate or a pleader for **not less than seven (7) years** and is recommended by the High Court.
2. **Other Judicial Officers (Article 234)**:
   - Appointed by the **Governor** in accordance with rules made by him after consultation with the **State Public Service Commission (SPSC)** and the **High Court**.
3. **Control over Subordinate Courts (Article 235)**:
   - The posting, promotion, grant of leave, and disciplinary control over judicial officers is vested **exclusively in the High Court** to ensure separation of powers from the executive.

## 5. Alternative Dispute Resolution: NALSA & Lok Adalats

### A. The Constitutional Mandate: Article 39A
Inserted by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**, Article 39A directs the State to secure that the operation of the legal system promotes justice on a basis of equal opportunity, and to provide **free legal aid** by suitable legislation or schemes.

---

### B. The Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987
Established a statutory hierarchy to deliver free and competent legal services to the underprivileged:
1. **National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)**:
   - **Patron-in-Chief**: Chief Justice of India.
   - **Executive Chairman**: A serving or retired Judge of the Supreme Court appointed by the President.
2. **State Legal Services Authority (SLSA)**:
   - Headed by the **Chief Justice of the High Court** as Patron-in-Chief.
   - Executive Chairman is a serving or retired High Court Judge nominated by the Governor.
3. **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)**:
   - Headed by the **District Judge** as Chairman.
4. **Taluk Legal Services Committee**:
   - Headed by a Senior Civil Judge.

---

### C. Lok Adalats (People's Courts)
Lok Adalats are statutory alternative dispute resolution forums established under Chapter VI of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.
- **Composition**: Presiding Officer (sitting or retired judicial officer) + a practicing advocate + a social worker.
- **Jurisdiction**:
  1. Any case pending before any court.
  2. Any dispute at the pre-litigation stage.
  - *Subject Matters Covered*: Matrimonial disputes, land partition, motor accident claims, bank recovery, labour disputes, and **compoundable criminal offences**. (Non-compoundable criminal offences **cannot** be settled by Lok Adalats!).

### D. Permanent Lok Adalats (2002 Amendment)
- Established under **Section 22B** of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (inserted by 2002 Amendment).
- Exclusively dedicated to **Public Utility Services (PUS)**: postal, telegraph, telephone, power, light, water supply, public conservancy, sanitation, hospitals, passenger/goods transport, insurance.
- **Pecuniary Jurisdiction**: Up to **₹1 Crore** (enhanced from earlier limits).
- **Adjudicatory Power**: If the parties fail to reach an amicable settlement, the Permanent Lok Adalat has the power to **decide the dispute on merits** (unlike ordinary Lok Adalats which can only mediate).
- Its award is final, binding, and non-appealable.

## 6. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. How is the sanctioned strength of a High Court determined under the Constitution, and how does 
    it differ from the Supreme Court?
A1. The strength of a High Court is determined by the PRESIDENT OF INDIA based on workload under 
    Article 216. In contrast, the strength of the Supreme Court is determined by PARLIAMENT BY LAW 
    under Article 124(1).

Q2. What is the retirement age of a High Court judge, and which constitutional amendment raised it 
    from 60 to 62?
A2. 62 years of age. It was increased from 60 to 62 by the 15th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1963.

Q3. In what key respect is the writ jurisdiction of a High Court under Article 226 broader than that 
    of the Supreme Court under Article 32?
A3. Under Article 32, the Supreme Court can issue writs ONLY for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights 
    (Part III). Under Article 226, the High Court can issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental 
    Rights AND "FOR ANY OTHER PURPOSE" (ordinary legal rights).

Q4. Can a death sentence awarded by a Sessions Judge be executed immediately?
A4. No. Under Section 366 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (and Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita), 
    any death sentence passed by a Sessions Court MUST BE CONFIRMED BY THE HIGH COURT before it can 
    be executed.

Q5. Can an appeal be filed in a High Court against the award passed by a statutory Lok Adalat?
A5. No. Under Section 21 of the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987, the award of a Lok Adalat is 
    deemed a civil court decree and is final and binding on all parties; NO APPEAL lies to any court 
    against it.
```
