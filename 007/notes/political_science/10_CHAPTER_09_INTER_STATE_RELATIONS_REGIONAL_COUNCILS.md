## 9.1 Epistemic Foundations: Horizontal Federalism

While Centre-State relations govern **Vertical Federalism**, the stability of the Indian Union depends equally on **Horizontal Federalism**—mechanisms that regulate coordination, resolve resource disputes, and foster cooperative economic integration among the constituent States themselves.

## 9.2 Inter-State Water Disputes (Article 262)

Inter-state river water disputes represent the most emotionally charged and politically contentious friction point in Indian federalism. Because river systems cross political borders, the constitutional framers recognized that ordinary adversarial litigation in courtrooms was ill-suited to resolve riparian conflicts.

### 1. The Constitutional Scheme of Article 262
Article 262 provides a unique two-fold statutory authorization:
* **Article 262(1)**: Parliament may by law provide for the **adjudication of any dispute or complaint** with respect to the use, distribution, or control of the waters of, or in, any **inter-State river or river valley**.
* **Article 262(2) (The Absolute Court Exclusion)**:  
  > *"Notwithstanding anything in this Constitution, Parliament may by law provide that **NEITHER the Supreme Court NOR any other court shall exercise jurisdiction** in respect of any such dispute or complaint."*

---

### 2. Parliamentary Enactments under Article 262
Acting under Article 262, Parliament enacted two major statutes in 1956:

```
└─────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 3. Key Provisions of the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956:
1. **Tribunal Constitution**: Nominated by the **Chief Justice of India**, consisting of a sitting Judge of the Supreme Court as Chairman and two other sitting or retired judges of the SC or High Courts.
2. **Statutory Finality**: The decision of the Tribunal has the **same force as an order or decree of the Supreme Court**.
3. **Jurisdiction Bar**: Section 11 of the Act bars the Supreme Court and all other courts from entertaining any suit or application in respect of any water dispute referred to a tribunal.
4. **The Article 136 Window (*State of Karnataka v. State of Tamil Nadu, 2018*)**:  
   While original suits under Article 131 are barred by Article 262(2), the Supreme Court ruled that the apex court's extraordinary jurisdiction under **Article 136 (Special Leave Petition)** cannot be ousted. The Supreme Court maintains supervisory constitutional review over the final awards of water tribunals!

## 9.3 The Inter-State Council (Article 263)

Article 263 empowers the **President** to establish an Inter-State Council if at any time it appears that the public interests would be served by its creation.

### 1. Constitutional Mandate (Article 263):
The President defines the nature of duties to be performed by the Council, which include:
* (a) Inquiring into and advising upon disputes which may have arisen between States.
* (b) Investigating and discussing subjects in which some or all of the States, or the Union and one or more States, have a common interest.
* (c) Making recommendations upon any such subject, and in particular, recommendations for the **better coordination of policy and action**.
* **Crucial Nature**: The Inter-State Council's function is **purely advisory and consultative**. Unlike the Supreme Court under Article 131 (which delivers legally binding decrees on federal disputes), the Council’s advice is not binding.

---

### 2. Establishment on Sarkaria Commission Recommendation (1990)
Following the recommendations of the **Sarkaria Commission (1988)**, the V.P. Singh Government formally established the **Inter-State Council by a Presidential Order in May 1990**.


* **Meeting Frequency**: The Council is mandated to meet **at least thrice a year**. All deliberations are held in camera, and decisions are reached by consensus.

## 9.4 Full Faith and Credit Clause (Article 261)

Under **Article 261**, full faith and credit shall be given throughout the territory of India to:
1. **Public Acts** (statutes and legislative records of the Union and every State).
2. **Records** (official documents and registers).
3. **Judicial Proceedings** (judgments, orders, and decrees of courts).

* **Civil Decrees vs. Criminal Judgments (Article 261(3))**:
  * Final judgments or orders delivered by **Civil Courts** in any part of India are **directly executable anywhere within India** without requiring a fresh suit.
  * **Critical Exception**: This rule **applies ONLY to civil judgments**. It **does NOT apply to penal or criminal judgments**; an individual convicted of a crime in one state cannot be automatically imprisoned by the civil courts of another state without following formal statutory extradition and criminal procedure codes.

## 9.6 Statutory Regional Bodies: The Zonal Councils

Zonal Councils are **STATUTORY BODIES**, not constitutional bodies. They were established under **Part III of the States Reorganisation Act of 1956** to promote regional cooperation, habituate states to working together, and mitigate regional linguistic discord post-1956.

```
├─────────────────┼───────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **1. Northern** │ New Delhi │ Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan, NCT Delhi,      │

```

### Organizational Structure of Zonal Councils:
* **Common Chairman**: The **Union Home Minister** acts as the common Chairman of all five Zonal Councils.
* **Vice-Chairman**: The **Chief Ministers** of the member states of the zone act as Vice-Chairman by **annual rotation**.
* **Members**: Two other ministers from each member state (nominated by the Governor), and two members from each Union Territory in the zone.
* **Advisors (Without Voting Rights)**: A person nominated by NITI Aayog, Chief Secretaries of member states, and Development Commissioners.
* **Functions**: Purely **advisory bodies** on matters of economic and social planning, border disputes, linguistic minorities, and inter-state transport.

## 9.7 The North Eastern Council (NEC) — Chapter 72 of Laxmikanth 8e

The northeastern states are excluded from the five statutory Zonal Councils under the 1956 Act. Instead, a dedicated regional statutory body was established for the North-East.

### 1. Statutory Genesis:
* Established under a separate statute: The **North Eastern Council Act, 1971**.
* Formally inaugurated on **7 November 1972** at Shillong (Meghalaya), which serves as its permanent headquarters.
* **The Sikkim Addition (2002)**: Originally covering the 7 northeastern states ("Seven Sisters"), the Act was amended by the **North Eastern Council (Amendment) Act, 2002** to formally include **Sikkim as the eighth member state**.

## 9.8 Publication Callout Boxes: Strategic Matrices & Warnings

### [CONSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM: ARTICLE 262 COURT EXCLUSION]
> **Can the Supreme Court hear a dispute regarding Cauvery or Krishna river waters?**  
> Under **Article 262(2)** read with Section 11 of the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956, the Supreme Court is **barred from exercising its original jurisdiction under Article 131** over inter-state water disputes referred to a tribunal.  
> **The Judicial Valve**: As affirmed in *State of Karnataka v. State of Tamil Nadu (2018)*, the Supreme Court **CAN hear appeals against the final tribunal award under Article 136 (Special Leave Petition)**. The constitutional bar under Article 262 excludes ordinary original suits, but cannot extinguish the basic-structure appellate supervisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court!

---

### [DISTINCTION MATRIX: ZONAL COUNCILS VS. INTER-STATE COUNCIL]
| Parameter | Zonal Councils | Inter-State Council |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Nature** | **Statutory Body** (States Reorganisation Act 1956). | **Constitutional Body** (**Article 263**). |
| **Presiding Head** | **Union Home Minister**. | **Prime Minister of India**. |
| **Jurisdictional Scope** | Regional grouping of specific neighbouring states. | Pan-India apex council covering all 28 States and 8 UTs. |
| **North-East Status** | Excluded (handled by statutory North Eastern Council). | All 8 North-Eastern states are permanent members. |

---

### [EXAM TRAP & WARNING: HIGH-YIELD PITFALLS FOR CANDIDATES]
> ⚠️ **Trap 1: "Zonal Councils are constitutional bodies established under Article 263."**  
> **FALSE**: Zonal Councils are **STATUTORY BODIES** created by the States Reorganisation Act, 1956. The Inter-State Council is the constitutional body under Article 263.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 2: "Sikkim is a member of the Eastern Zonal Council."**  
> **FALSE**: Sikkim is NOT a member of any of the 1956 Zonal Councils; it is a member of the **North Eastern Council (NEC)**, added in 2002!
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 3: "The Prime Minister chairs the Zonal Councils."**  
> **FALSE**: The **Union Home Minister** chairs all Zonal Councils (and also chairs the NEC). The Prime Minister chairs the **Inter-State Council**.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 4: "Article 261(3) full faith and credit applies to state criminal convictions."**  
> **FALSE**: It applies **ONLY to civil judgments and decrees**. It does NOT apply to criminal or penal judgments.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 5: "The Inter-State Council can deliver legally binding judgments on disputes between states."**  
> **FALSE**: The Inter-State Council is a **purely advisory and consultative body**. Only the Supreme Court under Article 131 delivers binding judgments on inter-state disputes.

## 9.9 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

1. **Under which article of the Constitution can Parliament exclude the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court in inter-state river water disputes?**  
   *Answer*: **Article 262(2)**.

2. **Can the Supreme Court exercise any jurisdiction over an inter-state river water tribunal award? Cite the constitutional mechanism.**  
   *Answer*: Yes. In *State of Karnataka v. State of Tamil Nadu (2018)*, the SC held it can review tribunal awards under its **Special Leave Petition jurisdiction (Article 136)**.

3. **In which year and on which commission's recommendation was the Inter-State Council formally established under Article 263?**  
   *Answer*: In **May 1990**, on the recommendation of the **Sarkaria Commission (1988)**.

4. **Who serves as the Chairman of the Inter-State Council, and who serves as the Chairman of the Zonal Councils?**  
   *Answer*: The **Prime Minister** chairs the Inter-State Council; the **Union Home Minister** chairs all Zonal Councils.

5. **Name the enactment under which the five original Zonal Councils were created.**  
   *Answer*: **Part III of the States Reorganisation Act of 1956** (they are statutory, not constitutional bodies).

6. **Which eighth state was added to the North Eastern Council via statutory amendment in 2002?**  
   *Answer*: **Sikkim** (via the North Eastern Council (Amendment) Act, 2002).

7. **Where are the permanent headquarters of the Central Zonal Council and the North Eastern Council located?**  
   *Answer*: Central Zonal Council is at **Prayagraj (Allahabad)**; North Eastern Council is at **Shillong (Meghalaya)**.

8. **Explain the limitation on Article 261 regarding the execution of out-of-state court orders.**  
   *Answer*: Article 261(3) provides for direct execution across India for **civil court decrees only**; it does not apply to criminal/penal sentences.

9. **Can a State Government impose discriminatory tariffs against goods imported from neighbouring states under Part XIII?**  
   *Answer*: No. Under Article 304(a), a state can tax imported goods only if similar goods manufactured within the state are taxed at the exact same rate (non-discriminatory taxation).

10. **Who acts as Vice-Chairman of a Zonal Council?**  
    *Answer*: The **Chief Ministers of the member states** of the zone, holding the office by **annual rotation**.
