## 1. Constitutional Epistemic Anchor & Structure

Part VI, Chapter III of the Constitution (Articles 168 to 212) establishes the **State Legislature**. Under **Article 168**, for every State, there shall be a Legislature which consists of the **Governor** and:
- In states with a bicameral legislature: **The Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)** and **The Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)**.
- In states with a unicameral legislature: **The Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)**.

 └───────────────┘                           └───────────────┘                           └───────────────┘

### The 6 Bicameral States in India:
Currently, only **6 States** have a bicameral legislature:
$$\text{Bicameral States (6): } \mathbf{U}\text{ttar Pradesh}, \mathbf{B}\text{ihar}, \mathbf{M}\text{aharashtra}, \mathbf{K}\text{arnataka}, \mathbf{A}\text{ndhra Pradesh}, \mathbf{T}\text{elangana (UB-MKAT)}$$
- *Historical Vector*:
  - Jammu & Kashmir had a Legislative Council until its reorganization in 2019 into Union Territories.
  - Tamil Nadu abolished its Council in 1986.
  - West Bengal abolished its Council in 1969; Punjab in 1970.
  - Andhra Pradesh abolished its Council in 1985 and revived it in 2007.

## 2. Creation and Abolition of Legislative Councils (Article 169)

Article 169 provides a unique constitutional mechanism enabling Parliament to create or abolish a State Legislative Council without invoking the cumbersome Article 368 amendment procedure:

```
                  ARTICLE 169: CREATION / ABOLITION OF LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL
                  
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ STEP 1: RESOLUTION IN STATE LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY                        │
   │   - The Legislative Assembly of the State passes a resolution.          │
   │   - MUST BE PASSED BY A SPECIAL MAJORITY:                               │
   │     (a) Majority of the TOTAL MEMBERSHIP of the Assembly, AND           │
   │     (b) Not less than TWO-THIRDS (2/3) of members PRESENT AND VOTING.   │
   └────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                        │
                                        ▼
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ STEP 2: ACT OF PARLIAMENT                                               │
   │   - Parliament of India may by law create or abolish the Council.       │
   │   - PASSED BY A SIMPLE MAJORITY (Ordinary Legislative Process).         │
   └────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                        │
                                        ▼
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ STEP 3: EXEMPTION FROM ARTICLE 368                                       │
   │   - Article 169(3): No such law passed by Parliament shall be deemed to │
   │     be an amendment of the Constitution for the purposes of Art 368.    │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP: The Parliament-Assembly Dynamic in Article 169**
> The passing of a resolution by the State Legislative Assembly **does not obligate Parliament** to pass a law creating or abolishing the Council. Parliament has absolute discretion to act or refuse to act on the resolution.

## 3. Composition of the Two Houses

### A. The Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha - Article 170)
- **Maximum & Minimum Numerical Limits**:
  - **Maximum Strength**: **500 members**.
  - **Minimum Strength**: **60 members**.
  - *Statutory Exceptions for Small States*:
    - **Goa**: 40 members
    - **Mizoram**: 40 members
    - **Sikkim**: 32 members
    - *(Union Territory of Puducherry has 30 elected members under the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963)*.
- **Electoral Basis**: Direct election from territorial constituencies on the basis of **Universal Adult Suffrage** (**Article 326**).
- **Nomination Abolished**: Article 333 (nomination of 1 Anglo-Indian by the Governor) was **discontinued** by the **104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019**.
- **106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (*Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*)**:
  - Inserts **Article 332A**: Mandates **one-third (33%) reservation** of seats for women in the Legislative Assemblies of every State.
  - Takes effect after the first delimitation post-census.
- **Duration (Article 172(1))**:
  - Normal term: **5 years** from the date of its first meeting.
  - Dissolution: Can be dissolved at any time by the Governor.
  - Extension: Can be extended during a **National Emergency (Article 352)** by Parliament by law for **one year at a time**, up to any length, but not beyond **six months** after the Emergency ceases.

---

### B. The Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad - Article 171)
- **Maximum & Minimum Numerical Limits**:
  $$\text{Maximum Strength} = \frac{1}{3}\text{rd of the Total Strength of the State Legislative Assembly}$$
  $$\text{Minimum Statutory Floor} = \mathbf{40 \text{ Members}}$$
- **Nature of Chamber**: Permanent body, not subject to dissolution; **one-third (1/3rd)** of its members retire every second year. Term of an individual member is **six years**.

> Exactly **5/6th** of the Council members are indirectly elected, and **1/6th** are nominated by the Governor.

## 5. Legislative Procedure: Assembly Primacy & The Dilatory Council

### A. The Fundamental Asymmetry: Parliament vs. State Legislature
In the Union Parliament, the Rajya Sabha enjoys substantial legislative powers: if the Rajya Sabha rejects an ordinary bill, a deadlock occurs which is resolved through a **Joint Sitting under Article 108**.
**In the State Legislature, there is NO provision for a Joint Sitting of both Houses under any circumstances.**
The Legislative Council is designed purely as a **subordinate, dilatory (delaying) chamber**. In lawmaking, the Legislative Assembly's will is absolute.

---

### B. Ordinary Bills: The Dilatory Mechanics (Articles 196 & 197)

```
                     ORDINARY BILL LIFECYCLE IN BICAMERAL STATE LEGISLATURE
                     
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ BILL ORIGINATES IN LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY: Passes & Transmitted to Council│
   └────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                        │
                                        ▼
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ FIRST INSTANCE: LEGISLATIVE COUNCIL'S OPTIONS (ARTICLE 197(1))          │
   │   The Council can:                                                      │
   │   (a) Pass the bill as received;                                        │
   │   (b) Reject the bill;                                                  │
   │   (c) Suggest amendments not acceptable to the Assembly; or             │
   │   (d) Take NO action and keep the bill pending.                         │
   │   • MAXIMUM DELAY ALLOWED IN FIRST INSTANCE: THREE (3) MONTHS           │
   └────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                        │
                                        ▼
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ASSEMBLY REPASSAGE & SECOND TRANSMISSION (ARTICLE 197(2))               │
   │   If the Assembly passes the bill a SECOND TIME (with or without        │
   │   amendments) and transmits it back to the Council:                     │
   │   • The Council can again reject, amend, or sit on it.                  │
   │   • MAXIMUM DELAY ALLOWED IN SECOND INSTANCE: ONE (1) MONTH             │
   └────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                        │
                                        ▼
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ AUTOMATIC PASSAGE:                                                      │
   │   Upon the expiry of ONE MONTH in the second instance, the Bill is      │
   │   DEEMED TO HAVE BEEN PASSED BY BOTH HOUSES in the form passed by the   │
   │   Assembly, even if the Council rejected it or sat on it!               │
   │   TOTAL MAXIMUM DELAY CAP: 3 Months + 1 Month = FOUR (4) MONTHS         │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!CAUTION]
> **What Happens If an Ordinary Bill Originates in the Legislative Council?**
> If an ordinary bill originates in the Legislative Council and is passed and transmitted to the Legislative Assembly:
> If the Legislative Assembly **rejects the bill**, the bill is **immediately DEAD**. There is no second transmission and no repassage mechanism for bills originating in the Council!

---

### C. Money Bills in State Legislature (Articles 198 & 199)
- Introduced **only in the Legislative Assembly**, and **only on the prior recommendation of the Governor**.
- After passage by the Assembly, transmitted to the Legislative Council.
- The Legislative Council has strictly **14 DAYS** to consider and return the bill with non-binding recommendations.
- The Council **cannot reject or amend** a Money Bill.
- If not returned within 14 days, the bill is deemed passed by both Houses in the form passed by the Assembly.

---

## 6. Parliamentary Privileges of the State Legislature (Article 194)

Article 194 confers privileges, immunities, and powers on State Legislatures identical in principle to Article 105 for Parliament:
1. **Freedom of Speech in Legislature (Article 194(1) & (2))**:
   - Absolute immunity from civil or criminal proceedings in any court for anything said or any vote given by an MLA/MLC in the legislature or any committee thereof.
   - Exempt from Article 19(2) restrictions; subject only to the constitutional provisions and Rules of Procedure.
2. **Immunity from Civil Arrest**:
   - Members cannot be arrested in **civil matters** during the session, and **40 days before and 40 days after** the session.
   - Zero immunity for criminal charges, preventive detention, or contempt of court.
3. **Collective Privileges**:
   - Right to publish and prohibit publication of proceedings.
   - Right to exclude strangers and conduct secret sittings.
   - Right to punish members and outsiders for breach of privilege or contempt of the House.

#### Landmark Constitutional Rallies on Article 194:
- ***M.S.M. Sharma v. Sri Krishna Sinha (Searchlight Case, 1959)***: A 5-judge bench held that legislative privileges under Article 194(3) prevail over the general right to freedom of speech under Article 19(1)(a).
- ***Keshav Singh Case (Presidential Reference, 1965 - Special Reference No. 1 of 1964)***: A 7-judge bench held that the Legislative Assembly's power to punish for contempt is **subject to judicial review** under Articles 21 and 226/32 if fundamental rights are violated, resolving the historic clash between the UP Legislative Assembly and the Allahabad High Court.

## 8. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. What is the precise procedure under Article 169 to create or abolish a State Legislative Council?
A1. The State Legislative Assembly must pass a resolution by a SPECIAL MAJORITY (majority of total 
    membership + not less than 2/3rd of members present and voting). Following this, Parliament passes 
    an ordinary Act by a SIMPLE MAJORITY. Under Article 169(3), this is not deemed an amendment under 
    Article 368.

Q2. What is the maximum total time period a State Legislative Council can delay an Ordinary Bill 
    originating in the Legislative Assembly?
A2. Exactly 4 MONTHS: A maximum of 3 months in the first instance, and a maximum of 1 month in the 
    second instance following repassage by the Legislative Assembly.

Q3. What happens if an Ordinary Bill originates in the State Legislative Council, is passed, and is 
    subsequently rejected by the State Legislative Assembly?
A3. The Bill is immediately dead and terminated. The Legislative Council has no repassage or overriding 
    power for bills originating in its own chamber.

Q4. What is the fundamental difference regarding presiding officers between the Rajya Sabha and the 
    State Legislative Council?
A4. The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Vice-President of India) is NOT a member of the Rajya Sabha. In 
    contrast, the Chairman of the State Legislative Council MUST be an elected member of the Council.

Q5. How are the members of the State Legislative Council distributed among different electorates 
    under Article 171(3)?
A5. 1/3rd elected by Local Bodies; 1/12th elected by Graduates (3 years); 1/12th elected by Teachers 
    (3 years); 1/3rd elected by State MLAs (from non-MLAs); and 1/6th nominated by the Governor 
    (Literature, Science, Art, Co-operative Movement, Social Service).
```
