## 1. Epistemic Architecture: What Constitutes a "Constitutional Body"?

In Indian administrative law, state institutions are classified into three epistemic tiers:
1. **Constitutional Bodies**: Established directly by explicit articles of the Constitution of India. Any alteration in their structure, powers, or tenure mandates a formal Constitutional Amendment under **Article 368** (e.g., ECI, CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission, GST Council).
2. **Statutory Bodies**: Created by an Act of Parliament or State Legislature. Can be modified or dissolved through ordinary legislative enactments (e.g., NHRC, SEBI, NGT, Lokpal).
3. **Executive / Extra-Constitutional Bodies**: Created through an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet without statutory backing (e.g., NITI Aayog).

## 2. The Election Commission of India (Article 324)

### A. Composition & Evolution of the Multi-Member Structure
- **Article 324(2)**: The Election Commission consists of the **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)** and such number of other **Election Commissioners (ECs)**, if any, as the President may from time to time fix.
- **Evolutionary Trajectory**:
  $$\text{1950–1989: Single-Member} \longrightarrow \text{Oct 1989: Multi-Member (1+2)} \longrightarrow \text{1990: Single-Member} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Oct\text{ 1993–Present: 3-Member (1+2)}}$$
- **The Equality Principle (*T.N. Seshan v. Union of India, 1995*)**:
  The Supreme Court affirmed that the Chief Election Commissioner and the two other Election Commissioners have **equal powers, draw identical salaries (equivalent to a Supreme Court Judge)**, and in case of difference of opinion, matters are decided by **majority vote**. The CEC is merely *primus inter pares* (first among equals) inside the commission.

---

### B. Appointment Architecture: The 2023 Statutory Transformation
- **The Pre-2023 Status**: Appointed unilaterally by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister/Cabinet.
- ***Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023)* (5-Judge Constitution Bench)**:
  The Supreme Court ruled that to preserve electoral democracy, until Parliament enacted a law, appointments must be made by the President on the recommendation of a Committee consisting of:
  $$\text{SC Benchmark: } \mathbf{Prime\text{ Minister}} + \mathbf{Leader\text{ of Opposition in Lok Sabha}} + \mathbf{Chief\text{ Justice of India}}$$
- **The Statutory Framework (*The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners Act, 2023*)**:
  Parliament enacted a statute replacing the CJI with a Union Cabinet Minister:
  1. **Search Committee**: Headed by the **Minister of Law and Justice** and two other members not below the rank of Secretary to GOI; prepares a panel of 5 candidates.
  2. **Selection Committee**:
     - **Prime Minister** (Chairperson)
     - **Leader of Opposition (or Leader of the Single Largest Opposition Party) in Lok Sabha**
     - **A Union Cabinet Minister** nominated by the Prime Minister.
  3. The President appoints the CEC and ECs on the recommendation of this Selection Committee.

## 3. Comptroller and Auditor General of India (Articles 148–151)

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar declared in the Constituent Assembly:
> *"The Comptroller and Auditor General of India shall be the most important officer under the Constitution of India. He is one of the four bulwarks of the democratic system of government in India (the others being the Supreme Court, UPSC, and Election Commission)."*

### A. Appointment, Oath & Tenure (Article 148)
- Appointed by the **President of India by warrant under his hand and seal**.
- **Tenure**: Holds office for a term of **six (6) years or until attaining the age of 65 years**, whichever is earlier.
- **Oath**: Administered by the President under the **Third Schedule**: to bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution and uphold the Constitution and laws without fear or favour.
- **Removal**: Removed by the President in the **same manner and on the same grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court** (proved misbehaviour or incapacity via Article 368 special majority in Parliament).

---

### B. Constitutional Independence Guarantees (Article 148)
1. **Security of Tenure**: Does not hold office during the pleasure of the President.
2. **Ineligibility for Further Office (Article 148(4))**:
   $$\text{CAG is strictly } \mathbf{INELIGIBLE} \text{ for further office under the Government of India or any State.}$$
3. **Salary Protection (Article 148(6))**: Administrative expenses of the office of CAG (salaries, pensions, allowances) are **charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India** (non-votable by Parliament).
4. Service conditions cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment.

---

### C. Duties, Audit Scope & Relationship with PAC (Articles 149–151)
- Under the *CAG's (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971*:
  - Audits all receipts and expenditure from the **Consolidated Fund of India**, the Consolidated Fund of each State, and each Union Territory having a legislative assembly.
  - Audits all transactions relating to the **Contingency Funds** and **Public Accounts** of the Union and States.
  - Audits all trading, manufacturing, profit and loss accounts of government departments.
  - Audits all Government Companies under the *Companies Act, 2013* and corporations established by parliamentary law.
- **Audit Reports (Article 151)**:
  - Submits **three audit reports** to the President relating to Union accounts:
    1. *Audit Report on Appropriation Accounts*
    2. *Audit Report on Finance Accounts*
    3. *Audit Report on Public Undertakings*
  - The President causes them to be laid before both Houses of Parliament.
  - These reports are examined by the **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)**; CAG acts as the **"friend, philosopher, and guide" of the PAC**.
- **Regulatory Audit vs. Propriety Audit**:
  - *Regulatory Audit*: Verifies that moneys shown in accounts were legally available, authorized, and expended according to rules.
  - *Propriety Audit*: Examines the wisdom, faithfulness, and economy of government expenditure to check whether public funds were wasted or mismanaged.

### B. Removal Procedure: The Binding Supreme Court Reference (Article 317)
- The President can remove the Chairman or any member of UPSC / SPSC directly on grounds of: (1) Insolvency, (2) Paid employment outside duties, or (3) Infirmity of mind or body.
- **On Ground of "Misbehaviour" (Article 317(1))**:
  - The President **MUST refer the matter to the Supreme Court of India** for an inquiry.
  - If the Supreme Court, after inquiry, advises that the Chairman or member ought to be removed, **the President MUST remove them**.
  - **The Advice of the Supreme Court is STRICTLY BINDING on the President!**

## 5. The Finance Commission of India (Article 280)

### A. Nature & Composition
- A **quasi-judicial body** constituted by the President of India **every fifth year**, or at such earlier time as the President considers necessary.
- **Composition (Finance Commission Act, 1951)**:
  - Consists of a **Chairman and four other members** appointed by the President.
  - *Chairman*: Experienced in public affairs.
  - *4 Members Qualifications*:
    1. A Judge of a High Court or qualified to be appointed as such.
    2. A person with specialized knowledge of finance and accounts of the Government.
    3. A person with wide experience in financial matters and administration.
    4. A person with special knowledge of economics.

---

### B. Four Core Constitutional Functions (Article 280(3))
1. **Vertical Devolution**: The distribution between the Union and the States of the net proceeds of taxes which are to be, or may be, divided between them under Chapter I of Part XII.
2. **Horizontal Allocation**: The allocation between the States of the respective shares of such tax proceeds.
3. **Grants-in-Aid Principles**: The principles that should govern the grants-in-aid of the revenues of the States out of the Consolidated Fund of India under **Article 275**.
4. **Panchayat & Municipal Resource Augmentation**:
   - Under **Article 280(3)(bb)**: Measures needed to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of the **Panchayats** on the basis of recommendations made by the State Finance Commission.
   - Under **Article 280(3)(c)**: Measures needed to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of the **Municipalities** on the basis of recommendations made by the State Finance Commission.

> [!NOTE]
> **Advisory Character**: The recommendations made by the Finance Commission are purely **advisory** in nature and not legally binding on the Government of India. However, successive governments have followed the convention of accepting devolution formulas.
> - *15th Finance Commission (N.K. Singh)*: Recommended **41% vertical devolution** to States for 2021–2026 (adjusted downward by 1% from 42% to account for J&K and Ladakh UT status).

## 6. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council (Article 279A)

Inserted by the **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**, the GST Council is a joint constitutional forum for the Centre and States.
- **Composition**:
  - Chairperson: **Union Finance Minister**
  - Member: **Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance**
  - Members: **Ministers in charge of Finance or Taxation** of each State Government.
  - Vice-Chairperson: Chosen by and from amongst the State Ministers.
- **Decision-Making & Weighted Voting Formula (Article 279A(9))**:
  - **Quorum**: Minimum **one-half (50%)** of the total number of members.
  - **Passage Majority**: Every decision is taken by a majority of **NOT LESS THAN THREE-FOURTHS (3/4th / 75%) of the weighted votes** of the members present and voting.
  - **Weighted Allocation**:
    $$\text{Vote of the Central Government} = \frac{1}{3}\text{rd (33.33%) of the Total Votes Cast}$$
    $$\text{Votes of ALL State Governments Combined} = \frac{2}{3}\text{rds (66.67%) of the Total Votes Cast}$$
  - *Federal Equilibrium*: Neither the Centre alone (33%) nor all States combined (67%) can pass a resolution unilaterally without a degree of cross-consensus to cross the 75% threshold!

## 8. Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (Article 350-B)

- Inserted by the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956** pursuant to the States Reorganisation Commission (Fazl Ali) recommendations.
- Appointed by the **President of India**.
- **Duty**: To investigate all matters relating to the safeguards provided for linguistic minorities under the Constitution.
- **Reporting**: Reports to the President, who causes the reports to be laid before each House of Parliament and sent to the Governments of the concerned States.
- Organizationally functions under the **Ministry of Minority Affairs** (Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities with headquarters at New Delhi, regional offices at Belgaum, Chennai, Kolkata).

## 10. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. Under what circumstance is the President of India constitutionally bound to accept the advice 
    tendered by the Supreme Court regarding the removal of an SPSC member?
A1. Under Article 317(1), when an SPSC or UPSC Chairman/Member is sought to be removed on the ground 
    of "misbehaviour", the President must refer the case to the Supreme Court for an inquiry. The 
    advice rendered by the Supreme Court after that inquiry is STRICTLY BINDING on the President.

Q2. What is the voting weightage distribution in the GST Council under Article 279A(9)?
A2. The vote of the Central Government has a weightage of ONE-THIRD (33.33%) of the total votes cast; 
    the votes of all State Governments taken together have a weightage of TWO-THIRDS (66.67%) of the 
    total votes cast. Decisions require a 3/4th (75%) weighted majority.

Q3. What makes the removal of the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) independent of the Union Cabinet?
A3. Under Article 148(1), the CAG can be removed ONLY in the same manner and on the same grounds as a 
    Judge of the Supreme Court (proved misbehaviour or incapacity, supported by a Special Majority 
    under Article 368 in both Houses of Parliament). He does not serve during the pleasure of the President.

Q4. Why is the Joint State Public Service Commission (JSPSC) classified as a statutory body and NOT 
    a constitutional body?
A4. Under Article 315(2), a JSPSC is created by an ACT OF PARLIAMENT upon the request of the concerned 
    State Legislatures, rather than being created directly by the text of the Constitution.

Q5. Does the Attorney General of India have the right to vote when attending a parliamentary committee 
    of which he is named a member?
A5. No. Under Article 88, the Attorney General has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings 
    of either House, any joint sitting, and any committee of Parliament, but is EXPLICITLY BARRED 
    from the right to vote.
```
