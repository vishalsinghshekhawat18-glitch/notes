## 1. Constitutional Epistemic Anchor: The Rise of Tribunals

The original Constitution of 1950 did not contain any specific provisions for administrative tribunals. As the administrative welfare state expanded, conventional civil courts were overwhelmed by docket explosion and lacked specialized technical expertise.
- **The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**:
  Inserted **Part XIV-A** into the Constitution, consisting of two foundational articles:
  1. **Article 323A**: Administrative Tribunals (public service disputes).
  2. **Article 323B**: Tribunals for Other Matters (taxation, foreign exchange, industrial disputes, land reforms, elections to local bodies, etc.).

## 3. Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) & State Administrative Tribunals (SAT)

### A. Genesis & Jurisdiction (Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985)
- Established in **1985** under Article 323A. Principal Bench at **New Delhi** with **19 regular benches** operating across India.
- **Jurisdiction**: Adjudicates recruitment and service conditions of:
  1. Members of the **All India Services** (IAS, IPS, IFS).
  2. Members of the **Central Civil Services** and holders of civil posts under the Union.
  3. Civilian employees of the **Defence Services**.
- **Entities Excluded from CAT Jurisdiction**:
  - Members of the **Armed Forces (Military)**.
  - Officers and servants of the **Supreme Court and High Courts**.
  - Secretariat staff of **both Houses of Parliament**.

---

### B. Composition & Guiding Principles
- Consists of a **Chairman** and both **Judicial and Administrative Members**.
- Appointed by the **President of India** on the recommendations of a High-Powered Search-cum-Selection Committee chaired by the **Chief Justice of India (or a Supreme Court Judge nominated by CJI)**.
- **Exemption from Technical CPC Procedures**:
  The CAT is **not bound by the strict procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908**. It is guided by the **Principles of Natural Justice**. Nominal application fee of **₹50** ensures low-cost, accessible justice.

---

### C. The Historic Jurisprudential Shift: *L. Chandra Kumar (1997)*
The constitutional validity and jurisdiction of tribunals underwent a complete transformation:
- ***S.P. Sampath Kumar v. Union of India (1987)***: A 5-judge bench initially held that tribunals were an "effective alternative institutional mechanism" to High Courts, upholding the statutory exclusion of High Court writ review under Articles 226/227.
- ***L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)* (7-Judge Constitution Bench)**:
  1. Overruled *Sampath Kumar*.
  2. Declared that the power of judicial review vested in the High Courts under **Articles 226/227** and the Supreme Court under **Article 32** is an **INVIOLABLE PART OF THE BASIC STRUCTURE OF THE CONSTITUTION**.
  3. Struck down Clause 2(d) of Article 323A and Clause 3(d) of Article 323B which excluded High Court jurisdiction.
  4. **The Modern Operational Rule**: Tribunals act as **courts of first instance**. An aggrieved party **cannot approach the Supreme Court directly under Article 136**; they must first appeal against the Tribunal's decision before a **Division Bench of the concerned High Court under Article 226/227**.

## 4. National Green Tribunal (NGT)

### A. Statutory Base & Global Precedence
- Established in **2010** under the ***National Green Tribunal Act, 2010***, drawing legitimacy from **Article 21** (Right to a healthy and clean environment).
- Made India the **third country in the world** (after Australia and New Zealand) to establish a specialized environmental tribunal, and the first developing nation to do so.
- **Principal Bench**: **New Delhi**; Zonal Benches at **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata, and Chennai**.

---

### B. Mandate, Composition & Guiding Principles
- **Mandatory Disposal Timeline**: Mandated by Section 18 to dispose of applications and appeals finally within **SIX (6) MONTHS** of filing.
- **Composition**: Full-time Chairperson (retired SC Judge or retired HC Chief Justice) + minimum 10 and maximum 20 Judicial Members + minimum 10 and maximum 20 Expert Members.
- **Guiding Principles (Section 20)**:
  Not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure (1908) or Indian Evidence Act (1872). Governed by Principles of Natural Justice and must apply:
  1. **The Polluter Pays Principle**
  2. **The Precautionary Principle**
  3. **The Principle of Sustainable Development & Inter-generational Equity**

## 7. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. Which constitutional amendment added Part XIV-A (Tribunals) to the Constitution of India, and 
    on the recommendation of which committee?
A1. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, inserted Part XIV-A (Articles 323A and 323B) on the 
    recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee.

Q2. What did the 7-judge Constitution Bench rule in L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997) regarding 
    the jurisdiction of High Courts over tribunals?
A2. The Supreme Court struck down provisions excluding High Court review, holding that the power of 
    judicial review under Articles 226/227 and 32 is an untruncatable part of the Basic Structure. 
    Tribunals act as courts of first instance; their decisions must be challenged before a Division 
    Bench of the High Court before appealing to the Supreme Court.

Q3. Name two prominent environmental statutes that are strictly EXCLUDED from the jurisdiction of 
    the National Green Tribunal (NGT).
A3. (1) The Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, and (2) The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest 
    Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (FRA) / Indian Forest Act, 1927.

Q4. What is the fundamental difference in legislative competence between Article 323A and Article 323B?
A4. Under Article 323A, administrative tribunals can be established ONLY BY PARLIAMENT. Under Article 
    323B, tribunals for other specified matters can be established by BOTH PARLIAMENT AND STATE 
    LEGISLATURES within their respective legislative competencies.

Q5. What is the statutory time limit within which the National Green Tribunal is mandated to dispose 
    of environmental disputes?
A5. Exactly SIX (6) MONTHS from the date of filing of the application or appeal (Section 18(5) of the 
    NGT Act, 2010).
```
