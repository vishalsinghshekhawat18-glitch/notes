## 32.1 Institutional Genesis & Mandate of the 2nd ARC

The **Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2nd ARC)** was constituted on **31 August 2005** under the Chairmanship of **M. Veerappa Moily** (succeeded briefly by V. Ramachandran in 2009). It was charged with recommending a comprehensive blueprint for revamping India’s public administration across Union, State, and Local tiers.
* Between 2006 and 2009, the Commission submitted **15 thematic reports**, establishing the benchmark administrative reform doctrines cited in UPSC Civil Services Mains (GS-2 & GS-4) and State Administrative Service examinations.

## 32.2 Master Compendium of the 15 Reports

### Report 1: Right to Information: Master Key to Good Governance (June 2006)
1. **Repeal of the Official Secrets Act, 1923**: The Commission argued that Section 5 of the OSA creates a pervasive culture of secrecy fundamentally incongruous with democratic transparency. National security safeguards should be transferred to a modernized chapter within the *National Security Act, 1980*.
2. **Earmarked Digital Infrastructure**: Exactly **1% of the budgetary outlay** of all flagship Central and State schemes must be allocated for 5 years specifically for public records management, digitization, and indexing.
3. **Diversification of Information Commissions**: At least **50% of Information Commissioners** (Central and State) must be drawn from non-civil-service backgrounds (academics, journalists, jurists, social workers) to prevent administrative insularity.
4. **Oath of Transparency**: The colonial *Oath of Secrecy* administered to Union and State Ministers under the Third Schedule must be replaced with an **Oath of Transparency**, excepting only classified state secrets.

### Report 2: Unlocking Human Capital: Entitlements and Public Authorities (MGNREGA) (July 2006)
1. **Statutory Right to Employment**: Institutionalize demand-driven wage employment as a justiciable right; prevent administrative rationing of job cards.
2. **Social Audit Institutionalization**: Make village Social Audits legally binding, conducted by the Gram Sabha with independent facilitators (separate from implementing engineers and Sarpanches).
3. **Muster Roll Transparency**: Mandatory biometric tracking and electronic muster rolls uploaded to public servers within 48 hours to eradicate phantom labor entries.

### Report 3: Crisis Management: From Despair to Hope (Disaster Governance) (October 2006)
1. **Incident Command System (ICS)**: Institutionalize specialized ICS protocol establishing unambiguous operational command across military, NDRF, police, and medical responders during emergencies.
2. **Revitalizing Civil Defence**: Expand the *Civil Defence Act, 1968* beyond conventional wartime boundaries to include all natural and anthropogenic disasters, transforming community volunteers into first responders.
3. **Decentralized Disaster Management Plans**: Every gram panchayat, urban local body, and district must possess an annually audited hazard-vulnerability-risk assessment.

### Report 4: Ethics in Governance (January 2007) *(Crucial for GS-4 & GS-2)*
1. **The Public Service Bill**: Enact a overarching statutory framework articulating core civil service values: integrity, impartiality, objectivity, political neutrality, dedication to public service, and empathy for weaker sections.
2. **Revamping the Prevention of Corruption Act (PCA), 1988**:
   * **Sanction for Prosecution (Section 19)**: Introduce a strict statutory time limit of **3 months** for competent authorities to grant or deny sanction. If no decision is communicated within 3 months, sanction is **deemed granted**.
   * **Gross Administrative Incompetence / Abuse**: Criminalize intentional collusive conduct and gross dereliction causing substantial wrongful loss to the exchequer.
   * **Forfeiture of Illegal Assets**: Confiscate properties of corrupt public servants through summary civil proceedings based on balance of probabilities, without awaiting protracted criminal appeals.
3. **National Ombudsman (Lokpal) & State Lokayuktas**:
   * Independent statutory selection committee consisting of the Prime Minister, Chief Justice of India, and Leader of the Opposition.
   * Mandatory creation of uniform Lokayukta institutions across all States with independent investigative machinery.
4. **Disqualification of Tainted Legislators**: Amend the *Representation of the People Act, 1951* to disqualify any person facing criminal charges involving heinous offences where charges have been framed by a court of law $> 6$ months prior to election.

### Report 5: Public Order: Justice for Each... Peace for All (June 2007)
1. **Bifurcation of Police Functions**: Functionally and structurally separate **Investigation** from **Law and Order maintenance** up to the police station level to eliminate investigative compromises caused by daily VIP escort and protest duty.
2. **State Police Board**: Establish an independent statutory oversight board in every state (chaired by State Home Minister with the Leader of the Opposition, Chief Secretary, and civil society jurists) to insulate police leadership from arbitrary political interference.
3. **Fixed Tenures**: Mandatory minimum **2-year tenure** for Director General of Police (DGP), Superintendents of Police (SP), and Station House Officers (SHO), subject to removal only via transparent State Police Board inquiry.
4. **Independent Directorate of Prosecution**: Place public prosecutors under an independent Director of Prosecution reporting to the Law Department, not the police hierarchy, preventing prosecutorial subservience.

### Report 6: Local Governance: An Inspiring Journey into the Future (October 2007)
1. **Clear Activity Mapping (The 3Fs)**: Unbundle functions, funds, and functionaries across Village, Intermediate, and District Panchayats using the **Principle of Subsidiarity** (what can be done at a lower level must not be usurped by a higher level).
2. **Operationalizing District Planning Committees (DPCs)**: Enforce Article 243-ZD; eliminate parallel bureaucratic district agencies (e.g., DRDA) and merge them directly into the elected Zila Parishad.
3. **Fiscal Devolution**: State Finance Commissions (SFCs) must be constituted strictly on time, and their devolution recommendations should be treated with the same constitutional weight as the Central Finance Commission.

### Report 7: Capacity Building for Conflict Resolution (February 2008)
1. **Water Disputes**: Amend the *Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956* to convert ad-hoc tribunals into a permanent National River Water Dispute Tribunal with a statutory 2-year verdict limit.
2. **Left-Wing Extremism (LWE)**: Synchronize security operations with rapid enforcement of the *Forest Rights Act, 2006*, PESA 1996, and land restoration to indigenous tribal communities.
3. **North-East Insurgency**: Transition from militarized containment to developmental integration; review AFSPA mechanisms; strengthen Autonomous District Councils under the Sixth Schedule.

### Report 8: Combating Terrorism: Protecting by Righteousness (September 2008)
1. **Comprehensive Anti-Terror Statutory Architecture**: Define terrorist acts with precise international legal criteria; establish specialized fast-track courts; introduce stringent bail restrictions for transnational terror syndicates.
2. **Federal Intelligence & Investigation Coordination**: Establish a National Counter-Terrorism Centre (NCTC) and National Investigation Agency (NIA) with pan-India federal jurisdiction over terrorism, cyber-warfare, and nuclear offenses.
3. **Witness Protection**: Statutory witness protection program ensuring identity concealment, relocation, and physical security.

### Report 9: Social Capital: A Shared Destiny (September 2008)
1. **National Accreditation of NGOs**: Establish a transparent National Accreditation Council for voluntary organizations to eliminate shell entities and monitor foreign donations (*FCRA*).
2. **SHG-Bank Linkage Federations**: Transition self-help groups from micro-credit consumers into rural producer enterprises and cooperatives.
3. **CSR Alignment**: Channel corporate social responsibility funding into backward aspirational districts rather than saturated urban areas.

### Report 10: Refurbishing of Personnel Administration: Scaling New Heights (November 2008)
1. **Entry Age Rationalization**: Lower the upper age limit for the Civil Services Examination to **25 years for general candidates**, 28 for OBC, and 30 for SC/ST, arguing that young minds are more pliable for public service ethics.
2. **Mid-Career Lifecycle Reviews**: Rigorous mandatory reviews of all officers at **14 years and 20 years of service**; officers deemed corrupt, incompetent, or deadwood should be retired prematurely under Fundamental Rule 56(j).
3. **Lateral Entry into Senior Civil Service**: Institutionalize open-market recruitment of domain experts on 3 to 5-year contracts for at least **10% to 15% of Joint Secretary and Director level posts** requiring technical expertise.
4. **Performance-Related Incentive Scheme (PRIS)**: Replace mechanical seniority-based pay increments with objective, quantified performance appraisal matrices.
5. **Central Civil Services Authority**: Independent statutory authority to supervise transfers, postings, and disciplinary appeals, eliminating politically motivated victimization.

### Report 11: Promoting e-Governance: The SMART Way Forward (December 2008)
1. **SMART Governance Mantra**: Public service delivery must become **Simple, Moral, Accountable, Responsive, and Transparent**.
2. **Enterprise Architecture**: Implement a mandatory unified government enterprise architecture (IndEA) ensuring open standards, metadata interoperability, and cross-departmental data sharing.
3. **Digital By Default**: Mandate that all citizen-facing services (birth/death certificates, land records, vehicle registration, revenue payments) transition to digital platforms with statutory delivery timelines.

### Report 12: Citizen-Centric Administration: The Heart of Governance (February 2009)
1. **The Sevottam Framework (Service Delivery Excellence)**: A 3-pillar institutional architecture:
   * **Pillar 1: Citizen's Charter Implementation**: Clear standards of service, time horizons, and remedies for default.
   * **Pillar 2: Public Grievance Redressal**: Centralized computerized portal (CPGRAMS), monthly Lok Adalats, designated grievance officers.
   * **Pillar 3: Service Delivery Capability**: Infrastructure, employee motivation, and continuous process re-engineering.
2. **Statutory Right to Public Services**: States should enact enforceable Public Service Guarantee Acts mandating financial compensation payable to citizens by defaulting officers if services are delayed.

### Report 13: Organisational Structure of the Government of India (May 2009)
1. **Portfolio Amalgamation**: Merge fragmented, overlapping Union ministries into coherent umbrella ministries (e.g., combine Power, Coal, New & Renewable Energy, and Petroleum into a unified *Ministry of Energy*; combine Surface Transport, Railways, Civil Aviation, and Shipping into a unified *Ministry of Transport*).
2. **Creation of Executive Agencies**: Separate ministerial policy secretariats from operational executive delivery agencies, giving agency heads managerial autonomy paired with strict output accountability.

### Report 14: Strengthening Financial Management Systems (May 2009)
1. **Shift to Accrual Accounting**: Transition government accounts from single-entry cash accounting to double-entry **accrual accounting** to reflect true liabilities, asset depreciation, and pension obligations.
2. **Medium-Term Expenditure Framework (MTEF)**: Institutionalize multi-year 3-year rolling expenditure budgets aligned with outcome targets rather than annual rush of March expenditure (*March Rush*).
3. **Internal Audit Modernization**: Transform internal audit wings from post-mortem voucher checkers into proactive enterprise risk managers.

### Report 15: State and District Administration (May 2009)
1. **District Administration Re-alignment**: The District Collector must transition from a colonial colonial revenue and regulatory commander into a **Chief Facilitator of democratic local development**.
2. **Unified District Administration**: Reduce parallel line department directorates; place district technical officers under the administrative coordination of the District Collector and Zila Parishad.
3. **Regional Autonomy**: Strengthen Divisional Commissioners as regional administrative troubleshooters while abolishing outdated intermediary administrative layers.

## 32.5 Examiner Traps & Warning Vault

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 1: Believing the 2nd ARC Recommended Abolishing All Age Concessions for Reserved Categories**
> *Exam Trap*: "The 2nd ARC recommended reducing the upper age limit to 25 years for all candidates including SC, ST, and OBC."
> *Correction*: FALSE. The 2nd ARC recommended lowering the general age limit to 25 years, while **maintaining age concessions of 3 years for OBC (28 years) and 5 years for SC/ST (30 years)**, alongside appropriate physically handicapped concessions.

> [!WARNING]
> **TRAP 2: Confusing the 1st ARC (1966) with the 2nd ARC (2005)**
> *Exam Trap*: "The 2nd ARC was chaired by Morarji Desai and K. Hanumanthaiah."
> *Correction*: FALSE. The **First ARC (1966)** was initially chaired by Morarji Desai and later by K. Hanumanthaiah. The **Second ARC (2005–2009)** was chaired by **M. Veerappa Moily** (and later V. Ramachandran).

## 32.7 Active Recall Diagnostic Cards

#### Card 1 (Anti-Corruption Architecture)
**Question**: Detail the 2nd ARC’s specific recommendation on Section 19 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 regarding sanction for prosecution, and identify the reform proposed for civil service protection under Article 311.
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
The 2nd ARC recommended that a statutory time limit of <strong>three (3) months</strong> be fixed for granting sanction for prosecution under Section 19 of the PCA. If the competent authority fails to communicate its decision within this 3-month window, sanction should be <strong>deemed to have been granted automatically</strong>. Regarding Article 311, the Commission recommended amending the Constitution to repeal Article 311(2)(c) (dispensing with inquiry in the interest of state security) and streamlining procedural safeguards to prevent Article 311 from functioning as a bureaucratic shield for corrupt or incompetent officers, while retaining adequate protection against malicious victimization through independent appellate tribunals.
</details>

#### Card 2 (Police Governance & Separation of Powers)
**Question**: Explain the rationale behind the 2nd ARC’s recommendation to establish an independent Directorate of Prosecution separate from the state police hierarchy.
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
In many states, public prosecutors function under the administrative influence or operational pressure of the police department, compromising their duty as impartial ministers of justice. The 2nd ARC observed that this subordination leads to poor scrutiny of chargesheets, flawed trial management, and compromised convictions. An independent Directorate of Prosecution reporting directly to the State Department of Law ensures that prosecutors objectively evaluate police investigation files, decline prosecution in meritless cases, prevent malicious framing of innocent citizens, and maintain professional independence inside the courtroom.
</details>

#### Card 3 (Public Service Delivery)
**Question**: Enumerate the three distinct pillars of the Sevottam Model of public service delivery formulated in the 2nd ARC's 12th Report.
<details>
<summary>View Rigorous Causal Answer</summary>
The three pillars of the Sevottam Model are:<br/>
1. <strong>Implementation of Citizen's Charters</strong>: Publicly declaring explicit service standards, delivery time limits, operational norms, and remedial recourse in case of service failure.<br/>
2. <strong>Public Grievance Redressal Mechanism</strong>: Establishing an accessible, multi-channel grievance receipt, time-bound acknowledgment (within 48 hours), fast-track resolution, and root-cause analysis of systemic administrative failure.<br/>
3. <strong>Service Delivery Capability</strong>: Continuous internal capacity building, technological infrastructure upgrading, process re-engineering, and staff behavioral training required to sustainably fulfill the commitments made in the Charter.
</details>
