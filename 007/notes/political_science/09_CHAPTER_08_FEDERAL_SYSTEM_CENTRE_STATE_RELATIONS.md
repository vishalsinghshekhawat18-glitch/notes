## 8.1 The Epistemic Character of Indian Federalism: The Quasi-Federal Paradigm

The Constitution of India establishes a federal system of government. However, the word **"Federation" is nowhere used in the text of the Constitution**. Instead, **Article 1** describes India as a **"Union of States"**.

The framers deliberately engineered a **"Federation with a Strong Centralizing Tendency"** (Ivor Jennings), prioritizing national unity, territorial integrity, and centralized socio-economic planning over absolute state autonomy.

## 8.3 Legislative Relations (Part XI, Articles 245 to 255)

The constitutional scheme of legislative relations is organized into three dimensions: territorial jurisdiction, subject-matter distribution, and central legislative predominance.

### 1. Territorial Extent of Legislative Powers (Article 245)
* **Parliament**: Can make laws for the **whole or any part of the territory of India**, and possesses exclusive power to enact **Extra-Territorial Legislation** (laws applying to Indian citizens and their property anywhere in the world).
* **State Legislatures**: Can make laws for the **whole or any part of the State**. State laws cannot have extra-territorial operation unless there is a clear **Territorial Nexus** between the state and the object (*State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C., 1957*).
* **Exceptions to Parliamentary Territorial Sweep**:
  1. **Four Union Territories**: Under **Article 240**, regulations made by the President for *Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, and Ladakh* have the same force as an Act of Parliament and can amend or repeal any existing parliamentary statute.
  2. **Fifth Schedule Areas**: The Governor can direct that an Act of Parliament shall not apply, or apply with modifications, to a **Scheduled Area** in the State.
  3. **Sixth Schedule Areas**: The Governor of **Assam** can direct that an Act of Parliament shall not apply to an Autonomous District. For **Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram**, this power is vested in the **President**.

### 3. Residuary Powers (Article 248)
* The power to make laws with respect to any matter not enumerated in the Concurrent List or State List (including the power of imposing a tax not mentioned in either list) vests **exclusively in Parliament**.
* **Contrast with Global Federations**: In the **United States, Australia, and Switzerland**, residuary powers are vested in the **States**. India follows the **Canadian model**, vesting residuary powers in the Centre.

### 4. Predominance of Central Legislation & The Article 254(2) Exception
* Under **Article 246**, in the event of an overlap:
  $$\text{Union List (List I)} \succ \text{Concurrent List (List III)} \succ \text{State List (List II)}$$
* **The Doctrine of Repugnancy (Article 254)**: If an enacted State law clashes with a Central law on a Concurrent List subject, the **Central law prevails** and the State law is void to the extent of the repugnancy.
* **The Article 254(2) Exception**: If a State law on a Concurrent subject:
  1. Contains provisions repugnant to an earlier parliamentary law; AND
  2. Was **reserved for the consideration of the President**; AND
  3. Has **received the President's assent**;
  **THEN the State law prevails in that State!**  
  *(However, Parliament can still override it later by enacting a new statute on the same subject).*

## 8.4 Administrative Relations (Part XI, Articles 256 to 263)

Executive power is co-extensive with legislative power: the Union’s executive power extends to matters in the Union List, while States exercise executive power over State List matters. Over the Concurrent List, executive power rests **ordinarily with the States**, unless a Central statute expressly confers it on the Union.

### 1. Constitutional Directives to States & The Article 365 Trigger
* **Article 256**: The executive power of every State shall be so exercised as to ensure compliance with the laws made by Parliament.
* **Article 257**: The executive power of every State shall not impede or prejudice the exercise of the executive power of the Union.
* **The Article 365 Enforcement Hammer**:  
  > *"Where any State has failed to comply with, or to give effect to, any directions given in the exercise of the executive power of the Union... it shall be lawful for the President to hold that a situation has arisen in which the government of the State **cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution**."*  
  *(Triggers the imposition of **President's Rule under Article 356**).*

### 2. Specific Union Administrative Directives
The Centre can issue mandatory administrative directions to States regarding:
1. Construction and maintenance of **means of communication declared to be of national or military importance** (Article 257(2)).
2. Measures to be taken for the **protection of Railways** within the State (Article 257(3)).
3. Provision of adequate facilities for instruction in the **mother tongue at the primary stage** to children belonging to linguistic minority groups (Article 350-A).
4. Drawing up and execution of schemes for the **welfare of Scheduled Tribes** in the State (Article 339(2)).

### 3. Mutual Delegation of Administrative Functions
* **Article 258(1)**: The President may, with the **consent of the State government**, entrust to that government any executive functions of the Union.
* **Article 258(2)**: Parliament can, **by law without state consent**, confer powers and impose duties upon a State in respect of Union matters.
* **Article 258-A** (Added by 7th Amendment 1956): The Governor of a State may, with the **consent of the Government of India**, entrust to the Union any executive functions of the State.

### 4. All-India Services (Article 312)
* Services common to both the Union and the States: Indian Administrative Service (**IAS**), Indian Police Service (**IPS**), and Indian Forest Service (**IFoS**, created in 1966).
* **Recruitment & Training**: Managed exclusively by the Centre (UPSC); officers serve in state cadres.
* **Disciplinary Authority**: Ultimate control lies with the **Central Government**; immediate control rests with the **State Government**.
* **Rajya Sabha’s Federal Prerogative**: Under **Article 312**, a new All-India Service can be created **ONLY IF the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of members present and voting**.

### 2. The Finance Commission (Article 280)
* A quasi-judicial body constituted by the President **every fifth year** (or earlier).
* **Core Mandates**:
  1. Distribution of the net proceeds of taxes between the Union and the States (**Vertical Devolution**), and allocation between States (**Horizontal Devolution**).
  2. Principles governing **Grants-in-Aid** to States under Article 275.
  3. Measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement resources of **Panchayats and Municipalities**.
* **15th Finance Commission (N.K. Singh, 2021–26)**:
  * Recommended **41% Vertical Devolution** to States (reduced from 42% under the 14th FC to adjust for the newly created UTs of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh).
  * Horizontal sharing criteria: Income Distance (45%), Area (15%), Population 2011 (15%), Forest & Ecology (10%), Demographic Performance (12.5%), Tax Effort (2.5%).

### 3. Borrowing Powers: Centre vs. States
* **The Union (Article 292)**: Can borrow upon the security of the Consolidated Fund of India **within India or from abroad**, subject to limits set by Parliament.
* **The States (Article 293)**:
  * Can borrow **ONLY WITHIN INDIA** upon the security of the Consolidated Fund of the State. A State **CANNOT borrow from foreign governments or international agencies directly**.
  * **The Consent Requirement (Article 293(3))**: A State **CANNOT raise any loan without the consent of the Government of India** if there is still outstanding any part of a central loan or a loan guaranteed by the Centre.

### 4. Inter-Governmental Tax Immunity
* **Exemption of Union Property from State Taxation (Article 285)**: Property of the Union is exempt from all taxes imposed by a State or local authority, unless Parliament provides otherwise.
* **Exemption of State Property and Income from Union Taxation (Article 289)**: Property and income of a State are exempt from Union taxation.
  * **Commercial Exception (Article 289(2))**: The Union **can tax any commercial or business operations** conducted by a State government, unless Parliament declares that business to be incidental to the ordinary functions of government.

## 8.7 Publication Callout Boxes: Strategic Matrices & Warnings

### [CONSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM: THE ARTICLE 252 BINDING TRAP]
> **Why State Assemblies must exercise caution before passing an Article 252 resolution:**  
> When two or more State Legislatures pass a resolution requesting Parliament to make a law on a State List subject, Parliament acquires plenary jurisdiction.  
> **The Constitutional Catch**: Once Parliament passes the Act, **the State Legislatures surrender all power over that subject**. Even if a state changes its mind later, **the State Legislature CANNOT amend or repeal the Act**! Only Parliament can amend or repeal a statute enacted under Article 252.

> ### 🔬 [SCHOLARLY DISCOURSE & EMPIRICAL METRICS: OXFORD HANDBOOK (2024)]
> *(Optional Deep Dive: Read for Mains Value-Addition & Academic Citations; Skip on Rapid First Read)*
>
> * **The Four Historical Epochs of Indian Federalism (Prof. Rekha Saxena, *Oxford Handbook of Indian Politics*)**:
>   - Saxena categorizes the evolution of Centre-State power dynamics into four empirical phases:
>     1. **One-Party Dominant "Cooperative" Federalism (1950–1967)**: Governed by the overarching "Congress System" (Rajni Kothari), where Centre-State disputes were settled through internal party consensus rather than inter-governmental friction.
>     2. **Confrontational & Centralizing Federalism (1967–1989)**: Triggered by regional parties capturing power in 8 states in 1967. Characterized by aggressive central intervention, with Article 356 invoked over 100 times to dismiss elected opposition state governments.
>     3. **Multiparty Coalition & "Market-Preserving" Federalism (1989–2014)**: The era of minority union governments dependent on regional coalition partners. The Supreme Court's *S.R. Bommai (1994)* ruling severely curtailed Article 356 abuses, while post-1991 economic liberalization forced States into competitive federalism for private capital.
>     4. **National Hegemony & Asymmetric Friction (Post-2014)**: Return of single-party majority rule. Institutionalized cooperation via the **GST Council** and NITI Aayog, contrasted with sharp friction over Governor discretion, centrally sponsored scheme (CSS) conditionalities, and fiscal transfers.
> * **Asymmetric Federalism as a Democratic Shock Absorber**:
>   - Unlike American federalism (which treats all 50 states identically), Indian federalism is deliberately **asymmetric** (Articles 371 to 371-J, Fifth and Sixth Schedules). Saxena demonstrates that differential constitutional autonomy has prevented centrifugal secession by accommodating diverse linguistic, tribal, and borderland sub-nationalisms.

### [EXAM TRAP & WARNING: HIGH-YIELD PITFALLS FOR CANDIDATES]
> ⚠️ **Trap 1: "State governments can borrow money from foreign international banks like the World Bank."**  
> **FALSE**: Under Article 293, States can borrow **ONLY WITHIN INDIA**. Only the Central Government (Article 292) can borrow externally.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 2: "The 42nd Amendment transferred five subjects from the Concurrent List to the State List."**  
> **FALSE**: It transferred five subjects **FROM the State List TO the Concurrent List** (Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Wildlife, Subordinate Courts).
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 3: "A Rajya Sabha resolution under Article 249 remains valid for three years."**  
> **FALSE**: An Article 249 resolution remains in force for a period **NOT EXCEEDING ONE YEAR** at a time.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 4: "Statutory grants under Article 275 are given equally to all States."**  
> **FALSE**: Statutory grants are differential, given only to **specific States in need of assistance** on the recommendation of the Finance Commission.
> 
> ⚠️ **Trap 5: "The word 'Federation' is used in Article 1 of the Constitution."**  
> **FALSE**: Article 1 uses the phrase **"Union of States"**. The word "Federation" does not appear anywhere in the text of the Constitution!

## 8.8 Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

1. **Which article of the Constitution empowers the Rajya Sabha to authorize Parliament to make laws on a State List subject in the national interest?**  
   *Answer*: **Article 249** (requires a resolution passed by a two-thirds majority of members present and voting).

2. **Differentiate between the Canadian model and the American model regarding the vesting of residuary powers.**  
   *Answer*: The American model vests residuary powers in the States; the Canadian model (followed by India under Article 248) vests residuary powers in the Federal Centre.

3. **What is the constitutional consequence under Article 365 if a State fails to comply with administrative directions issued by the Union?**  
   *Answer*: The President can hold that the constitutional machinery of the State has failed, triggering the imposition of President's Rule under **Article 356**.

4. **Under what condition can a State law on a Concurrent List subject override a conflicting Central law within that State?**  
   *Answer*: Under **Article 254(2)**, if the State bill was reserved for the consideration of the President and received the **President's assent**.

5. **Which five subjects were transferred from the State List to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976?**  
   *Answer*: (1) Education, (2) Forests, (3) Weights and Measures, (4) Protection of Wild Animals and Birds, and (5) Administration of Justice (subordinate courts).

6. **What is the difference between Statutory Grants (Article 275) and Discretionary Grants (Article 282)?**  
   *Answer*: Statutory Grants are recommended by the Finance Commission and charged on the Consolidated Fund of India for states in need; Discretionary Grants are executive grants made for any public purpose.

7. **Explain the borrowing limitation imposed on State Governments under Article 293(3).**  
   *Answer*: A State cannot raise any loan without the consent of the Centre if any part of an earlier central loan or central guarantee remains outstanding.

8. **Which commission on Centre-State relations recommended that Governors should be removed only through an impeachment process by the State Legislature?**  
   *Answer*: The **M.M. Punchhi Commission (2010)**.

9. **What percentage of vertical tax devolution was recommended by the 15th Finance Commission under N.K. Singh?**  
   *Answer*: **41%** (adjusted by 1% from the 14th FC's 42% to accommodate Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh).

10. **Can Parliament enact a law on a State List subject to implement an international treaty without the consent of the affected States?**  
    *Answer*: Yes. Under **Article 253**, Parliament has plenary power to legislate on any State subject to implement international treaties, agreements, or conventions.
