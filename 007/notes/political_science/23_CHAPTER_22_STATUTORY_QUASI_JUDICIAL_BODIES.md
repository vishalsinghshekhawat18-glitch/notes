## 1. Statutory vs. Executive Bodies: Epistemic Distinctions

Non-constitutional public institutions in India fall into two broad structural classes:
1. **Statutory Bodies**: Established by an **Act of Parliament**. They derive their powers, mandate, and autonomy from parent legislation (e.g., NHRC, CIC, CVC, Lokpal, NIA, NDMA).
2. **Executive (Non-Statutory / Extra-Constitutional) Bodies**: Created by an **executive order or resolution of the Union Cabinet**. They have no constitutional base and no statutory parent act (e.g., NITI Aayog, Law Commission).

## 2. NITI Aayog: The National Think Tank

### A. Genesis & Departure from the Planning Commission
- Established on **January 1, 2015** by a Cabinet Resolution, replacing the 65-year-old **Planning Commission** (set up in March 1950).
- **Core Philosophy**: Shift from a centralized, top-down, command-and-control Soviet model of "one-size-fits-all" planning to **Cooperative Federalism** and **Competitive Federalism** ("Team India").

---

### B. Composition & Organisational Framework
- **Chairperson**: **Prime Minister of India**.
- **Governing Council**: Chief Ministers of all States, Chief Ministers of UTs with legislative assemblies (Delhi, Puducherry, J&K), and Lieutenant Governors of other UTs.
- **Regional Councils**: Convened to address specific inter-state or regional contingencies; chaired by PM or his nominee.
- **Vice-Chairperson**: Appointed by the PM; enjoys the rank of a **Cabinet Minister**.
- **Full-Time Members**: Enjoy the rank of a **Minister of State**.
- **Part-Time Members**: Maximum of 2 members from leading universities and research organizations in an *ex-officio* capacity.
- **Ex-Officio Members**: Maximum of 4 members of the Union Council of Ministers nominated by the PM.
- **Chief Executive Officer (CEO)**: Appointed by the PM for a fixed tenure; holds the rank of **Secretary to the Government of India**.
- **Attached Specialized Wings**:
  1. *Atal Innovation Mission (AIM)*: Cultivates entrepreneurship and tinkering labs across schools.
  2. *Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO)*: Monitors output-outcome frameworks of central schemes.

## 3. National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

### A. Statutory Base & Composition (2019 Amendments)
Established under the *Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993* (PHRA) to safeguard human rights defined as life, liberty, equality, and dignity guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in International Covenants.
- **Composition (under PHRA Amendment Act, 2019)**:
  - **Chairperson**: Has been a **Chief Justice of India OR a Judge of the Supreme Court**.
  - **Members (5 Whole-Time)**:
    - 1 Member who is or has been a **Judge of the Supreme Court**.
    - 1 Member who is or has been the **Chief Justice of a High Court**.
    - 3 Members having knowledge of human rights, **at least one of whom MUST BE A WOMAN**.
  - **Ex-Officio Members (7)**: Chairpersons of NCSC, NCST, NCBC, National Commission for Women (NCW), National Commission for Minorities, NCPCR (Child Rights), and the Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities.

---

### C. Powers & Critical Functional Limitations
1. **Civil Court Powers**: Enjoys all powers of a civil court trying a suit under the CPC: summoning witnesses, discovery of documents, requisitioning public records.
2. **RECOMMENDATORY CHARACTER**:
   - The NHRC has **no power to punish the violators of human rights**, nor to award monetary relief to the victim directly.
   - Its recommendations are **purely advisory**. The concerned government must inform the commission of action taken within **one month**.
3. **Statutory Limitation Period**: Cannot inquire into any matter after the expiration of **ONE (1) YEAR** from the date on which the human rights violation occurred (**Section 36(2)**).
4. **Armed Forces Barrier**: When investigating human rights violations by the Armed Forces, NHRC cannot investigate directly; it can only seek a factual report from the Central Government.

## 4. Central Information Commission (CIC)

### A. Statutory Base & Composition (RTI Act, 2005)
Established under the *Right to Information Act, 2005*.
- **Composition**: Chief Information Commissioner (CIC) and not more than **ten (10) Information Commissioners (ICs)**.
- **Selection Committee (3 Members)**:
  1. Prime Minister (Chairperson)
  2. Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha
  3. A Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.

---

## 5. Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)

### A. Origin & Statutory Evolution
- Set up in **1964** on the recommendations of the **K. Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption (1962–64)** as an apex vigilance institution.
- Conferred **statutory status** via the *Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003* pursuant to the Supreme Court's directives in the landmark ***Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1998)*** judgment.

---

### B. Composition, Unique Tenure & Selection (CVC Act, 2003)
- **Composition**: Central Vigilance Commissioner (Chairperson) + not more than **two (2) Vigilance Commissioners**.
- **Selection Committee (3 Members)**:
  1. **Prime Minister** (Chairperson)
  2. **Union Minister of Home Affairs**
  3. **Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha** (or leader of single largest opposition party).
- **The Unique 4-Year Tenure**:
  $$\text{Tenure of CVC: } \mathbf{FOUR\text{ (4) YEARS}} \text{ or until attaining the age of } \mathbf{65\text{ YEARS}}, \text{ whichever is earlier.}$$
- **Post-Tenure Ban**: On ceasing to hold office, the CVC and Commissioners are **ineligible for further employment** under the Government of India or any State.
- **Supervisory Authority**: Exercises superintendence over the functioning of the **Delhi Special Police Establishment (CBI)** in so far as it relates to the investigation of offences under the *Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988*.

## 6. The Lokpal of India & State Lokayuktas

### A. Genesis of the Ombudsman Concept
- Concept originated in **Sweden in 1809** (Ombudsman = grievance redressal agent).
- Term *"Lokpal"* was coined by **Dr. L.M. Singhvi** in 1963.
- First Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC, 1966) recommended the creation of a two-tier anti-corruption ombudsman: Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayukta in the States.
- Enacted as ***The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013*** following the Anna Hazare anti-corruption movement.

---

### B. Jurisdiction & Purview of the Lokpal
The Lokpal has anti-corruption jurisdiction over:
1. **Prime Minister of India**: Subject to strict safeguards—allegations relating to *international relations, external and internal security, public order, atomic energy, and space* are **exempted** from Lokpal inquiry. Inquiry must be held *in-camera*, and must be approved by **two-thirds of the full bench of the Lokpal**.
2. **Union Ministers & Members of Parliament**: (Excludes votes given or speeches delivered in Parliament under Article 105).
3. **Public Servants**: All Group A, B, C, and D officers of the Central Government.
4. **NGOs & Trusts**: Directors and managers of entities receiving government funding or foreign contributions exceeding **₹10 Lakh per annum** under FCRA.

---

## 7. Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) & National Investigation Agency (NIA)

### A. Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)
- Set up in **1963** by a Resolution of the Ministry of Home Affairs; later transferred to the **Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (DoPT)**.
- **Not a Statutory Body**: CBI is not created by an Act of Parliament; it derives its police investigating powers from the ***Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946***.
- **Director of CBI**:
  - Tenure: Fixed minimum tenure of **2 years** under the CVC Act 2003 (*Vineet Narain*).
  - *2021 DSPE Amendment*: Allowed government to extend tenure of CBI Director up to **5 years** (one year at a time).
  - *Selection Committee*: (1) Prime Minister, (2) Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, (3) Chief Justice of India (or nominee).
- **State Consent Dynamics (§6 DSPE Act)**:
  - CBI **cannot** investigate offences in a State without the consent of that State Government.
  - *General Consent vs. Specific Consent*: Many states have withdrawn "General Consent", requiring CBI to seek case-by-case permission.
  - *Exception*: Supreme Court and High Courts can direct CBI to investigate any crime anywhere in India **without the consent of the State** (*State of West Bengal v. Committee for Protection of Democratic Rights, 2010*).

---

### B. National Investigation Agency (NIA)
- Established under the ***National Investigation Agency Act, 2008*** post-26/11 Mumbai terror attacks.
- India's premier **central counter-terrorism agency**.
- **No State Consent Required**: Unlike the CBI, the NIA possesses **sovereign statutory power to enter any State and investigate scheduled terror offences WITHOUT obtaining prior consent from the State Government**.
- **2019 NIA Amendment Act Expansions**:
  1. Jurisdiction expanded to investigate: Human trafficking, counterfeit currency, cyber-terrorism, and offences under the Explosive Substances Act.
  2. Extra-territorial jurisdiction: Empowered to investigate offences committed **outside India** affecting Indian citizens or Indian interests abroad.

## 8. National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)

- Established under the ***Disaster Management Act, 2005***.
- Apex constitutional/statutory body for disaster management policies, planning, and guidelines.
- **Institutional Hierarchy**:
  1. **NDMA (National Level)**: Headed by the **Prime Minister as ex-officio Chairperson**; up to 9 members; Vice-Chairperson holds Cabinet Minister rank.
  2. **SDMA (State Level)**: Headed by the **Chief Minister as Chairperson**.
  3. **DDMA (District Level)**: Co-chaired by the **District Magistrate / Collector** and the **elected Chairperson of the Zilla Parishad**.
- **National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)**: Constituted under the 2005 Act for specialist response to natural and man-made disasters; headed by a Director General (IPS).

## 10. Active Recall Diagnostic Vault

```
Q1. What is the fundamental difference between the statutory status of the National Investigation 
    Agency (NIA) and the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)?
A1. The NIA is a full-fledged statutory body created directly by the NIA Act, 2008, and needs no 
    state consent to investigate terror crimes. The CBI is NOT a statutory body (created by executive 
    resolution in 1963) and derives its police powers from the DSPE Act, 1946; it requires state consent 
    under Section 6 unless directed by the SC or HC.

Q2. What is the unique tenure prescribed for the Central Vigilance Commissioner (CVC) under the CVC Act?
A2. A unique term of FOUR (4) YEARS or until attaining the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier. 
    (Almost all other statutory bodies feature 3-year or 5-year terms).

Q3. What changes did the 2019 Amendment to the Protection of Human Rights Act make regarding the 
    eligibility of the NHRC Chairperson?
A3. Earlier, only a retired Chief Justice of India was eligible. The 2019 amendment made a retired 
    Judge of the Supreme Court ALSO eligible to be appointed as Chairperson of the NHRC, and reduced 
    the tenure from 5 years to 3 years.

Q4. Under what constitutional or statutory power did NITI Aayog lose the authority to allocate central 
    plan funds to the States?
A4. The Planning Commission had acquired extra-constitutional fund allocation powers under Article 282 
    grants. NITI Aayog was conceptualized purely as a policy think-tank and catalyst; its founding 
    cabinet resolution in 2015 explicitly removed fund allocation powers and restored that authority 
    to the Union Ministry of Finance.

Q5. Who co-chairs the District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA) under the Disaster Management Act, 2005?
A5. The District Collector / District Magistrate serves as Chairperson, and the elected Chairperson 
    of the Zilla Parishad serves as the Co-Chairperson (blending administrative command with democratic 
    legitimacy).
```
