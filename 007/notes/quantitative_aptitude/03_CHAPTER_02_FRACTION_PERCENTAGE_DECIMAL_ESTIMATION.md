## 2.1 The Epistemic Foundation: Fractional Representation of Proportions

In quantitative problem solving, numbers exist in three isomorphic mathematical representations:
1. **Fractions ($\frac{p}{q}$)**: The exact, unrounded rational quotient.
2. **Decimals ($0.d_1d_2\dots$)**: The base-10 positional valuation.
3. **Percentages ($\%$)**: The normalized comparison against a standard baseline of 100 parts.

The core cognitive advantage of mastering **Fraction $\leftrightarrow$ Percentage conversions** is the complete elimination of division in commercial arithmetic. A calculation such as *"find $37.5\%$ of $6,400$"* requires cumbersome 3-digit multiplication if approached as $0.375 \times 6400$, but becomes a 3-second mental calculation when recognized as:
$$\frac{3}{8} \times 6400 = 3 \times 800 = \mathbf{2,400}$$

## 2.3 Recurring Decimals: Algebraic Conversion to Vulgar Fractions

A recurring (or repeating) decimal has digits that repeat infinitely after the decimal point. They are converted to exact rational fractions ($\frac{p}{q}$) through modular algebraic cancellation.

### 1. Pure Recurring Decimals ($0.\overline{a_1a_2\dots a_n}$)
In a pure recurring decimal, the repetition begins immediately after the decimal point.

#### The First-Principles Algebraic Proof
Let $x = 0.\overline{a_1a_2\dots a_n}$.  
Multiplying by $10^n$, where $n$ is the period (number of repeating digits):
$$10^n x = a_1a_2\dots a_n.\overline{a_1a_2\dots a_n}$$

Subtracting the original equation from this:
$$10^n x - x = a_1a_2\dots a_n$$
$$x(10^n - 1) = a_1a_2\dots a_n \implies x = \frac{a_1a_2\dots a_n}{10^n - 1} = \frac{\text{Repeating Digits}}{\underbrace{99\dots9}_{n \text{ nines}}}$$

#### Canonical Rules:
* $0.\overline{7} = \frac{7}{9}$
* $0.\overline{37} = \frac{37}{99}$
* $0.\overline{148} = \frac{148}{999} = \frac{4}{27}$

---

### 2. Mixed Recurring Decimals ($0.b_1b_2\dots b_m\overline{a_1a_2\dots a_n}$)
In a mixed recurring decimal, there are $m$ non-repeating digits followed by $n$ repeating digits.

#### The First-Principles Algebraic Formula
$$\text{Fraction} = \frac{(\text{Complete number formed by non-repeating and repeating digits}) - (\text{Number formed by non-repeating digits})}{\underbrace{99\dots9}_{n \text{ nines}} \underbrace{00\dots0}_{m \text{ zeros}}}$$

#### Multi-Tier Worked Exemplars:
* **Exemplar A**: Convert $0.1\overline{6}$ to a fraction.
  $$\text{Fraction} = \frac{16 - 1}{90} = \frac{15}{90} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{6}}$$
* **Exemplar B**: Convert $0.22\overline{73}$ to a fraction.
  $$\text{Fraction} = \frac{2273 - 22}{9900} = \frac{2251}{9900}$$
* **Exemplar C**: Convert $3.4\overline{7}$ to an improper fraction.
  $$3.4\overline{7} = 3 + \frac{47 - 4}{90} = 3 + \frac{43}{90} = \mathbf{\frac{313}{90}}$$

## 2.4 BODMAS & The Operational Hierarchy of Simplification

Mathematical operations are evaluated in strict hierarchical precedence:

```
┌──────────┬─────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Precedence│ Operation Symbol            │ Critical Operational Rule                              │
├──────────┼─────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1 (Top)  │ V - Vinculum (Bar: x̄)       │ Evaluated first, binds signs under the bar.           │
│ 2        │ B - Brackets: (), {}, []    │ Innermost (parentheses) ➔ curly {} ➔ square [].       │
│ 3        │ O - "Of" (Orders / Powers)  │ Higher priority than regular division / multiplication.│
│ 4        │ D - Division (÷)            │ Evaluated strictly from left to right.                 │
│ 5        │ M - Multiplication (×)      │ Associative with addition boundaries.                 │
│ 6        │ A - Addition (+)            │ Commutative.                                           │
│ 7 (Base) │ S - Subtraction (-)         │ Evaluated in natural sequential order.                 │
└──────────┴─────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Fatal "Of" vs. "$\times$" Distinction
Consider the expression: $20 \div 5 \text{ of } 2$.
* **Incorrect Interpretation** (treating "of" as plain $\times$ after division):
  $$20 \div 5 \times 2 = 4 \times 2 = 8 \quad \text{[FATAL EXAM ERROR]}$$
* **Correct BODMAS Interpretation** ("of" has precedence over "$\div$"):
  $$20 \div (5 \times 2) = 20 \div 10 = \mathbf{2}$$

## 2.5 Structured Estimation & Bounding Mechanics for Timed Exams

In high-speed banking and CSAT exams, exact computation of multi-digit division is inefficient. The **Relative Error Minimization Technique** isolates the correct option without full calculation:

### 1. The Denominator Percentage Shift Rule
When adjusting a denominator to a round benchmark, shift the numerator by the **exact same percentage**:
$$\frac{N}{D} \approx \frac{N(1 + \delta)}{D(1 + \delta)}$$

#### Problem: Estimate $\frac{4,720}{784}$
1. The denominator $784$ is close to $800$.
2. Increase $D = 784$ to $800$, which is an increase of $+16$.
3. What percentage is $16$ of $784$? Since $10\%$ is $78.4$ and $1\%$ is $7.84$, $16 \approx 2\%$.
4. Increase the numerator $4,720$ by the same $+2\%$:
   $$2\% \text{ of } 4720 \approx 94.4 \implies 4720 + 94.4 = 4814.4$$
5. New simplified fraction:
   $$\frac{4814.4}{800} = \frac{48.144}{8} = \mathbf{6.018}$$
*(Exact calculator value: $4720 / 784 = 6.0204$. Estimation error is $<0.04\%$!)*

## 2.6 Diagnostic Worked Exemplars (R.S. Aggarwal Benchmark Traps)

### Exemplar 1 (Chained Left-to-Right Division):
Evaluate: $48 \div 12 \div 4 \div 2$.
* **The Rule of Chained Division**: $a \div b \div c \div d = \frac{a}{b \times c \times d}$.
* **Solution**:
  $$\frac{48}{12 \times 4 \times 2} = \frac{48}{96} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}} = \mathbf{0.5}$$

### Exemplar 2 (Vinculum Sign Binding Trap):
Simplify: $15 - [8 - \{6 - (4 - \overline{3 - 1})\}]$.
* **Step 1 (Vinculum)**: The bar covers $3 - 1$, so $(3 - 1) = 2$.
  $$\implies 4 - 2 = 2$$
* **Step 2 (Parentheses)**: $6 - 2 = 4$.
* **Step 3 (Curly Braces)**: $8 - 4 = 4$.
* **Step 4 (Square Brackets)**: $15 - 4 = \mathbf{11}$.

## 2.7 Top Examiner Traps in Fractions & Estimation

1. **The Compounded Decimal Placement Fallacy**:
   When multiplying $0.04 \times 0.005$, calculate $4 \times 5 = 20$. The number of decimal places must be $2 + 3 = 5$. Writing $0.002$ instead of $0.00020$ is an instant disqualifier. Count from the right: `0.00020` $= 0.0002$.
2. **The Partial Recurring Subtraction Trap**:
   In $0.\overline{57} - 0.\overline{23}$, direct subtraction is valid: $\frac{57-23}{99} = \frac{34}{99}$. But for $0.5\overline{7} - 0.2\overline{3}$, denominators are $90$. Never subtract recurring parts independently across mismatched periods!
3. **The Denominator Inversion Trap in Approximation**:
   Increasing a denominator reduces the fraction's value. If you round $437 / 49$ to $437 / 50$, your result is an **underestimate**. The true value is strictly higher than $8.74$.
