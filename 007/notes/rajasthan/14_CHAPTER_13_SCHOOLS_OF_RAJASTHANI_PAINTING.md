## 1. Historiography, Classification & Canonical Nomenclature

Rajasthani painting (*राजस्थानी चित्रकला*) represents one of the most vibrant, emotionally intense, and stylistically distinct traditions of visual art in South Asia. Derived from the ancient Western Indian miniature style (Apabhramsha and Jain manuscript painting), it blossomed into an independent indigenous aesthetic between the 15th and 19th centuries under the royal patronage of Rajput principalities.

### 1.1 The Pioneer Scholars & Terminological Debate
1. **Dr. Anand Kentish Coomaraswamy (1916)**:
   - In his groundbreaking two-volume treatise **"Rajput Painting" (1916)**, Coomaraswamy became the first scholar to scientifically classify traditional Indian painting.
   - He drew a sharp aesthetic and philosophical distinction between **Mughal Painting** (secular, courtly, aristocratic, portraiture-driven, realistic) and **Rajput Painting** (sacred, devotional, folk-grounded, lyrical, romantic).
   - Under the umbrella of *Rajput Painting*, he grouped two major geographical branches: (a) **Rajasthani School** (Plains of Rajputana) and (b) **Pahari School** (Himalayan sub-states such as Kangra, Basohli, and Guler).
2. **O.C. Gangoly & Basil Gray**: Authored *Masterpieces of Rajput Painting*, supporting the dynastic and aristocratic nomenclature "Rajput School".
3. **Rai Krishnadas**: In his seminal work *Bharat ki Chitrakala*, he strongly argued that the term "Rajput Painting" was misleadingly communal/dynastic, since artists, subjects, and themes encompassed all social strata. He firmly established the universally accepted academic term **"Rajasthani Chitrasheili" (राजस्थानी चित्रशैली)** based on geographical and linguistic sovereignty.
4. **Other Canonical Historians**: W.G. Archer (*Indian Paintings in Rajasthan*), Dr. Moti Chandra, Dr. Shridhar Andhare, Karl Khandalavala, and Ramgopal Vijayvargiya.

## 2. The Mewar School (मेवाड़ स्कूल) — The Genesis & Mother Style

The Mewar School is universally revered as the **fountainhead / mother style** of all Rajasthani painting. Being the most geographically insulated from Delhi and fiercely resistant to Mughal subjugation, Mewar preserved the purest indigenous traits of Western Indian art.

### 2.1 Earliest Manuscript Inscriptions
1. **Shravaka Pratikramana Sutra Churni (श्रावक प्रतिक्रमण सूत्र चूर्णी)**:
   - **Date & Location**: Executed in **1260 CE (V.S. 1317)** at **Ahar (Mewar)**.
   - **Patron Monarch**: Guhil king **Rawal Tejasimha (रावल तेजसिंह)**.
   - **Master Scribe-Artist**: Illustrated on palm-leaves by **Kamalchandra (कमलचंद्र)**.
   - **Historical Status**: The **earliest dated illustrated manuscript** of Rajasthani painting discovered to date.
2. **Supasanahachariyam (सुपासनाहचरियं / पार्श्वनाथ चरित्र)**:
   - **Date & Location**: Executed in **1422–1423 CE** at **Delwara (Mewar)**.
   - **Patron Monarch**: Maharana **Mokal**.
   - **Master Artist**: Scribe-painter **Hirānanda (हीरानंद)**.
   - Shows the transition from Apabhramsha angularity to softer indigenous Rajput facial forms.

---

### 2.2 Udaipur Sub-style: The Golden Age of Sahibdin & Manohar
Under **Maharana Jagat Singh I (1628–1652 CE)**, Udaipur painting attained its zenith. Jagat Singh I established an official royal art studio within the Udaipur City Palace named:
> **"Chiteron ki Ovari" (चितेरों की ओवरी)**, popularly known across Rajputana as **"Tasveeraan ro Karkhano" (तस्वीराँ रो कारखानो)**.

#### The Twin Masters: Sahibdin & Manohar
- **Sahibdin (साहिबदीन)**: A master Muslim court artist deeply steeped in Hindu epics. He executed monumental narrative series:
  - *Ragamala Set* (1628 CE)
  - *Gitagovinda* of Jayadeva (1629 CE)
  - *Rasikapriya* of Keshavdas (1630 CE)
  - *Suryavansha* (1645 CE)
  - *Arsha Ramayana* (1648–1652 CE, specifically Yuddha Kanda and Sundar Kanda)
- **Manohar (मनोहर)**: A master Hindu contemporary who illustrated the *Bal Kanda* of the Ramayana in 1649 CE.
- **Visual Characteristics**:
  - Eyes shaped like fish (*Meenakshi* / मीनाक्षी).
  - Short, sturdy human statures; males wearing Mewari turbans (*Pheta*), waistbands (*Patka*), and *Gherdar Jama*.
  - Rich mineral colors: deep cinnabar red (*Hinglu*), brilliant saffron yellow (*Peedi*), lapis lazuli blue, and emerald green.
  - Distinctive vegetative motifs: lush **Kadamba tree (कदंब वृक्ष)** and stylized lotus ponds.

---

### 2.3 Chavand Sub-style (चावंड उपशैली)
- **Genesis**: Founded during the intense guerrilla resistance of **Maharana Pratap** after making Chavand his sovereign hill capital in **1585 CE**.
- **Golden Era**: Flourished under **Maharana Amar Singh I (1597–1620 CE)**.
- **The Chavand Ragamala (1605 CE)**: Master artist **Nasiruddin (Nisardi / निसारदीन)** painted the celebrated **Ragamala Set** at Chavand on **10 April 1605 CE**. It is considered the finest benchmark of pre-Mughal indigenous Rajput expressionism.

---

### 2.4 Nathdwara Sub-style: Pichhwai Art (नाथद्वारा उपशैली)
- **Historical Genesis**: Emerged after the sacred black marble idol of **Bhagwan Shrinathji** was transported from Govardhan (Braj) and permanently installed at **Sihad village (modern Nathdwara)** by **Maharana Raj Singh I in 1672 CE**.
- **Stylistic Synthesis**: A devotional blend of indigenous Mewari boldness and Braj (Vallabh/Pushtimarg) lyrical sweetness.

  - **Pichhwai (पिछवाई)**: Intricately painted cloth backdrops hung directly behind the idol of Shrinathji, changing according to the 8 daily *Jhankis* (worship rituals), festivals (Annakoot, Janmashtami, Radhashtami), and seasons (Sharad Purnima).
  - Prominent motifs: Blissful **cows of Braj (*Kamdhenu*)**, banana trees (*Kela*), Yamuna river cascades, and celestial aerial vehicles (*Vimana*).
  - **Celebrated Women Painters**: Nathdwara holds a unique distinction in Rajasthan's art history for its prominent master female painters: **Kamala (कमला)** and **Ilaichi (इलायची)**.

### 2.5 Devgarh Sub-style (देवगढ़ उपशैली)
- **Feudal Origin**: Developed in the first-class *Thikana* of Devgarh (Rajsamand), founded in 1680 CE by **Rawat Dwarkadas Chundawat** (a 16th Umrao of Mewar).
- **Discovery**: Brought to national and international academic recognition by art historian **Dr. Shridhar Andhare (डॉ. श्रीधर अंधारे)**.
- **Stylistic Fusion**: A unique blend of **Mewar, Marwar, and Dhundhar** styles.
- **Master Artists**: **Bagta (बगता)**, **Chokha (चोखा)**, and **Kavala (कवला)**.
- **Subject Matter**: Dynamic wild elephant fights (*Hathi ki Ladai*), raw royal boar hunts (*Shikar*), and courtly durbar ceremonies dominated by rich yellow-green palettes.

## 3. The Marwar School (मारवाड़ स्कूल) — The Desert Masters

Covering the vast arid expanses of Jodhpur, Bikaner, Kishangarh, Nagaur, and Jaisalmer, the Marwar School is distinguished by monumental scale, heroic expressions, radiant primary colors, and dramatic seasonal landscapes.

### 3.1 Jodhpur Sub-style (जोधपुर उपशैली)
- **Early Evolution**: Emerged under **Rao Maldeo (1531–1562 CE)**; showcased in the vibrant battle murals and celestial paintings of **Chokhelao Mahal (Mehrangarh Fort)** and the illustrated *Uttaradhyayana Sutra* (1591 CE).
- **Mughal Synthesis**: Initiated under Mota Raja Udai Singh and Maharaja Gaj Singh; refined under Maharaja Jaswant Singh I (*Barahmasa* and *Ragamala* sets).
- **The Golden Age of the Nath Sect**: Reached its unique zenith under **Maharaja Man Singh (1803–1843 CE)**. Inspired by his spiritual devotion to Ayas Devnath, Man Singh commissioned colossal illustrated sacred manuscripts:
  - *Nath Charitra*
  - *Shiva Purana*
  - *Durgacharit*
  - *Siddha Siddhanta Paddhati*
- **Visual Markers**:
  - Dominance of bright **yellow ochre (पीला रंग)**, scarlet red, and lapis blue.
  - Dramatic swirling, spiral monsoon clouds (*Ghumadte Badal*).
  - Tall, powerful male physiques wearing high pointed Marwari turbans (**Khidkiya Pagdi** / खिड़किया पगड़ी) and carrying shields and swords.
  - Almond-shaped eyes (*Badam si Aankhein*).
  - **Master Artists**: Amar Das, Bhati Shivdas, Dan Bhati, Shankar Das, Chhagan Das, Jeewan Das, and Ram Singh Bhati.

### 3.2 Kishangarh Sub-style: The Lyrical Zenith & Bani Thani
The Kishangarh school represents the absolute pinnacle of aristocratic romantic lyricism, mystical eroticism, and refined spiritual beauty in Indian art.

#### 1. Historical & Romantic Backdrop
- **Maharaja Savant Singh (1748–1764 CE)**: A deeply spiritual Rathore prince and lyrical poet who composed devotional verses under the pseudonym **Nagari Das (नागरीदास)**.
- **Bani Thani (बणी-ठणी / Rasik Bihari)**: A singer, poetess, and consort of Sawai Savant Singh whose exquisite grooming, fashionable refinement, and spiritual beauty earned her the title *Bani-Thani* (The Well-Attired One). In their devotional imagination, Savant Singh visualized himself as Krishna and Bani Thani as Radha.
- In 1757 CE, Savant Singh abdicated his throne and retired with Bani Thani to the holy groves of **Vrindavan**, where both spent their remaining years in mystical contemplation.

#### 2. The Master Painter: Moradhwaj Nihal Chand (मोरध्वज निहाल चंद)
- Serving as the premier court painter from 1735 to 1757, Nihal Chand transformed the likeness of Bani Thani into the eternal, idealized archetype of the goddess **Radha**:
  - Extremely elongated, half-closed lotus-petal eyes curving upward toward the temples (**Khanjan Nayana** / खंजन नयन).
  - High, sharply arched brows.
  - A long, aristocratic, sharply pointed nose.
  - A delicate, protruding chin and serpentine locks of black hair caressing the cheek.
  - Draped in a diaphanous, translucent muslin veil (*Chunari / Odhani*).
  - Adorned with a delicate pearl nose-stud: the **Besari (बेसरी)** — the ultimate diagnostic ornament of Kishangarh painting!

#### 3. Global Inscription & Postal Stamp
- **Eric Dickinson & Dr. Fayaz Ali**: British scholar Eric Dickinson and Indian researcher Dr. Fayaz Ali brought Kishangarh art to international prominence in the 1940s. Dickinson famously christened Bani Thani as the **"Mona Lisa of India"** (*भारत की मोनालिसा*).
- **Government of India Postal Stamp**: On **5 May 1973**, the Indian Postal Department released a **20-paise commemorative postage stamp** featuring Nihal Chand’s portrait of Bani Thani.
- **Other Masterpieces & Painters**:
  - *Chandni Raat ki Sangeet Goshthi* (Music Party on a Moonlit Night) — painted by master artist **Amarchand (अमरचंद)**.
  - Other artists: Sitaram, Surdhwaj, Badan Singh, Nanakram, and Sawai Ram.

### 3.3 Bikaner Sub-style: Usta & Matheerna Heritage
- **Historical Genesis**: Developed under **Rao Bika** and accelerated during **Maharaja Rai Singh's** close alliance with Akbar.
- **Golden Age**: Reached peak maturity under **Maharaja Anup Singh (1669–1698 CE)**, who patronized royal ateliers and collected illustrated manuscripts (*Anup Sanskrit Library*).
- **The Unique Epigraphic Hallmark**: Unlike all other schools of Rajputana where artists remained anonymous, Bikaner artists **systematically inscribed their names, their father's name, their genealogy, and the precise date of completion** on the back of their paintings! The artists were given the respectful court title of **"Ustad" (उस्ताद)**.

### 3.4 Nagaur & Jaisalmer Sub-styles
- **Nagaur Sub-style**:
  - Reached its height under Maharaja Bakht Singh (1728–1751 CE).
  - Famous for **subdued, faint, chalky pastel colors (बुझे हुए रंग)**: pale terracotta, grey, and cream.
  - Delicate woodcut-like shading and remarkably sheer, transparent drapes on female figures.
- **Jaisalmer Sub-style**:
  - Uniquely celebrated as the **only painting school in Rajasthan that remained 100% immune to Mughal, British, or foreign influence**.
  - Patronized by Maharawal Akhai Singh and Moolraj II.
  - The universal, defining portraiture theme across Jaisalmer art is **Moomal (मूमल)** — the legendary princess of Lodharva.

Encompassing Amber, Jaipur, Alwar, Shekhawati, and Uniara, the Dhundhar School is characterized by close diplomatic ties with the Mughal court, monumental life-sized portraiture, opulent gold-leaf embellishments, and sprawling fresco murals.

### 4.1 Amer & Jaipur Sub-styles

#### 1. Amer Sub-style (Early Phase)
- **Epoch**: Commenced during the reign of **Raja Man Singh I (1589–1614 CE)**.
- **Key Murals & Manuscripts**:
  - Murals at **Bharmal ki Chhatri (1599 CE)** and the garden pavilions of Amer Palace.
  - In 1588 CE, imperial artists at Amer illustrated the **Razmnama (रज्मनामा)** — the monumental Persian translation of the Mahabharata commissioned by Akbar (containing 169 miniatures).

#### 2. Jaipur Sub-style: The Suratkhana & Life-Sized Portraits
- **Sawai Jai Singh II (1700–1743 CE)**: Shifted the capital to Jaipur in 1727 and established **36 royal production ateliers (*Karkhanas*)**, including the dedicated painting studio named the **Suratkhana (सूरतखाना)**.
- **The Golden Age of Sawai Pratap Singh (1778–1803 CE)**: An ardent devotee of Krishna (*Brijnidhi*), Pratap Singh employed over 50 master artists in the Suratkhana, producing hundreds of paintings illustrating the Bhagavata Purana, Gitagovinda, and Ragamala.
- **The Master Painters**:
  1. **Sahibram (साहिबराम)**: The undisputed master of **monumental life-sized portraits (आदमकद चित्र / Aadmakad Chitra)**. He painted the famous life-sized standing portraits of Sawai Ishwari Singh, Sawai Madho Singh I, and Sawai Pratap Singh.
  2. **Mohammad Shah (मोहम्मद शाह)**: Famous for dynamic Krishna Lila depictions, court durbar scenes, and floral border arabesques.
- **Visual Characteristics**: Large fish-shaped eyes, soft round faces, extensive use of emerald green, ruby red, and liquid gold (*Halkari*), and rich jewel-inlay (*Moti-Panna Jadau*) work.

---

### 4.2 Alwar Sub-style (अलवर उपशैली)
- **Foundational Era**: Initiated when Rao Raja Pratap Singh established Alwar as an independent state in 1775 CE, bringing court painters Daluram and Saligram from Jaipur.
- **Golden Era of Maharaja Vinay Singh (1815–1857 CE)**:
  - Deeply patronized book illustrations and miniature art.
  - Commissioned the world-famous illustrated manuscript of Sheikh Saadi’s **Gulistan (गुलिस्तां)**, executed over decades by master artists **Baldev** and **Ghulam Ali** using real gold, pearl, and lapis lazuli pigments.
- **Two Unique Diagnostic Signatures**:
  1. **Miniature Paintings on Elephant Tusk / Ivory (हाथी दाँत के फलकों पर चित्रण)**: The only school in Rajasthan where artists perfected painting fine micro-miniatures directly onto polished elephant ivory sheets.
  2. **Portraits of Courtesans (*Veshyas / Ganikas* / वेश्याओं के चित्र)**: Alwar is unique among Rajput principalities for commissioning dedicated, respectful portrait studies of royal courtesans, nautch dancers, and female court musicians.
- **Master Artists**: Daluram, Baldev, Ghulam Ali, Saligram, Jamnadas, and Chhote Lal.

---

### 4.3 Shekhawati & Uniara Sub-styles
- **Shekhawati Sub-style**: Flourished as a wall-painting (*Fresco*) idiom rather than paper miniatures. Local painters (*Chiteras*) covered thousands of square meters of haveli walls with wet-plaster *Ala-Gila* murals, moving from mythological Krishna Lila scenes to depictions of British technology (steam locomotives, motorcars, and airplanes).
- **Uniara Sub-style**: Developed in the Uniara Thikana (Tonk), representing an exquisite cross-pollination between **Dhundhar and Hadoti** styles. Patronized by Rao Sardar Singh; renowned master artists include **Dheema (धीमा)**, **Mir Baksh (मीर बख्श)**, Kashi, and Ramlakhan.

---

## 5. The Hadoti School (हाड़ौती स्कूल) — Nature, Wilderness & The Royal Hunt

The Hadoti School, centered in the rugged gorges and verdant forests of the Chambal river basin (Bundi and Kota), is internationally recognized for its dynamic depiction of wild flora and fauna, monsoon squalls, and dramatic hunting expeditions.

### 5.1 Bundi Sub-style: The School of Birds & Animals
- **International Acclaim**: Celebrated globally as the **"School of Birds and Animals" (पशु-पक्षियों की चित्रशैली)** due to its unmatched intimacy with the natural world.
- **Landscape Anatomy**: Paintings are laden with dense jungle hills, cascading waterfalls, banyan and plantain trees, blossoming lotuses in stepped tanks, and vivid fauna (dancing peacocks, roaring tigers, caparisoned elephants, and water birds).
- **The Chitrashala / Rang Mahal of Bundi**:
  - Commissioned inside the Bundi Garh Palace by **Maharao Ummed Singh (1749–1770 CE)**.
  - Consists of world-famous continuous wall-to-ceiling fresco galleries depicting Krishna swinging with the Gopis, royal monsoon processions, and Rama’s coronation.
  - Famous as the **"Paradise of Murals"** (*भित्ति चित्रों का स्वर्ग*).
- **Color Palette & Lighting**: Bright red, emerald green, and golden yellow; famous for nocturnal scenes illuminated by flashes of lightning against inky black monsoon clouds (*Bijli ki Chamak*).
- **Master Artists**: Surjan (सुर्जन), Ahmad Ali (अहमद अली), Ramlal, Sadhu Ram, and Kishan.

---

### 5.2 Kota Sub-style: The School of the Royal Hunt (*Shikar*)
- **Historical Partition**: Originally an offshoot of Bundi art; emerged as an independent school under **Maharao Ram Singh I (1696–1707 CE)**.
- **Golden Era of Maharao Ummed Singh I (1770–1819 CE)**: Ummed Singh’s passion for big-game hunting in the dense ravines of the Chambal river elevated hunting imagery to high art.
- **The Supreme Hallmark — Women Hunting in Jungles**:
  - The single most distinguishing feature of Kota painting: **queens, royal princesses, and noblewomen are depicted actively hunting wild animals alongside kings**, riding galloping stallions or mounted on elephants, armed with matchlock guns, spears, and bows!
  - No other art tradition in India portrays royal women engaged in fierce wilderness combat with such power and frequency.
- **Pushtimarg Religious Fervor**: Deeply influenced by the Vallabh sect (Krishna as *Brajraj* and *Nathji*).
- **Master Artists & Masterpieces**:
  - Master artist **Dalu (डालू)** executed the celebrated **Kota Ragamala Set in 1768 CE** (the largest illustrated Ragamala manuscript in Hadoti, containing 36 miniatures).
  - Other master painters: Nuru Mohammad (नूर मोहम्मद), Lachhiram, Govindram, and Raghunath.

## 7. The 25 Deadly Prelims Traps: Rajasthani Painting

1. **The Pioneer of Scientific Classification**: **Dr. Anand Kentish Coomaraswamy** classified Rajasthani painting scientifically in his 1916 book **"Rajput Painting"**, NOT James Tod.
2. **"Rajput Painting" vs "Rajasthani Chitrasheili"**: Coomaraswamy coined *"Rajput Painting"*; **Rai Krishnadas** discarded dynastic titles and established the geographical term **"Rajasthani Chitrasheili"**.
3. **Earliest Dated Illustrated Manuscript**: **Shravaka Pratikramana Sutra Churni (1260 CE)**, painted by **Kamalchandra** at Ahar during Guhil King **Rawal Tejasimha's** reign, NOT Kumbha's reign.
4. **The "Chiteron ki Ovari" Founder**: Established in Udaipur City Palace by **Maharana Jagat Singh I** (1628–1652 CE), popularly known as **"Tasveeraan ro Karkhano"**.
5. **Sahibdin vs Sahibram Trap (Top RPSC Killer Trap!)**:
   - **Sahibdin (साहिबदीन)**: Master painter of the **Mewar School (Udaipur)** under Jagat Singh I (painted *Ragamala*, *Gitagovinda*, *Rasikapriya*, *Arsha Ramayana*).
   - **Sahibram (साहिबराम)**: Master painter of the **Dhundhar School (Jaipur)** under Ishwari Singh, Madho Singh, and Pratap Singh (pioneered **Aadmakad life-sized portraits**).
6. **The Chavand Ragamala Date & Artist**: Painted in **1605 CE** at Chavand by **Nasiruddin (Nisardi)** during the reign of **Maharana Amar Singh I**, NOT during Kumbha or Sanga.
7. **Pichhwai Fabric & Subject**: Pichhwai is painting executed on **cloth** (NOT paper or wood) hung behind the Shrinathji idol depicting Krishna Leela and Kamdhenu cows.
8. **Celebrated Women Painters of Rajasthan**:
   - **Kamala & Ilaichi**: Master painters of the **Nathdwara Sub-style (Mewar School)**.
   - **Sahiba (साहिबा)**: Female painter of the **Ajmer Sub-style (Marwar School)**.
9. **Devgarh School Discoverer**: Brought to light by art historian **Dr. Shridhar Andhare**; master painters were **Bagta, Chokha, and Kavala**.
10. **The "Mona Lisa of India" Coiner**: **Eric Dickinson** termed Bani Thani the "Mona Lisa of India", NOT Karl Khandalavala or Anand Coomaraswamy.
11. **Bani Thani Postal Stamp Details**: Issued on **5 May 1973** by the Government of India; denomination was **20 paise**.
12. **The Signature Diagnostic Ornament of Bani Thani**: The **Besari (बेसरी)** — a delicate pearl nose-ring/stud worn on the nose.
13. **"Chandni Raat ki Sangeet Goshthi" Artist**: Painted by **Amarchand (अमरचंद)** of the **Kishangarh School**, NOT Nihal Chand.
14. **The Only School Where Artists Signed Their Works**: **Bikaner Sub-style**; Muslim master artists titled **Ustad** inscribed their names, father's names, and dates (*Samvat*).
15. **Usta Art vs Matheerna Art**:
    - **Usta Art (Munawwati)**: Embossed gold work on **camel leather** (Hisam-ud-din Usta).
    - **Matheerna Art**: Traditional religious murals and wooden *Patra* paintings executed by the local **Jain community of Bikaner**.
16. **The School with "Muted / Chalky Colors"**: **Nagaur Sub-style** is characterized by faint, subdued, chalky pastel colors (*बुझे हुए रंग*).
17. **The 100% Indigenous, Uninfluenced School**: **Jaisalmer Sub-style** remained completely untouched by Mughal or British elements; its defining feminine portrait theme is **Moomal**.
18. **The Suratkhana of Jaipur**: Established by **Sawai Jai Singh II** as one of the 36 royal ateliers (*Karkhanas*); reached its zenith under **Sawai Pratap Singh**.
19. **Elephant Tusk Miniature Painting**: Exclusively practiced in the **Alwar Sub-style**, along with dedicated portraits of courtesans (*Ganikas*).
20. **Illustrated "Gulistan" Artists**: Master painters **Baldev and Ghulam Ali** illustrated Sheikh Saadi’s *Gulistan* for **Maharaja Vinay Singh of Alwar**.
21. **The "School of Birds and Animals"**: **Bundi Sub-style** is universally known as the *Pashu-Pakshiyon ki Chitrasheili*.
22. **The Chitrashala of Bundi Patron**: Built inside Garh Palace, Bundi by **Maharao Ummed Singh (1749–1770 CE)**; known as the *"Paradise of Murals"*.
23. **Women Depicted Hunting**: The exclusive hallmark of the **Kota Sub-style** — royal queens and princesses are depicted shooting lions and wild boars in the jungles.
24. **Kota Ragamala (1768 CE) Artist**: Executed by master artist **Dalu (डालू)**, comprising 36 miniatures.
25. **Uniara Sub-style Patron & Artists**: Sub-school of Dhundhar with Hadoti traits, patronized by Sardar Singh; artists were **Dheema, Mir Baksh, Kashi, and Ramlakhan**.

## 8. RPSC RAS Mains High-Yield Model Answers

### 8.1 2-Markers (15–20 Words)

#### Q1: What is "Chiteron ki Ovari"?
**Answer**:
A royal painting studio established in Udaipur City Palace by Maharana Jagat Singh I (1628–1652), popularly called *"Tasveeraan ro Karkhano"*, marking the golden age of Mewar miniature painting.

---

#### Q2: Who gave Bani Thani the title "Mona Lisa of India", and who painted it?
**Answer**:
Painted in 1778 CE by master artist **Moradhwaj Nihal Chand** under patron-poet **Sawai Savant Singh (Nagari Das)**; christened the *"Mona Lisa of India"* by art scholar **Eric Dickinson**.

---

#### Q3: Name two prominent female painters of Rajasthani art and their sub-school.
**Answer**:
**Kamala** and **Ilaichi**, who flourished as master painters in the **Nathdwara Sub-style** (Mewar School), creating celebrated Shrinathji *Pichhwai* paintings.

---

#### Q4: Why is the Bikaner painting school considered unique in Rajasthan?
**Answer**:
Unlike other anonymous Rajput schools, Bikaner painters systematically inscribed their **names, their father's names, and precise completion dates** (*Samvat*) on the back of their artworks under the title *Ustad*.

---

#### Q5: State the defining stylistic characteristic of the Kota painting school.
**Answer**:
Dynamic wilderness hunting scenes (*Shikar*), featuring the unique portrayal of **royal women and queens actively hunting lions and wild boars** alongside kings in the Chambal ravines.

---

### 8.2 5-Markers (50 Words)

#### Q6: Analyze the thematic and artistic features of Pichhwai art of Nathdwara.
**Answer**:
Originating after Shrinathji's idol installation at Nathdwara (1672 CE) by Maharana Raj Singh I, **Pichhwai** is a sacred textile art tradition of the Pushtimarg sect:
1. **Material & Function**: Monumental paintings executed on cloth, suspended directly behind the sanctum idol to reflect the liturgical season or festival (*Annakoot*, *Sharad Purnima*).
2. **Iconography**: Centered on Shrinathji’s Govardhan Leela, flanked by sacred *Kamdhenu* cows, Kadamba trees, lotus ponds, and celestial Gopis.
3. **Master Artists**: Narottam, Ghasiram, and renowned female painters **Kamala and Ilaichi**.

---

#### Q7: Delineate the distinct artistic characteristics of the Kishangarh School of Painting.
**Answer**:
Flourishing under **Maharaja Savant Singh (Nagari Das)** and master artist **Moradhwaj Nihal Chand**, Kishangarh art represents lyrical romanticism:
1. **Facial Idealization**: Radha portrayed as **Bani Thani** with curved lotus eyes (*Khanjan Nayana*), arched brows, pointed chin, serpentine curls, and the signature pearl nose-ring (**Besari**).
2. **Atmospheric Settings**: Lyrical nocturnal landscapes, sprawling white marble terraces, Gundolav lake, and boating pageants (*Chandni Raat ki Goshthi* by Amarchand).
3. **Color Palette**: Dominated by pearl white, soft rose pink, shimmering gold, and azure blue.

---

#### Q8: Differentiate between Sahibdin and Sahibram in Rajasthan's painting traditions.
**Answer**:
Despite similar phonetics, they belong to entirely different schools, eras, and stylistic genres:
1. **Sahibdin (Mewar School)**: 17th-century court painter at Udaipur under Maharana Jagat Singh I; illustrated sacred epics (*Ragamala 1628*, *Gitagovinda 1629*, *Arsha Ramayana 1648*) with bright red/yellow mineral tones and fish-shaped eyes.
2. **Sahibram (Dhundhar/Jaipur School)**: 18th-century court painter at Jaipur under Sawai Ishwari Singh and Pratap Singh; pioneered monumental **life-sized portraits (Aadmakad Chitra)** using subtle shading and gem-inlay work.

---
