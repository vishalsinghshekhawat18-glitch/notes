## 1. Historiography, Classification & Canonical Nomenclature

Rajasthani painting (*राजस्थानी चित्रकला*) developed from the ancient Western Indian miniature tradition (Apabhramsha and Jain manuscript illustration), emerging as an independent aesthetic between the 15th and 19th centuries across the royal courts and thikanas of Rajputana.

### 1.1 The Pioneer Scholars & Terminological Debate
1. **Dr. Anand Kentish Coomaraswamy (1916)**:
   - In his seminal two-volume treatise **"Rajput Painting" (1916)**, Coomaraswamy provided the first scientific classification of Indian painting.
   - He drew an aesthetic distinction between **Mughal Painting** (secular, court-centered, portraiture-driven, realistic) and **Rajput Painting** (sacred, devotional, folk-rooted, lyrical, and romantic).
   - Under the umbrella of *Rajput Painting*, he identified two distinct geographical branches:
     - (a) **Rajasthani School** (plains of Rajputana/Central India);
     - (b) **Pahari School** (Himalayan sub-Himalayan valleys such as Kangra, Basohli, and Guler).
2. **O.C. Gangoly (1926) & Basil Gray (1948)**:
   - Authored *Masterpieces of Rajput Painting* (1926) and *Rajput Painting* (1948), further documenting regional collections in Western institutions.
3. **Rai Krishnadas**:
   - In *Bharat ki Chitrakala*, he argued that the term "Rajput Painting" was overly dynastic and communal, since artists, themes, and community participation encompassed all strata of society. He established the standard academic term **"Rajasthani Chitrasheili" (राजस्थानी चित्रशैली)** based on geographical and linguistic identity.
4. **Other Canonical Scholars**:
   - W.G. Archer (*Indian Paintings from Rajasthan*), Dr. Moti Chandra, Dr. Shridhar Andhare (who brought the Devgarh sub-style to light), Karl Khandalavala, and Ramgopal Vijayvargiya.

## 2. The Mewar School (मेवाड़ स्कूल) — The Fountainhead

The Mewar School is widely recognized in art history as the **fountainhead or mother style** of Rajasthani painting. Because Mewar maintained long political resistance against central Delhi authority, its painters retained early indigenous conventions derived from Western Indian manuscript painting longer than other courts.

### 2.1 Earliest Dated Illustrated Manuscripts
1. **Shravaka Pratikramana Sutra Churni (श्रावक प्रतिक्रमण सूत्र चूर्णी)**:
   - **Date & Site**: Executed in **1260 CE (Vikram Samvat 1317)** at **Ahar (Mewar)**.
   - **Patron Monarch**: Guhil king **Rawal Tejasimha (रावल तेजसिंह)**.
   - **Master Scribe-Artist**: Illustrated on palm leaf by **Kamalchandra (कमलचंद्र)**.
   - **Historical Status**: The **earliest dated illustrated manuscript** of Rajasthani painting identified to date (preserved in the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston).
2. **Supasanahachariyam (सुपासनाहचरियं / पार्श्वनाथ चरित्र)**:
   - **Date & Site**: Executed in **1422–1423 CE** at **Delwara (Mewar)**.
   - **Patron Monarch**: Maharana **Mokal**.
   - **Master Scribe-Artist**: Illustrated by **Hirananda (हीरानंद)**.
   - **Stylistic Significance**: Demonstrates the gradual transition from angular Apabhramsha conventions toward softer indigenous Rajput facial contours.
3. **Earlier Western Indian Palm-Leaf Holdings**:
   - The *Oudha Niryukti Vritti* and *Dasa Vaikalika Sutra Churni* (dated **1060 CE**, preserved in the Jinbhadra Suri Grantha Bhandar, Jaisalmer Fort) represent even earlier Western Indian palm-leaf manuscripts preserved in Rajasthan, forming the palaeographical precursor to regional miniature painting.

### 2.2 Udaipur Sub-style: The Golden Age of Sahibdin & Manohar
Under **Maharana Jagat Singh I (1628–1652 CE)**, Udaipur painting reached its classical maturity. Jagat Singh I established an official royal painting studio within the City Palace of Udaipur:
> **"Chiteron ki Ovari" (चितेरों की ओवरी)**, popularly celebrated in Rajasthani records as **"Tasveeraan ro Karkhano" (तस्वीराँ रो कारखानो)**.

#### The Master Painters: Sahibdin & Manohar
- **Sahibdin (साहिबदीन)**: A master court artist who illustrated major epic and lyrical series:
  - *Ragamala Set* (1628 CE)
  - *Gitagovinda* (1629 CE)
  - *Rasikapriya* of Keshavdas (1630 CE)
  - *Suryavansha* (1645 CE)
  - *Arsha Ramayana* (1648–1652 CE; illustrated the Yuddha Kanda and Sundar Kanda)
- **Manohar (मनोहर)**: A contemporary court painter who illustrated the *Bal Kanda* of the Ramayana in 1649 CE.
- **Visual Characteristics**:
  - Fish-shaped eyes (*Meenakshi* / मीनाक्षी).
  - Short, sturdy human figures; male figures wearing Mewari turbans (*Pheta*), waistbands (*Patka*), and four-pointed tunics.
  - Saturated mineral pigments: deep cinnabar red (*Hinglu*), saffron yellow (*Peedi*), lapis lazuli blue, and emerald green.
  - Vegetative motifs: stylized **Kadamba trees (कदंब वृक्ष)**, flowering shrubs, and lotus-filled pools.

---

### 2.3 Chavand Sub-style (चावंड उपशैली)
- **Historical Genesis**: Developed when **Maharana Pratap** made Chavand his independent hill capital in **1585 CE** during guerrilla resistance against Mughal forces.
- **The Chavand Ragamala (1605 CE)**: Master artist **Nasiruddin (Nisardi / निसारदीन)** painted the celebrated **Ragamala Set** at Chavand, bearing the completion date of **10 April 1605 CE** during the early reign of **Maharana Amar Singh I**.
- **Significance**: Represents a crucial transitional benchmark of pre-Mughal indigenous Rajput expression, featuring bold primary colors and unpretentious folk vitality.

---

### 2.4 Nathdwara Sub-style: Pichhwai Cloth Art (नाथद्वारा उपशैली)
- **Historical Origin**: Began after the sacred black marble idol of **Bhagwan Shrinathji** was relocated from Govardhan (Braj) and installed at **Sihad village (modern Nathdwara)** by **Maharana Raj Singh I in 1672 CE**.
- **Aesthetic Synthesis**: Merged indigenous Mewari vigor with the devotional lyricism of the Pushtimarg (Vallabh) sect.

- **Pichhwai (पिछवाई)**: Large textile paintings executed on coarse cotton cloth hung behind the principal idol of Shrinathji. They change according to festivals (*Annakoot*, *Janmashtami*, *Radhashtami*) and liturgical seasons (*Sharad Purnima*, *Holi*).
- **Iconography**: Shrinathji depicted with left hand raised lifting Mount Govardhan; surrounded by sacred **Kamdhenu cows**, banana grooves (*Kela*), Kadamba foliage, and celestial aerial vehicles (*Vimana*).
- **Celebrated Women Painters**: Nathdwara holds a distinct place in Rajasthan art history for its prominent documented women master artists: **Kamala (कमला)** and **Ilaichi (इलायची)**.

### 2.5 Devgarh & Shahpura Sub-styles
- **Devgarh Sub-style (देवगढ़)**:
  - Developed in the first-class *Thikana* of Devgarh, founded in 1680 CE by **Rawat Dwarkadas Chundawat** (a 16th Umrao of Mewar).
  - Brought to international academic recognition by art historian **Dr. Shridhar Andhare**.
  - Stylistically represents a blend of **Mewar, Marwar, and Dhundhar** elements.
  - Master artists: **Bagta (बगता)**, **Chokha (चोखा)**, and **Kavala (कवला)**.
  - Key subjects: Dynamic wild elephant fights (*Hathi ki Ladai*), raw royal hunting expeditions (*Shikar*), and court ceremonies dominated by rich yellow-green palettes.
- **Shahpura Sub-style (शाहपुरा)**:
  - Known as the center of **Phad Painting (फड़ चित्रांकन)**, practiced by the **Joshi family** (Chhipa community).
  - Long scroll paintings on coarse cloth depicting folk-deity epics: **Pabuji ki Phad** (accompanied by the Ravanhatha played by Bhopa priests) and **Devnarayanji ki Phad** (accompanied by the Jantar played by Gurjar priests).
  - Renowned masters: Shree Lal Joshi (Padma Shri recipient), Pradeep Joshi, and pioneer female artist **Parvati Joshi**.

## 3. The Marwar School (मारवाड़ स्कूल) — The Desert Masters

Covering the vast arid terrain of Jodhpur, Bikaner, Kishangarh, Nagaur, and Jaisalmer, the Marwar School is distinguished by expansive desert landscapes, radiant primary colors (especially yellow ochre), and heroic equestrian portraiture.

### 3.1 Jodhpur Sub-style (जोधपुर उपशैली)
- **Early Evolution**: Developed during the reign of **Rao Maldeo (1531–1562 CE)**, exemplified by the bold battle murals in **Chokhelao Mahal (Mehrangarh Fort)** and the illustrated *Uttaradhyayana Sutra* (1591 CE).
- **Mughal Synthesis**: Progressed under Mota Raja Udai Singh and Maharaja Gaj Singh; refined under Maharaja Jaswant Singh I (*Barahmasa* and *Ragamala* albums).
- **The Golden Age of Nath Sect Manuscripts**: Under **Maharaja Man Singh (1803–1843 CE)**, who was an ardent devotee of Ayas Devnath, Jodhpur artists produced monumental illuminated manuscripts:
  - *Nath Charitra*
  - *Shiva Purana*
  - *Durgacharit*
  - *Siddha Siddhanta Paddhati*
- **Visual Markers**:
  - Dominance of bright **yellow ochre (पीला रंग)** and deep red.
  - Swirling, spiral monsoon clouds (*Ghumadte Badal*).
  - Tall male figures wearing high pointed Marwari turbans (**Khidkiya Pagdi** / खिड़किया पगड़ी), broad sashes, and carrying swords.
  - Almond-shaped eyes (*Badam si Aankhein*).
  - Master artists: Amar Das, Bhati Shivdas, Dan Bhati, Shankar Das, Chhagan Das, Jeewan Das, and Ram Singh Bhati.

### 3.2 Kishangarh Sub-style: The Lyrical Zenith & Bani Thani
The Kishangarh school represents a distinctive expression of Vaishnava romantic lyricism and aristocratic courtly elegance.

#### 1. Historical & Devotional Background
- **Maharaja Savant Singh (1748–1764 CE)**: A Rathore ruler and devotional poet who composed under the pen name **Nagari Das (नागरीदास)**.
- **Bani Thani (बणी-ठणी / Rasik Bihari)**: A singer, poetess, and royal consort whose refined styling and spiritual devotion inspired the title *Bani-Thani* ("The Well-Attired One"). In courtly devotional verse, Savant Singh and Bani Thani were visualized as Krishna and Radha.
- In 1757 CE, Savant Singh renounced his throne and relocated with Bani Thani to the holy town of **Vrindavan**, where both spent their remaining years in religious contemplation.

#### 2. Master Painter Moradhwaj Nihal Chand (मोरध्वज निहाल चंद)
- Court painter from 1735 to 1757; credited with establishing the idealized Kishangarh female facial type:
  - Elongated, upward-curving lotus-petal eyes (**Khanjan Nayana** / खंजन नयन).
  - Sharply arched brows and a long, slender, pointed nose.
  - A receding forehead, delicate pointed chin, and serpentine curls of hair falling along the cheek.
  - Draped in a sheer, patterned muslin veil (*Odhani*).
  - Adorned with a small pearl nose-stud: the **Besari (बेसरी)** — the diagnostic ornament of Kishangarh painting in state exams.

#### 3. Scholarly Recognition & Postal Stamp
- **Eric Dickinson & Dr. Fayaz Ali**: Brought Kishangarh art to international attention in the 1940s. Dickinson coined the popular comparison **"Mona Lisa of India"** (*भारत की मोनालिसा*).
- **Commemorative Postage Stamp**: On **5 May 1973**, the Government of India Department of Posts issued a **20-paise commemorative postage stamp** featuring Nihal Chand’s portrait of Bani Thani.
- **Other Masterpieces & Artists**:
  - *Chandni Raat ki Sangeet Goshthi* (Musical Gathering on a Moonlit Night) — painted by master artist **Amarchand (अमरचंद)**.
  - Other artists: Sitaram, Surdhwaj, Badan Singh, Nanakram, and Sawai Ram.

### 3.3 Bikaner Sub-style: Usta & Matheerna Heritage
- **Historical Genesis**: Emerged under Rao Bika; gained courtly refinement during **Maharaja Rai Singh's** close diplomatic and military ties with Akbar's court.
- **Golden Age**: Reached peak maturity under **Maharaja Anup Singh (1669–1698 CE)**, who patronized royal ateliers and assembled illuminated manuscripts in the *Anup Sanskrit Library*.
- **The Unique Epigraphic Hallmark**: Unlike most other Rajput schools where paintings remained unsigned, Bikaner artists **routinely inscribed their name, their father's name, their genealogy, and the precise Vikram Samvat completion date** on the reverse of their paintings. The master artists held the official court title of **"Ustad" (उस्ताद)**.

### 3.4 Nagaur & Jaisalmer Sub-styles
- **Nagaur Sub-style**:
  - Reached its height under Maharaja Bakht Singh (1728–1751 CE).
  - Famed for **subdued, chalky pastel colors (बुझे हुए रंग)**: muted terracotta, ash grey, pale cream, and olive.
  - Characterized by delicate stippled shading and translucent textiles.
- **Jaisalmer Sub-style**:
  - Noted in regional art literature for retaining indigenous folk conventions with minimal outside courtly influence.
  - Patronized by Maharawal Akhai Singh and Moolraj II.
  - The central, recurring female portrait theme is **Moomal (मूमल)** — the legendary princess of Lodharva.

## 4. The Dhundhar School (ढूंढाड़ स्कूल) — Courtly Synthesis

Covering Amber, Jaipur, Alwar, Shekhawati, and Uniara, the Dhundhar School is characterized by historical contact with Mughal imperial ateliers, life-sized portraiture, extensive gold-leaf detailing, and vast haveli fresco cycles.

### 4.1 Amer & Jaipur Sub-styles

#### 1. Amer Sub-style (Early Phase)
- **Epoch**: Commenced during the reign of **Raja Man Singh I (1589–1614 CE)**.
- **Key Murals & Manuscripts**:
  - Murals at **Bharmal ki Chhatri (1599 CE)** and the early pavilions of Amer Palace.
  - In **1588 CE**, imperial artists at Amer illustrated the **Razmnama (रज्मनामा)** — the Persian translation of the Mahabharata commissioned by Akbar (now preserved in the City Palace Museum, Jaipur).

#### 2. Jaipur Sub-style: The Suratkhana & Life-Sized Portraits
- **Sawai Jai Singh II (1700–1743 CE)**: Shifted the capital to Jaipur in 1727 and established **36 royal production departments (*Karkhanas*)**, including the dedicated painting atelier named the **Suratkhana (सूरतखाना)**.
- **The Golden Age of Sawai Pratap Singh (1778–1803 CE)**: A devotee of Krishna who wrote under the pen name *Brijnidhi*, Pratap Singh employed numerous artists in the Suratkhana, producing extensive series illustrating the Bhagavata Purana, Gitagovinda, and Ragamala.
- **The Master Painters**:
  1. **Sahibram (साहिबराम)**: Renowned master of **life-sized standing portraits (आदमकद चित्र / Aadmakad Chitra)**. He painted large portraits of Sawai Ishwari Singh, Sawai Madho Singh I, and Sawai Pratap Singh.
  2. **Mohammad Shah (मोहम्मद शाह)**: Known for court durbar scenes, Krishna Lila narratives, and decorative floral borders.
- **Visual Markers**: Rounded faces, large fish-like eyes, rich emerald green (*Hara Rang*), ruby red, liquid gold wash (*Halkari*), and applied jewel-inlay (*Jadau*) work.

---

### 4.2 Alwar Sub-style (अलवर उपशैली)
- **Foundational Era**: Began when Rao Raja Pratap Singh established Alwar as an independent state in 1775 CE, inviting painters Daluram and Saligram from Jaipur.
- **Golden Era of Maharaja Vinay Singh (1815–1857 CE)**:
  - Extensively patronized manuscript illustration.
  - Commissioned the famous illustrated manuscript of Sheikh Saadi’s **Gulistan (गुलिस्तां)**, executed over years by master artists **Baldev** and **Ghulam Ali** using gold leaf and lapis lazuli pigments.
- **Two Diagnostic Signatures in State Exams**:
  1. **Miniatures on Elephant Ivory Sheets (हाथी दाँत के फलकों पर चित्रण)**: Fine micro-miniature portraits painted directly onto polished sheets of ivory.
  2. **Portraits of Courtesans (*Veshyas / Ganikas* / वेश्याओं के चित्र)**: Alwar is unique among Rajasthani courts for commissioning formal, dignified portrait studies of court dancers, musicians, and courtesans.
- **Master Artists**: Daluram, Baldev, Ghulam Ali, Saligram, Jamnadas, and Chhote Lal.

---

### 4.3 Shekhawati & Uniara Sub-styles
- **Shekhawati Sub-style**: Flourished as a mural idiom on the interior and exterior walls of merchant mansions. Local artisans (*Chiteras*) applied wet-plaster (*Ala-Gila*) and dry-wash (*Secco*) methods, depicting mythological stories, folk epics (Dhola-Maru), and late 19th/early 20th-century colonial technologies (trains, cars, biplanes, gramophones). In **June 2026**, *The Painted Haveli Towns of Shekhawati* was submitted to India's **UNESCO Tentative List**.
- **Uniara Sub-style**: Developed in the Uniara Thikana (Tonk), displaying an artistic synthesis of **Dhundhar and Hadoti** styles. Patronized by Rao Sardar Singh; master painters include **Dheema (धीमा)**, **Mir Baksh (मीर बख्श)**, Kashi, and Ramlakhan.

## 5. The Hadoti School (हाड़ौती स्कूल) — Wilderness & The Hunt

Centered along the Chambal river basin in Bundi and Kota, the Hadoti School is celebrated for its dynamic depictions of wildlife, dense forests, seasonal storms, and royal hunts.

### 5.1 Bundi Sub-style: The School of Birds & Animals
- **Conventional Epithet**: Celebrated in art history and competitive exams as the **"School of Birds and Animals" (पशु-पक्षियों की चित्रशैली)** due to its rich observation of natural fauna.
- **Landscape Features**: Hillsides covered with dense jungle, plantain and banyan trees, cascading springs, lotus-filled reservoirs, dancing peacocks, and wild ducks.
- **The Chitrashala / Rang Mahal of Bundi**:
  - Commissioned inside the Bundi Garh Palace complex by **Maharao Ummed Singh (1749–1770 CE)**.
  - A world-renowned open gallery of wall murals depicting Krishna Leela, the Gopis, royal processions, and seasonal landscapes.
  - Popularly referred to in state heritage literature as the **"Paradise of Murals"** (*भित्ति चित्रों का स्वर्ग*).
- **Color & Atmospheric Treatment**: Rich reds, bright greens, and golden yellows; famous for nighttime scenes with lightning forks flashing against inky black monsoon clouds (*Bijli ki Chamak*).
- **Master Artists**: Surjan (सुर्जन), Ahmad Ali (अहमद अली), Ramlal, Sadhu Ram, and Kishan.

---

### 5.2 Kota Sub-style: The School of the Royal Hunt (*Shikar*)
- **Historical Emergence**: Began as an offshoot of Bundi art; became an independent court style under **Maharao Ram Singh I (1696–1707 CE)**.
- **Golden Era of Maharao Ummed Singh I (1770–1819 CE)**: Ummed Singh’s active participation in hunting in the dense Chambal ravines made royal hunting (*Shikar*) the primary subject of Kota painting.
- **Supreme Exam Hallmark — Women Depicted Hunting**:
  - The unique diagnostic feature of Kota painting: **queens, royal princesses, and noblewomen are depicted actively hunting wild animals alongside men**, riding galloping horses or riding atop elephants, aiming matchlock guns, spears, and bows at tigers, lions, and wild boars.
  - No other traditional Indian painting school depicts women participating in big-game hunting with this frequency and prominence.
- **Vallabh Sect (Pushtimarg) Influence**: Alongside hunting scenes, artists produced portraits of Krishna as *Brajraj* and *Shrinathji*.
- **Master Artists & Masterpieces**:
  - Master artist **Dalu (डालू)** painted the celebrated **Kota Ragamala Set in 1768 CE** (the largest known Ragamala set of Hadoti, containing 36 miniatures).
  - Other master painters: Nuru Mohammad (नूर मोहम्मद), Lachhiram, Govindram, and Raghunath.

## 6. Materials, Pigments, Supports & Technical Vocabulary

Traditional Rajasthani painting required specialized techniques, natural materials, and rigorous craft discipline:

1. **Supports**:
   - **Vasli (वसली)**: Layered paper support made by pasting sheets of handmade paper (*Kagaz*) together with starch paste (*Leyi*), pressed flat, and polished with smooth agate river stones (*Ghutai*) to produce an even drawing surface.
   - **Cloth**: Coarse cotton fabric treated with rice starch, used for *Pichhwais* (Nathdwara) and *Phads* (Shahpura).
   - **Wood**: Fine wooden book covers (*Patras*) used in Bikaner and Jain libraries.
   - **Ivory Sheets**: Polished elephant tusk slabs used for fine micro-miniatures in Alwar.
2. **Brushes**:
   - Made from fine animal hair, especially squirrel tail hair (*Gilehri ki dum ke baal*) inserted into pigeon or eagle feather quills for hairline precision.
3. **Natural Pigments**:
   - **Red**: Cinnabar (*Hinglu* / vermilion), red ochre (*Geru*).
   - **Yellow**: Orpiment (*Hartal*), yellow ochre (*Peedi* / *Ramaraj*).
   - **Blue**: Lapis lazuli (*Lajward*), indigo (*Neel*).
   - **Green**: Malachite (*Dana-Farang*), verdigris (*Zangar*).
   - **White**: Powdered conch shell (*Shankha*), lead white (*Safeda*), chalk (*Khadia*).
   - **Black**: Lampblack soot (*Kajol*).
   - **Gold & Silver**: Applied as gold leaf (*Varq*) or ground into liquid suspension (*Halkari*) using gum arabic.
4. **Thematic Genres**:
   - **Ragamala (रागमाला)**: Visual personification of Indian musical modes (6 principal Ragas and their associated Raginis).
   - **Barahmasa (बारहमासा)**: Poetic cycle depicting the twelve months of the year, describing seasonal human emotions and romantic longing based on Keshavdas's poetry.
   - **Nayika Bheda (नायिका भेद)**: Classical classification of heroines according to emotional states and circumstances (*Abhisarika*, *Khandita*, *Vipralabdha*, *Proshitpatika*).

## 8. Twenty Examination Traps to Avoid

1. **The Sahibdin vs Sahibram Distinction (Deadliest RPSC Trap!)**:
   - **Sahibdin (साहिबदीन)**: 17th-century master of **Mewar (Udaipur)** under Jagat Singh I (painted *Ragamala 1628*, *Gitagovinda 1629*, *Arsha Ramayana 1648–52*).
   - **Sahibram (साहिबराम)**: 18th-century master of **Dhundhar (Jaipur)** under Ishwari Singh, Madho Singh, and Pratap Singh (pioneered **Aadmakad life-sized portraits**).
2. **Pioneer of Scientific Classification**:
   - **Dr. Anand Kentish Coomaraswamy** classified Rajasthani painting scientifically in his 1916 work **"Rajput Painting"**, NOT James Tod.
3. **"Rajput Painting" vs "Rajasthani Chitrasheili"**:
   - Coomaraswamy coined *"Rajput Painting"*; **Rai Krishnadas** advocated the geographic and cultural term **"Rajasthani Chitrasheili"** in *Bharat ki Chitrakala*.
4. **Earliest Dated Illustrated Manuscript**:
   - **Shravaka Pratikramana Sutra Churni (1260 CE / VS 1317)**, painted by **Kamalchandra** at Ahar under Guhil king **Rawal Tejasimha**, NOT Maharana Kumbha.
5. **The "Chiteron ki Ovari" Founder**:
   - Founded in Udaipur City Palace by **Maharana Jagat Singh I** (1628–1652 CE), popularly known as **"Tasveeraan ro Karkhano"**.
6. **Chavand Ragamala Date & Artist**:
   - Painted in **1605 CE** at Chavand by **Nasiruddin (Nisardi)** during the reign of **Maharana Amar Singh I**, NOT during Rana Sanga or Kumbha.
7. **Pichhwai Material & Function**:
   - Pichhwai is painting executed on **cloth** (NOT paper or wood) hung behind the Shrinathji idol depicting Krishna Leela and Kamdhenu cows.
8. **Celebrated Women Painters of Rajasthan**:
   - **Kamala & Ilaichi**: Master painters of the **Nathdwara Sub-style** (Mewar School).
   - **Sahiba (साहिबा)**: Female painter of the **Ajmer Sub-style** (Marwar School).
9. **Devgarh School Discoverer**:
   - Brought to academic light by art historian **Dr. Shridhar Andhare**; master painters were **Bagta, Chokha, and Kavala**.
10. **The "Mona Lisa of India" Attribution**:
    - Coined by British scholar **Eric Dickinson**, NOT Karl Khandalavala or Coomaraswamy.
11. **Bani Thani Postal Stamp Details**:
    - Issued on **5 May 1973** by the Government of India Department of Posts; face value was **20 paise**.
12. **Signature Diagnostic Ornament of Bani Thani**:
    - The **Besari (बेसरी)** — a delicate pearl nose-ring/stud, NOT a necklace or earring.
13. **"Chandni Raat ki Sangeet Goshthi" Artist**:
    - Painted by **Amarchand (अमरचंद)** of Kishangarh, NOT Nihal Chand.
14. **The School That Systematically Signed Artworks**:
    - **Bikaner Sub-style**; Muslim master artists titled **Ustad** inscribed their names, father's names, and precise dates (*Samvat*) on the reverse.
15. **Usta Art vs Matheerna Art in Bikaner**:
    - **Usta Art (Munawwati)**: Embossed gilded relief on **camel leather** (Hisam-ud-din Usta).
    - **Matheerna Art**: Traditional religious murals and wooden *Patra* paintings executed by the local **Jain community of Bikaner**.
16. **Muted / Chalky Colors**:
    - **Nagaur Sub-style** is characterized by faint, subdued, chalky pastel colors (*बुझे हुए रंग*).
17. **Elephant Tusk Miniature Painting**:
    - Practiced extensively in the **Alwar Sub-style**, along with dedicated portraits of courtesans (*Ganikas*).
18. **The "School of Birds and Animals"**:
    - **Bundi Sub-style** is universally recognized as the *Pashu-Pakshiyon ki Chitrasheili*.
19. **The Chitrashala of Bundi Patron**:
    - Built inside Garh Palace, Bundi by **Maharao Ummed Singh (1749–1770 CE)**; known as the *"Paradise of Murals"*.
20. **Women Depicted Hunting**:
    - The exclusive hallmark of the **Kota Sub-style** — royal queens and princesses are depicted shooting lions, tigers, and boars in the jungles.

## 10. RPSC RAS Mains Examination Frameworks: Model Answers

### 10.1 2-Markers (15–20 Words / 2 Marks Each)

#### Q1: What was "Chiteron ki Ovari"?
**Answer**:
A royal painting studio established within Udaipur City Palace by **Maharana Jagat Singh I** (1628–1652 CE), popularly called *"Tasveeraan ro Karkhano"*, marking Mewar miniature painting's golden age.

---

#### Q2: What is the significance of the Chavand Ragamala of 1605 CE?
**Answer**:
Painted on 10 April 1605 CE at Chavand by master artist **Nasiruddin (Nisardi)** during Maharana Amar Singh I's reign, it represents the foundational benchmark of pre-Mughal indigenous Rajput expression.

---

#### Q3: State the defining thematic feature of the Kota painting school.
**Answer**:
Dynamic wilderness hunting scenes (*Shikar*), featuring the unique portrayal of **royal women actively hunting lions, tigers, and wild boars** alongside men in the Chambal ravines.

---

#### Q4: Why is the Bikaner painting school considered unique in Rajasthan?
**Answer**:
Unlike other anonymous Rajput traditions, Muslim master painters (*Ustads*) systematically inscribed their **names, fathers' names, and precise completion dates** on the back of their artworks.

---

#### Q5: Name two celebrated female painters of Rajasthan and their associated sub-school.
**Answer**:
**Kamala** and **Ilaichi**, who flourished as master painters in the **Nathdwara Sub-style** (Mewar School), creating devotional *Pichhwai* textile art for Shrinathji.

### 10.2 5-Markers (50 Words / 5 Marks Each)

#### Q6: Analyze the thematic and artistic features of Pichhwai art of Nathdwara.
**Answer**:
Originating after Shrinathji's idol installation at Nathdwara (1672 CE) under Maharana Raj Singh I, **Pichhwai** is a sacred textile art tradition of the Pushtimarg (Vallabh) sect:
1. **Material & Function**: Monumental paintings executed on starched cotton cloth, suspended directly behind the sanctum idol to match liturgical seasons (*Sharad Purnima*) or festivals (*Annakoot*).
2. **Iconography**: Centered on Shrinathji lifting Mount Govardhan, accompanied by Kamdhenu cows, Kadamba trees, lotus ponds, and celestial Gopis.
3. **Master Artists**: Narottam, Ghasiram, and documented female painters **Kamala and Ilaichi**.

---

#### Q7: Delineate the distinct artistic characteristics of the Kishangarh School of Painting.
**Answer**:
Flourishing under **Maharaja Savant Singh (Nagari Das)** and master painter **Moradhwaj Nihal Chand**, Kishangarh art represents refined romantic lyricism:
1. **Facial Type**: Radha portrayed as **Bani Thani** with elongated curved lotus eyes (*Khanjan Nayana*), arched brows, pointed chin, serpentine curls, and the signature pearl nose-ring (**Besari**).
2. **Atmospheric Settings**: Nocturnal panoramic landscapes, marble palace terraces, Gundolav lake, and boating parties (*Chandni Raat ki Goshthi* by Amarchand).
3. **Color Palette**: Dominated by pearl white, soft rose pink, shimmering gold, and azure blue.

---

#### Q8: Differentiate between Sahibdin and Sahibram in Rajasthan's painting traditions.
**Answer**:
Despite phonetically similar names, they belong to different schools, centuries, and genres:
1. **Sahibdin (Mewar School)**: 17th-century court painter at Udaipur under Maharana Jagat Singh I; illustrated sacred manuscripts (*Ragamala 1628*, *Gitagovinda 1629*, *Arsha Ramayana 1648–52*) using bold red and yellow mineral pigments and fish-shaped eyes.
2. **Sahibram (Dhundhar/Jaipur School)**: 18th-century court painter at Jaipur under Sawai Ishwari Singh and Pratap Singh; pioneered monumental **life-sized portraits (Aadmakad Chitra)** using delicate shading and applied jewel-inlay work.
