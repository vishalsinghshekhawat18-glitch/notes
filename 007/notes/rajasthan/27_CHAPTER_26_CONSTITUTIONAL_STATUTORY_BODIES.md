# CHAPTER 26: Constitutional & Statutory Bodies of Rajasthan

---

## 1. Architectural Taxonomy of State Accountability & Regulatory Bodies

In the constitutional and administrative matrix of Rajasthan, public administration is held to account, regulated, and staffed by a dual tier of institutions:
1. **Constitutional Bodies**: Conceived directly within the text of the Constitution of India, deriving plenary powers, independence, and operational autonomy from constitutional mandates (e.g., Rajasthan Public Service Commission under Article 315, State Election Commission under Article 243K/243ZA, and State Finance Commission under Article 243-I/243-Y).
2. **Statutory Bodies**: Created by specific enactments of the Parliament of India or the Rajasthan State Legislative Assembly to safeguard human rights, enforce transparency, police administrative corruption, and protect vulnerable classes (e.g., Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission, Rajasthan State Information Commission, Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas, and Rajasthan State Commission for Women).

```
                            STATE ACCOUNTABILITY ARCHITECTURE
                                          │
            ┌─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐
            │                                                           │
   CONSTITUTIONAL BODIES                                        STATUTORY BODIES
   (Direct Constitutional Mandate)                              (Legislative Enactments)
            │                                                           │
   ├── RPSC (Art. 315–323)                                     ├── State Human Rights Comm. (PHRA 1993)
   ├── State Election Comm. (Art. 243K/ZA)                     ├── State Information Comm. (RTI Act 2005)
   └── State Finance Comm. (Art. 243-I/Y)                      ├── Lokayukta & Up-Lokayukta (Act 1973)
                                                               └── State Women's Comm. (Act 1999)
```

## 2. Rajasthan Public Service Commission (RPSC)

### 2.1 Genesis and Historical Evolution
Prior to the integration of Rajputana into Rajasthan, only three princely states possessed their own institutional public service commissions: **Jodhpur (1939)**, **Bikaner (1946)**, and **Jaipur (1940/1947)**. Following the formation of the United State of Greater Rajasthan:
- On **16 August 1949**, the Rajpramukh of Rajasthan promulgated the **Rajasthan Public Service Commission Ordinance, 1949**.
- It was published in the Rajasthan Government Gazette on **20 August 1949**, which marks the formal date of promulgation.
- The Commission became fully functional and inaugurated on **22 December 1949**.
- **Initial Headquarters**: Established initially at **Jaipur**.
- **Shift to Ajmer**: In accordance with the recommendations of the **Satyanarayan Rao Committee (1958)** (which aimed at balanced institutional distribution across major regions following the merger of Ajmer-Merwara on 1 November 1956), the headquarters of the RPSC was permanently shifted from Jaipur to **Ghooghara Ghati, Ajmer**, where it operates today.

### 2.2 Constitutional Provisions (Articles 315 to 323)
- **Article 315**: Mandates a Public Service Commission for the Union and a State Public Service Commission (SPSC) for each State. (Also provides for a Joint State Public Service Commission [JSPSC] created by an Act of Parliament on request of concerned State Legislatures).
- **Article 316**: Appointment and term of office of members.
- **Article 317**: Removal and suspension of a member of a Public Service Commission.
- **Article 318**: Power of the Governor to make regulations determining the number of members of the Commission and their conditions of service.
- **Article 319**: Prohibition as to the holding of offices by members of Commission upon ceasing to be such members.
- **Article 320**: Functions of Public Service Commissions (Recruitment examinations, advisory consultations).
- **Article 321**: Power to extend functions by State Legislature.
- **Article 322**: Expenses of the Commission (salaries, allowances, pensions) charged upon the **Consolidated Fund of the State**.
- **Article 323**: Duty of SPSC to present annually to the Governor a report of work done.

### 2.3 Composition and Service Conditions
- **Strength Evolution**:
  - *1949*: 1 Chairman and 2 Members.
  - *1968*: 1 Chairman and 3 Members.
  - *1973*: 1 Chairman and 4 Members.
  - *1981*: 1 Chairman and 5 Members.
  - *2011 onward*: **1 Chairman and 7 Members** (Total sanctioned strength = **8**).
- **Constitutional Qualification Requirement (Art. 316(1) Proviso)**:
  - As nearly as may be, **one-half (50%)** of the members must be persons who, at the dates of their respective appointments, have held office for at least **ten years** either under the Government of India or under the Government of a State (administrative quota).
  - The remaining members are drawn from non-official/academic/eminent professional backgrounds.
- **Appointing Authority**: The **Governor of Rajasthan** appoints the Chairman and members (Article 316(1)).
- **Tenure**: A member holds office for a term of **6 years** from the date of entering upon office or until attaining the age of **62 years**, whichever is earlier (Article 316(2)). *(Note: The age limit was raised from 60 to 62 years by the 41st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976)*.
- **Resignation**: Addressed in writing under hand to the **Governor of Rajasthan** (Article 316(2)(a)).

### 2.4 Removal and Suspension Protocol (Article 317)
*RPSC Exam Trap: Even though the Governor appoints the Chairman and members of the RPSC, the Governor CANNOT remove them.*

              ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
              │                                                 │
      SC Upholds Charges                                SC Clears Charges
              │                                                 │

- **Grounds for Direct Removal by the President (without SC reference - Art. 317(3))**:
  1. If adjudged an insolvent;
  2. If engaged during the term of office in any paid employment outside the duties of office;
  3. If, in the opinion of the President, unfit to continue in office by reason of infirmity of mind or body.

### 2.5 Post-Retirement Ineligibility & Independence Safeguards (Article 319)
To prevent the executive from dangling future post-retirement patronage:
1. **The Chairman of RPSC** is eligible for appointment as:
   - Chairman of the UPSC, OR
   - Member of the UPSC, OR
   - Chairman of any other State Public Service Commission.
   - *Ineligible for any other employment under the Government of India or the Government of a State.*
2. **A Member of RPSC** is eligible for appointment as:
   - Chairman or Member of the UPSC, OR
   - Chairman of the RPSC, OR
   - Chairman of any other State Public Service Commission.
   - *Ineligible for any other employment under the Government of India or the Government of a State.*
3. **Bar on Reappointment**: A person who holds office as a member of RPSC is, on the expiration of their term, **ineligible for reappointment to that same office** (Article 316(3)).
4. **Financial Autonomy (Article 322)**: Administrative expenses, salaries, allowances, and pensions of members and staff are **charged** upon the Consolidated Fund of the State (non-votable by the Legislative Assembly).
5. **Protection of Service Conditions (Article 318 Proviso)**: Conditions of service of a member cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.

### 2.6 Functions and Limitations (Articles 320 & 323)
- **Primary Duties**:
  - Conduct examinations for appointment to the administrative, police, accounts, and subordinate services of the State (e.g., RAS, RPS, Accounts Service, School Lecturers, Assistant Professors, Sub-Inspectors).
  - Consulted on methods of recruitment to civil services and posts.
  - Consulted on principles to be followed in making appointments, promotions, and transfers from one service to another, and on the suitability of candidates.
  - Consulted on disciplinary matters affecting a person serving under the State Government in a civil capacity (censure, withholding increments, reduction in rank, compulsory retirement, removal, dismissal).
  - Consulted on claims for reimbursement of legal expenses incurred by a civil servant defending legal proceedings.
- **Non-Binding Advisory Role**: The recommendations of the RPSC are purely advisory in nature. The State Government may reject or deviate from the advice, but under **Article 323(2)**:
  - The Commission submits an **Annual Report** to the Governor.
  - The Governor causes the report to be laid before the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly, along with a memorandum explaining the reasons why the advice of the Commission was **not accepted** in respect of any matter.
- **Matters Excluded from RPSC Consultation**:
  - While making reservations of appointments or posts in favour of any backward class of citizens (Article 16(4)).
  - While taking into consideration claims of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the making of appointments (Article 335).
  - The Governor can make regulations specifying the matters in which it shall not be necessary for the RPSC to be consulted (**RPSC (Exemption from Consultation) Regulations**).

### 2.7 Chronology & Key Historical Facts of RPSC
- **First Chairman**: **Sir S.K. Ghosh** (Chief Justice of Rajasthan High Court who served concurrently as the 1st Chairman from 20 August 1949 to 25 January 1950).
- **First Regular Full-Time Chairman**: **Devi Shankar Tiwari** (Served from 1951 to 1958; holds the record for the **longest tenure** as Chairman in RPSC history).
- **Shortest Tenure as Chairman**: **P.S. Yadav** (served for just a few days).
- **First Woman Member**: **Kanta Kathuria** (subsequently also appointed first Chairperson of the State Commission for Women); later **Prakashwati Sharma** was also a prominent woman member.
- **RPSC Chairmen who also served as Chief Secretary**: **S.W. Shiveshwarkar**, **B.L. Rawat**.

## 3. State Election Commission (SEC) Rajasthan

### 3.1 Constitutional Architecture (Articles 243K & 243ZA)
Prior to 1992, elections to local bodies in Rajasthan were conducted by the Panchayat & Development Department under executive instructions, resulting in arbitrary postponements and political manipulation.
- By the **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts, 1992**, a permanent, independent constitutional body was mandated:
  - **Article 243K**: Superintendence, direction, and control of the preparation of electoral rolls for, and the conduct of, all elections to the **Panchayats** (Gram Panchayats, Panchayat Samitis, Zila Parishads).
  - **Article 243ZA**: Applies the provisions of Article 243K mutatis mutandis to all elections to the **Municipalities** (Municipal Corporations / Nagar Nigams, Municipal Councils / Nagar Parishads, Municipal Boards / Nagar Palikas).
- **Constitution in Rajasthan**: The State Election Commission, Rajasthan was formally constituted in **July 1994**.

```
                ELECTION CONDUCT IN RAJASTHAN: STRICT SEPARATION
                                       │
            ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
            │                                                     │
   ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA                               STATE ELECTION COMMISSION
          (Art. 324)                                             (Art. 243K / 243ZA)
            │                                                     │
   ├── Lok Sabha Elections                                     ├── Gram Panchayat Elections
   ├── Rajya Sabha Elections                                   ├── Panchayat Samiti Elections
   ├── Rajasthan Vidhan Sabha Elections                        ├── Zila Parishad Elections
   └── Presidential/VP Elections                               └── All Urban Local Body (ULB) Elections
            │                                                     │
   Executed locally by: Chief Electoral Officer                Executed locally by: State Election
   (CEO - IAS Officer under deputation to ECI)                 Commissioner (SEC) assisted by Secretary
```

### 3.2 Composition, Appointment, and Service Conditions
- **Single-Member Commission**: The State Election Commission of Rajasthan consists of a single member — the **State Election Commissioner** (Article 243K(1)).
- **Appointing Authority**: The **Governor of Rajasthan** appoints the State Election Commissioner.
- **Status & Independence Safeguards (Article 243K(2))**:
  - The State Election Commissioner can be removed from office **only in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of a High Court**.
  - That is, removal can only occur by an **Order of the President of India**, following an address passed by **both Houses of Parliament** with a special majority (two-thirds present and voting, and absolute majority of total membership) on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  - The Governor *cannot* remove the State Election Commissioner.
  - The conditions of service and tenure of office of the State Election Commissioner shall not be varied to his/her disadvantage after appointment.
- **Tenure**: In Rajasthan, governed by the Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994 and executive rules, the term is fixed at **5 years or 65 years of age**, whichever is earlier.

### 3.4 Functions and Statutory Scope
1. **Preparation of Electoral Rolls**: Complete ward-wise enumeration, updating, and publication of voter lists for rural and urban local bodies.
2. **Conduct of Elections**: Direct elections of Ward Panchs, Sarpanchs, Panchayat Samiti Members, Zila Parishad Members, and Urban Municipal Ward Councillors; and indirect elections of Pradhans, Up-Pradhans, Zila Pramukhs, Up-Zila Pramukhs, Mayors, Council Presidents, and Municipal Chairpersons.
3. **Disqualification Inquiries**: Power to review election petitions and statutory disqualifications (e.g., two-child norm under Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, educational qualification criteria during its period of enforcement, toilet functionality affidavits).
4. **Requisitioning of Staff (Art. 243K(3))**: The Governor, when requested by the SEC, must make available to the Commission such staff as may be necessary for the discharge of the functions.
5. **Bar to Interference by Courts in Electoral Matters (Art. 243-O & 243ZG)**: The validity of any law relating to delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats cannot be called in question in any court; no election to any Panchayat or Municipality can be questioned except by an **election petition** presented to such authority and in such manner as provided by State law.

## 4. State Finance Commission (SFC) Rajasthan

### 4.1 Constitutional Mandate (Articles 243-I & 243-Y)
- **Article 243-I**: Mandates that the Governor of a State shall, within one year from the commencement of the 73rd Amendment Act, 1992, and thereafter at the expiration of every **fifth year**, constitute a Finance Commission to review the financial position of the Panchayats.
- **Article 243-Y**: Mandates that the Finance Commission constituted under Article 243-I shall also review the financial position of the **Municipalities** and make recommendations to the Governor.
- **Core Duties**:
  1. Determine the principles governing the distribution between the State and local bodies of the net proceeds of taxes, duties, tolls, and fees leviable by the State.
  2. Determine the allocation of such proceeds between the various tiers of Panchayati Raj Institutions and Urban Local Bodies.
  3. Determine the taxes, duties, tolls, and fees that may be assigned to, or appropriated by, the local bodies.
  4. Recommend grants-in-aid to Panchayats and Municipalities from the Consolidated Fund of the State.
  5. Recommend measures needed to improve the financial position of Panchayats and Municipalities.

### 4.3 Sixth State Finance Commission (6th SFC) Key Recommendations
- **Devolution Share**: Recommended the devolution of **6.75%** of the State's net own tax revenue to local self-governing bodies.
- **Distribution Between Rural and Urban Local Bodies**:
  - Based on the 2011 Census population ratio (approx. 75.1% rural, 24.9% urban):
  - **75.10%** allocated to **Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)**.
  - **24.90%** allocated to **Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)**.
- **Inter-Tier Distribution within PRIs (75.10% pool)**:
  - **Gram Panchayats**: **75%** of the rural share (maximum share to grassroots delivery).
  - **Panchayat Samitis**: **20%** of the rural share.
  - **Zila Parishads**: **5%** of the rural share.
- **Distribution within ULBs (24.90% pool)**: Distributed among Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils, and Municipal Boards based on weightage given to population (80%) and area (20%).

## 5. Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission (RSHRC)

### 5.1 Legislative Framework and Genesis
- Established under Section 21 of the **Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA), 1993** (a Central Act).
- The State Government issued a notification for its constitution on **18 January 1999**.
- The Commission became fully operational with the appointment of its first Chairperson and members on **23 March 2000**.
- **Headquarters**: **Jaipur**.

### 5.2 Composition and Structural Amendments (2006 & 2019)
- **Original Composition (1993 Act)**: 1 Chairperson + 4 Members = 5 total.
- **Amended Composition under PHRA Amendment Act, 2006**: Reduced to **1 Chairperson + 2 Members** (Total = **3**).
- **Eligibility Criteria (Amended by PHRA Amendment Act, 2019)**:
  - **Chairperson**: Must have been a **Chief Justice of a High Court** OR a **Judge of a High Court**. *(Prior to 2019, ONLY a retired Chief Justice of a High Court could be appointed)*.
  - **Member 1**: Must be or have been a **Judge of a High Court** OR a **District Judge** in the State with a minimum of **7 years of experience** as a District Judge.
  - **Member 2**: A person having knowledge of, or practical experience in, matters relating to human rights.

### 5.3 The High-Powered Selection Committee (Section 22)
The Chairperson and Members are formally appointed by the **Governor of Rajasthan**, but only upon the binding recommendation of a statutory Selection Committee consisting of:
1. **Chief Minister** (Chairperson of the Committee)
2. **Home Minister of Rajasthan** (Member)
3. **Speaker of the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly** (Member)
4. **Leader of the Opposition in the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly** (Member)
*(Note: If the State had a Legislative Council / Vidhan Parishad, the Chairman of the Council and the Leader of the Opposition in the Council would also be statutory members).*

```

            ┌─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┐
            │                         │                         │
     Chief Minister             Home Minister           Speaker of Vidhan Sabha
      (Chairperson)               (Member)                      (Member)
                                      │
                                      └─────────────────────────┐
                                                                │
                                                      Leader of Opposition
                                                            (Member)
```

### 5.4 Tenure, Service Conditions & Removal Protocol
- **Tenure (Amended by 2019 Act)**:
  - Hold office for a term of **3 years** or until attaining the age of **70 years**, whichever is earlier. *(Reduced from 5 years / 70 years by the 2019 Amendment Act)*.
  - Eligible for **re-appointment** (subject to the 70-year ceiling).
- **Removal from Office (Section 23)**:
  - Even though appointed by the Governor, the Chairperson or any Member of the RSHRC can be removed from office **ONLY by Order of the President of India**.
  - *On ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity*: Only after the **Supreme Court of India**, on a reference made to it by the President, has held an inquiry and reported that the member ought on any such ground to be removed.
  - *Direct Removal by President (without inquiry)*: If adjudged insolvent, accepts paid employment outside duties, or is unfit by reason of infirmity of mind or body.

### 5.5 Jurisdiction, Powers, and Operational Limitations
- **Scope of Inquiries (Section 12)**:
  - Inquire suo motu or on a petition presented to it into violations of human rights or negligence in the prevention of such violations by a **public servant**.
  - Intervene in proceedings involving allegations of human rights violations pending before a court with the approval of that court.
  - Visit jails and custodial institutions to inspect living conditions of inmates.
  - Review constitutional and legal safeguards for human rights.
- **Powers of a Civil Court (Section 13)**: While inquiring into complaints, it enjoys all powers of a Civil Court under the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), 1908:
  - Summoning and enforcing attendance of witnesses and examining them on oath;
  - Discovery and production of documents;
  - Receiving evidence on affidavits;
  - Requisitioning any public record from any court or office.
- **Critical Operational Limitations (RPSC Traps)**:
  1. **Strict 1-Year Limitation Period (Section 36(2))**: The Commission **shall not inquire** into any matter after the expiry of **one year** from the date on which the act constituting the human rights violation is alleged to have been committed.
  2. **No Power to Inquire into Central/Union List Subjects**: Jurisdiction is restricted strictly to matters falling under **List II (State List)** and **List III (Concurrent List)** of the Seventh Schedule. If the NHRC or any other statutory commission is already inquiring into the matter, the RSHRC cannot inquire.
  3. **Recommendatory Powers Only**: It has no authority to punish offenders directly or award binding compensation on its own authority. It makes recommendations to the State Government.
  4. **Government Response Mandate**: The State Government must inform the Commission within **one month** (or such extended time) regarding the action taken on its recommendations.
  5. **Annual Report**: Submitted to the State Government, which must lay it before the State Legislative Assembly along with a memorandum explaining reasons for non-acceptance, if any.

## 6. Rajasthan State Information Commission (RSIC)

### 6.1 Genesis under the Right to Information Act, 2005
- The Right to Information movement in India traces its intellectual birthplace to **Devdungri (Rajsamand district, Rajasthan)** in 1990, led by Aruna Roy, Nikhil Dey, and the **Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS)**, which demanded access to muster rolls and village development bills, giving birth to the slogan: *"Hamara Paisa, Hamara Hisaab"* and *"Janne Ka Haq, Jeene Ka Haq"*.
- Rajasthan was among the pioneers, passing the **Rajasthan Right to Information Act, 2000** (one of the earliest state-level sunshine laws).
- Following Parliament's enactment of the comprehensive **Right to Information Act, 2005**, the **Rajasthan State Information Commission** was formally constituted on **18 April 2006** under Section 15 of the Act.
- **Headquarters**: **Jaipur**.

### 6.2 Composition and Appointment Process (Section 15)
- **Composition**:
  - One **State Chief Information Commissioner (SCIC)**.
  - Such number of **State Information Commissioners (SICs)**, not exceeding **ten (10)**, as may be deemed necessary.
- **Appointing Authority**: The **Governor of Rajasthan** appoints the SCIC and SICs on the recommendation of a statutory Selection Committee.
- **Selection Committee (Section 15(3))**:
  1. **Chief Minister** (Chairperson of the Committee)
  2. **Leader of the Opposition in the Legislative Assembly** (Member)
  3. **One Cabinet Minister** nominated by the Chief Minister (Member)

### 6.3 Tenure and Service Conditions (RTI Amendment Act, 2019)
- **Original 2005 Provision**: Fixed term of 5 years or 65 years of age; salaries of SCIC equated to Election Commissioner and SIC equated to Chief Secretary.
- **Current Position (Post-2019 Amendment)**:
  - Section 16 amended: The term of office and salaries/allowances of the SCIC and SICs are **prescribed by the Central Government**.
  - Current Term: Fixed by Central Rules at **3 years** or until attaining **65 years of age**, whichever is earlier.
  - **Ineligibility for Re-appointment**: The SCIC or SIC is **not eligible for re-appointment** to the same office. However, an SIC can be appointed as SCIC, provided the aggregate tenure as both does not exceed **5 years** (now 3 years per revised central notification).

### 6.4 Removal Protocol (Section 17)
- The SCIC or any SIC may be removed from office by the **Governor** on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity after the **Supreme Court of India**, on a reference made to it by the Governor, has conducted an inquiry and reported that the member ought to be removed.
- The Governor may suspend the Commissioner during the inquiry.
- *Direct Removal by Governor without SC Reference*: If adjudged an insolvent, convicted of an offense involving moral turpitude, engages in paid employment, or suffers from infirmity of mind or body.

### 6.5 Penal and Adjudicatory Powers (Section 19 & 20)
- **Appellate Forum**: Hears **Second Appeals** under Section 19(3) against decisions of the First Appellate Authority (FAA), and direct **Complaints** under Section 18 for refusal to accept RTI applications or failure to designate PIOs.
- **Penal Powers (Section 20(1))**:
  - If a Public Information Officer (PIO) has without reasonable cause refused to receive an application, delayed information, knowingly given false/misleading information, or destroyed information:
  - The Commission can impose a penalty of **Rs. 250 per day** until the application is received or information furnished.
  - The maximum total penalty that can be levied on a single defaulting officer is capped at **Rs. 25,000**.
- **Disciplinary Recommendation (Section 20(2))**: Can recommend disciplinary action against the defaulting PIO under the applicable service rules.
- **Compensation**: Can require the public authority to compensate the complainant for any loss or detriment suffered.

### 6.6 Chronology of State Chief Information Commissioners (SCIC)

2. **T. Srinivasan** (Former IAS).
3. **Suresh Chaudhary** (Former IPS).
4. **D.B. Gupta** (Former Chief Secretary of Rajasthan; December 2020 – December 2023).

## 7. Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas of Rajasthan

### 7.1 Historical Genesis and Statutory Basis
- In India, the concept of the Ombudsman was first officially recommended by the **First Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC, 1966)** headed by Morarji Desai (and later K. Hanumanthaiah).
- Odisha passed the first Lokayukta Act in 1970 (operationalised in 1983); Maharashtra was the first to establish and operationalise the office in 1971.
- In Rajasthan, the State Legislature passed the **Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973**.
  - Assented to by the President of India on **26 March 1973**.
  - Came into force retrospectively from **3 February 1973**.
- **First Lokayukta**: **Justice I.D. Dua** (Former Judge of the Supreme Court of India), who assumed office on **28 August 1973**.
- **Only Up-Lokayukta**: **K.P.U. Menon** (Assumed office on 5 June 1973). *No other Up-Lokayukta has been appointed in Rajasthan since K.P.U. Menon retired, making the institution functionally a single-member body.*

### 7.2 Appointment Protocol and Qualifications
- **Appointed by**: The **Governor of Rajasthan** by warrant under hand and seal (Section 3).
- **Consultation Mandate (Section 3(1))**:
  - The Lokayukta is appointed by the Governor after mandatory consultation with:
    1. The **Chief Justice of the High Court of Rajasthan**; AND
    2. The **Leader of the Opposition in the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly** (or if no leader is recognized, a person elected by opposition members).
- **Qualifications**:
  - A person appointed as Lokayukta must have been a **Judge of the Supreme Court of India** OR a **Chief Justice / Judge of a High Court**.
- **Disqualifications for Appointment (Section 4)**:
  - Must not be a Member of Parliament or Member of any State Legislature.
  - Must not be associated with any political party.
  - Must not hold any office of trust or profit, or carry on any business or practice any profession.

### 7.3 Tenure and the 2018–2019 Legislative Reversal
- **Original Tenure (1973 Act)**: **5 years** or until attaining **65 years of age**, whichever is earlier.
- **The 2018 Amendment Controversy**: In March 2018, the Rajasthan Government amended the Act to increase the tenure of the Lokayukta from 5 years to **8 years** (which extended the tenure of then incumbent Justice S.S. Kothari).
- **The 2019 Repeal Act**: In July 2019, the newly elected Legislative Assembly passed the **Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas (Amendment) Act, 2019**, which rolled back the tenure **back to 5 years**, bringing an immediate conclusion to Justice S.S. Kothari's extended term.
- **Ineligibility for Reappointment**: On ceasing to hold office, the Lokayukta is ineligible for further employment under the Government of Rajasthan or in any statutory board, corporation, or local authority.

### 7.5 Investigative Procedure and Nature of Powers
- **Two Forms of Action**:
  1. **Grievance**: A claim that a person sustained injustice or undue hardship in consequence of maladministration.
  2. **Allegation**: A charge that a public servant abused position for personal gain, acted corruptly, or failed to act with integrity.
- **Statutory Limitation**: Lokayukta cannot conduct an investigation into any complaint if it is made after the expiry of **5 years** from the date on which the action complained against took place (Section 8(2)).
- **Advisory Powers**:
  - The Lokayukta does **not possess prosecutorial powers** or the power to directly penalize or convict a corrupt official.
  - Upon finding charges substantiated, the Lokayukta sends a report with recommendations to the "Competent Authority" (Governor for Ministers, Chief Minister for Secretaries/Collectors).
  - The Competent Authority must examine the report and notify the Lokayukta within **3 months** of the action taken or proposed to be taken.
  - Submits an **Annual Consolidated Report** to the Governor, which is laid before the Legislative Assembly.

## 8. Rajasthan State Commission for Women (RSCW)

### 8.1 Genesis and Statutory Foundation
- Constituted pursuant to the enactment of the **Rajasthan State Commission for Women Act, 1999** by the State Legislature.
- The Commission came into formal existence on **15 May 1999**.
- **Headquarters**: **Jaipur**.

### 8.2 Composition and Appointment
- The Commission consists of:
  1. A **Chairperson** (an eminent woman committed to the cause of women);
  2. **Three Non-Official Members** (nominated by the State Government, of whom **at least one shall belong to the Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes**, and **one shall belong to the Other Backward Classes**);
  3. A **Member Secretary** (an officer of the State Government, not below the rank of Joint Secretary / senior administrative officer, managing finances and administration).
- **Tenure**: The Chairperson and non-official members hold office for a period of **3 years**.
- **Resignation**: Addressed to the **State Government**.

### 8.3 Functions and Powers
- Investigate and examine all matters relating to the safeguards provided for women under the Constitution and other laws.
- Inquire into complaints relating to:
  - Deprivation of women's rights;
  - Non-implementation of laws enacted to protect women (e.g., Dowry Prohibition Act, Domestic Violence Act, POCSO, Sexual Harassment at Workplace Act [POSH]);
  - Non-compliance with policy decisions and guidelines aimed at mitigating hardships to women.
- Inspect jails, remand homes, women's shelters, and institutional homes where women are kept as prisoners or for custody.
- **Civil Court Powers**: While investigating complaints, the Commission has the powers of a Civil Court in respect of summoning witnesses, requiring discovery and production of documents, and receiving affidavits.
- **Advisory Nature**: Recommends welfare measures, law amendments, and corrective steps to the State Government. Submits an annual report to the Government to be laid before the Legislative Assembly.

## 10. Critical Exam Traps & Conceptual Nuances

1. **Governor Appoints, but President Removes**:
   - **RPSC Chairman and Members**: Appointed by Governor $\rightarrow$ Removed exclusively by **President** (Art. 317).
   - **State Election Commissioner**: Appointed by Governor $\rightarrow$ Removed exclusively by **President** (like a High Court Judge).
   - **State Human Rights Commission Chairperson & Members**: Appointed by Governor $\rightarrow$ Removed exclusively by **President** (PHRA Sec. 23).
   - **State Information Commissioners**: Appointed by Governor $\rightarrow$ Removed by **Governor** (RTI Act Sec. 17, following SC inquiry).
   - **Lokayukta**: Appointed by Governor $\rightarrow$ Removed by **Governor** (following inquiry and legislative address).

2. **The Lokayukta Jurisdictional Fault-Lines**:
   - *Is the Chief Minister covered?* **NO.** (Unlike Karnataka, the CM of Rajasthan is strictly excluded).
   - *Are MLAs covered?* **NO.** Members of Legislative Assembly are excluded.
   - *Are Sarpanchs and Ward Panchs covered?* **NO.** Only rural local body officials at the Panchayat Samiti (Pradhan/Up-Pradhan) and Zila Parishad (Pramukh/Up-Pramukh) level are covered.
   - *Are Municipal Chairpersons covered?* **YES.** All ULB heads (Mayors, Council Presidents, Board Chairpersons) are covered.
   - *Are RPSC Chairman and Members covered?* **NO.** Explicitly excluded.

3. **Tenure Reversals to Memorize**:
   - **RSHRC**: Changed from 5 years / 70 years to **3 years / 70 years** by the 2019 Amendment.
   - **RSIC**: Changed from 5 years / 65 years to **3 years / 65 years** by the 2019 Central RTI Rules.
   - **Lokayukta**: Extended to 8 years in 2018, but **rolled back to 5 years** in 2019.

4. **Composition Symmetry**:
   - Selection Committee for **RSHRC** has **4 members**: CM, Home Minister, Speaker, Leader of Opposition.
   - Selection Committee for **RSIC** has **3 members**: CM, Leader of Opposition, one Cabinet Minister nominated by CM.

## 11. Practice Questions & Model Mains Frameworks

### 11.1 2-Marker Questions (20 Words / 2 Lines)

**Q1. Under what constitutional provision can the Chairman of RPSC be removed from office?**
- **Model Answer**: The Chairman of RPSC can be removed only by an order of the President of India on grounds of proved misbehaviour after a mandatory Supreme Court inquiry under Article 317(1), or on grounds of insolvency, paid employment, or infirmity under Article 317(3).

**Q2. State the composition of the Selection Committee for the appointment of the Chairperson of RSHRC.**
- **Model Answer**: Under Section 22 of PHRA 1993, a four-member committee: Chief Minister (Chairperson), Home Minister of the State, Speaker of the Legislative Assembly, and Leader of the Opposition in the Assembly.

**Q3. Name the first and current State Election Commissioners of Rajasthan.**
- **Model Answer**: The first State Election Commissioner was **Amar Singh Rathore** (1994–2000), and the incumbent Commissioner is **Madhukar Gupta** (appointed August 2022).

**Q4. List four authorities explicitly excluded from the jurisdiction of the Rajasthan Lokayukta.**
- **Model Answer**: (1) Chief Minister, (2) Judges of High Court and Subordinate Courts, (3) Chairman and Members of RPSC, and (4) Sarpanch and Ward Panchs of Gram Panchayats.

### 11.2 5-Marker Questions (50 Words / 5 Lines)

**Q1. Explain the post-retirement restrictions imposed on the Chairman and Members of RPSC under Article 319.**
- **Model Answer**:
  - **Chairman**: Eligible only for appointment as Chairman or Member of UPSC, or Chairman of any other SPSC. Ineligible for any other central or state government employment.
  - **Member**: Eligible for appointment as Chairman/Member of UPSC, Chairman of RPSC, or Chairman of another SPSC. Ineligible for any other government post.
  - **Purpose**: Insulates the Commission from executive patronage and post-retirement political favours, guaranteeing institutional independence.

**Q2. Outline the financial devolution formula recommended by the Sixth State Finance Commission of Rajasthan.**
- **Model Answer**:
  - Devolution quantum: Recommended devolution of **6.75%** of the State's net own tax revenue.
  - Rural-Urban split: Allocated based on 2011 Census population in a ratio of **75.10%** to Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) and **24.90%** to Urban Local Bodies (ULBs).
  - Intra-PRI allocation: Gram Panchayats receive **75%**, Panchayat Samitis **20%**, and Zila Parishads **5%**.

**Q3. Discuss the powers of the State Information Commission under Section 20 of the RTI Act, 2005.**
- **Model Answer**:
  - **Financial Penalty**: Imposes a fine of Rs. 250 per day up to a maximum limit of Rs. 25,000 on defaulting PIOs for malafide denial, delay, or obstruction of information without reasonable cause.
  - **Disciplinary Action**: Recommends departmental disciplinary proceedings against the PIO under applicable service conduct rules.
  - **Compensation**: Directs the public authority to compensate applicants for losses or detriment suffered.

### 11.3 10-Marker Questions (100 Words / Full Page)

**Q1. Critically evaluate the institutional effectiveness, jurisdiction, and limitations of the Lokayukta in Rajasthan. Suggest structural reforms.**
- **Model Answer Framework**:
  1. **Introduction**:
     - Established under the Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973 as an ombudsman mechanism to investigate administrative corruption and grievances against public servants.
  2. **Institutional Strengths**:
     - Wide coverage across the bureaucracy, ministers, municipal chairs, and PRI heads (Pramukhs/Pradhans).
     - Statutory independence insulated by consultation with Chief Justice and Leader of Opposition.
     - Civil Court powers under CPC for investigations, summoning, and record discovery.
  3. **Critical Limitations**:
     - *Exclusion of Key Offices*: Exclusion of the Chief Minister, MLAs, RPSC members, and Sarpanchs leaves large swathes of political and grassroots administrative conduct unpoliced.
     - *Toothless Advisory Status*: No independent prosecutorial wing; cannot directly register FIRs, attach assets, or file chargesheets. Relies entirely on the "Competent Authority" (Executive).
     - *Single-Member Weakness*: Up-Lokayukta post has remained vacant since 1973; massive pendency of cases.
     - *Statutory Limitation Barrier*: 5-year bar prevents inquiry into historical scams.
  4. **Required Reforms**:
     - Bring the Chief Minister and MLAs within its purview with procedural safeguards.
     - Grant direct powers of prosecution and an autonomous, dedicated investigative cadre (on the lines of Karnataka Lokayukta Police).
     - Empower the Lokayukta to initiate contempt proceedings for non-compliance with recommendations.

**Q2. Compare and contrast the constitutional status, appointment mechanisms, functional mandates, and removal protocols of the RPSC and the Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission.**
- **Model Answer Framework**:
  - Structure comparison along tabular vectors:
    - *Origin*: Constitutional (Articles 315–323) vs. Statutory (PHRA, 1993 Section 21).
    - *Appointment*: Governor acting on Cabinet advice without formal committee vs. Governor acting on binding advice of a 4-member statutory Selection Committee (CM, HM, Speaker, LoP).
    - *Tenure*: 6 years or 62 years (RPSC) vs. 3 years or 70 years (RSHRC post-2019).
    - *Removal*: Both removed exclusively by the President of India on proved misbehaviour after Supreme Court inquiry.
    - *Mandate*: Meritocratic recruitment, disciplinary consultations, and personnel examinations vs. Investigation of human rights violations, custodial checks, and civil liberties protection.
    - *Advisory Character*: Both submit annual reports to the Governor/State Government which are laid before the Legislative Assembly with explanatory memoranda for non-acceptance.
