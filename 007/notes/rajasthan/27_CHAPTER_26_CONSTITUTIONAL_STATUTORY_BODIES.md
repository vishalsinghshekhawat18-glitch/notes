# CHAPTER 26: CONSTITUTIONAL & STATUTORY BODIES OF RAJASTHAN — REVISED MASTER NOTES

**Scope:** RPSC RAS Rajasthan Polity & Governance  
**Research/status cut-off:** 17 October 2026  
**Use:** Static concepts for repeated revision; dated officeholder details and commission reports must be refreshed before an examination.

> **Reading rule:** Constitutional provisions and enacted statutes are the legal baseline. Government reports establish the status of recommendations and implementation. A coaching-note claim or a list of current officeholders is not equivalent to a statutory rule. Where an official source is internally inconsistent, this chapter flags the conflict instead of manufacturing certainty.

## 1. Taxonomy of Rajasthan’s accountability institutions

| Body | Legal status | Principal legal basis | Main function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rajasthan Public Service Commission (RPSC) | Constitutional | Articles 315–323 | Recruitment examinations and consultation on specified public-service matters |
| State Election Commission (SEC), Rajasthan | Constitutional | Articles 243K and 243ZA | Elections to Panchayats and Municipalities |
| State Finance Commission (SFC), Rajasthan | Constitutional | Articles 243-I and 243-Y | Review of local-body finances and recommendations on fiscal devolution |
| Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission (RSHRC) | Statutory | Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 (PHRA), especially Sections 21–29 | Inquiry and recommendations on human-rights violations within its statutory jurisdiction |
| Rajasthan State Information Commission (RSIC) | Statutory | Right to Information Act, 2005, especially Sections 15–20 | Complaints, second appeals and statutory penalties under the RTI Act |
| Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas of Rajasthan | Statutory | Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973 | Investigation of specified allegations against ministers and public servants |
| Rajasthan State Commission for Women (RSCW) | Statutory | Rajasthan State Commission for Women Act, 1999 | Inquiry, review and recommendations concerning women’s rights and safeguards |

**Conceptual distinction:** A constitutional body is created by the Constitution itself. A statutory body is created under legislation. This does not mean that every statutory body is powerless or that every constitutional body makes binding decisions: each body’s legal authority depends on its governing provisions.

## 2. Rajasthan Public Service Commission (RPSC)

### 2.1 Origin, headquarters and historical facts

- The Rajasthan Public Service Commission was established through an ordinance promulgated on **16 August 1949** by the successor administration of Greater Rajasthan. The Rajasthan Government Gazette publication commonly cited is **20 August 1949**. These are different events and should not be compressed into a single “promulgation date.”
- The RPSC’s official history states that, among the constituent princely states, public-service-commission-type institutions existed in **Jaipur, Jodhpur and Bikaner**.
- The Commission’s headquarters is at **Ghooghra Ghati, Ajmer**, on Jaipur Road.
- The familiar date **22 December 1949** is cited for the Commission’s formal inauguration/commencement in many Rajasthan reference works. It is not the date the establishing ordinance was promulgated.
- The office of the RPSC is independent of the State Government’s recruitment departments in its constitutional position, but the Commission’s advice is not universally binding on the executive.

### 2.2 Constitutional articles: 315–323

| Article | Exam-ready provision | Important nuance |
|---|---|---|
| 315 | Public Service Commissions for the Union and States | A Joint State Public Service Commission may be created by Parliament where the concerned State Legislatures request it. |
| 316 | Appointment and term of office | State PSC members, including the Chairperson, are appointed by the Governor. A member’s term is six years or until age 62, whichever is earlier. |
| 317 | Removal and suspension | Removal is by the President under the constitutional procedure; the Governor does not possess a general power to remove or suspend RPSC members. |
| 318 | Regulations on composition and conditions | The Governor determines the number of members and may make regulations on conditions of service, subject to the Constitution. |
| 319 | Restrictions on further employment | Post-tenure eligibility differs for Chairpersons and members and is designed to limit executive patronage. |
| 320 | Functions | Covers examinations and consultation on recruitment, appointments, promotions, transfers, disciplinary matters, legal-expense claims, injury-pension claims and other referred matters. |
| 321 | Extension of functions | The appropriate legislature may extend the functions of a PSC. |
| 322 | Expenses | Commission expenses, including salaries and allowances, are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State. |
| 323 | Reports | The Commission submits an annual report to the Governor; the report is laid before the State Legislature with a memorandum explaining cases where its advice was not accepted. |

### 2.3 Composition, appointment and tenure

- **Appointment:** The Governor appoints the Chairperson and members under Article 316(1).
- **Tenure:** Six years from entering office or age **62**, whichever occurs first. The 41st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 raised the age limit from 60 to 62.
- **Administrative experience condition:** As nearly as may be, one-half of the members must be persons who, at the dates of their respective appointments, have held office for at least ten years under the Government of India or a State Government. This is a constitutional composition condition, not an invitation to invent a precise “administrative quota” for every appointment.
- **Financial protection:** Article 322 charges the Commission’s expenses to the Consolidated Fund of Rajasthan. Conditions of service cannot be varied to the member’s disadvantage after appointment (Article 318 proviso).
- **Current-strength caution:** RPSC’s official Information Handbook updated **20 August 2026** lists Lt. Col. (Retd.) **Kesari Singh Rathore as officiating Chairperson**. The same handbook has an internally inconsistent sanctioned-strength line (“Eight (1+10)”) while its personnel schedule shows additional member posts and vacancies. Do not memorise an exact sanctioned total from that contradictory line; check the latest valid regulation/notification before the exam.

### 2.4 Removal and suspension under Article 317 — corrected protocol

**Removal for misbehaviour** follows this constitutional sequence:

1. The **President of India** refers the question of removal to the Supreme Court.
2. The Supreme Court conducts the inquiry under the applicable procedure and reports its findings.
3. If the Supreme Court reports that the Chairperson/member ought to be removed, the **President** may issue the removal order.
4. Under Article 317(2), the **President**, not the Governor, may suspend the Chairperson/member while a reference is pending before the Supreme Court.

The President may also remove a member without a Supreme Court reference on the direct grounds specified in Article 317(3):

- adjudged insolvent;
- during the term, engaging in paid employment outside the duties of office; or
- in the President’s opinion, unfit to continue by reason of infirmity of mind or body.

A person is deemed guilty of misbehaviour for Article 317(1) purposes if the person becomes interested in a contract or agreement made on behalf of the Government of India or a State Government, or participates in its benefit or profit in the constitutionally specified manner.

### 2.5 Post-tenure restrictions: Article 319

| Person leaving office | Eligible future appointments under Article 319 | Main restriction |
|---|---|---|
| Chairperson of a State PSC | Chairperson/member of UPSC, or Chairperson of another State PSC | No other employment under the Union or a State Government |
| Member of a State PSC | Chairperson/member of UPSC, or Chairperson of the same or another State PSC | No other employment under the Union or a State Government |

A person who has held office as a member of a State PSC is not eligible for reappointment to that same office (Article 316(3)). Do not confuse this no-reappointment rule with the limited further appointments expressly allowed under Article 319.

### 2.6 Functions and limitations

The Commission’s constitutional functions include:

- conducting examinations for appointments to State civil services and posts;
- advising on recruitment methods;
- advising on principles for appointments, promotions and transfers and the suitability of candidates;
- advising on specified disciplinary matters;
- considering qualifying claims for legal expenses and injury-related pensions; and
- undertaking other matters referred by the Government.

The RPSC ordinarily **recommends** candidates; the legally competent appointing authority makes appointments under the relevant service rules. Its advice is generally advisory rather than automatically binding. Article 320(3) includes consultation requirements, but constitutional provisions and valid regulations specify matters where consultation is not necessary. Article 320(4) preserves the stated exceptions relating to reservation policy and consideration of claims of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.

### 2.7 Current officeholders: how to study safely

Current RPSC leadership and member vacancies are dynamic. The official handbook dated 20 August 2026 lists **Kesari Singh Rathore (officiating Chairperson)**. Keep that as a dated current-affairs fact, not a permanent static fact. The exact sanctioned number of members should be verified from the latest legal notification because the official handbook’s sanctioned-strength text is inconsistent with its roster.

## 3. State Election Commission (SEC), Rajasthan

### 3.1 Constitutional basis and jurisdiction

- The **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts, 1992** introduced the constitutional framework for independent local-body elections.
- **Article 243K** governs the State Election Commission for Panchayat elections.
- **Article 243ZA** applies the Article 243K framework, with necessary modifications, to Municipality elections.
- Rajasthan’s SEC was constituted in **1994**. The official SEC site was issuing notices for **Panchayati Raj Institutions’ general elections 2026** in October 2026.

### 3.2 Election jurisdiction: do not confuse SEC and ECI

| Election | Responsible election authority |
|---|---|
| Lok Sabha | Election Commission of India (ECI) |
| Rajasthan Legislative Assembly | ECI |
| Rajya Sabha | ECI, within the constitutional/statutory framework |
| President and Vice-President of India | ECI |
| Gram Panchayats, Panchayat Samitis and Zila Parishads | Rajasthan SEC |
| Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils and Municipal Boards | Rajasthan SEC |

The Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) works in the framework for national and State Assembly elections. The State Election Commissioner is a separate constitutional office for local-body elections.

### 3.3 Appointment, independence and tenure

- The State Election Commissioner is appointed by the **Governor**.
- Article 243K(2) protects the office: the Commissioner cannot be removed except in the same manner and on the same grounds as a High Court Judge. Conditions of service cannot be varied to the Commissioner’s disadvantage after appointment.
- Rajasthan’s State Election Commissioner (Conditions of Service) Rules, as amended in 2017 and 2019, prescribe a term of **five years or age 65**, whichever is earlier. These service rules also prescribe eligibility requirements relating to senior administrative service experience.
- Removal on the High Court Judge model must not be described as an ordinary removal by the Governor. The constitutional removal process requires the parliamentary address and presidential order framework applicable to a High Court Judge.

### 3.4 Core functions and limits

- Prepare and update electoral rolls for local bodies and conduct their elections.
- Conduct direct elections for ward members and other directly elected local representatives, and supervise the indirect elections that State law assigns to local bodies.
- Request the necessary staff under Article 243K(3); the Governor must make staff available as required by the constitutional provision.
- Articles **243-O** (Panchayats) and **243-ZG** (Municipalities) restrict ordinary judicial interference in delimitation/allotment-of-seat questions and provide that elections are challenged through election petitions under State law. These provisions do not mean every question touching an election is forever immune from judicial review.

**Current-officeholder rule:** The official SEC website should be used for the current Commissioner and the latest election orders. This chapter does not reproduce an unverified name from an old chronology; the portal’s election notices are dynamic and were updated in October 2026.

## 4. State Finance Commission (SFC), Rajasthan

### 4.1 Constitutional mandate

- **Article 243-I:** The Governor constitutes a State Finance Commission to review the financial position of Panchayats, initially within the constitutional timetable and thereafter at the expiration of every fifth year.
- **Article 243-Y:** The Commission also reviews the financial position of Municipalities and makes recommendations to the Governor.
- The SFC recommends how State-level revenue and other resources should be allocated among the State and local bodies, how proceeds should be distributed across tiers, which taxes/fees may be assigned or appropriated, grants-in-aid from the State Consolidated Fund, and measures to improve local-body finances.
- An SFC is not the Union Finance Commission. It does **not** decide the constitutional distribution of Union taxes between the Union and States.

### 4.2 Rajasthan’s sequence and current update

Rajasthan’s Finance Department lists reports for the First through Sixth SFCs and the **interim report of the Seventh SFC for 2025–2026**. Therefore, a chapter that stops at the Sixth SFC as though it were the latest commission is incomplete as at October 2026.

| Commission | Broad reference period |
|---|---|
| First SFC | 1995–2000 |
| Second SFC | 2000–2005 |
| Third SFC | 2005–2010 |
| Fourth SFC | 2010–2015 |
| Fifth SFC | 2015–2020 |
| Sixth SFC | 2020–2025 |
| Seventh SFC | Official Finance Department lists an interim report for 2025–2026; consult the report for the precise recommendations and period covered. |

### 4.3 Sixth SFC: recommendation versus Government action

This distinction is essential:

- The **Sixth SFC’s final recommendation** on devolution was **7.25% of the State’s net own tax revenue** for local bodies.
- The Government’s Action Taken material records acceptance of **6.75%** for 2020–21 through 2023–24 and **7.00%** for 2024–25.
- Accordingly, “the Sixth SFC recommended 6.75%” is inaccurate if the question asks for the Commission’s recommendation. The figure 6.75% describes the accepted arrangement for specified years.
- The Sixth SFC’s commonly cited distribution approach allocated **75.10%** of the pool to Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) and **24.90%** to Urban Local Bodies (ULBs); within the PRI share, the stated ratio was **75:20:5** for Gram Panchayats, Panchayat Samitis and Zila Parishads. Use the final report/Action Taken Report to answer whether a question asks “recommended by Commission” or “accepted/implemented by Government.”

### 4.4 SFC functions and exam traps

1. It makes recommendations; they become Government transfers according to the Government’s decision and implementation.
2. The Finance Commission is constituted by the **Governor**, but operational work/reporting and implementation involve the State’s institutional framework.
3. The Commission addresses Panchayats **and** Municipalities; omission of ULBs is a frequent mistake.
4. Its constitutional cycle is every five years; a late or interim report does not change the wording of Articles 243-I and 243-Y.

## 5. Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission (RSHRC)

### 5.1 Establishment and legal basis

- The RSHRC is a **statutory** commission under Section 21 of the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**, a Central Act as amended.
- Rajasthan notified the constitution of the Commission on **18 January 1999**. It became operational after the appointment of its first Chairperson and members on **23 March 2000**.
- Headquarters: **Jaipur**.
- Official Rajasthan pages listed **Justice Ganga Ram Moolchandani** as Chairperson in 2026. Treat names as dated officeholder facts, not permanent constitutional facts.

### 5.2 Composition after the 2019 amendment

Under the amended Section 21, a State Commission consists of:

1. A Chairperson who has been a **Chief Justice or a Judge of a High Court**;
2. One member who is or has been a High Court Judge, or a District Judge in the State with at least seven years’ experience as District Judge; and
3. One member with knowledge of or practical experience in human-rights matters.

The Secretary is the Commission’s Chief Executive Officer; under the amended Act the Secretary exercises administrative and financial powers subject to the Chairperson’s control. The composition is **one Chairperson plus two statutory members**—not the original five-person arrangement in the pre-amendment Act.

### 5.3 Appointment and tenure

- The Chairperson and members are appointed by the **Governor** after obtaining recommendations of a statutory selection committee.
- In a unicameral State such as Rajasthan, the committee comprises the **Chief Minister (Chairperson), Speaker of the Legislative Assembly, Home Minister, and Leader of the Opposition**. The PHRA provides additional members for States having a Legislative Council.
- Under the 2019 amendment, tenure is **three years or age 70, whichever is earlier**, with eligibility for reappointment subject to the statutory conditions.

### 5.4 Removal

A Chairperson/member is removed by the **President of India**, not by the Governor acting alone. For proved misbehaviour or incapacity, the statutory Supreme Court inquiry/reference process applies. Other grounds specified in PHRA Section 23 for removal by the President without that inquiry include being adjudged insolvent; engaging in paid employment outside official duties; being unfit because of infirmity of mind or body; being declared of unsound mind by a competent court; and conviction and sentence for an offence which, in the President’s opinion, involves moral turpitude. Do **not** import meeting-absence or prejudicial-interest grounds from other statutes into this PHRA list.

### 5.5 Powers, jurisdiction and limitations

Under the PHRA, the Commission may:

- inquire suo motu or on a petition into alleged human-rights violations or negligence by a public servant in preventing them;
- intervene in court proceedings involving human-rights allegations with the court’s approval;
- visit jails and custodial institutions;
- review constitutional and legal safeguards and recommend remedial action; and
- exercise specified civil-court powers during inquiry, such as summoning witnesses and seeking documents.

**Limitations:**

- Under Section 21(5), its subject-matter jurisdiction is limited to matters relatable to entries in the **State List and Concurrent List**. A matter already being inquired into by another duly constituted Commission is not to be inquired into again by the State Commission.
- Section 36(2) bars inquiry into a matter after **one year** from the date of the alleged act.
- The Commission primarily makes recommendations; it is not a criminal court and does not itself convict offenders.
- The State Government must report action taken or proposed in the statutory period (one month or further time permitted by the Act).
- Annual reports are submitted through the statutory State-government/Legislative-Assembly process.

**Exam distinction:** The Commission’s investigatory/civil-court powers do not turn it into a court with plenary power to impose criminal punishment. Recommendations, statutory directions and court orders must not be treated as synonyms.

## 6. Rajasthan State Information Commission (RSIC)

### 6.1 Constitutional/statutory position and establishment

- The RSIC is a **statutory body**, not a constitutional body.
- It was constituted on **18 April 2006** under Section 15 of the Right to Information Act, 2005.
- Headquarters: Jaipur.
- The RTI movement in Rajasthan is strongly associated with the work of the Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS) in Devdungri and the demand for public access to muster rolls and public expenditure records. This historical contribution should not be misdescribed as the sole legal origin of the national RTI Act.

### 6.2 Composition and appointment

- Composition: one **State Chief Information Commissioner (SCIC)** and such number of State Information Commissioners as may be considered necessary, not exceeding **ten**.
- The Governor appoints the SCIC and State Information Commissioners on the recommendation of the Section 15(3) committee:
  1. Chief Minister — Chairperson;
  2. Leader of the Opposition in the Legislative Assembly; and
  3. One Cabinet Minister nominated by the Chief Minister.

### 6.3 Term, age and reappointment: post-2019 rules

The 2019 amendment changed the Act so that terms and service conditions are prescribed by the Central Government. Under the applicable Central Rules, the term for a newly appointed SCIC/Information Commissioner is **three years**, subject to the statutory age ceiling of **65 years**. The Information Commissioner is not eligible for reappointment to the same office. An Information Commissioner may be appointed as SCIC, but the aggregate term in both posts must not exceed **five years**.

> **Frequent error corrected:** The aggregate term for a person moving from Information Commissioner to SCIC is not “three years in total.” The Act expressly caps the combined term at **five years**, while each office’s prescribed term is governed by the rules and age ceiling.

### 6.4 Removal

- The Governor may remove the SCIC or an Information Commissioner after the Supreme Court, on a reference made by the Governor, has inquired into proved misbehaviour or incapacity and reported that the person ought to be removed.
- During the inquiry, the Governor may suspend the Commissioner as the Act provides.
- The Act also sets out direct grounds such as insolvency, conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude, paid employment outside official duties, incapacity and acquisition of a financial or other interest likely to prejudice the discharge of functions. The original chapter’s shorter list omitted statutory grounds.

### 6.5 Complaints, appeals and penalties

| Provision | Role |
|---|---|
| Section 18 | Complaints, including where an application is not accepted, no PIO is appointed, or access is obstructed in the specified ways |
| Section 19 | First and second appeals; the State Commission hears second appeals under Section 19(3) |
| Section 20(1) | Penalty on a PIO of **₹250 per day**, subject to a maximum of **₹25,000**, in cases specified by law and after giving an opportunity to be heard |
| Section 20(2) | Recommendation for disciplinary action against the PIO under applicable service rules |
| Section 19(8)(b) | Power to require a public authority to compensate the complainant for loss or detriment, where the statutory conditions are met |

Do not attribute the compensation power to Section 20; it is found in the appellate powers under Section 19(8)(b). Nor does Section 20 make every delayed response automatically punishable: the statutory conditions and an opportunity of hearing apply.

### 6.6 RTI exemptions: a necessary qualification

Section 24 exempts the organisations listed in the Second Schedule, subject to the Act’s provisos. It is too broad to say that every item of information held by every intelligence/security organisation is absolutely exempt: the statute contains exceptions for allegations of corruption and human-rights violations, with a special approval and time-limit framework for human-rights information. The exact notified organisation and exception must be checked against the current Act and notifications.

**Current roster caution:** The Commission’s official website has current CIC/IC pages and notifications, but the landing page’s descriptive text includes historical names. For officeholder questions, use the current CIC/IC directory rather than treating an old introductory paragraph as a current roster.

## 7. Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas of Rajasthan

### 7.1 Foundation and historical markers

- Governing statute: **Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973** (Act No. 9 of 1973).
- The Act received Presidential assent on **26 March 1973** and is deemed to have come into force from **3 February 1973**. Assent/publication and retrospective commencement are different dates.
- First Lokayukta: **Justice I.D. Dua**, who assumed office in August 1973.
- **K.P.U. Menon** is commonly recorded as the first and sole person to have served as Up-Lokayukta, from **5 June 1973 to 25 June 1974**. The Act itself permits the Governor to appoint one or more Up-Lokayuktas, after consultation with the Lokayukta; the historical absence of later appointments does not remove the statutory power.

### 7.2 Appointment, qualifications and tenure

- Appointment is made by the **Governor** by warrant.
- The Lokayukta must be appointed after consultation with the Chief Justice of the Rajasthan High Court and the Leader of the Opposition in the Legislative Assembly (or, if there is no such Leader, a person elected by the opposition members in the prescribed manner).
- Up-Lokayuktas are appointed after consultation with the Lokayukta.
- The statutory qualification for Lokayukta is a person who has been a Supreme Court Judge or a Chief Justice/Judge of a High Court.
- The Act’s original five-year tenure was amended to eight years in 2018; the 2019 legislative reversal restored the **five-year** term. The applicable term is five years or age 65, whichever is earlier, subject to the Act’s current text and any successor-continuity clause that applies.
- The 2018 and 2019 changes should be learned as a *legislative reversal*, not as two simultaneous operative terms.

### 7.3 Who is covered? Read the statutory definition, not a coaching list

The Act defines “Minister” as a member of the Council of Ministers **other than the Chief Minister**. “Public servant” includes the listed categories of State officers, ministers, specified Zila Parishad and Panchayat Samiti officeholders, specified municipal officeholders, and persons serving in or paid by designated local authorities, State-controlled corporations/companies and notified societies.

Accordingly:

- **Chief Minister:** excluded from the Act’s definition of “Minister.”
- **MLAs:** not included merely by virtue of being members of the Legislative Assembly; do not describe them as covered public servants unless a specific legal provision applies.
- **Sarpanchs and Ward Panchs:** not included merely by their elected Gram Panchayat roles in the Act’s enumerated local-body categories. The Act specifically names certain Panchayat Samiti and Zila Parishad officeholders.
- **Covered elected local-body offices:** include specified Pramukhs/Up-Pramukhs of Zila Parishads, Pradhans/Up-Pradhans of Panchayat Samitis and specified municipal heads, including Mayors and Deputy Mayors after amendment.
- **Express Section 22 exclusions:** include judges and members of the judicial service, court officers/staff, Accountant General Rajasthan, RPSC Chairperson/member, the Chief Election Commissioner/Election Commissioners/Regional Commissioners under Article 324 and the Chief Electoral Officer of Rajasthan, and Secretariat staff of the Legislative Assembly. Do not casually replace the statute’s Article 324 list with “State Election Commissioner”: those are different offices.

### 7.4 Jurisdiction, limitation and procedure

- Under Section 7, the Lokayukta investigates action taken by or with the specified approval of Ministers (excluding the Chief Minister in the statutory definition), Secretaries and defined public servants, subject to the Act.
- Under Section 8(3), a complaint involving an allegation cannot be investigated if made after **five years** from the date of the alleged action.
- Allegations include abuse of position to obtain gain/favour or cause undue harm, improper/personal/corrupt motives in discharge of functions, or corruption/lack of integrity in public office.
- Investigations are ordinarily conducted **in private**; a matter of definite public importance may be investigated publicly for reasons recorded in writing.
- The Lokayukta does not itself operate as a criminal court or automatically convict and sentence an accused. It investigates and reports/recommends within the statutory scheme; follow-up action depends on the competent authority and other lawful proceedings.
- Under the Act’s definition of “competent authority,” in a case concerning a **Minister or Secretary, the Chief Minister** is the competent authority; in other cases, the prescribed authority applies. The source chapter’s statement that the Governor is the competent authority for Ministers is wrong.
- The competent authority must respond to the report within the statutory period (commonly tested as **three months** under the Act’s reporting framework), stating action taken or proposed. Confirm the latest amended text when a question asks for the exact section/period.

### 7.5 Removal

The Lokayukta/Up-Lokayukta may be removed by the Governor on grounds of misbehaviour or incapacity and no other ground, subject to Article 311 and the procedure in Section 6 of the Rajasthan Act. The inquiry must be conducted by the class of senior judge specified in the Act, the report is laid before the State Legislature, and removal requires an address passed by the required **special majority** in the Legislative Assembly. It is inaccurate to conflate this with the Article 317 removal process for PSC members or the President’s removal process for State Human Rights Commission members.

## 8. Rajasthan State Commission for Women (RSCW)

### 8.1 Legal status and formation

- The Commission is statutory under the **Rajasthan State Commission for Women Act, 1999** (Act No. 4 of 1999).
- The Act was published/commenced on **28 April 1999**. Reference books commonly cite **15 May 1999** as the Commission’s formal constitution/operational date; do not confuse that administrative date with the Act’s commencement.
- Headquarters: Jaipur.

### 8.2 Composition and tenure — statutory wording

- Section 3(2) provides for a Chairperson and **not more than four members, including the Member Secretary**, appointed by the State Government.
- Among the members, the Act requires representation for a woman belonging to the Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes and a member from the Other Backward Classes. The Act also separately refers to appointment of a Secretary under Section 3(5); preserve the statute’s terms instead of simplifying the full structure to a fixed “one Chairperson + three members + one Member Secretary” in all circumstances.
- The Chairperson must be an eminent woman committed to women’s causes, with adequate knowledge and experience. Members must be women of ability, integrity and standing with relevant knowledge/experience.
- Under Section 4, the Chairperson and members serve **three years**, subject to the Act.

### 8.3 Powers, functions and accountability

The Commission can examine constitutional/statutory safeguards for women; inquire into complaints of deprivation of rights, non-implementation of protective laws or non-compliance with women’s welfare policies; inspect specified custodial/shelter institutions; and recommend corrective measures or legal/policy reforms.

For listed inquiry purposes, it has specified civil-court powers, including summoning witnesses, requiring documents, accepting affidavits and requisitioning records. These powers do not mean it is a criminal trial court.

### 8.4 Removal and vacancies

Section 8 permits removal by the **State Government** on specified statutory grounds, including insolvency, conviction and sentence for an offence involving moral turpitude or other criminal activity, unsoundness of mind, refusal/incapacity to act, unauthorised absence from three consecutive meetings, and abuse of position so as to render continuance detrimental to public interest. A member must be given a reasonable opportunity of being heard before removal. “Removed for misconduct/public interest” is too vague to substitute for the grounds enumerated in the Act.

The Commission’s chairperson/member roster is a current-affairs fact. Before memorising a name, check the official RSCW portal and appointment notification; a three-year term means older lists may be stale.

## 10. Twenty-five examination traps — corrected

1. **RPSC dates:** Ordinance promulgated **16 August 1949**; Gazette publication is commonly recorded as **20 August 1949**. Neither date is the same as the reported formal inauguration date.
2. **RPSC appointment vs removal:** Governor appoints; the **President** removes under Article 317.
3. **RPSC suspension during SC inquiry:** The **President**, not the Governor, may suspend a member once a reference is before the Supreme Court under Article 317(2).
4. **RPSC term:** Six years or age **62**, whichever is earlier.
5. **RPSC 50% composition condition:** As nearly as may be, half the members have at least ten years’ prior government service; this is not an exact rigid quota for every appointment.
6. **Charged expenditure:** RPSC expenses are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of Rajasthan** under Article 322.
7. **Article 319:** The permitted next appointments differ for Chairpersons and members; do not say all former members are barred from every public-service role.
8. **RPSC current sanctioned strength:** The official 20 August 2026 handbook contains an inconsistent strength line; verify the latest governing notification rather than relying on the contradictory arithmetic.
9. **Election authority:** SEC = local bodies; ECI = Parliament/State Assembly/President/Vice-President elections.
10. **SEC removal:** Same manner and grounds as a High Court Judge; not an ordinary Governor’s dismissal.
11. **SEC tenure:** Rajasthan service rules say five years or age 65, whichever is earlier.
12. **SFC coverage:** Both Panchayats and Municipalities are included under Articles 243-I and 243-Y.
13. **SFC is not the Union Finance Commission:** It deals with State/local-body fiscal relations, not constitutional distribution of Union taxes.
14. **Sixth SFC percentage:** 7.25% was the final Commission recommendation; the Government’s accepted share was 6.75% for 2020–24 and 7% for 2024–25.
15. **Latest SFC status:** Finance Department lists a Seventh SFC interim report for 2025–26; Sixth SFC is not the latest commission.
16. **RSHRC composition:** After the 2019 amendment: Chairperson (former Chief Justice or Judge of a High Court) + two members of the statutory categories.
17. **RSHRC term:** Three years or age 70, whichever is earlier; appointment is by the Governor on the statutory committee’s recommendation.
18. **RSHRC removal:** President under PHRA Section 23 procedure—not Governor alone.
19. **RSHRC one-year bar:** Section 36(2) prevents inquiry after one year from the alleged act.
20. **RSIC term:** Three years under Central Rules for newly appointed Commissioners, subject to age 65; an Information Commissioner promoted to SCIC has an aggregate cap of five years.
21. **RTI penalty:** Section 20 penalty is ₹250 per day up to ₹25,000, subject to statutory conditions and hearing.
22. **Compensation under RTI:** Section 19(8)(b), not Section 20.
23. **Lokayukta and CM:** “Minister” in the Rajasthan Act expressly excludes the Chief Minister.
24. **Lokayukta competent authority:** For a Minister or Secretary, the Chief Minister is the competent authority under the Act’s definition; do not substitute the Governor.
25. **Women’s Commission structure/removal:** Act allows Chairperson plus no more than four members including Member Secretary; three-year tenure; removal by the State Government on enumerated grounds after an opportunity to be heard.

## 11. Practice questions and model answer frameworks

### 2-markers

**Q1. Who can suspend an RPSC Chairperson/member during a Supreme Court inquiry under Article 317?**  
**Model answer:** The President of India may suspend the person under Article 317(2) while the Supreme Court reference is pending.

**Q2. Which elections fall under the Rajasthan State Election Commission?**  
**Model answer:** Elections to Panchayats and Municipalities under Articles 243K and 243ZA; Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections fall under the ECI.

**Q3. Distinguish the Sixth SFC’s recommendation from the Government’s accepted devolution.**  
**Model answer:** The Sixth SFC recommended 7.25% of State net own tax revenue; the Government accepted 6.75% for 2020–24 and 7% for 2024–25.

**Q4. State the RTI penalty imposed under Section 20(1).**  
**Model answer:** ₹250 per day, up to ₹25,000, where statutory grounds are met and the PIO has received an opportunity to be heard.

### 5-markers

**Q5. Explain the independence safeguards for RPSC.**  
**Model answer framework:** Appointment by Governor; removal by President under Article 317; Supreme Court inquiry for misbehaviour; President’s suspension power during the inquiry; charged expenditure under Article 322; protection against disadvantageous service-condition changes; post-tenure restrictions under Article 319; annual reporting under Article 323.

**Q6. Outline the powers and limits of the RSHRC.**  
**Model answer framework:** PHRA, 1993; complaint/suo motu inquiry into human-rights violation or negligence by public servant; custodial visits and review of safeguards; specified civil-court powers; limited to State/Concurrent List matters; one-year limitation; recommendations rather than criminal convictions.

**Q7. Compare the RSIC and Lokayukta.**  
**Model answer framework:** Both are statutory, but their legal bases differ (RTI Act, 2005 vs Rajasthan Lokayukta Act, 1973); RSIC hears RTI complaints/appeals and may impose Section 20 penalties; Lokayukta investigates specified allegations against defined public servants; RSIC removal is governed by Section 17, while Lokayukta removal follows Section 6’s special procedure; never generalise their jurisdiction or remedies.

### 10-marker: governance and accountability

**Question:** “Constitutional and statutory commissions improve accountability but often depend on executive follow-through.” Critically discuss with reference to Rajasthan.

**Suggested framework:**
1. **Introduction:** Distinguish constitutional commissions (RPSC, SEC, SFC) from statutory commissions (RSHRC, RSIC, Lokayukta, RSCW).
2. **Autonomy tools:** Constitutional/statutory appointment procedures, protected removal processes for selected offices, charged expenses where provided, defined tenure and reporting duties.
3. **Distinct mandates:** Merit recruitment; local-body elections; fiscal devolution; human-rights inquiries; transparency and RTI penalties; investigation of allegations against public servants; women’s rights safeguards.
4. **Limits:** Narrow statutory jurisdiction, time bars, recommendatory reports, vacant posts, dependence on other authorities for implementation and public awareness/record access.
5. **Reforms:** Timely appointments, annual reports laid and discussed, reasoned action-taken memoranda, digitised case/status disclosure, independent staffing/budgets where legally permitted, periodic evaluation and transparent follow-up.
6. **Conclusion:** Effectiveness depends on the right balance between independence, legal accountability, transparent process and implementation—not on institutional labels alone.
