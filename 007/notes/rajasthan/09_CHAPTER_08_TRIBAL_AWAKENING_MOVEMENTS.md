# Chapter 08: Tribal Awakening Movements of Rajasthan

## 1. Socio-Historical Background & Colonial Marginalization

### 1.1 The Indigenous Tribes of Southern & Eastern Rajasthan
The forested ranges of the Aravallis in southern and south-eastern Rajasthan have served as the ancestral habitat of ancient indigenous communities, primarily the **Bhils (भील)**, **Garasias (गरासिया)**, and **Meenas (मीणा)**. 
- **The Historic Bhil Autonomy**: The Bhils held sovereign mastery over the forested tracts (*Bhomat / Pal*), governed through traditional clan heads (*Gameti*). They maintained a sacred, symbiotic feudal compact with the Rajput rulers:
  - Applying the royal coronation mark (*Raj-Tilak*) with blood upon the Sisodia Maharanas of Mewar.
  - Bearing the royal insignia on the Mewar coat of arms alongside the Sisodia Sun (*"जो दृढ़ राखे धर्म को तिही राखे करतार"*).
- **The Meenas of Dhundhar**: Ancient rulers of Amer and Manch, displaced by the Kachhwahas in the 12th century, who retained the hereditary privilege of guarding royal treasuries and applying the coronation mark at Amer's Kadami Mahal.

#### Creation of the Mewar Bhil Corps (MBC, 1841 CE)
To neutralize the military potential of the Bhils and police the turbulent Bhomat frontier, the British East India Company established the **Mewar Bhil Corps (मेवाड़ भील कोर — MBC)** in **1841** with headquarters at **Kherwara (खेरवाड़ा, modern Udaipur)** and a secondary cantonment at **Kotra (कोटड़ा)**. Financed directly from Mewar's state treasury, the MBC was deployed by British officers to crush tribal revolts across southern Rajasthan.

- **Dhooni (धूनी) & Nishan (निशान)**: Govind Guru established sacred fire hearths (*Dhoonis*) and hoisted sacred white flags (*Nishan*) across tribal hamlets in Dungarpur, Banswara, Idar, Sunth-Rampur, and Kushalgarh.

Over **1.5 lakh Bhil devotees** gathered peacefully to participate in the sacred Yajna and sing spiritual bhajans.

## 3. The Eki Movement & Motilal Tejawat (एकी आंदोलन, 1921)

### 3.1 Motilal Tejawat (1886–1963 CE) — "Bavji" & The Tribal Gandhi
- **Birth & Identity**: Born in 1886 at **Koliyari village (कोलियारी, Jhadol, Udaipur)** in a prosperous **Oswal Jain** merchant family.
- **The Moral Turning Point**: Employed as the *Kamdar* (estate manager) of the Thikana of Jhadol. Witnessing firsthand the unspeakable torture, lashings, and forced labor (*Begar*) inflicted upon the Bhils and Garasias by feudal landlords, Tejawat resigned in protest and dedicated his life to their emancipation.
- **Revered Epithets**:
  1. **Bavji (बावजी — Respected Father)**: Bestowed with filial love by the tribal population.
  2. **The Gandhi of the Tribals (आदिवासियों का गांधी)**.
  3. **Moti Maharaj (मोती महाराज)**.

---

### 3.2 Launch at Matrikundiya (May 1921 CE)
Inspired by the Non-Cooperation Movement and the parallel successes of Bijolia, Tejawat launched the **Eki Movement (एकी आंदोलन — The Unity Movement)**, also known as the **Bhomat Bhil Movement (भौमट भील आंदोलन)**:
- **Location**: Consecrated on Chaitra/Vaishakh Purnima in May 1921 at **Matrikundiya (मातृकुंडिया, Rashmi tehsil, Chittorgarh)** on the banks of river Banas.
- **Cultural Epithet**: Matrikundiya is celebrated across Rajasthan as the **"Haridwar of Rajasthan" (राजस्थान का हरिद्वार)**.
- **The Sacred Eki Pledge**: The Bhil and Garasia panchs took a solemn oath (*Eki*) of absolute mutual solidarity:
  > *"न हाकिम, न हुकम!"*  
  > *(Neither the British Magistrate, nor the Feudal Order!)*
  Peasants pledged complete non-cooperation: refusing to pay arbitrary cesses, refusing begar, and boycotting state courts.

---

### 3.3 "Mewar Ki Pukar" (मेवाड़ की पुकार — The 21 Demands)
Tejawat drafted a historic manifesto of grievances entitled **"Mewar Ki Pukar" (The Cry of Mewar)**, presenting it directly to **Maharana Fateh Singh** of Mewar in 1921:
- **Structure**: Contained **21 critical demands** addressing forced labor, timber restrictions, grazing cesses, and police brutality.
- **Maharana’s Decision**: The Maharana accepted **18 demands**; however, he flatly rejected the **3 most crucial demands**:
  1. The total abolition of compulsory forced labor (**Begar**).
  2. The lifting of state restrictions on **Forest Rights (वनाधिकार)** (collecting timber and minor forest produce).
  3. The lifting of the prohibition on hunting **wild boars** that ravaged agricultural crops.
- Tejawat resolved to intensify the civil disobedience campaign into an active tax-boycott.

#### Subsequent Repression: The Rasia-Valoriya Massacre (May 1922)
In May 1922, British Major Pritchard surrounded the Garasia villages of **Rasia and Valoriya (Sirohi)**, setting fire to over **1,800 residential dwellings**, burning grain stores, and killing dozens of tribals.

#### Tejawat's Surrender & Final Contributions
- In 1929, acting upon the urgent counsel of **Mahatma Gandhi** (conveyed through Gandhian envoy **Manilal Kothari**), Motilal Tejawat peacefully surrendered before the police at **Khedbrahma (Idar state)**.
- Handed over to Udaipur state, he remained imprisoned without trial in Central Jail until **1936**, released only after pledging not to enter Bhomat without permission.
- Post-1947, Tejawat organized the *Vanvasi Seva Sangh* and was elected to the Legislative Assembly, passing away in 1963.

## 4. The Meena Movement & The Jairam Pesha Agitation (1924–1952)

### 4.1 Historical Roots: The Division of the Meenas
Following the rise of the Kachhwahas in Dhundhar, the Meenas split into two socio-economic strata:
1. **Zamindari Meena (जमींदारी मीणा)**: Landed agriculturists enjoying proprietary peasant rights over ancestral soil.
2. **Chaukidari Meena (चौकीदारी मीणा)**: Entrusted with hereditary night-watch duty (*Chaukidari*) over villages, trade routes, and royal palaces. 
   - **The Draconian Liability**: Under customary state rules, if any theft occurred within their assigned village, the Chaukidari Meena was legally bound to compensate the victim for the full value of the stolen goods or be imprisoned.

#### Muni Magan Sagar & The Neem Ka Thana Conference (April 1944)
- **Identity**: A venerable Jain ascetic hailing from the Meena community who conducted pioneering historical and genealogical research into Meena origins.
- **Meena Puran (मीणा पुराण)**: Composed this definitive religious and historical epic tracing the descent of the Meenas to the sacred **Matsya Avatar (Fish Incarnation of Lord Vishnu)**, instilling profound self-respect and Kshatriya consciousness across the community.
- **The Great Neem Ka Thana Convention (April 1944)**:
  - Convened a monumental assembly of over **50,000 Meena representatives** at **Neem Ka Thana (Sikar)** in April 1944.
  - Formally established the **All-India Meena Mahasabha (अखिल भारतीय मीणा महासभा)**.
  - Prominent leaders: **Pandit Bansidhar Sharma** (President), **Lakshmi Narayan Jharwal**, and **Kishan Swaroop**.

#### The Baghawas Summit & Mukti Diwas (28 October 1946)
- On **28 October 1946**, at a mammoth convention held at **Baghawas (बागावास)**, over **26,000 Chaukidari Meenas** dramatically walked up to the dias and surrendered their hereditary royal batons, **resigning en masse from their Chaukidari positions**.
- Commemorated as **"Mukti Diwas" (मुक्ति दिवस — Liberation Day)** across Meena history.
- The Jaipur state was compelled to immediately exempt all women and children from the mandatory daily police roll-call.

#### Final Abolition (1952 CE)
Following Indian independence, prominent freedom fighters and democratic leaders, including **Hiralal Shastri** and Chief Minister **Tikaram Paliwal**, championed the cause in the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly. In **1952**, the draconian **Jairam Pesha Act was permanently repealed**, ending 81 years of institutionalized ethnic criminalization.

## 6. The 25 Deadliest Examination Traps (Forensic Autopsy)

1. **Trap 1: The Birthplace of Govind Guru**:
   - *Trap*: Claiming he was born at Mangarh or Sirohi.
   - *Fact*: Born at **Bansia / Bedsa village (Dungarpur)** in 1858 in a **Banjara** family.

2. **Trap 2: Founding Location of Samp Sabha**:
   - *Trap*: Stating it was founded at Dungarpur or Mangarh.
   - *Fact*: Founded at **Sirohi in 1883 CE**.

3. **Trap 3: Spiritual Inspiration of Govind Guru**:
   - *Trap*: Swami Vivekananda or Ramakrishna Paramahamsa.
   - *Fact*: **Swami Dayanand Saraswati**, whom he met at Udaipur in 1880–81.

4. **Trap 4: Date of the Mangarh Dham Massacre**:
   - *Trap*: Stating 1919 (conflating with Jallianwala Bagh).
   - *Fact*: Occurred on **17 November 1913 (Margashirsha / Ashwin Purnima)** — 6 full years before Jallianwala Bagh!

5. **Trap 5: The Toll of Mangarh Dham vs Jallianwala Bagh**:
   - *Trap*: Believing 400 or 500 were killed.
   - *Fact*: Over **1,500 Bhil devotees** were slaughtered at Mangarh Dham, far exceeding Jallianwala Bagh's official death toll.

6. **Trap 6: Final Resting Place of Govind Guru**:
   - *Trap*: Deshnoke, Mangarh, or Banswara.
   - *Fact*: **Kamboi village (near Jhalod, Gujarat)**, where his Samadhi is located.

7. **Trap 7: Caste Lineage of Motilal Tejawat**:
   - *Trap*: Assuming he was a Bhil tribal leader by birth.
   - *Fact*: Born in a prosperous **Oswal Jain merchant family** at Koliyari (Jhadol).

8. **Trap 8: Consecration Site of the Eki Movement**:
   - *Trap*: Neemra or Koliyari.
   - *Fact*: Launched at **Matrikundiya (Rashmi, Chittorgarh)**, celebrated as the *"Haridwar of Rajasthan"*.

9. **Trap 9: "Mewar Ki Pukar" Demands Structure**:
   - *Trap*: Claiming all 21 demands were rejected.
   - *Fact*: The Maharana **accepted 18 demands**; only **3 demands** (Begar, Forest Rights, and Wild Boars) were rejected.

10. **Trap 10: Date & Casualties of the Neemra Massacre**:
    - *Trap*: Confusing Neemra with Mangarh.
    - *Fact*: **Neemra occurred on 7 March 1922**; British MBC firing killed over **1,200 Bhils and Garasias**.

11. **Trap 11: The British Regiment Deploying Fire at Mangarh & Neemra**:
    - *Trap*: 15th Bengal Native Infantry.

    - *Trap*: Pt. Bansidhar Sharma or Hiralal Shastri.
    - *Fact*: Jain ascetic **Muni Magan Sagar**.

    - *Trap*: Jaipur or Baghawas.
    - *Fact*: **Neem Ka Thana (Sikar)**, presided over by Muni Magan Sagar.

    - *Trap*: 15 August 1947.
    - *Fact*: **28 October 1946 at Baghawas**, when 26,000 Chaukidari Meenas simultaneously resigned.

16. **Trap 16: Final Repeal Year of the Jairam Pesha Act**:
    - *Trap*: Stating 1947 or 1950.

**Answer**: A 21-point charter of tribal grievances drafted by **Motilal Tejawat** in 1921 presented to Maharana Fateh Singh, demanding abolition of Begar and restoration of forest rights.

#### Q4: What is the historical significance of the Neemra Massacre (7 March 1922)?
**Answer**: Mewar Bhil Corps under Major Sutton opened machine-gun fire upon an Eki conference at Neemra village, martyring over **1,200 Bhils and Garasias**.

### 7.2 Section B: 5-Mark Analytical Blueprints (50 Words)

#### Q8: Analyze the socio-religious and political contributions of Govind Guru to tribal emancipation.
**Answer**:
Govind Guru (1858–1931 CE) transformed Vagad's oppressed Bhil community from fatalism to self-respect:
- **Moral Renaissance**: Founded **Samp Sabha (1883)**, establishing 10 ethical vows: banned alcohol, animal sacrifice, and superstitions while instituting daily Yajna (*Dhooni*).
- **Parallel Judicial System**: Appointed village *Kotwals* to arbitrate disputes, freeing tribals from corrupt colonial courts.
- **Anti-Feudal Awakening**: Mobilized refusal against unpaid labor (*Begar*) and illegal cesses, culminating in the heroic martyrdom of 1,500 Bhils at **Mangarh Dham (1913)**.

#### Q9: Detail the catalysts, trajectory, and significance of the Eki Movement under Motilal Tejawat.
**Answer**:
Launched in May 1921 at Matrikundiya by Motilal Tejawat ("Bavji"):
- **Catalysts**: Excessive cesses, loss of traditional forest rights (*Vana Adhikar*), and oppressive feudal Begar in the Bhomat tract.
- **Trajectory**: United Bhils and Garasias under the slogan *"न हाकिम, न हुकम"*; presented 21-point *Mewar Ki Pukar*; endured brutal state slaughter at **Neemra (1922)** where 1,200 tribals fell.
- **Significance**: Successfully integrated tribal struggles with the mainstream Indian national movement, breaking feudal submissiveness.

#### Q10: Trace the legislative and social struggle that led to the repeal of the Jairam Pesha Act in 1952.
**Answer**:
Branded a criminal tribe under the 1871 British Act and 1930 Jaipur Jairam Pesha Act, the Meenas waged sustained institutional resistance:
- **Socio-Cultural Mobilization**: **Meena Kshatriya Sabha (1933)** and Muni Magan Sagar’s **Meena Puran** led to the 50,000-strong **Neem Ka Thana Convention (1944)**.
- **Mass Civil Disobedience**: On **28 October 1946 (Baghawas)**, 26,000 Chaukidars resigned (*Mukti Diwas*), compelling exemption of women and children.
- **Legislative Victory**: Abolished permanently in **1952** under Chief Minister Tikaram Paliwal.

#### Q12: Trace the evolution of the Meena community's struggle against colonial criminalization from the Criminal Tribes Act of 1871 to the 1952 emancipation.
**Answer**:
The emancipation of Rajasthan’s Meena community represents one of the most heroic non-violent civil rights triumphs in modern Indian history:

1. **The Colonial Yoke of Criminalization (1871–1930)**:
   - **Criminal Tribes Act (1871)**: The British imperial state branded the entire community a hereditary criminal tribe.
   - **Jairam Pesha Act (1930)**: Enacted by Jaipur state, requiring all Meenas aged 12 and above to mark daily in-person attendance (*Haziri*) at local police stations, inflicting generational psychological and social humiliation.

2. **The Era of Intellectual & Cultural Awakening (1933–1944)**:
   - In 1933, the **Meena Kshatriya Sabha** was established to spearhead legal petitions.
   - **Muni Magan Sagar's Renaissance**: Researched tribal genealogies, authored **Meena Puran**, and traced descent to Vishnu’s Matsya Avatar.
   - Consecrated the historic **Neem Ka Thana Convention (April 1944)** with 50,000 attendees, establishing the **All-India Meena Mahasabha** under Pt. Bansidhar Sharma.

3. **Mass Civil Disobedience to Liberation (1946–1952)**:
   - **Baghawas Summit (28 October 1946)**: 26,000 Chaukidari Meenas simultaneously resigned en masse (*Mukti Diwas*), shattering the state's policing infrastructure and securing exemption for women and children.
   - **Final Legislative Triumph (1952)**: Following national independence, democratic mobilizations culminated in the complete and irrevocable repeal of the Jairam Pesha Act in 1952 under Chief Minister Tikaram Paliwal, restoring full constitutional dignity.
