# PART I — COLONIAL SUBJUGATION & 19TH-CENTURY TRIBAL UPRISINGS

## 1. Ecological Encroachment & The Collapse of Tribal Autonomy

Before the British subsidiary alliances of 1818, the indigenous **Bhil**, **Garasia**, and **Meena** communities maintained substantial internal autonomy across the Aravalli hills:

```
                    ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │      PILLARS OF COLONIAL-FEUDAL DISENFRANCHISEMENT     │
                    └───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                                │
         ┌──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┐
         │                                      │                                      │
┌────────┴────────────────────────┐   ┌─────────┴───────────────────────┐   ┌──────────┴─────────────────────────┐
│ Deprivation of Forest Rights    │   │ Outlawing of Traditional Cesses │   │ Demilitarization & State Monopoly  │
├─────────────────────────────────┤   ├─────────────────────────────────┤   ├────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Customary rights to timber,   │   │ • Abolished traditional transit │   │ • State auction of liquor distillation│
│   firewood, and Minor Forest    │     security fee (*Bolai*) and      │     displaced domestic Mahua brewing,  │
│   Produce (MFP) terminated.     │     watch-and-ward cess (*Rakhwali*).│     indebting tribals to usurers.      │
│ • Commercial state forestry.    │   │ • Stripped chiefs of income.    │   │ • Created Mewar Bhil Corps (1841). │
└─────────────────────────────────┘   └─────────────────────────────────┘   └────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **The Feudal Compact Subverted [Secure Core]:** Historically, the Bhils exercised customary sovereignty over the forested hill tracts (*Bhomat / Pal*), governed by traditional village headmen (*Gametis*). They held a symbolic status in Mewar: applying the royal coronation mark (*Raj-Tilak*) with blood upon the Sisodia Maharanas and sharing the royal crest of Mewar alongside the sun symbol (*"जो दृढ़ राखे धर्म को तिही राखे करतार"*).
2. **Abolition of *Bolai* and *Rakhwali* [Secure Core]:** The British Company banned traditional tribal levies:
   - *Bolai (बोलाई):* A traditional transit duty collected by Bhils from traders and travellers in exchange for guaranteed safe passage through hill passes.
   - *Rakhwali (रखवाली):* A village protection cess collected by Bhil headmen for guarding rural boundaries against raids.
   Deprived of these incomes, tribal chiefs were reduced to acute poverty.
3. **Creation of the Mewar Bhil Corps (MBC, 1841 CE) [Secure Core]:**
   - Established by the British East India Company in **1841** to demilitarize the Bhils and pacify the turbulent Bhomat frontier.
   - Headquartered at **Kherwara (खेरवाड़ा, modern Udaipur)** with a secondary military cantonment at **Kotra (कोटड़ा)**.
   - Financed entirely from Mewar's state treasury, the MBC was commanded by British officers and frequently deployed to suppress tribal insurrections.

## 2. Early 19th-Century Tribal Uprisings (1818–1881)

Prior to Govind Guru's socio-religious movement, southern Rajasthan witnessed repeated spontaneous armed uprisings:
- **1818–1825 Bhil Revolts [Secure Core]:** In response to British-Mewar treaty arrangements, Bhils of the Bhomat region rose in open rebellion. In 1823–1825, British troops under **Colonel Lumley** launched punitive expeditions into the hills to force submission.
- **The Rikhabdeo / Barapal Outbreak (1881–1882 CE) [Secure Core]:**
  - **Triggers:** Introduction of the 1881 British population census (which tribals feared was a prelude to forced military conscription or physical taxation), state bans on domestic Mahua liquor distillation, and the establishment of new police checkposts (*Thanas*).
  - **The Clash:** On 26 March 1881, state police forces killed Bhil gametis at Padona and Barapal. Thousands of Bhils besieged state garrisons, cutting lines of communication between Udaipur and Ahmedabad.
  - **The Sacred Rikhabdeo Accord (25 April 1881):** Recognizing the military danger, Mewar minister **Shyamaldas** (author of *Vir Vinod*) and the British Political Agent met Bhil chiefs at the sacred **Rikhabdeo (Kesariyaji) Temple**. Under the divine witness of Lord Rishabhdev, the state signed a 21-clause compromise: land taxes were reduced by half, the census was suspended in tribal areas, and customary forest usage was temporarily recognized.

# PART II — THE BHAGAT MOVEMENT & GOVIND GURU (1883–1913)

### The Institutional Architecture: Samp Sabha (1883 CE)
- **Founding Location [High-Yield Trap]:** Founded at **Sirohi in 1883 CE**, NOT at Dungarpur, Banswara, or Mangarh.
- **The Concept of 'Samp' [Secure Core]:** In the local Vagadi/Rajasthani dialect, **'Samp' (संप) signifies mutual love, brotherhood, peaceful cooperation, and solidarity**.
- **Ten Moral Vows (दश नियम):** Disciples (called *Bhagats*) pledged strict adherence to ethical conduct:
  1. Complete abstinence from distilled liquor (*Madira*) and intoxicating drugs.
  2. Strict vegetarianism and compassion towards animals (abolishing animal sacrifice).
  3. Cultivating daily hygiene, honesty, and truthfulness.
  4. Performing daily sacred Vedic fire rites (*Dhooni*) and unfurling sacred white flags (*Nishan*).
  5. Refusing to perform unremunerated forced labor (**Begar**) for feudal lords.
  6. Boycotting British and state civil courts by resolving all civil and criminal disputes through village *Panchayats*.
  7. Promoting primary education through local schools (*Pathshalas*).

### The Parallel Administrative Network
- **Dhoonis (धूनी) & Nishan (निशान) [Secure Core]:** Govind Guru established permanent sacred fire altars (*Dhoonis*) across tribal villages in Dungarpur, Banswara, Idar, Sunth-Rampur, and Kushalgarh.
- **Village Kotwals (कोतवाल):** Appointed trusted tribal leaders as local arbitrators and moral officers (*Kotwals*), creating a functioning parallel dispute-resolution system.
- **Alarm Among Rulers & British Contractors [Secure Core]:**
  - The mass abstention from alcohol caused the collapse of state excise revenues and ruined British liquor contractors.
  - The refusal of *Bhagat* tribals to perform free *Begar* directly challenged the feudal economic foundation of the southern princely states.

# PART III — THE MANGARH DHAM MASSACRE (17 NOVEMBER 1913)

## 4. "The Jallianwala Bagh of Rajasthan"

The confrontation between colonial-feudal authority and the Bhagat movement culminated in one of the bloodiest massacres in modern imperial history:

### The Gathering at Mangarh Hill
- **Location & Sacred Date [Secure Core]:** In October–November 1913, Govind Guru summoned his disciples to assemble atop **Mangarh Hill (मानगढ़ की पहाड़ी)**—located on the disputed border between **Banswara state** (Rajasthan) and **Sunth-Rampur state** (Gujarat)—to celebrate the annual religious festival of **Margashirsha Purnima (17 November 1913)**.
- **Scale of Assembly:** Over **1.5 lakh Bhil devotees** arrived with provisions to conduct a colossal Yajna, unfurl white flags, and chant sacred hymns.
- **Feudal-Colonial Panic:** Alarmed by rumors that Govind Guru intended to overthrow the princely rulers and establish an independent "Bhil Raj", the British Political Agent mobilized a joint imperial force comprising the **Mewar Bhil Corps**, **104th Wellesley Rifles**, **7th Rajput Regiment**, and Baroda artillery, commanded by **Major Hamilton** and **Captain Stoppford**.

### The Massacre (17 November 1913)
- Surrounded on all sides with no escape routes, the British opened fire with machine guns and mountain artillery into the dense gathering of unarmed families.
- **The Toll [Textbook Convention]:** Over **1,500 Bhil men, women, and children were martyred** on the slopes.
- **Historical Milestone:** This tragedy occurred on 17 November 1913—nearly **six years before the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)**.

### Capture, Trial & The Kamboi Exile
- Govind Guru was wounded in the leg and taken into custody alongside his primary lieutenant, **Punja Dhirji (पूंजा धीरजी)**.
- A special British tribunal sentenced Govind Guru to death by hanging. Upon review, the appellate court commuted the sentence to life imprisonment, and subsequently reduced it to ten years in Ahmedabad Central Jail.
- **Conditions of Release (1923 CE) [Secure Core]:** Released in 1923 on the strict legal condition that he would never enter the princely states of Dungarpur, Banswara, or Sunth.
- **Final Days at Kamboi:** Settled in **Kamboi village (near Jhalod, Dahod district, Gujarat)**, continuing social reform and Vedic education until his demise on **30 October 1931**, where his sacred memorial shrine (*Samadhi*) remains an international pilgrimage center.

# PART IV — THE EKI (BHOMAT) MOVEMENT & MOTILAL TEJAWAT (1921–1929)

## 5. Motilal Tejawat (1886–1963 CE): "Bavji" & The Tribal Gandhi

While Govind Guru’s movement was primarily socio-religious, the **Eki Movement** launched by Motilal Tejawat was an explicitly militant, anti-feudal, economic rights struggle:

```
[MOTILAL TEJAWAT: Born 1886 at Koliyari (Jhadol) in Oswal Jain family; Kamdar of Jhadol Thikana]
                                      │
[Resigned in disgust over feudal Begar & atrocities inflicted on Bhils ➔ Dedicated life to tribal uplift]
                                      │
[TITLES: "Bavji" (Respected Father) • "Gandhi of the Tribals" • "Moti Maharaj"]
                                      │
[MAY 1921: Consecrated EKI MOVEMENT at Matrikundiya ("Haridwar of Rajasthan") ➔ Slogan: "न हाकिम, न हुकम"]
                                      │
[DRAFTED "MEWAR KI PUKAR": 21-point charter presented to Maharana Fateh Singh (18 accepted, 3 rejected)]
```

### Launch at Matrikundiya (May 1921 CE)
- **Sacred Consecration [Secure Core]:** Launched on Chaitra/Vaishakh Purnima in May 1921 at **Matrikundiya** (Rashmi tehsil, Chittorgarh) on the banks of the Banas River. Celebrated across Rajasthan as the **"Haridwar of Rajasthan" (राजस्थान का हरिद्वार)**.
- **The Eki Oath [Secure Core]:** *Eki* means **"Unity" (एकता)**. Tribal delegates swore a solemn collective oath:
  > *"न हाकिम, न हुकम!"*  
  > *(Neither the British Magistrate, nor the Feudal Order!)*  
  Pledging complete non-cooperation: refusing to pay arbitrary cesses, refusing *Begar*, and boycotting feudal courts.

### "Mewar Ki Pukar" (The 21 Demands) [Secure Core]
Tejawat formulated an exhaustive 21-point manifesto of tribal rights entitled **"Mewar Ki Pukar" (मेवाड़ की पुकार)**, submitting it directly to Maharana Fateh Singh of Mewar:
- **Maharana's Partial Concession:** The Mewar Darbar accepted **18 demands** concerning administrative bribery and local extortion.
- **The 3 Core Demands Rejected [High-Yield Exam Trap]:** The Maharana categorically rejected the three most critical structural demands:
  1. The total and immediate abolition of compulsory forced labor (**Begar**).
  2. The restoration of customary **Forest Rights** (free gathering of firewood, timber, and forest produce).
  3. The lifting of the prohibition on killing crop-destroying **wild boars**.
- Following this rejection, Tejawat declared full civil disobedience and a complete tax boycott.

## 6. The Neemra Massacre (7 March 1922) & Sirohi Repression

### The Carnage at Neemra (Vijayanagar / Idar Border)
- **The Assembly [Secure Core]:** On **7 March 1922**, thousands of Bhils and Garasias gathered at **Neemra village (नीमड़ा)** to hold a grievance panchayat.
- **The Military Strike [Secure Core]:** British officer **Major Sutton**, commanding the **Mewar Bhil Corps**, surrounded the canyon without issuing a dispersal warning and ordered sustained machine-gun fire into the enclosed assembly.
- **The Casualties [Textbook Convention]:** Over **1,200 Bhils and Garasias were slaughtered** in the canyon.
- **Tejawat's Escape:** Tejawat was shot in both thighs. Rescued by loyal Bhil fighters, he was carried into the inaccessible caves of the Bhomat hills. The British placed a bounty of Rs 500 on his head, but tribal solidarity kept him sheltered underground for seven years.

### The Rasia-Valoriya Massacre (May 1922) [Secure Core]
Following Neemra, British troops under **Major Pritchard** attacked the Garasia villages of **Rasia and Valoriya** in Sirohi state, setting fire to over **1,800 dwellings**, burning grain stores, and killing dozens of tribals to break the Eki network.

### Surrender & Final Phase (1929–1963 CE)
- In June 1929, acting upon the urgent personal advice of **Mahatma Gandhi** (delivered through emissary **Manilal Kothari**), Motilal Tejawat surrendered peacefully before police at **Khedbrahma (Idar state)**.
- Handed over to Mewar, he was detained without formal trial in Udaipur Central Jail until **1936**, released only after signing a bond restricting his travel.
- Following independence, he organized the *Vanvasi Seva Sangh* and served in the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly until his death in 1963.

# PART V — THE MEENA REFORM MOVEMENT & ANTI-JAIRAM PESHA STRUGGLE (1924–1952)

## 7. Colonial Criminalization & Feudal Division

The Meenas of Dhundhar and eastern Rajasthan experienced severe legal-administrative oppression following British paramountcy:

```
[12th c. Kachhwaha displacement ➔ Meenas divided into Zamindari (agriculturists) and Chaukidari (guards)]
                                      │
[DRACO-LIABILITY: Chaukidari Meenas held financially liable for any theft occurring in their village]
                                      │
[CRIMINAL TRIBES ACT (1871): British Government branded entire Meena community a "criminal tribe"]
                                      │
[JAIRAM PESHA ACT (1930): Jaipur State mandated daily in-person police roll-call for all Meenas 12+ yrs]
                                      │
[MUNI MAGAN SAGAR: Author of "Meena Puran" ➔ Neem Ka Thana Convention (April 1944, 50,000 Meenas)]
                                      │
[MUKTI DIWAS: Baghawas Summit (28 Oct 1946) ➔ 26,000 Chaukidars resign en masse ➔ Act repealed in 1952]
```

### The Two Socio-Economic Strata [Secure Core]
1. **Zamindari Meena (जमींदारी मीणा):** Landowning cultivators possessing secure proprietary tenure.
2. **Chaukidari Meena (चौकीदारी मीणा):** Traditional hereditary watchmen guarding forts, palaces, and villages.
   - Under colonial and princely codes, if any burglary occurred within their assigned beat, the Chaukidari Meena was legally required to compensate the owner for the full value of the stolen goods or suffer imprisonment.

### Draconian Statutory Enactments
1. **Criminal Tribes Act (CTA, 1871 CE) [Secure Core]:** Enacted by the British Government of India, arbitrarily branding the entire Meena community as a "hereditary criminal tribe", restricting their travel and subjecting them to continuous police surveillance.
2. **Jairam Pesha Act (1930 CE) [Secure Core]:** Promulgated by the Jaipur State administration. It required every Meena male, female, and child aged **12 and above** to appear in person for a **daily morning and evening roll-call (*Haziri*)** at the nearest police station, under penalty of immediate public flogging.

## 8. The Thirty-Year Struggle for Emancipation (1924–1952)

### Organizational Resistance
1. **Meena Kshatriya Sabha (1933 CE) [Secure Core]:** Formed to lead social reform, promote temperance, and submit legal petitions against police harassment.
2. **Muni Magan Sagar & *Meena Puran* [Secure Core]:**
   - Venerable Jain ascetic hailing from the Meena community who conducted extensive genealogical research.
   - Authored **Meena Puran (मीणा पुराण)**, tracing the community's descent to the **Matsya Avatar (Fish Incarnation of Lord Vishnu)**, instilling profound self-respect and Kshatriya consciousness across eastern Rajasthan.
3. **The Neem Ka Thana Convention (April 1944) [Secure Core]:**
   - Convened a monumental assembly of over **50,000 Meenas** at **Neem Ka Thana (Sikar)** in April 1944 under the spiritual presidency of Muni Magan Sagar.
   - Founded the **All-India Meena Mahasabha (अखिल भारतीय मीणा महासभा)**; elected **Pandit Bansidhar Sharma** as its first formal president, joined by **Lakshmi Narayan Jharwal** and Kishan Swaroop.

### Mass Civil Disobedience: The Baghawas Summit (28 October 1946)
- On **28 October 1946**, at a mammoth convention held at **Baghawas (बागावास)**, over **26,000 Chaukidari Meenas** dramatically walked up to the stage, threw down their hereditary royal batons, and **resigned en masse from their Chaukidari posts**.
- Celebrated across community history as **"Mukti Diwas" (मुक्ति दिवस — Liberation Day)**.
- Compelled by the policing crisis, the Jaipur administration immediately exempted all women and children from the mandatory daily police roll-call.

### Final Legislative Abolition (1952 CE) [Secure Core]
Following national independence, sustained political advocacy led by leaders such as **Hiralal Shastri** and **Tikaram Paliwal** culminated in the **permanent repeal of the Jairam Pesha Act in 1952**, restoring full constitutional dignity to the community.

#### Q1: What was the primary objective of the Samp Sabha (1883 CE)?
**Answer:** Established by Govind Guru at Sirohi to foster brotherhood (*Samp*), enforce moral temperance (abstinence from alcohol/meat), and resist feudal Begar among Bhils.

#### Q2: Why is Mangarh Dham (1913 CE) termed the "Jallianwala Bagh of Rajasthan"?
**Answer:** On 17 November 1913, British forces and the Mewar Bhil Corps machine-gunned an unarmed assembly of Samp Sabha, martyring over **1,500 Bhil devotees**.

#### Q3: What was "Mewar Ki Pukar"?
**Answer:** A 21-point charter of tribal grievances drafted by Motilal Tejawat in 1921 presented to Maharana Fateh Singh, demanding abolition of Begar and restoration of forest rights.

#### Q4: What is the historical significance of the Neemra Massacre (7 March 1922)?
**Answer:** Mewar Bhil Corps under Major Sutton opened machine-gun fire upon an Eki conference at Neemra village, martyring over **1,200 Bhils and Garasias**.

#### Q5: Define the repressive essence of the Jairam Pesha Act (1930 CE).
**Answer:** Enacted by Jaipur state, requiring all Meena males, females, and children aged 12 and above to mark daily in-person attendance (*Haziri*) at local police stations.

#### Q6: What was the "Baghawas Summit" (28 October 1946)?
**Answer:** A historic convention where 26,000 Chaukidari Meenas resigned en masse from their posts (*Mukti Diwas*), forcing the state to exempt women and children from daily roll-call.

### 5-Mark Analytical Blueprints (50 Words)

#### Q7: Analyze the socio-religious and political contributions of Govind Guru to tribal emancipation.
**Answer:**
Govind Guru (1858–1931 CE) transformed Vagad's oppressed Bhil community from fatalism to self-respect:
- **Moral Renaissance:** Founded **Samp Sabha (1883)**, enforcing 10 ethical vows: prohibited alcohol, meat, and animal sacrifice; instituted sacred fire worship (*Dhooni*).
- **Parallel Judicial System:** Appointed village *Kotwals* to arbitrate disputes, freeing tribals from corrupt colonial courts.
- **Anti-Feudal Resistance:** Mobilized resistance against unpaid labor (*Begar*) and illegal cesses, culminating in the supreme martyrdom of 1,500 Bhils at **Mangarh Dham (1913)**.

#### Q8: Detail the catalysts, trajectory, and significance of the Eki Movement under Motilal Tejawat.
**Answer:**
Launched in May 1921 at Matrikundiya by Motilal Tejawat ("Bavji"):
- **Catalysts:** Heavy cesses, loss of traditional forest rights, and oppressive feudal Begar in the Bhomat tract.
- **Trajectory:** United Bhils and Garasias under the slogan *"न हाकिम, न हुकम"*; presented 21-point *Mewar Ki Pukar*; endured brutal state slaughter at **Neemra (1922)** where 1,200 tribals fell.
- **Significance:** Successfully integrated tribal struggles with the mainstream Indian national movement, breaking feudal submissiveness.

#### Q9: Trace the legislative and social struggle that led to the repeal of the Jairam Pesha Act in 1952.
**Answer:**
Branded a criminal tribe under the 1871 British Act and 1930 Jaipur Jairam Pesha Act, the Meenas waged sustained institutional resistance:
- **Socio-Cultural Mobilization:** **Meena Kshatriya Sabha (1933)** and Muni Magan Sagar’s **Meena Puran** led to the 50,000-strong **Neem Ka Thana Convention (1944)**.
- **Mass Civil Disobedience:** On **28 October 1946 (Baghawas)**, 26,000 Chaukidars resigned (*Mukti Diwas*), compelling exemption of women and children.
- **Legislative Victory:** Abolished permanently in **1952** under Chief Minister Tikaram Paliwal.

#### Q11: Trace the evolution of the Meena community's struggle against colonial criminalization from the Criminal Tribes Act of 1871 to the 1952 emancipation.
**Answer:**
The emancipation of Rajasthan’s Meena community represents one of the most heroic non-violent civil rights triumphs in modern Indian history:

1. **The Colonial Yoke of Criminalization (1871–1930):**
   - **Criminal Tribes Act (1871):** The British imperial state branded the entire community a hereditary criminal tribe.
   - **Jairam Pesha Act (1930):** Enacted by Jaipur state, requiring all Meenas aged 12 and above to mark daily in-person attendance (*Haziri*) at local police stations, inflicting severe psychological and social humiliation.

2. **The Era of Intellectual & Cultural Awakening (1933–1944):**
   - In 1933, the **Meena Kshatriya Sabha** was established to spearhead legal petitions.
   - **Muni Magan Sagar's Renaissance:** Researched tribal genealogies, authored **Meena Puran**, and traced descent to Vishnu’s Matsya Avatar.
   - Consecrated the historic **Neem Ka Thana Convention (April 1944)** with 50,000 attendees, establishing the **All-India Meena Mahasabha** under Pt. Bansidhar Sharma.

3. **Mass Civil Disobedience to Liberation (1946–1952):**
   - **Baghawas Summit (28 October 1946):** 26,000 Chaukidari Meenas simultaneously resigned en masse (*Mukti Diwas*), shattering the state's policing infrastructure and securing exemption for women and children.
   - **Final Legislative Triumph (1952):** Following national independence, democratic mobilizations culminated in the complete and irrevocable repeal of the Jairam Pesha Act in 1952 under Chief Minister Tikaram Paliwal, restoring full constitutional dignity.
