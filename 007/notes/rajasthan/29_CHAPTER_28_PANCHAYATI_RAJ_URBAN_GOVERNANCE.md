# CHAPTER 28: PANCHAYATI RAJ & URBAN LOCAL GOVERNANCE IN RAJASTHAN
## राजस्थान में पंचायती राज एवं नगरीय स्थानीय शासन

> **Study scope:** RPSC RAS / Rajasthan state examinations — Rajasthan polity, governance, local self-government, rural development and urban administration.  
> **Legal-status cut-off:** 17 October 2026. Always check the latest Gazette and election notification before memorising a changeable rule.

### How to use this chapter

Keep three layers separate throughout:
1. **Constitutional baseline** — Part IX and Part IX-A establish the minimum framework.
2. **Rajasthan legislation and rules** — the Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994; Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Rules, 1996; the Rajasthan Municipalities Act, 2009; and PESA-related state law modify and operationalise that framework.
3. **Current administrative orders** — meeting calendars, reservation rosters, election schedules, delimitation and local-body counts can change. Date these facts rather than learning them as permanent constitutional rules.

## 1. Historical evolution of local self-government

### 1.1 From princely-state village bodies to a statewide framework

Modern statutory local-government institutions in the former princely states developed at different times. The chapter’s source cites Bikaner (1928), Jaipur (1938), Mewar (1940) and Marwar (1945) as early examples. These historical dates are often repeated in Rajasthan polity material, but exact Act titles and “first” claims should be verified against the relevant princely-state gazettes before being treated as definitive.

After integration, two different state laws must not be conflated:

- **Rajasthan Panchayat Act, 1953:** provided the legal basis for village panchayats.
- **Rajasthan Panchayat Samitis and Zila Parishads Act, 1959:** provided the block- and district-level institutions needed for a three-tier arrangement.

The three-tier system began functioning on **2 October 1959** at **Nagaur**, inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, with village panchayats, Panchayat Samitis and Zila Parishads. Rajasthan is conventionally identified as the first state in independent India to launch Panchayati Raj; Andhra Pradesh followed later in October 1959. The 1994 Act later replaced the earlier two principal state Acts.

### 1.2 Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957)

The national committee examined the Community Development Programme and National Extension Service. It recommended **democratic decentralisation** through three tiers:

| Tier | Institution | Core role in the model |
|---|---|---|
| Village | Gram Panchayat | Local civic works and delivery; accountable to the Gram Sabha and electors |
| Intermediate / block | Panchayat Samiti | Planning and coordination across villages; historically a key executive tier |
| District | Zila Parishad | District-level coordination, planning and supervision |

Do not say that the Constitution itself assigns every one of the 29 Eleventh Schedule subjects automatically to panchayats. Article 243G enables state legislatures to endow panchayats with powers and responsibilities, and actual devolution depends on state law and orders.

### 1.3 Rajasthan-level committees

Rajasthan polity textbooks commonly list committees associated with Harish Chandra Mathur, Sadiq Ali, Girdhari Lal Vyas, Harlal Singh Kharra and a later Gulab Chand Kataria–led exercise on the “3 Fs”. Dates and recommendation summaries vary between secondary sources. Treat individual recommendations as exam facts only where the original report, government order or reliable official historical publication is available. The stable policy idea is the same: strengthen elected institutions through **functions, funds and functionaries**, clearer accountability and regular meetings/audits.

## 2. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment and the Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act

### 2.1 Constitutional architecture

The **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** inserted **Part IX (Articles 243–243O)** and the **Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)**. It came into force on **24 April 1993**, now observed as National Panchayati Raj Day.

| Article | High-yield provision | Exam clarification |
|---|---|---|
| 243A | Gram Sabha | State law defines its powers and functions |
| 243B | Constitution of Panchayats | Three tiers are the general rule; a state with population not exceeding 20 lakh may omit the intermediate tier |
| 243C | Composition | Direct election of seats; state law governs composition and the village-level chairperson, while intermediate- and district-level chairpersons are elected from among elected members |
| 243D | Reservation | SC/ST reservation in proportion to population; at least one-third of seats and chairperson offices reserved for women under the constitutional minimum; Rajasthan provides 50% reservation under state law |
| 243E | Duration | Five-year term; election before expiry; after dissolution, election within six months unless the remaining term is less than six months |
| 243F | Disqualification | Includes statutory disqualifications and a minimum age of 21 years; **this is the Panchayat provision** |
| 243G | Powers and responsibilities | State legislature may devolve functions, plans and scheme implementation powers |
| 243H | Taxes and funds | State law may authorise taxes, duties, tolls, fees and grants |
| 243-I | State Finance Commission | Constitution every five years to review panchayat finances |
| 243J | Audit | State law provides for maintenance and audit of panchayat accounts |
| 243K | State Election Commission | Local-body elections are under the State Election Commission, not the Election Commission of India |
| 243O | Bar to court interference | Delimitation/allotment matters are protected as provided by the Article; election disputes are raised through election petitions under state law |

**Frequent article-number trap:** Article **243V** concerns disqualification in municipalities under Part IX-A, not Panchayats. For Panchayats, remember Article **243F**.

### 2.2 Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994

The Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994 (Act 13 of 1994) consolidated and replaced the Rajasthan Panchayat Act, 1953 and the Rajasthan Panchayat Samitis and Zila Parishads Act, 1959. The Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Rules, 1996 provide important operating details, including meeting frequency and notice procedure.

#### Important legal update: candidate disqualification

- The **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act No. 6 of 2026)** received the Governor’s assent on **24 March 2026** and removed the statutory disqualification for a person having more than two children by deleting the relevant clause and connected text in Section 19.
- The corresponding **Rajasthan Municipalities (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act No. 7 of 2026)** removed the two-child disqualification from the Municipalities Act. The amendment also removed leprosy-related wording identified in the same amendment process.
- Therefore, **the old two-child rule is not current law for Rajasthan Panchayat or municipal elections as of this chapter’s cut-off**. Do not reproduce older notes that still treat it as a live disqualification.
- Rajasthan’s 2015 candidate-education rules were later removed; they should not be described as current educational eligibility conditions.
- A separate statutory condition related to a functional sanitary toilet and open defecation appears in the Panchayati Raj Act. Do not conflate it with the removed education or two-child provisions; for live election advice, consult the latest consolidated Act and election notification.

## 3. The three-tier Panchayati Raj system in Rajasthan

### 3.1 Institutional structure

| Level | Elected political head | Other elected members | Main administrative support |
|---|---|---|---|
| Village | Sarpanch | Ward Panchs | Gram Vikas Adhikari / Village Development Officer and other assigned staff |
| Intermediate / block | Pradhan | Panchayat Samiti members | Vikas Adhikari / Block Development Officer and block staff |
| District | Zila Pramukh | Zila Parishad members | Chief Executive Officer and district-level staff |

The names, cadres and reporting relationships of administrative officers can change through state service rules. Avoid absolute claims that the administrative head of every Zila Parishad must be an IAS officer or that every BDO belongs to one specific cadre.

### 3.2 Election method: rural institutions

- **Sarpanch and Ward Panchs:** direct election by eligible voters of the relevant Panchayat circle/ward under Rajasthan law.
- **Up-Sarpanch:** elected indirectly by elected Ward Panchs under the applicable procedure.
- **Pradhan and Up-Pradhan:** elected from among the relevant elected Panchayat Samiti members under state law.
- **Zila Pramukh and Up-Zila Pramukh:** elected from among the relevant elected Zila Parishad members under state law.

At the constitutional level, Article 243C makes direct election of seats the default. Do not over-generalise this into a rule that every chairperson at every tier must be directly elected; the Constitution and the Rajasthan Act distinguish the levels and offices.

### 3.3 Meeting frequency and quorum

The **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Rules, 1996, Rule 39** provides the key exam comparison:

| Institution | Minimum ordinary meeting frequency |
|---|---|
| Gram Panchayat | At least once a fortnight |
| Panchayat Samiti | At least once a month |
| Zila Parishad | At least once a quarter; the Act also limits the gap between meetings |

The source’s wording “twice every month” is only an approximation for a Gram Panchayat; **once a fortnight** is the rule wording and should be memorised.

For ordinary meetings of Panchayat institutions, the general quorum is **one-third of total membership**, subject to the governing Act/rules and any applicable special provision.

### 3.4 Gram Sabha

Under Section 8A of the Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, a Gram Sabha comprises persons registered in the electoral roll for the village or group of villages within the Panchayat circle. The Act specifies **at least two meetings per year**, one in the first quarter and one in the last quarter of the financial year. The law also provides for a meeting on requisition or direction in the circumstances it specifies.

Rajasthan’s commonly taught calendar identifies four major Gram Sabha dates:

- **26 January** — Republic Day
- **1 May** — Labour Day
- **15 August** — Independence Day
- **2 October** — Gandhi Jayanti

These four dates are a familiar state-level meeting schedule, but distinguish that schedule from the express minimum written in Section 8A: **at least two statutory meetings annually**. Additional meetings may be convened under the Act, rules or government directions.

**Quorum:** one-tenth of the total members, with the statutory requirement concerning the representation/presence of SC, ST, Backward Class and women members as set out in the Act.

**Functions worth remembering:** review of accounts and audit, discussion of development programmes and budgets, beneficiary identification where the relevant scheme/law assigns it that role, and social accountability. Calling the Gram Sabha “sovereign” is rhetorical; its powers are defined by the Constitution and statute.

### 3.5 Ward Sabha

Rajasthan’s Ward Sabha provisions allow voters within a ward to raise and prioritise local needs and make recommendations for the Gram Panchayat process. A Ward Sabha is distinct from a Gram Sabha and from an urban **Wards Committee** under Article 243S. Do not merge their membership, legal basis or thresholds.

### 3.6 Rural reservation and eligibility — updated position

- The constitutional minimum reservation for women is one-third; Rajasthan provides **50% reservation** across Panchayati Raj institutions under state legislation.
- SC and ST seats are reserved in relation to their population; state law also provides for Backward Class reservation subject to statutory conditions and judicial requirements.
- Minimum age is generally **21 years** for contesting Panchayat elections under the constitutional/statutory scheme.
- **Two-child disqualification:** removed in Rajasthan by the 2026 amendment. Any historical questions should be explicitly framed as asking about the *former* rule.
- **Educational qualifications:** the 2015 experiment was removed in 2019 and should not be shown as a current universal eligibility condition.
- **Sanitary toilet condition:** check the current text of Section 19 and the election notification; it is a distinct provision from the repealed two-child clause.

For an examination, separate a constitutional minimum (such as five-year tenure) from a state-law choice (such as 50% women’s reservation) and from a provision that has since been repealed (the two-child disqualification).

## 4. PESA and Scheduled Areas in Rajasthan

### 4.1 Constitutional basis

The **Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA)** extends the Panchayati Raj framework to Fifth Schedule Scheduled Areas with specified modifications. It was enacted to recognise Gram Sabha authority, protect customary practices and strengthen control over local resources. Rajasthan’s implementation includes the **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj (Modification of Provisions in their Application to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1999** and the **2011 Rules**.

### 4.2 Geographic precision: Scheduled Areas are not whole districts by default

Rajasthan’s Scheduled Area spans **eight districts in whole or in part**. Three are fully covered—**Banswara, Dungarpur and Pratapgarh**—while specified tehsils, blocks, Gram Panchayats and villages in **Udaipur, Rajsamand, Chittorgarh, Pali and Sirohi** are also included. The President’s 2018 notification and the exact village/area list determine legal coverage.

Do not write “all of eight districts are PESA areas.” District names are a convenient index; statutory Scheduled Area boundaries determine applicability.

### 4.3 High-yield PESA powers

| Field | What the law provides (in broad terms) |
|---|---|
| Custom and tradition | Gram Sabha/Panchayat institutions are to safeguard traditions, cultural identity and customary community resources, subject to the Act |
| Development | Gram Sabha approval of plans/programmes/projects for social and economic development before implementation and identification of beneficiaries, as specified by PESA |
| Natural resources | Powers concerning minor forest produce and management of local resources under PESA/state law |
| Land acquisition and rehabilitation | Consultation with Gram Sabha/Panchayat at the appropriate level in the cases provided by law |
| Minor minerals | Prior recommendation of Gram Sabha or Panchayat at the appropriate level for prospecting licences or mining leases for minor minerals in Scheduled Areas, where the Act requires it |
| Chairperson reservation | Reservation for Scheduled Tribe chairpersons at Panchayat levels in Scheduled Areas under PESA’s rule; apply the rule to legally notified Scheduled Areas, not all villages in a named district |
| Representation | ST reservation in Panchayat seats in Scheduled Areas is subject to PESA’s special minimum and state implementation law |

PESA should not be summarised as giving a Gram Sabha unlimited or “sovereign” power over all land, minerals or government departments. Use the particular statutory power and level of authority prescribed for the matter.

## 5. Urban local governance in Rajasthan

### 5.1 The 74th Constitutional Amendment

The **74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** inserted **Part IX-A (Articles 243P–243ZG)** and the **Twelfth Schedule (18 functional subjects)**. It constitutionalised municipalities but, like Part IX, leaves important details to state law.

| Article | Subject | Exam use |
|---|---|---|
| 243Q | Constitution of Municipalities | Transitional area, smaller urban area, larger urban area; industrial-township exception |
| 243R | Composition | State law governs details within constitutional boundaries |
| 243S | Wards Committees | Required in municipalities with population of 3 lakh or more |
| 243T | Reservation | SC/ST representation, and at least one-third for women constitutionally; Rajasthan state law provides 50% reservation for women |
| 243U | Duration | Five-year term; election timing and dissolution rules parallel the constitutional framework |
| 243V | Disqualification | Municipal candidate/member disqualification provision — not Article 243F |
| 243W | Powers and responsibilities | State law may assign functions from the Twelfth Schedule |
| 243X | Taxes and funds | State law may authorise municipal taxes/fees and grants |
| 243Y | Finance Commission | SFC reviews municipal as well as Panchayat finances |
| 243ZA | Elections | SEC conducts municipal elections |
| 243ZD | District Planning Committee | Integrates Panchayat and municipal plans at district level |
| 243ZE | Metropolitan Planning Committee | Draft plan for qualifying metropolitan area |
| 243ZG | Bar to court interference | Election disputes raised through the prescribed election-petition route, subject to constitutional text |

### 5.2 Rajasthan Municipalities Act, 2009

The **Rajasthan Municipalities Act, 2009 (Act 18 of 2009)** governs Municipal Boards, Municipal Councils and Municipal Corporations. It came into force in September 2009 and replaced the earlier main municipal statute. Use the current consolidated text along with later amendment Acts and rules, not only the original 2009 print.

### 5.3 Three types of municipality

| Type | Constitutional category | Political chairperson | Administrative officer (usual designation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Municipal Board / Nagar Palika | Transitional area | Chairman | Executive Officer |
| Municipal Council / Nagar Parishad | Smaller urban area | President | Commissioner / Chief Municipal Officer, as applicable |
| Municipal Corporation / Nagar Nigam | Larger urban area | Mayor | Commissioner |

**Threshold trap:** Population ranges such as 20,000–1 lakh, 1–5 lakh and above 5 lakh are commonly used in textbooks as practical descriptions, but Article 243Q’s constitutional test also considers factors such as population, density, local revenue, non-agricultural employment and economic importance. Do not present a simple population threshold as the complete constitutional test unless the question cites a specific current state notification/rule.

### 5.4 Election, reservation and committees

- Ward members/councillors are directly elected from municipal wards.
- The mode of election of Mayor, President or Chairman is controlled by Rajasthan’s current law and rules. The current Rajasthan arrangement is indirect election by elected members; because this area has changed by amendment in recent years, verify the latest rules/notification if answering a current election question.
- Rajasthan provides **50% reservation for women** in municipal seats under state law, beyond the one-third constitutional minimum.
- **Wards Committees:** Section 54 of the Rajasthan Municipalities Act provides for Wards Committees in municipalities with population of **three lakh or more**. The Act permits a committee to cover one or more wards.
- Reservation rosters for chairperson posts and wards are notified by the state and rotate; do not memorise a roster without its notification date.

### 5.5 District Planning Committee (DPC) and Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC)

**DPC — Article 243ZD:** The DPC consolidates plans prepared by Panchayats and municipalities in the district and prepares a draft development plan for the district as a whole. At least four-fifths of its members are to be elected by and from elected members of the district-level Panchayat and municipalities, in proportion to rural and urban population, as provided by the Constitution and state law. Rajasthan’s Municipalities Act recognises the DPC constituted under Section 121 of the Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act.

The exact office of chairperson, member-secretary and other ex-officio members must be taken from the applicable current state provision/order. Avoid asserting an officeholder arrangement without the supporting current rule or notification.

**MPC — Article 243ZE:** The Constitution requires an MPC for a **metropolitan area** as defined in Article 243P; the population test is 10 lakh or more, along with the constitutional definition involving one or more districts and two or more municipalities/panchayats. Rajasthan’s Municipalities Act, Section 157, provides for such a committee. The existence of a statutory requirement is not proof that a particular named committee is operational: do not claim that a “Jaipur Metropolitan Planning Committee” has been constituted unless the relevant notification is identified.

## 6. Finance and the “3 Fs” challenge

Local governance effectiveness depends on more than elections. The familiar **3 Fs** frame describes the main capacity constraints:

1. **Functions:** Have responsibilities actually been assigned to local bodies? The Eleventh Schedule lists 29 subjects and the Twelfth Schedule lists 18, but listing is not automatic devolution.
2. **Funds:** Can institutions raise own-source revenue, receive predictable untied transfers and control expenditure against their responsibilities?
3. **Functionaries:** Are technical and administrative staff available to deliver the assigned functions, and are reporting lines clear between elected heads and state departments?

### 6.1 State Finance Commission (SFC)

Articles 243-I and 243-Y require a State Finance Commission every five years to review Panchayat and municipal finances and recommend principles for distribution of state revenues, grants-in-aid and revenue-raising measures. An SFC recommendation must not be presented automatically as the government’s accepted transfer rate for every year.

Rajasthan’s Sixth SFC recommendation, government acceptance and year-wise transfers need to be distinguished from one another. Avoid the unsupported evergreen statement that local bodies currently receive a fixed “6.75%” share in every year. State Finance Department orders and later SFC reports take precedence for a current fiscal-year question.

### 6.2 Common constraints and reforms

| Constraint | What it looks like | Reform direction |
|---|---|---|
| Weak own-source revenue | Low collection of property/house tax, user charges or fees | Update assessments, simplify collection, improve transparency and link grants to performance without excluding poorer areas |
| Incomplete devolution | Line departments retain budgets and decisions on nominally local subjects | Publish clear activity maps and devolution orders by tier |
| Staff dependence | Local officials rely on state-controlled technical staff and transfers | Clarify professional reporting, staffing standards and accountability |
| Weak audit/social accountability | Delayed accounts, limited follow-up on Gram Sabha findings | Timely audits, public disclosure, social audit and action-taken reporting |
| Fragmented urban planning | Municipal master plans diverge from wider transport, water and land-use systems | Functional DPC/MPC institutions and integrated district/metropolitan planning |

## 8. Common exam traps — updated

1. **1953 versus 1959:** The 1953 Act provided village panchayats; the 1959 law established the block- and district-level institutions that completed the three-tier system.
2. **Nagaur date:** 2 October 1959 is the standard date for the launch of Panchayati Raj in Rajasthan.
3. **Constitutional amendments:** 73rd = Part IX + Eleventh Schedule; 74th = Part IX-A + Twelfth Schedule.
4. **Article 243F versus 243V:** Panchayat disqualification is 243F; municipality disqualification is 243V.
5. **Article 243B:** A state with population not exceeding 20 lakh may omit the intermediate tier under the constitutional exception.
6. **Sarpanch and other chairpersons:** Do not assume the same election method at all three levels. In Rajasthan, Sarpanch is directly elected; intermediate/district chairpersons are elected indirectly under state law.
7. **Tenure:** Panchayat/municipality term is five years, subject to constitutional provisions for dissolution and elections.
8. **Gram Sabha meetings:** Section 8A states at least two meetings yearly; the four widely taught dates describe Rajasthan’s scheduled meeting calendar. Check the current government directions for an exact date-based question.
9. **Gram Panchayat meeting frequency:** Rules say at least once a **fortnight**, not merely “twice a month”.
10. **PESA scope:** Apply PESA to legally notified Scheduled Areas, not automatically every village in all eight named districts.
11. **PESA rules:** Rajasthan’s Scheduled Areas modification Act dates from 1999; rules were notified in 2011.
12. **Two-child disqualification:** Repealed for Panchayat elections by Rajasthan Act 6 of 2026, effective on assent/commencement in March 2026; do not teach as current.
13. **Urban two-child disqualification:** Removed by Rajasthan Municipalities (Amendment) Act 7 of 2026; likewise historical, not current.
14. **Educational qualifications:** The 2015 qualifications were later removed; do not present them as current criteria.
15. **Toilet provision:** Distinct from the former two-child rule; consult the latest consolidated text and election notification.
16. **Urban categories:** Municipal Boards, Councils and Corporations correspond to transitional, smaller and larger urban areas; simple population bands alone do not define the constitutional test.
17. **Wards Committees:** Required in municipalities with population of three lakh or more under Article 243S and Rajasthan law.
18. **DPC:** Consolidates rural and urban local plans for the district; not the same as an urban development authority.
19. **MPC:** Required for a qualifying metropolitan area; a statutory requirement does not itself prove a named MPC is operational.
20. **3 Fs:** A subject listed in a Schedule has not necessarily been fully devolved in functions, funds and functionaries.
21. **Women’s reservation:** Rajasthan’s 50% rule is a state-law provision; the constitutional minimum is one-third.
22. **State Finance Commission:** Recommended devolution is not automatically identical to the amount accepted and paid in a given year.
23. **Local elections:** ECI conducts Parliamentary/Assembly elections; the State Election Commission conducts Panchayat and municipal elections.
24. **Current rosters:** Ward reservations, chairperson rosters, district counts and election schedules are notification-sensitive facts.
25. **Terms such as “sovereign Gram Sabha”:** Use as rhetorical language only; legal authority is bounded by the Constitution, PESA and state law.

## 9. Practice questions and answer frameworks

### 2-marker questions

**Q1. When and where did the three-tier Panchayati Raj system begin functioning in Rajasthan?**  
**Model answer:** It began functioning on 2 October 1959 at Nagaur, after the Rajasthan Panchayat Samitis and Zila Parishads Act, 1959 established the intermediate and district levels alongside village panchayats.

**Q2. Distinguish Articles 243F and 243V.**  
**Model answer:** Article 243F concerns disqualification for Panchayat membership; Article 243V deals with disqualification for membership of a Municipality under Part IX-A.

**Q3. What is the current position of the two-child disqualification for local elections in Rajasthan?**  
**Model answer:** The disqualification was removed by Rajasthan’s 2026 amendments to the Panchayati Raj Act and Municipalities Act; it is no longer a current general disqualification under those provisions.

**Q4. How often must a Gram Panchayat ordinarily meet?**  
**Model answer:** At least once a fortnight under Rule 39 of the Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Rules, 1996.

### 5-marker questions

**Q5. Explain the constitutional position and limitations of Gram Sabha.**  
**Framework:** Define membership by electoral roll; cite Article 243A and Section 8A; distinguish statutory minimum meetings from the state meeting calendar; explain budget/account review, programme discussion and beneficiary/social-accountability functions; conclude that powers are law-defined rather than unlimited.

**Q6. Outline the purpose of PESA and its special relevance to Rajasthan.**  
**Framework:** Fifth Schedule/Article 244(1); PESA Act 1996; Rajasthan modification Act 1999 and 2011 Rules; Scheduled Area coverage in eight districts whole/part; Gram Sabha roles in local planning, customary matters, resources, land acquisition consultation and minor-mineral leases; avoid equating the entire named districts with Scheduled Areas.

**Q7. Differentiate DPC and MPC.**  
**Framework:** DPC under Article 243ZD consolidates Panchayat and municipal plans for a district; MPC under Article 243ZE drafts a plan for a qualifying metropolitan area. Both coordinate rural/urban interests but operate at different geographic scales. Mention the constitutional composition requirements and verify the current state-specific chairperson/operational notification before naming officeholders.

### 10-marker answer framework

**Q8. “Political representation has expanded faster than local fiscal and administrative autonomy.” Critically examine Rajasthan’s local governance through the 3 Fs.**

Suggested structure:
1. **Introduction:** Constitutional status under the 73rd/74th Amendments and Rajasthan’s early Panchayati Raj experiment.
2. **Functions:** Explain the difference between a Schedule listing subjects and actual administrative assignment.
3. **Funds:** Discuss own-source revenue, predictable transfers, SFC recommendations versus government acceptance, and timely audits.
4. **Functionaries:** Explain staff shortages, technical capacity and dual control between elected institutions and state departments.
5. **Accountability:** Gram Sabha/Ward Sabha, social audit, disclosure and citizen participation.
6. **Reforms:** Activity mapping, untied but accountable grants, stronger local revenue administration, capacity building, open budgets and effective DPC/MPC planning.
7. **Conclusion:** Measure decentralisation not merely by elections or seat reservation but by whether local institutions possess clear responsibilities, resources, personnel and accountability.
