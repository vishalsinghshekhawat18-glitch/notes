## 1. Historical Evolution of Local Self-Government in Rajasthan

### 1.1 Pre-Independence Traditions and Princely Enactments
Local self-governing village communities have deep civilizational roots in Rajasthan, functioning through informal village caste and elder councils (*Panch Parameshwar*). In the modern institutional sense:
- **Bikaner State**: Pioneered formal statutory village governance by enacting the **Bikaner State Gram Panchayat Act, 1928**, becoming the first princely state in Rajputana to establish statutory village panchayats.
- **Jaipur State**: Enacted the **Jaipur Village Panchayat Act, 1938** (later amended in 1944).
- **Mewar State**: Promulgated the **Mewar Gram Panchayat Act, 1940**.
- **Marwar State**: Enacted the **Marwar Gram Panchayat Act, 1945**.

### 1.2 Post-Independence Consolidation: Rajasthan Panchayat Act, 1953
Following the integration of Rajasthan, the unified State Legislature enacted the **Rajasthan Panchayat Act, 1953**:
- Established a **two-tier structure**: **Village Panchayats** at the village level and **Tehsil Panchayats** at the sub-district level.
- Tehsil Panchayats functioned primarily as appellate revenue and administrative oversight bodies over village panchayats.

### 1.3 The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) & The Historic Launch at Nagaur
In 1957, the Planning Commission of India appointed the **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** to study the Community Development Programme (CDP, 1952) and National Extension Service (NES, 1953). The Committee recommended the establishment of the scheme of **Democratic Decentralisation (लोकतांत्रिक विकेंद्रीकरण)** through a **three-tier Panchayati Raj system**:
1. Gram Panchayat (Village level)
2. Panchayat Samiti (Block level - core executive unit)
3. Zila Parishad (District level - advisory and supervisory unit)

## 3. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment & Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994

### 3.1 Constitutional Architecture (Part IX & Eleventh Schedule)
- The **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** inserted:
  - **Part IX** (Articles 243 to 243-O).
  - **Eleventh Schedule**: Enlisting **29 functional subjects** (agriculture, minor irrigation, animal husbandry, rural housing, drinking water, roads, poverty alleviation, primary education, sanitation, etc.).
- **Mandatory (Compulsory) Constitutional Provisions**:
  - Organization of Gram Sabha (Art. 243A).
  - Three-tier system of Panchayats in all states with population > 20 Lakh (Art. 243B).
  - Direct elections to all seats in Panchayats at all three levels (Art. 243C(2)).
  - Indirect elections to the office of Chairperson at intermediate and district levels (Art. 243C(5)).
  - Minimum age of **21 years** for contesting elections (Art. 243V).
  - Reservation for SCs and STs proportionate to population, and minimum 1/3rd (33.3%) reservation for women (Art. 243D).
  - Fixed **5-year tenure**, with mandatory fresh elections within **6 months** of dissolution (Art. 243E).
  - Establishment of an independent **State Election Commission** (Art. 243K).
  - Constitution of a **State Finance Commission** every 5 years (Art. 243-I).

### 3.2 Enactment of Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994
To harmonize the state legal framework with Part IX:
- The Rajasthan Legislative Assembly passed the **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994** (Act No. 13 of 1994).
- Assented to by the Governor on **23 April 1994** (came into force on the same day).
- Accompanied by the **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Rules, 1996** governing election administration, budget audits, and service conditions.

### 4.1 Tier 1: Gram Panchayat (Grassroots Village Tier)
1. **Composition**:
   - **Sarpanch**: The executive head, **directly elected** by all registered voters of the Panchayat circle.
   - **Ward Panchs**: Directly elected from territorial wards within the Panchayat circle.
   - **Up-Sarpanch**: **Indirectly elected** by the elected Ward Panchs from amongst themselves at the first meeting of the Panchayat.
2. **Permanent Administrative Cadre**:
   - **Village Development Officer (VDO / ग्राम विकास अधिकारी)** (formerly designated *Gram Sevak*): Appointed by the Rural Development & Panchayati Raj Department; acts as the administrative secretary, custodian of records, and financial disbursing officer under joint signature with the Sarpanch.
   - **Gram Rozgar Sahayak**: Field assistant for MGNREGS implementation.
3. **Meetings & Quorum**:
   - Must meet at least **twice every month** at the Panchayat headquarters.
   - Quorum: **One-third (1/3rd)** of the total members.
4. **Resignation Protocol**:
   - Sarpanch, Up-Sarpanch, and Ward Panch submit their resignation in writing to the **Block Development Officer (BDO)**.

### 4.2 The Gram Sabha & Ward Sabha
- **Gram Sabha (Article 243A & Section 8-A of Rajasthan Act)**:
  - The sovereign deliberative body of direct grassroots democracy, comprising **all persons registered in the electoral rolls** of a revenue village within the Panchayat circle.
  - **Statutory Mandatory Meetings in Rajasthan**: Must convene at least **four times a year** on specific commemorative dates:
    1. **26 January** (Republic Day)
    2. **1 May** (International Labour Day)
    3. **15 August** (Independence Day)
    4. **2 October** (Gandhi Jayanti)
  - Quorum for Gram Sabha in Rajasthan: **One-tenth (1/10th)** of total registered voters.
  - Powers: Approval of annual budget, beneficiary identification (MGNREGS, PMAY), social audit of expenditures, vigilance committee appointments.
- **Ward Sabha (Section 8-B)**:
  - Introduced in Rajasthan via the **Panchayati Raj (Amendment) Act, 2000**.
  - Comprises all voters residing within an individual ward; deliberates on ultra-local drinking water, street-lighting, and drainage works prior to Gram Sabha meetings.

### 4.3 Tier 2: Panchayat Samiti (Intermediate / Block Tier)
1. **Composition**:
   - Directly elected members from territorial constituencies (wards).
   - Ex-officio members: All **Members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs)** representing constituencies that fall wholly or partly within the Panchayat Samiti area.
2. **Political Leadership**:
   - **Pradhan & Up-Pradhan**: **Indirectly elected** by the directly elected members of the Panchayat Samiti from amongst themselves.
3. **Administrative Head**:
   - **Block Development Officer (BDO / विकास अधिकारी)**: An officer of the Rajasthan Administrative Service (RAS) or Rajasthan Rural Development State Service (RRDSS).
4. **Meetings & Quorum**:
   - Must convene at least **once a month**.
   - Quorum: **One-third (1/3rd)** of total membership.
5. **Resignations**:
   - Directly elected Members and Up-Pradhan tender resignation to the **Pradhan**.
   - The Pradhan tenders resignation in writing to the **Zila Pramukh**.

### 4.4 Tier 3: Zila Parishad (Apex District Tier)
1. **Composition**:
   - Directly elected members from territorial district wards.
   - Ex-officio members:
     - All **Members of the Lok Sabha** and **MLAs** representing constituencies falling within the district.
     - Members of the **Rajya Sabha** registered as electors within the district.
     - All Pradhans of Panchayat Samitis within the district (permanent invitees).
2. **Political Leadership**:
   - **Zila Pramukh & Up-Zila Pramukh**: **Indirectly elected** by the directly elected members of the Zila Parishad from amongst themselves.
3. **Administrative Head**:
   - **Chief Executive Officer (CEO)**: An officer of the Indian Administrative Service (IAS - Senior Scale) or Super-Time Scale Rajasthan Administrative Service (RAS).
   - Assisted by an **Additional Chief Executive Officer (ACEO)**.
4. **Meetings & Quorum**:
   - Must convene at least **once every three months**.
   - Quorum: **One-third (1/3rd)** of total membership.
5. **Resignations**:
   - Members and Up-Zila Pramukh tender resignation to the **Zila Pramukh**.
   - The Zila Pramukh tenders resignation in writing to the **Divisional Commissioner**.

### 5.1 The 50% Women's Reservation Revolution
- Article 243D(3) mandates at least 33.3% (one-third) reservation for women.
- **The 2008 Progressive Amendment**: By the **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj (Amendment) Act, 2008**, the State Government of Rajasthan raised the reservation for women to **50% across all three tiers** of Panchayati Raj Institutions (covering Ward Panchs, Sarpanchs, Panchayat Samiti members, Pradhans, Zila Parishad members, and Zila Pramukhs).
- Implemented on the ground starting from the **January 2010 PRI General Elections**.

### 5.2 The Two-Child Disqualification Norm (Section 19(l))
- Under Section 19(l) of the 1994 Act, a person is disqualified from contesting or holding office in a Panchayat if they have **more than two children**.
- *Cut-off Date*: Applies if an additional child was born after **27 November 1995**.
- *Exceptions*: Multiple births in a single subsequent delivery resulting in more than two children does not trigger disqualification; children born with disabilities were later granted statutory relaxation by amendment.

### 5.3 The Educational Qualification Experiment & Its Repeal (2015–2019)
- **The 2015 Ordinance / Amendment Act (Vasundhara Raje Govt)**:
  - First state in India to mandate minimum educational qualifications for PRI contestants:
    - *Sarpanch (General Areas)*: Passed **Class 8**.
    - *Sarpanch (TSP Scheduled Areas)*: Passed **Class 5**.
    - *Zila Parishad / Panchayat Samiti Members*: Passed **Class 10**.
    - *Sanitary Condition*: Mandatory functional sanitary toilet in the contestant's residence and no family member practicing open defecation.
  - The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of similar norms in *Rajbala v. State of Haryana (2015)*.
- **The 2019 Repeal Act (Ashok Gehlot Govt)**:
  - In **February 2019**, the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly passed the **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj (Amendment) Act, 2019**, which **completely repealed and abolished all educational qualification criteria**.
  - *Rationale*: Stigmatized and disenfranchised millions of elderly, impoverished, and female citizens who were systematically denied educational access in rural Rajasthan.
  - *Current Law*: **No educational qualification is required** to contest PRI elections in Rajasthan.
  - *Retention*: The requirement of having a **functional sanitary toilet** at residence remains in force.

## 6. PESA Act, 1996 in Rajasthan (Fifth Schedule TSP Areas)

### 6.1 Genesis and Constitutional Purpose
- The 73rd Amendment did not automatically apply to the **Scheduled Areas** specified in Article 244(1) of the Constitution (Fifth Schedule).
- Parliament enacted the **Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA)** on the recommendations of the **Dilib Singh Bhuria Committee (1995)**.
- Purpose: Restore tribal self-rule (*Gram Swaraj*), safeguard tribal customary traditions, and prevent alienation of tribal land and water bodies.

### 6.2 Application and PESA Rules in Rajasthan
- **Coverage in Rajasthan**: Covers the Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP) regions comprising **8 districts**:
  - Wholly covered: **Banswara, Dungarpur, Pratapgarh**.
  - Partially covered: **Udaipur, Sirohi (Abu Road), Pali (Bali), Rajsamand, Chittorgarh**.
- **Rajasthan PESA Rules**: Formally notified and enforced by the State Government in **2011**.

## 7. Urban Local Governance (Nagarpalika & 74th Amendment)

### 7.1 Constitutional Framework (Part IX-A & Twelfth Schedule)
- The **74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** inserted:
  - **Part IX-A** (Articles 243P to 243ZG).
  - **Twelfth Schedule**: Enlisting **18 functional subjects** (urban planning, regulation of land use, roads & bridges, water supply, public health, fire services, solid waste management, slum improvement, urban poverty alleviation, etc.).

### 7.2 The Rajasthan Municipalities Act, 2009
- Replaced the outdated *Rajasthan Municipalities Act, 1959*.
- Passed by the State Legislature as **Act No. 18 of 2009** (received Governor's assent on **11 September 2009**).
- Governs the constitution, election, powers, taxation, and administrative functioning of all urban local bodies in the state.

### 7.4 Key Structural Mechanisms under Urban Governance
1. **Election Method in Rajasthan**:
   - Ward Councillors (*Ward Parshad*) are **directly elected** by voters residing in municipal wards.
   - The Mayor, Sabhapati, and Adhyaksh are **indirectly elected** by the elected Councillors from amongst themselves. *(Note: The state witnessed legislative experiments oscillating between direct and indirect election of Mayors; under current law, election is indirect)*.
2. **50% Reservation for Women**: In alignment with PRIs, Rajasthan provides **50% reservation for women** in urban local bodies.
3. **Wards Committees (Article 243S & Section 54 of 2009 Act)**:
   - Mandatory constitution of Wards Committees in all municipalities with a **population of three lakh (3,00,000) or more**.
4. **District Planning Committee (DPC - Article 243ZD & Section 121 of 1994 Act)**:
   - Mandated to consolidate the developmental plans prepared by the Panchayats and Municipalities in the district into a unified **Draft Development Plan** for the district as a whole.
   - Composition: **Four-fifths (4/5th or 80%)** of the members are elected by, and from amongst, the elected members of the Zila Parishad and Municipalities in proportion to the rural and urban population of the district.
   - Ex-officio Chairman: In Rajasthan, the **Zila Pramukh** functions as the ex-officio Chairperson of the DPC; the **District Collector** functions as the Member Secretary; and the **CEO of Zila Parishad** acts as Additional Member Secretary.
5. **Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC - Article 243ZE)**:
   - Mandated for metropolitan areas having a population of **10 Lakh or more**. In Rajasthan, constituted for the **Jaipur Metropolitan Area**.

## 8. Financial and Functional Devolution: The 3 Fs Challenge

Despite constitutional guarantees, local governance in Rajasthan confronts structural impediments:
1. **Funds (Financial Autonomy)**:
   - Heavy dependency on State Finance Commission (6.75% devolution) and Central Finance Commission grants.
   - Low internal revenue mobilization: Rural bodies collect negligible property/house taxes; octroi was abolished, and local bodies rely on statutory state compensations.
2. **Functions (Operational Devolution)**:
   - Under the Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects) and Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects), theoretical devolution has occurred, but line departments (Education, Public Health Engineering Department [PHED], Public Works Department [PWD], Irrigation) retain de facto administrative control and budgetary expenditure sanction.
3. **Functionaries (Bureaucratic Asymmetry)**:
   - Elected Sarpanchs and Pradhans often lack technical staff. The VDO, BDO, and CEO report administratively to the State Government Department of Rural Development, creating dual command friction.

## 10. Critical Exam Traps & Conceptual Nuances

1. **First State in India vs. First Princely State in Rajputana**:
   - First State in Independent India: **Rajasthan** (2 October 1959, Nagaur).
   - First Princely State in Rajputana to enact Gram Panchayat Act: **Bikaner** (1928).

2. **Election Mode Nuances**:
   - **Sarpanch**: **Directly elected** by the registered voters of the Panchayat circle.
   - **Up-Sarpanch**: **Indirectly elected** by Ward Panchs.
   - **Pradhan & Up-Pradhan**: **Indirectly elected** by Panchayat Samiti members.
   - **Zila Pramukh & Up-Zila Pramukh**: **Indirectly elected** by Zila Parishad members.
   - **Urban Heads (Adhyaksh, Sabhapati, Mayor)**: **Indirectly elected** by elected Ward Councillors.

3. **Resignation Chain Traps**:
   - *Sarpanch, Up-Sarpanch & Ward Panch* resign to the **BDO**.
   - *Pradhan* resigns to the **Zila Pramukh**.
   - *Zila Pramukh* resigns to the **Divisional Commissioner**.

4. **Educational Qualifications: What is the Current Law?**:
   - Introduced in 2015 $\rightarrow$ **Abolished in February 2019**.
   - Today, **NO educational qualifications** exist for any local body elections in Rajasthan.
   - The requirement of an operational sanitary toilet **remains mandatory**.

5. **PESA vs. Normal PRIs**:
   - In PESA areas (8 TSP districts of Rajasthan), **100% of the seats of Chairpersons** (Sarpanch, Pradhan, Zila Pramukh) are reserved exclusively for **Scheduled Tribes (ST)**.
   - At least **50% of total member seats** are reserved for STs.
   - Gram Sabha is endowed with mandatory ownership over Minor Forest Produce and minor mineral quarrying advice.

6. **DPC Chairmanship**:
   - In Rajasthan, the **Zila Pramukh** is the Chairperson of the District Planning Committee (DPC), NOT the District Collector or Divisional Commissioner! The Collector is Member Secretary.

## 11. Practice Questions & Model Mains Frameworks

### 11.1 2-Marker Questions (20 Words / 2 Lines)

**Q1. Where and when was Panchayati Raj formally inaugurated in India?**
- **Model Answer**: On **2 October 1959** at **Nagaur, Rajasthan** by Prime Minister **Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru**, making Rajasthan the first state in independent India to adopt Panchayati Raj.

**Q2. What is the current statutory requirement regarding educational qualifications for contesting PRI elections in Rajasthan?**
- **Model Answer**: Following the **Rajasthan Panchayati Raj (Amendment) Act, 2019**, all minimum educational qualification requirements were **abolished**; currently, no educational qualification is required.

**Q3. State the statutory dates for mandatory meetings of the Gram Sabha in Rajasthan.**
- **Model Answer**: The Gram Sabha must meet at least four times annually: **26 January** (Republic Day), **1 May** (Labour Day), **15 August** (Independence Day), and **2 October** (Gandhi Jayanti).

**Q4. To whom do the Sarpanch and the Zila Pramukh submit their resignations?**
- **Model Answer**: The Sarpanch submits resignation to the **Block Development Officer (BDO)**; the Zila Pramukh submits resignation to the **Divisional Commissioner**.

### 11.2 5-Marker Questions (50 Words / 5 Lines)

**Q1. Describe the key recommendations of the Sadiq Ali Committee (1964) on Panchayati Raj in Rajasthan.**
- **Model Answer**:
  - **Electoral Reforms**: Recommended secret ballot voting for Sarpanch elections and broadening the electoral college for Pradhan and Pramukh.
  - **Gram Sabha Revitalization**: Highlighted systemic voter apathy and proposed statutory enforcement of Gram Sabha meetings and audits.
  - **Administrative Strengthening**: Advocated creating a dedicated, professional cadre for Block Development Officers (BDOs) and empowering Panchayats with autonomous tax powers.

**Q2. Outline the special powers vested in the Gram Sabha under PESA Act, 1996 in the Scheduled Areas of Rajasthan.**
- **Model Answer**:
  - **Natural Resource Sovereignty**: Vested with absolute ownership and right to collect and sell Minor Forest Produce (MFP / MFPs) like Tendu, Mahua, and bamboo.
  - **Land & Mineral Protection**: Mandatory consultation before land acquisition in TSP areas; power to prevent and restore alienated tribal lands; recommendation power for minor mineral leases.
  - **Customary Self-Rule**: Authority to preserve tribal customary laws, traditions, and resolve community disputes.

**Q3. Explain the composition and role of the District Planning Committee (DPC) under Article 243ZD in Rajasthan.**

  - **Mandate**: Consolidates developmental plans formulated by PRIs and Municipalities into a unified Draft Development Plan for the entire district.

  - **Leadership**: Chaired ex-officio by the **Zila Pramukh**; the **District Collector** serves as Member Secretary.

### 11.3 10-Marker Questions (100 Words / Full Page)

**Q1. "Democratic decentralization in Rajasthan has transformed political representation but struggles to achieve genuine functional autonomy." Critically examine this statement with reference to the 3 Fs (Funds, Functions, and Functionaries).**
- **Model Answer Framework**:
  1. **Introduction**:
     - Rajasthan is the birthplace of Panchayati Raj (Nagaur, 1959) and a pioneer in democratic representation (50% reservation for women in 2008).
  2. **Transformative Political Representation**:
     - Deepened grassroots democracy: Over 11,000 Sarpanchs and 352 Panchayat Samitis.
     - 50% reservation for women has empowered hundreds of thousands of female grassroots leaders, challenging entrenched patriarchal orthodoxies (*Sarpanch Pati* phenomenon is declining with education and digital monitoring).
     - Full inclusion of SC/ST/OBC and sovereign tribal self-rule under PESA (1996).
  3. **The 3 Fs Challenge (Impediments to Autonomy)**:
     - *Funds*: Severe fiscal starvation; PRIs raise <5% of their total expenditure internally; perpetual reliance on SFC (6.75% devolution) and CFC tied grants; lack of political will to levy house/property taxes.
     - *Functions*: Although 29 Eleventh Schedule subjects were nominally transferred, real executive and financial sanctioning authority remains locked within line departments (PWD, PHED, Irrigation, Health).
     - *Functionaries*: Bureaucratic asymmetry; VDOs and BDOs remain state government employees subject to external transfers, frequently creating administrative friction with elected Sarpanchs and Pradhans.
  4. **Way Forward / Reforms**:
     - Implement the Gulab Chand Kataria Committee recommendations on complete administrative transfer of staff.
     - Create an independent Local Government Engineering & Accounts Cadre.
     - Incentivize local revenue generation through matching performance grants.

**Q2. Detail the structure, classification, and key provisions of the Rajasthan Municipalities Act, 2009. How does it address the challenges of rapid urbanization in Rajasthan?**
- **Model Answer Framework**:
  - **Legislative Genesis**: Enacted in 2009 replacing the 1959 Act; comprehensive framework aligned with the 74th Constitutional Amendment Act.
  - **Three-Tier Typology**:
    - Nagar Nigam (Population > 5 Lakh, headed by Mayor and Municipal Commissioner IAS).
    - Nagar Parishad (Population 1 to 5 Lakh, headed by Sabhapati and Commissioner).
    - Nagar Palika (Transitional towns, population 20,000 to 1 Lakh, headed by Adhyaksh and EO).
  - **Key Structural Innovations**:
    - 50% reservation for women.
    - Constitution of Wards Committees in cities with population > 3 Lakh.
    - Statutory powers for municipal property taxation, user charges for solid waste, and building bylaws enforcement.
    - Provisions for public-private partnerships (PPP) in municipal infrastructure.
  - **Urban Challenges Addressed**:
    - Tackling unorganized peri-urban growth, slum regularization, solid waste processing, and stormwater drainage.
    - Streamlining online building plan approvals (*Bhu-Upayog Parivartan*).
