## 1. Mineral wealth: the “Museum of Minerals”

Rajasthan’s varied geology includes ancient crystalline basement rocks, the Aravalli and Delhi geological belts, sedimentary basins, and younger deposits. This diversity supports metallic minerals, industrial minerals, building stones, lignite, petroleum, natural gas and prospects for several strategic minerals. “Museum of Minerals” is a conventional description of the state’s mineral diversity, not a statutory designation.

### State-level snapshot

| Indicator | Verified formulation | Reference period / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral production basket | **22 major minerals and 36 minor minerals** are reported as produced in the state | Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024; do not conflate with older counts of minerals identified or commercially exploited |
| State revenue from minerals | **₹7,491 crore** | FY 2023–24, Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024; the figure is mineral revenue received by the state, not the value of mineral output |
| Major-mineral revenue | **₹3,945 crore** | FY 2023–24, state policy |
| Mining sector contribution to GSDP | **3.4%** | FY 2023–24, state policy |
| MCDR mineral-production value | **₹21,922 crore**, approximately **15.5% of India’s total** | Provisional FY 2023–24 figure on Rajasthan’s investment portal; this is a specific reporting category, not the value of all mining including every minor mineral and fuel |
| Mining footprint | About **0.68% of geographical area** | Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024; area under mining activities/concessions, not all land with geological resources |

Sources: [Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024](https://mines.rajasthan.gov.in/dmgcms/link_to_external_file/Rajasthan_Mineral_Policy%202024.pdf); [Rajasthan government — Mines and Minerals](https://rising.rajasthan.gov.in/mines-and-minerals).

### “Sole producer” claims: how to memorise safely

The Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024 describes Rajasthan as India’s sole producer of **lead, zinc, wollastonite, selenite, calcite and gypsum**. The Department of Mines and Geology’s public overview specifically lists **lead and zinc ores, selenite and wollastonite** as sole-producer minerals and says almost all national calcite, natural-gypsum and silver production has historically come from Rajasthan. These descriptions are not interchangeable: a state may have a large share of *resources* or *production* without being the sole producer. Always attach the source year and mineral category to any percentage.

**Do not treat the following as current national production shares:** “99–100% lead-zinc”, “95% feldspar”, “90% rock phosphate” or “94% gypsum”. Some older reference tables mix estimated national *resources* with production shares, and the percentages change by year and mineral grade. The safer core facts are the major Rajasthan deposits and the dated official statistics above. [DMG overview](https://mines.rajasthan.gov.in/dmgcms/page?menuName=YzCjPSj%2FAU7RGgRfpUoY5A%3D%3D).

### Lead, zinc and silver: connected, not identical

- **Galena (PbS)** is the principal lead ore; **sphalerite (ZnS)** is the principal zinc ore.
- Lead and zinc deposits commonly occur together in Rajasthan, and silver can be recovered as an associated/by-product metal from some ores. They are not invariably present in the same proportions or every deposit.
- **Rampura Agucha** is a major, internationally significant lead-zinc deposit and mine in Bhilwara district. Avoid treating “world’s richest” as an absolute fact unless a current technical source specifies the metric (grade, contained metal, or production).
- **Zawar** is historically important for early zinc extraction and metallurgy. “One of the world’s oldest known zinc-production sites” is safer than presenting one exact starting year as uncontested.
- **Chanderiya** (Chittorgarh) and **Debari** (Udaipur) are important Hindustan Zinc processing/smelting locations. Capacity rankings such as “Asia’s largest” require a dated company source and a defined metric.

### Copper and other metals

The Khetri Copper Belt is the state’s principal traditional copper belt. Khetri, Kolihan and associated workings are linked to Hindustan Copper Limited (HCL). Other copper-bearing areas include parts of Alwar, Sikar and Bhilwara. For exam answers, distinguish **ore resources**, **mine output**, **concentrate production**, and **refined copper**; state rankings can differ across those measures.

Rajasthan also has iron ore deposits in the north-eastern Aravalli region and south-eastern/central belts. Some deposits are low-grade and require beneficiation. Resource tonnage alone does not establish economic viability: grade, overburden, depth, access, clearances and processing cost matter.

### Gold and uranium: resources versus production

**Gold — Bhukia–Jagpura:** The state DMG reports approximately **118.88 million tonnes of gold-ore resources statewide**, including about **105.81 million tonnes** in the Bhukia–Jagpura–Delwara belt at a stated cut-off grade of 0.50 g/t in its published resource summary. This is **ore tonnage**, not 118.88 million tonnes of gold, not the recoverable metal content, and not evidence that commercial production has begun. The state has auctioned the Bhukia–Jagpura block; auction, statutory approvals, mine development and commercial production are separate stages. [DMG mineral information](https://mines.rajasthan.gov.in/dmgcms/page?lang=Hindi&menuName=U%2FTcLwXbxWrpRTAziIP1YF2XHCB9gDpO); [DMG auction document](https://mines.rajasthan.gov.in/dmgcms/link_to_external_file/%2013122024.pdf).

**Uranium — Rohil, Sikar:** The Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) has identified a uranium deposit in Sikar district and nominated Uranium Corporation of India Limited (UCIL) as the prospective lessee. In a parliamentary reply dated **4 February 2026**, the government said statutory clearances were still being pursued; a further reply in March 2026 described the proposed mine-and-mill project as being at various stages of clearance. Therefore, do not describe Rohil as an operating uranium mine or memorise “12 million tonnes of uranium” as contained uranium. An ore-tonnage or resource estimate must be attributed to its geological report, grade and unit. [DAE / PIB, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2223266&lang=1&reg=1); [DAE / PIB, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2241745&lang=1&reg=6).

| Mineral | Key Rajasthan location | Uses and exam distinction |

| Rock phosphate / phosphorite | Jhamarkotra, Udaipur; Birmania, Jaisalmer; Sallopat, Banswara | Important fertilizer raw material. RSMML operates a beneficiation facility at Jhamarkotra to upgrade ore/concentrate. “Largest/richest in India” should be used only with a matching technical source. |
| Gypsum | Jamsar and Lunkaransar (Bikaner); Badopal/Rawatsar (Hanumangarh); Mohangarh (Jaisalmer); Bhadwasi/Manglod (Nagaur) | Used in plaster of Paris, cement and soil amendment for sodic soils. | Calcite | Sikar, Sirohi, Udaipur and Pali belts | Calcium carbonate; use in fillers, ceramics and other industrial applications. Resource share and output share must not be confused. |
| Feldspar | Ajmer region, Beawar, Bhilwara and other pegmatite-rich areas | Important in glass and ceramics. Do not repeat “95% of India’s feldspar” without an appropriately dated production source. |
| Mica | Bhilwara and other pegmatite/metamorphic belts | Electrical/thermal insulation uses; historical mining labels such as “Mica City” are local sobriquets, not formal rankings. |
| Asbestos-bearing minerals | Occurrences are reported in parts of southern Rajasthan, including the Udaipur region | Asbestos fibres are serious health hazards. Treat occurrence as a geology fact, not as a positive industrial recommendation; extraction and use are subject to stringent health/environmental controls. |
| Potash and other critical/strategic minerals | Several explored or prospective areas | Presence of a resource does not mean operating production. Keep exploration status and auction/clearance progress date-stamped. |

### Soil and mineral reclamation distinction

Gypsum is commonly used to reclaim **sodic/alkaline soils** because calcium can replace exchangeable sodium, after which adequate drainage and leaching are required. It is not a universal remedy for every saline soil. Treatment depends on soil chemistry, electrical conductivity, exchangeable sodium percentage, drainage and water quality. This chapter covers mineral resources; detailed soil classification belongs in the soils chapter.

## 3. Building and dimensional stones

Rajasthan is an important national source of marble, granite, sandstone and limestone products. Avoid saying that the state has a “global monopoly” in all decorative stone: comparable deposits and producers exist elsewhere.

| Stone / variety | Major centre | Safe association |
|---|---|---|
| Makrana marble | Makrana, Didwana-Kuchaman district | White marble widely associated with the Taj Mahal; recognised in 2019 as a Global Heritage Stone Resource by the International Union of Geological Sciences. |
| Green marble / serpentinous decorative stone | Rishabhdeo–Udaipur area | Common ornamental and cladding stone in the trade; commercial names may not map perfectly to strict geological nomenclature. |
| Black marble | Bhainslana area, Kotputli-Behror region | Decorative slabs and statues; varieties are traded under colour names. |
| Jaisalmer yellow/golden sandstone | Jaisalmer region | Used in local fort and haveli architecture. |
| Dholpur/Bansi Paharpur sandstone | Dholpur and Bansi Paharpur region (Bharatpur district in older references; check present administrative map) | Red/buff sandstone used in historic and modern buildings. Bansi Paharpur stone was used for the Ayodhya Ram temple project. |
| Kota stone | Kota and Ramganj Mandi region | Fine-grained limestone used extensively in flooring. |
| Granite | Jalore and other western/southern Rajasthan localities | Important dimension stone; “Granite City” is a popular trade label for Jalore. |

**Kishangarh marble market:** Kishangarh is a major national marble processing and trading hub. “Asia’s largest” is a market-slogan claim rather than a stable statistical category unless a current source defines how size is measured.

## 4. Mineral policy and institutions

### Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024

The policy’s stated targets include:

- Increase the number of minerals under extraction from **58 to 70 by 2047**.
- Expand the area under mineral concessions from about **0.68%** to **1% by 2029–30** and **2% by 2046–47**.
- Auction **50 major-mineral blocks with pre-embedded clearances by 2029–30**, beginning with an initial group in 2025–26.
- Raise the sector’s GSDP contribution from **3.4% in FY 2023–24** to **5% by 2029–30** and **8% by 2046–47**.
- Raise mineral-sector revenue and employment through exploration, transparent auctions, mineral processing and more efficient use of mine waste.

Targets are policy intentions, not proof that outcomes have already occurred. Cite the policy year whenever quoting them. [Official policy PDF](https://mines.rajasthan.gov.in/dmgcms/link_to_external_file/Rajasthan_Mineral_Policy%202024.pdf).

### Rajasthan State Mines and Minerals Limited (RSMML)

RSMML is a Rajasthan state public-sector undertaking headquartered in Udaipur. Its major activities have included rock phosphate, lignite, gypsum and limestone. Jhamarkotra is associated with rock phosphate; Jamsar with gypsum; Sanu (Jaisalmer) with limestone; and selected lignite fields with RSMML operations. Corporate portfolios and unit names can change, so the list should be treated as a functional study aid rather than a fixed legal organisation chart.

### District Mineral Foundation (DMF) and PMKKKY

- DMFs are established under Section 9B of the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act, 1957, to benefit people and areas affected by mining.
- DMF contribution is generally **10% of royalty for leases granted on or after 12 January 2015** and **30% of royalty for leases granted before that date**, subject to the applicable rules.
- The **revised PMKKKY guidelines issued in January 2024** require at least **70%** of DMF funds to be used for high-priority sectors, including drinking water, environment, health, education, welfare, skill development, sanitation, housing, agriculture and animal husbandry. Up to 30% is for other priority sectors such as physical infrastructure, irrigation, energy and watershed development.
- The 2024 guidelines also emphasise directly affected areas, transparency, audits, grievance redressal and monitoring.

Do not use the older 60:40 allocation as the current national guideline. [Ministry of Mines — PMKKKY](https://mines.gov.in/webportal/content/about-dmfpmkkky); [PIB summary](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2099731&lang=2&reg=48).

### Manufactured sand (M-Sand)

M-Sand is processed, graded sand manufactured by crushing suitable rock, including some quarry/mining by-products. The policy framework has been updated: Rajasthan’s DMG now lists an **M-Sand Mineral Policy 2024**. The original chapter’s references to a “2020 policy,” a 25 January 2021 launch date and an across-the-board 25% mandatory procurement rule should not be memorised without checking the specific government order, applicable department and current amendment. [DMG policy portal](https://mines.rajasthan.gov.in/dmgcms/page?lang=Hindi&menuName=yfH6eem4pxZ732BN%3B455611%3BnXoogBssewZfMz1o1rsSrScYJQ%3D&theme=Red).

## 5. Hydrocarbons of Rajasthan

Rajasthan has important onshore petroleum and natural-gas resources, especially in the western sedimentary basins. Production shares change each year; avoid the unqualified statement that Rajasthan always supplies 20–22% of India’s crude oil. Use the latest PPAC/Ministry of Petroleum production series if a question asks for a specific annual share.

### Four-basin exam framework

The four-basin scheme below is a conventional Rajasthan geography framework. Basin boundaries and quoted areas vary among petroleum-geology maps; exact area totals should be used only with their map/source.

| Basin / geological unit | Broad region | Key association |
|---|---|---|
| Barmer–Sanchore | Western Rajasthan, including the Barmer–Balotra–Sanchore/Jalore belt in current administrative terms | Major onshore oil discoveries and production; Mangala, Bhagyam and Aishwariya are key field names. |
| Jaisalmer basin | Jaisalmer region | Natural gas and petroleum exploration; Tanot, Dandewala and other gas-field names appear in regional literature. |
| Bikaner–Nagaur basin | Bikaner–Nagaur and adjoining north-western areas | Sedimentary basin with lignite and hydrocarbon prospectivity; Baghewala is a commonly cited heavy-oil field. |
| Rajasthan Shelf / Vindhyan-related exploration areas | South-eastern and eastern Rajasthan, including parts of the Kota–Bundi–Baran–Jhalawar region in some classifications | Exploration province; do not describe every basin-prospect area as a producing commercial oilfield. |

### Barmer oilfields and chronology

- The **Mangala field** was discovered in 2004 by Cairn’s exploration programme in the Barmer basin.
- Commercial production from the Mangala field began on **29 August 2009**.
- Other field names commonly associated with the Barmer basin include **Bhagyam** and **Aishwariya**; Raageshwari is associated with gas as well as the broader hydrocarbon project area.
- A field’s discovery, appraisal, development plan, production start and present production level are separate milestones. Do not convert a historical production share into a current one.

The HPCL Rajasthan Refinery Limited (HRRL) project at **Pachpadra, Balotra district**, is a 9 million metric tonnes per annum (MMTPA) greenfield refinery-cum-petrochemical complex.

| Item | Verified status |
|---|---|

| Refining capacity | **9 MMTPA** |
| Petrochemical capacity | About **2.4 MMTPA** |

## 7. Lignite

Lignite is low-rank brown coal, generally with higher moisture and lower calorific value than higher-rank bituminous coal. In Rajasthan it is associated with Tertiary sedimentary basins.

| Field / area | District-region | Exam association |
|---|---|---|
| Palana | Bikaner | Historic lignite field frequently cited in Rajasthan geography |
| Barsingsar | Bikaner | Lignite mine and thermal-power association |
| Giral | Barmer region | Lignite-based power project; exact “first” claims should be tied to a dated official source |
| Kapurdi and Jalipa | Barmer–Balotra region | Important lignite fields/projects |
| Kasnau–Matasukh | Nagaur region | Important lignite deposit and mining association |

Do not memorise a fixed national reserve rank for lignite without the reporting year and the definition of resources/reserves. A resource can exist without current extraction or power generation.

## 8. Renewable energy: current position and long-term targets

Rajasthan’s high solar radiation, extensive open land, strong western wind resources and access to expanding transmission infrastructure support large-scale renewable generation. These advantages do not eliminate constraints: land and habitat impacts, grid evacuation, curtailment, storage needs, dust and module-cleaning water, and impacts on the Great Indian Bustard (Godawan) all matter.

### Installed capacity: use the date

According to the Rajasthan government’s renewable-energy sector page, as of **30 September 2026**:

| Metric | Figure | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Total installed renewable-energy capacity | **51,595.50 MW (51.60 GW)** | State government reported total as of 30 Sep 2026; Rajasthan ranked second among states in total renewable capacity |
| Installed solar capacity | **45,121.44 MW (45.12 GW)** | Rajasthan ranked first among states in installed solar capacity in this source |
| Installed wind capacity | **5,815.29 MW (5.82 GW)** | Rajasthan ranked fifth among states in wind capacity in the same source |
| Renewable electricity generation | **69.12 billion units** in FY 2025–26 | Annual generation, not installed capacity; the state government source reports Rajasthan ranked first nationally for renewable generation that year |

Source: [Rajasthan government — Renewable Energy](https://rising.rajasthan.gov.in/renewable-energy). Rankings and capacity numbers are snapshot data and should be updated for future exam cycles.

### Potential estimates: avoid mixing methods

- Rajasthan’s current investor-facing state material cites assessed **solar potential of 829 GW** and **wind potential of 284 GW at 150 m hub height**. The MNRE/National Institute of Wind Energy assessment gives Rajasthan **127.75 GW at 120 m** and **284.2 GW at 150 m** for wind. These are estimates of gross resource potential under specified assumptions—not commissioned capacity.
- The often-repeated solar potential of **142 GW** comes from an older assessment and should not be presented as the latest estimate without explaining the assessment method and date.
- Potential, project pipeline, awarded capacity, installed capacity and actual generation are separate indicators.

Sources: [Rajasthan government — Renewable Energy](https://rising.rajasthan.gov.in/renewable-energy); [MNRE — Wind Overview](https://mnre.gov.in/en/wind-overview/).

### Bhadla and other solar projects

**Bhadla Solar Park**, in the Bhadla/Phalodi area of western Rajasthan, is a landmark large-scale solar development with approximately **2,245 MW** of capacity often reported across its phases. It is safely described as one of the world’s largest solar-park developments. Avoid an unqualified “world’s largest operational solar park” ranking unless a current comparable source defines the boundary and compares operating capacity across sites.

Other important project clusters include Fatehgarh and Nokh in the Jaisalmer region and Pugal in Bikaner. The current state investor page describes a Pugal integrated solar-plus-storage tender of **2,450 MW solar paired with 1,600 MW / 6,400 MWh battery storage**. This is a tender/development milestone, not an assurance that the capacity is already commissioned.

### Policy targets

The state’s **Rajasthan Integrated Clean Energy Policy 2024** aims for **125 GW of renewable-energy capacity by FY 2029–30**, comprising **90 GW solar, 25 GW wind/hybrid and 10 GW storage/hydro** in the current official state sector summary. Do not conflate this with an older/other target of 90 GW total renewable capacity by 2030.

### Wind energy

- Western Rajasthan, especially the Jaisalmer region, has strong site-specific wind resources.
- MNRE/NIWE’s assessment is **127.75 GW at 120 m** and **284.2 GW at 150 m**. The hub height must accompany the figure.
- **Amar Sagar (Jaisalmer)** is commonly cited as an early wind-power project location, with commissioning dated 1999 in Rajasthan exam literature. Because “first project” depends on the definition of commissioned grid-connected project, retain the date as a traditional exam association rather than a universal technical ranking unless a primary commissioning record is supplied.

### Renewable energy challenges and solutions

1. **Evacuation and grid congestion:** Generation build-out can outrun transmission; use Green Energy Corridor investments, upgraded substations, forecasting and transparent access rules.
2. **Intermittency and storage:** Combine solar/wind with batteries, pumped storage, flexible generation and demand response.
3. **Godawan and other wildlife:** Overhead lines in bustard habitat can cause fatal collisions. Route selection, insulation/marking, undergrounding where technically and legally directed, and habitat planning must follow current court and conservation guidance—not an oversimplified claim that undergrounding was universally ordered across every line.
4. **Land and livelihood:** Assess pasture, community-use land, biodiversity and local livelihoods before siting projects.
5. **Water and dust:** Dry cleaning, robotic cleaning and careful water budgeting can reduce the pressure created by washing panels in arid regions.
6. **End-of-life materials:** Plan recycling and safe management of photovoltaic panels, batteries, turbine blades and electrical equipment.

## 9. Twenty-five high-risk exam traps

1. **58 minerals produced ≠ 81 minerals identified.** The Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024 reports 22 major and 36 minor minerals produced. Older counts describe different categories.
2. **Resource ≠ reserve ≠ production.** Keep the reporting category and year with every mineral statistic.
3. **Rajasthan’s sole-producer list:** The Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024 lists lead, zinc, wollastonite, selenite, calcite and gypsum. Cross-check the exact mineral category before quoting a rank.
4. **Do not call old resource shares production shares.** The often-repeated feldspar 95%, rock phosphate 90% and gypsum 94% figures are not safe as current production shares without year-specific evidence.
5. **Rampura Agucha** is a major lead-zinc deposit in Bhilwara; “world’s richest” must be tied to a defined metric and current technical source.
6. **Zawar** is historically associated with early zinc extraction; avoid presenting a single disputed exact year as settled.
7. **Galena = PbS; sphalerite = ZnS.** Silver may be an associated/by-product metal, not a guaranteed identical share in every deposit.
8. **Khetri** is the classic copper-belt association; distinguish ore, concentrate and refined metal.
9. **Degana/Rewat Hill** is the traditional tungsten location in Nagaur; do not equate historical importance with current largest operating mine.
10. **Bhukia–Jagpura:** 118.88 million tonnes refers to gold-bearing ore resources in a state DMG summary, not tonnes of gold metal.
11. **Rohil uranium deposit:** as of early 2026, UCIL was the prospective lessee and statutory clearances remained in progress; it was not to be memorised as an operating mine.
12. **Jhamarkotra** is the key rock-phosphate association; beneficiation improves grade/concentrate quality.
13. **Gypsum for sodic soils** is not the universal treatment for every saline soil.
14. **Makrana marble** was recognised as a Global Heritage Stone Resource in 2019; the title is not the same as a UNESCO World Heritage property designation.
15. **Kota stone** is limestone, not marble in strict geological classification, despite broad commercial naming practices for decorative stone.
16. **DMFT contribution rates** differ by lease grant date: generally 10% for leases granted on/after 12 January 2015 and 30% for older leases.
17. **PMKKKY:** the revised January 2024 guideline uses at least 70% for high-priority sectors, not the older 60% figure.
18. **M-Sand policy:** Rajasthan has a DMG-listed M-Sand Mineral Policy 2024; verify mandatory procurement percentages from the relevant current order.
19. **Four hydrocarbon basins** is a conventional exam framework; exact basin areas and boundaries depend on the geological map/classification.
20. **Mangala:** discovery in 2004; commercial production began on 29 August 2009.
21. **Rajasthan’s oil share** varies by financial year; do not use 20–22% as a timeless invariant.
22. **Pachpadra refinery** is in Balotra district, not simply “Barmer” under the current district map.
23. **HRRL equity = 74% HPCL : 26% Rajasthan government; capacity = 9 MMTPA.** Current project cost revision is ₹79,459 crore (April 2026).
24. **Solar potential is not installed capacity.** Current state material cites 829 GW solar potential; the 142 GW figure is an older estimate.
25. **Bhadla’s 2,245 MW is a reported solar-park capacity figure.** “World’s largest” is a time-sensitive ranking; use a qualified description and date.

## 10. Model examination questions

### 2-marker answers

**Q1. Why is Rajasthan called the “Museum of Minerals”?**  
Because its varied geological formations host a broad range of metallic minerals, industrial minerals, building stones, lignite, petroleum and natural-gas resources.

**Q2. Distinguish mineral resources from reserves.**  
Resources are estimated concentrations with reasonable prospects for eventual extraction; reserves are the economically mineable part demonstrated under defined technical, economic and legal assumptions. Neither term means actual annual production.

**Q3. Name four key lead-zinc mining centres in Rajasthan.**  
Rampura Agucha, Zawar, Rajpura Dariba/Sindesar Khurd and Kayad.

**Q4. What is the present status of the Rohil uranium deposit?**  
A uranium deposit in Sikar district has been identified; UCIL was nominated as prospective lessee, with statutory clearances still being pursued in official replies during early 2026.

**Q5. State HRRL’s equity ratio and refining capacity.**  
HPCL holds 74% and Rajasthan government 26%; design refining capacity is 9 MMTPA.

### 5-marker answer framework

**Q6. Explain the contribution of mineral resources to Rajasthan’s economy and the need for sustainable mining.**

1. **Resource base:** lead-zinc, copper, rock phosphate, gypsum, limestone, lignite and dimension stones underpin metal, fertilizer, cement, ceramics and construction industries.
2. **Economic role:** the state’s Mineral Policy 2024 reports mineral revenue of ₹7,491 crore and a 3.4% GSDP contribution in FY 2023–24.
3. **Employment and linkages:** mining supports processing, transport, construction materials and local services.
4. **Risks:** land degradation, dust, water demand, tailings, health and safety hazards, and unequal distribution of benefits.
5. **Governance:** stronger exploration standards, scientific mine closure, recycling, environmental monitoring and transparent DMF/PMKKKY spending in affected communities.

**Q7. Assess Rajasthan’s renewable-energy leadership and constraints.**

Rajasthan’s solar radiation and wind resources have made it a national leader. As of 30 September 2026, state government figures recorded 45.12 GW installed solar capacity and 5.82 GW wind capacity, with total renewable capacity of 51.60 GW. Its 2024 policy targets 125 GW of renewable capacity by FY 2029–30. Realising the target depends on transmission evacuation, storage, land and biodiversity safeguards, reliable forecasts, water-conscious panel cleaning and lifecycle recycling. The Great Indian Bustard is a critical test of whether renewable expansion can be combined with habitat protection.

### 10-marker essay architecture

**“Rajasthan’s mineral and renewable-energy endowments are economic assets, but their value depends on evidence-led resource governance.”**

- **Resource advantage:** diverse minerals and stones; onshore petroleum and gas; high solar radiation; significant wind potential.
- **Industrial conversion:** mineral beneficiation and smelting; HRRL’s 9 MMTPA refinery-petrochemical complex; grid-scale renewable generation.
- **Governance and measurement:** distinguish resources, reserves, production, installed capacity and generation; use dated official statistics.
- **Environmental and social costs:** mine waste, dust, water stress, habitat fragmentation, transmission impacts and waste from solar/battery equipment.
- **Path ahead:** responsible exploration, transparent auctions, science-based mine closure, DMF/PMKKKY effectiveness, storage and transmission investment, nature-sensitive project siting and recycling.
