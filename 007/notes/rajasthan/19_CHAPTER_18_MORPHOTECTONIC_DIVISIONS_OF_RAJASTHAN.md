## 1. Geomorphological Evolution, Tectonic Framework & Classification

The physical landscape of Rajasthan is an ancient geological palimpsest spanning more than two billion years of Earth history. It encompasses geological formations from the **Archaean Bhilwara Supergroup** (among the oldest crystalline basements on the planet) to modern Quaternary wind-blown aeolian sands.

### 1.1 The Great Boundary Fault of Rajasthan (GBF / महान सीमा भ्रंश)
- **Tectonic Nature**: A massive reverse fault line marking the suture zone between the ancient **Aravalli-Delhi Metamorphic Complex** (to the northwest) and the flat, undeformed **Vindhyan Sedimentary Basin** (to the southeast).
- **Extent & Trace**: Extends approximately **800 kilometers** across southeastern Rajasthan, traversing through **Chittorgarh $\rightarrow$ Kota $\rightarrow$ Bundi $\rightarrow$ Sawai Madhopur $\rightarrow$ Karauli $\rightarrow$ Dholpur**.
- **Geomorphological Expression**: Expressed on the surface as steep, vertical sandstone escarpments (*Vindhyan Scarps*) overlooking the Chambal and Banas drainage basins.

---

### 1.2 Historiography of Geographic Regionalization
1. **Dr. V.C. Misra (1967)**: In his seminal treatise *Geography of Rajasthan* (National Book Trust), Dr. Misra pioneered the first academic regionalization, dividing Rajasthan into **7 Geographic Regions**:
   - (1) Western Arid Region, (2) Semi-Arid Region, (3) Canal Irrigated Region (Ganganagar), (4) Aravalli Region, (5) Eastern Agricultural Region, (6) South-Eastern Agricultural Region, and (7) Chambal Ravine Region.
2. **Dr. A.K. Sen (1968)**: Classified the state based on agro-climatic boundaries and rainfall isohyets.
3. **Prof. Ram Lochan Singh (1971)**: Divided Rajasthan into **2 Macro Regions** (Western Rajasthan and Eastern Rajasthan), subdivided into **4 Meso Regions** and **12 Micro Regions**.
4. **The Standard Fourfold Canonical Division (Dr. H.M. Saxena & Dr. L.R. Bhalla)**:
   Universally adopted by the Rajasthan Board (RBSE) and RPSC for examination benchmarking:

## 2. Division I: The Western Sandy Plain (Thar Desert / मरुस्थलीय प्रदेश)

Covering **61.11% of the state’s geographic territory (approx. 209,000 sq km)** across western and northwestern Rajasthan, the Thar Desert is the most densely populated desert in the world (83 persons/sq km vs. global desert average of 7).

### 2.1 Demarcating Climatic Isohyets (समवर्षा रेखाएँ)
- **50 cm Isohyet Line**: Runs along the western foothills of the Aravalli range; separates the entire Western Sandy Plain from Eastern Rajasthan.
- **25 cm Isohyet Line**: Bisects the Western Sandy Plain into two distinct zones:
  - Western flank: **Pure Arid Marusthali** (< 25 cm annual precipitation).
  - Eastern flank: **Semi-Arid Transitional Plain (Rajasthan Bagar)** (25 cm to 50 cm annual precipitation).

---

### 2.2 Macro-Zone A: The Pure Arid Marusthali (< 25 cm)

#### 1. Desert Surface Typology
- **Erg (इर्ग)**: Pure sandy desert composed of shifting, undulating sand dunes (*Dhrian*); prevalent in Jaisalmer and western Bikaner.
- **Hamada (हम्मादा)**: Bleak, rocky, boulder-strewn desert pavement barren of sand; runs in a broad arc from **Pokhran (Jaisalmer) to Phalodi and Ramgarh**.
- **Reg (रेग)**: Mixed stony-gravel desert combining sandy patches with rocky debris.

#### 3. The Dune-Free Tract (बालुका स्तूप मुक्त प्रदेश)
- Encompasses **41.5%** of the arid desert zone, stretching across **Pokhran, Ramgarh (Jaisalmer), and Phalodi**.
- Underlain by marine sedimentary limestone, sandstone, and shale of the Jurassic and Eocene epochs, providing incontrovertible proof that this region was submerged beneath the **Tethys Sea**.
- **Akal Wood Fossil Park (आकल वुड फॉसिल पार्क)**: Located 21 km southeast of Jaisalmer; preserves 25 petrified fossilized tree trunks dating back **180 million years (Jurassic Period)**, proving the ancient desert was once a lush, warm equatorial rain forest.
- **Lathi Series (लाठी सीरीज)**:
  - A 60-kilometer-long subterranean geological aquifer stretching from **Pokhran to Mohangarh** along the paleo-channel of the mythical **Saraswati river**.
  - Yields sweet, perennial potable water trapped in Cretaceous sandstone strata.
  - **Chandan Tube Well (चांदन नलकूप)**: Located here; dubbed the **"Thar ka Ghada" (घड़ा of Thar)** due to its inexhaustible flow.
  - Ground cover dominated by protein-rich **Sewan grass (*Lasiurus scindicus*)**, the prime nesting sanctuary of Rajasthan’s State Bird, the critically endangered **Great Indian Bustard (Godawan)**.

### 2.3 Macro-Zone B: The Semi-Arid Transitional Plain (Rajasthan Bagar)
Bounded by the 25 cm isohyet to the west and the 50 cm isohyet (Aravalli foothills) to the east, this transitional eco-zone is subdivided into four morphotectonic units:

1. **Luni Basin (Godwar Tract / लूनी बेसिन)**:
   - Drained by the ephemeral Luni river and its left-bank tributaries across **Pali, Jalore, Sanchor, and Balotra**.
   - Characterized by granitic inselbergs and volcanic domes:
     - **Chhappan ki Pahadiyan (56 Hills / छप्पन की पहाड़ियाँ)**: Fifty-six round granitic hills located around **Siwana (Balotra)**; home to the sacred Jain pilgrimage of **Nakoda Parvat** (Mewa Nagar) and the hill station of **Haldeshwar Mahadev** (titled *"Mount Abu of Marwar"*).
     - **Jalore Granite & Rhyolite**: Rose-pink granitic dome hills (*Inselbergs*) around Jalore city.
2. **Nagauri Upland (नागौरी उच्च भूमि)**:
   - Elevation 300–500 meters; underlain by subterranean sodium, gypsum, and limestone beds.
   - **Kobar Patti / Banka Patti (Hump / Fluorosis Belt / कूबड़ पट्टी)**:
     - An infamous subterranean groundwater zone stretching between **Nagaur and Didwana-Kuchaman to Ajmer**.
     - Extremely high fluoride concentrations in drinking water cause widespread dental fluorosis and skeletal deformities (hunched backs, *Kobar*), bending the spines of the local population.
3. **Shekhawati Internal Drainage Tract (कांतली बेसिन / शेखावाटी)**:
   - Covers Sikar, Jhunjhunu, Neem Ka Thana, and Churu. Characterized by shifting dunes and streams that terminate in sand before reaching any sea (e.g., the Kantli river).
   - **Johad (जोहड़)**: Traditional vernacular raw rainwater storage wells dug down to the limestone hardpan; revolutionized by waterman Rajendra Singh.
   - **Beed (बीड़)**: Fenced community grasslands utilized for rotational village livestock grazing.
4. **Ghaggar Plain (घग्घर का मैदान)**:
   - Covers **Ganganagar and Hanumangarh**; formed by rich alluvial silt deposited by the ancient Saraswati and Drishadvati rivers (now represented by the ephemeral Ghaggar).
   - **Nali (नाली)**: The flat, broad, fertile river bed of the Ghaggar in Hanumangarh; famous for intensive wheat, mustard, and long-staple cotton cultivation.
   - **Hakra (हकरा)**: The spillover course of the Ghaggar when monsoon floods cross into Fort Abbas, Pakistan.

### 3.3 High Plateaus & Geological Formations of the Southern Aravallis
1. **Oriya Plateau (उड़िया का पठार)**:
   - Elevated at **1,360 meters** above sea level; situated immediately beneath Guru Shikhar in Mount Abu (Sirohi).
   - The **highest plateau in Rajasthan**.
2. **Abu Block Plateau**: Second highest plateau (1,200 m); a granitic batholith with steep fault scarps.
3. **Bhorat Plateau (भोराट का पठार)**:
   - Elevated at **1,225 meters**; stretches between **Kumbhalgarh (Rajsamand)** and **Gogunda (Udaipur)**.
   - Functions as the continental water divide between the Arabian Sea (Sabarmati/Vakil) and the Bay of Bengal (Banas).
4. **Mesa Plateau (मेसा का पठार)**:
   - Elevated at **616 meters** in Chittorgarh at the confluence of the Gambhiri and Bedach rivers; supports the colossal **Chittorgarh Fort**.
5. **Uparmal Plateau (ऊपरमाल का पठार)**:
   - Elevated rocky tableland stretching from **Bijolia (Bhilwara)** to **Bhainsrorgarh (Chittorgarh)**.
6. **Lashadiya Plateau (लसाड़िया का पठार)**:
   - Severely dissected, jagged, wave-eroded rocky plateau situated immediately east of Jaisamand Lake in newly organized **Salumbar district** (formerly Udaipur).
7. **Desh-Haro (देशहरो)**:
   - The high valley nestled between the **Jarga and Raga mountain peaks** (Udaipur); remains perpetually lush and verdant throughout the year (*Desh-Haro* = Ever-Green Country).
8. **Bhakar (भाकर)**:
   - Steep, extremely sharp, jagged ridges characteristic of the **western Sirohi** district.
9. **Girwa (गिरवा)**:
   - The amphitheater-like, saucer-shaped ring of hills surrounding the city of **Udaipur** (*Girwa* = Neck of hills).

### 3.4 Key Mountain Passes (*Naal / Ghat*)
- **Connecting Marwar to Mewar / Ajmer**:
  - **Bar Pass (बर दर्रा)**: Located in Beawar district; traversed by National Highway connecting Ajmer to Jodhpur.
  - **Desuri ki Naal (देसूरी की नाल)**: Connects Pali (Desuri) with Rajsamand (Charbhuja).
  - **Jhilwa / Paglya Naal (झीलवा / पगल्या नाल)**: Historical military pass connecting Marwar to Mewar through the Aravalis.
  - **Someshwar Naal**: Rugged gorge in Rajsamand.
  - **Hathi Guda Naal (हाथी गूड़ा की नाल)**: Scenic mountain pass leading to Kumbhalgarh Fort.

## 4. Division III: The Eastern Plains (पूर्वी मैदानी प्रदेश)

Covering **23.0% of the state’s area** and sheltering **39.0% of its total population**, the Eastern Plains exhibit the highest demographic density and agricultural productivity in Rajasthan. Formed by alluvial silt deposited by the Banas, Banganga, Chambal, and Mahi river systems.

---

### 4.1 Chambal Basin: Utkhat Bhumi & Dang Region
- **Gully Erosion (अवनलिका अपरदन)**: Heavy monsoon discharges flowing over friable alluvial clay have gouged out deep vertical trenches and ravines (*Beehad* / बीहड़) reaching depths of 15 to 30 meters.
- **Utkhat Bhumi (Badland Topography / उत्खात भूमि)**: The most extensive badland labyrinth in India, covering 4,500 sq km across **Dholpur, Karauli, and Sawai Madhopur**.
- **The Dang Tract (डांग क्षेत्र)**: Elevated, barren, scrub-covered rocky ridges between the ravines; **Karauli** is officially known as the **"Queen of Dang" (डांग की रानी)**.

---

### 4.2 Banas-Banganga Basin
- **Piedmont Plain**: A gently undulating erosion plain (*Peneplain*) underlain by Archaean gneiss and granite, elevation 280–500 meters.
- **Two Sub-Basins**:
  1. **Mewar Plain (Western)**: Elevated higher (Bhilwara, Chittorgarh, Rajsamand); drained by the Berach, Kothari, and Khari rivers.
  2. **Malpura-Karauli Plain (Eastern)**: Flat alluvial plain covering Tonk, Sawai Madhopur, and Dausa; deep tertiary silt deposits.

---

### 4.3 Mahi Basin: Chappan Plain & Kanthal
- **Chappan ka Maidan (छप्पन का मैदान)**:
  - The fertile alluvial basin located between **Banswara and Pratapgarh**.
  - Derives its name from the **56 drainage streams (*Chhappan Nale*) and 56 agricultural villages (*Chhappan Gaon*)** drained by the Mahi river and its tributaries.
- **Kanthal (कांठल)**:
  - The narrow coastal bank strip along the Mahi river in **Pratapgarh district** (*Kanthal* = Shore/Edge); hence the local rulers were titled *Kanthal-Pati*.

---

### 5.1 Specialized Micro-Units of Hadoti
1. **Mukundara Hills (मुकुंदरा की पहाड़ियाँ)**:
   - S-shaped crescentic ridgelines extending from **Kota to Jhalawar**; slopes drain into the Ahu and Kali Sindh rivers.
   - Highest peak: **Chandwadi (चाँदवाड़ी — 517 m)** in Jhalawar.
2. **Shahbad Upland (शाहबाद उच्च भूमि — Baran)**:
   - Eastern highland elevation up to 450 meters; highest peak: **Kastoorba (456 m)**.
   - **Ramgarh Crater (रामगढ़ क्रेटर)**: A circular, horseshoe-shaped geological depression formed by a **meteorite impact** millions of years ago; declared the 106th National Geological Monument and India's 3rd recognized meteorite impact crater (after Lonar in Maharashtra and Dhala in MP).
3. **Dag-Gangdhar Upland (डग-गंगधार उच्च भूमि)**:
   - Low, rolling basaltic plateau region situated in southwestern **Jhalawar district**.

## 7. The 25 Deadly Prelims Traps: Morphotectonic Divisions

1. **Gondwanaland vs Tethys Sea Origins**:
   - Remnants of **Gondwanaland**: **Aravalli Range** and **Hadoti Plateau**.
   - Remnants of **Tethys Sea**: **Western Sandy Plain** and **Eastern Alluvial Plain**.
2. **The Isohyet Boundary Numbers**:
   - **50 cm Isohyet**: Separates the Western Sandy Plain from the rest of Rajasthan.
   - **25 cm Isohyet**: Bisects the Thar Desert into Pure Arid Marusthali and Semi-Arid Bagar.
3. **The Most Widespread Sand Dune**: **Parabolic Dunes** are the most widespread in Rajasthan (present across all desert districts), NOT Barkhans!
4. **The Most Dangerous Dune for Desertification**: **Barkhan (Barchan)** dunes; highly mobile, crescent-shaped, fast-moving.
5. **Dune-Covered vs Dune-Free Tract Percentages**:
   - Dune-Covered Tract: **58.5%**.
   - Dune-Free Tract (*Hamada*): **41.5%** (Pokhran, Ramgarh, Phalodi).
6. **Akal Wood Fossil Park Epoch**: **Jurassic Period (180 million years old)**, NOT Cretaceous or Tertiary.
7. **Lathi Series Water & Grass**: Groundwater aquifer between Pokhran and Mohangarh; supports sweet-water **Chandan Tube Well** and protein-rich **Sewan grass (*Lasiurus scindicus*)**.
8. **Chhappan ka Maidan vs Chhappan ki Pahadiyan (Top Trap!)**:
   - **Chhappan ka Maidan**: Alluvial plain of 56 streams between **Banswara and Pratapgarh** (Mahi Basin).
   - **Chhappan ki Pahadiyan**: Fifty-six granitic round hills located in **Siwana (Balotra)**.
9. **Kobar Patti (Banka Patti) Geography**: A fluoride groundwater belt stretching between **Nagaur/Didwana and Ajmer**, causing fluorosis.
10. **Aravalli Length: Total vs Rajasthan**:
    - Total length: **692 km** (Palanpur, Gujarat to Raisina Hill, Delhi).
    - Length within Rajasthan: **550 km** (approx. **79.6% ~ 80%** of total length).
11. **Highest Peak in North Aravalli**: **Raghunathgarh (1,055 m)** in Sikar, NOT Kho or Bairat.
12. **Highest Peak in Central Aravalli**: **Goramji (934 m)** in Beawar, followed closely by **Moramji / Todgarh (933 m)** and Taragarh (870 m).
13. **Guru Shikhar's Elevation Distinction**: **1,722 meters** (1,727 meters if the Dattatreya temple on the summit is included). Tod called it *"Santoka Shikhar"*.
14. **Highest Plateau in Rajasthan**: **Oriya Plateau (1,360 m)** in Mount Abu, NOT Bhorat.
15. **Bhorat Plateau Boundaries**: Stretches between **Kumbhalgarh (Rajsamand)** and **Gogunda (Udaipur)**, average elevation 1,225 m.
16. **Lashadiya Plateau Updated District**: Now located in **Salumbar district** (formerly Udaipur), situated east of Jaisamand Lake.
17. **Desh-Haro Location**: The perennially green valley between **Jarga and Raga hills** in Udaipur.
18. **Bhakar Topography**: Extremely steep, sharp-edged jagged hills situated in **western Sirohi**.
19. **Girwa Topography**: Saucer-shaped, amphitheater-like ring of hills encircling **Udaipur city**.
20. **Utkhat Bhumi (Badland) Epicenter**: Caused by Chambal’s gully erosion in **Dholpur, Karauli, and Sawai Madhopur**.
21. **"Queen of Dang" (डांग की रानी)**: **Karauli district** is officially called Dang ki Rani.
22. **Kanthal Region**: The narrow shore plain along the Mahi river in **Pratapgarh district**.
23. **Ramgarh Meteorite Crater**: Located in **Baran district** (Shahbad Upland); recognized as India's 3rd meteorite impact crater.
24. **Mukundara Hills Peak**: Highest peak is **Chandwadi (517 m)** in Jhalawar.
25. **Great Boundary Fault (GBF) Alignment**: Fault line separating Aravallis and Vindhyans; runs from **Chittorgarh to Dholpur**.

## 8. RPSC RAS Mains High-Yield Model Answers

### 8.1 2-Markers (15–20 Words)

#### Q1: What is the "Lathi Series" in the Thar Desert?
**Answer**:
A 60-km subterranean paleo-aquifer stretching from Pokhran to Mohangarh (Jaisalmer) underlain by sweet groundwater, famous for Chandan Tube Well and Sewan grasslands.

---

#### Q2: Distinguish between Barkhan and Seif sand dunes.
**Answer**:
Barkhans are mobile crescent-shaped dunes formed perpendicular to wind with downwind horns; Seifs are elongated longitudinal ridges formed parallel to wind directions.

---

#### Q3: What is the Bhorat Plateau? State its geographical boundary.
**Answer**:
A high Aravalli plateau (1,225 m) serving as a continental water divide, situated between **Kumbhalgarh (Rajsamand)** in the north and **Gogunda (Udaipur)** in the south.

---

#### Q4: What is the "Kobar Patti" of Rajasthan?
**Answer**:
A subterranean geological belt between Nagaur and Ajmer containing hazardous fluoride concentrations, causing widespread skeletal and dental fluorosis (*Banka Patti*).

---

#### Q5: State the geological significance of the Great Boundary Fault (GBF).
**Answer**:
An 800-km regional reverse fault running from Chittorgarh to Dholpur, marking the structural boundary between the folded Pre-Cambrian Aravallis and the Vindhyan sedimentary plateau.

---

### 8.2 5-Markers (50 Words)

#### Q6: Explain the morphotectonic characteristics of the South-Eastern Hadoti Plateau.
**Answer**:
Covering 6.89% of Rajasthan across Kota, Baran, Bundi, and Jhalawar, the Hadoti Plateau forms the northwestern edge of the Deccan Traps:
1. **Lithology**: Underlain by Cretaceous basaltic lava sheets and sandstone scarps of the Vindhyan Supergroup, weathering into fertile black **Vertisols (Regur)**.
2. **Relief Features**:
   - *Vindhyan Scarpland*: Massive sandstone escarpments facing the Chambal river.
   - *Mukundara Hills*: S-shaped crescentic ridges (highest peak: Chandwadi 517 m).
   - *Shahbad Upland*: Houses the horseshoe-shaped **Ramgarh Meteorite Crater**.

---

#### Q7: Differentiate between Chhappan ka Maidan and Chhappan ki Pahadiyan.
**Answer**:
Despite phonetic similarity, they represent completely different geographic divisions, rock systems, and locations:
1. **Chhappan ka Maidan (Mahi Basin)**: A fertile alluvial plain located in southern Rajasthan between **Banswara and Pratapgarh**, formed by the drainage network of **56 streams and 56 villages** of the Mahi river.
2. **Chhappan ki Pahadiyan (Luni Basin)**: A cluster of fifty-six round, dome-shaped granitic hills (*Inselbergs*) located around **Siwana (Balotra district)** in the arid Thar, housing Nakoda Parvat and Haldeshwar Mahadev.

---

#### Q8: Write a concise note on the physical divisions and significance of the Eastern Plains.
**Answer**:
Formed by Pleistocene alluvium and occupying 23% of Rajasthan’s area, the Eastern Plains support 39% of the state's population (highest density):
1. **Sub-Basins**:
   - *Chambal Basin*: Characterized by intense gully erosion forming deep ravines (**Utkhat Bhumi / Badlands**) across Dholpur and Karauli.
   - *Banas-Banganga Plain*: Vast fertile *Piedmont* plain divided into Mewar and Malpura-Karauli plains.
   - *Mahi Basin*: Southern alluvial tract encompassing the *Chappan Plain* and *Kanthal*.
2. **Economic Role**: Represents the agricultural heartland of the state, yielding intensive wheat, mustard, and gram harvests under Alfisols.
