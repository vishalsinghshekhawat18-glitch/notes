## 2.1 The Gurjara-Pratiharas: Guardians of the Western Frontier

From the 6th to the 11th century CE, the Gurjara-Pratiharas established political hegemony over northern and western India. Historian **R.C. Majumdar** noted that their historical significance lies in serving as an unyielding defensive barrier (*प्रतिहार — Door-keeper / Protector*) that halted Arab-Islamic invasions from Sindh for more than three centuries.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        DYNASTIC GENESIS & DUAL BRANCH ARCHITECTURE                     │
├───────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. MANDORE (JODHPUR) BRANCH           │ 2. BHINMAL (JALORE) - KANNAUJ BRANCH           │
├───────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Founder: Harishchandra (Rohilliddhi)│ • Founder: Nagabhata I (c. 730–756 CE)         │
│ • Oldest branch in Rajasthan          │ • Capital: Bhinmal ➔ Ujjain ➔ Kannauj          │
│ • Notable Rulers: Rajjil, Nagabhata I,│ • Notable Rulers: Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II,     │
│   Bauka (837 CE), Kakkuka (861 CE)    │   Mihir Bhoja (Greatest), Mahendrapala I       │

└───────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Origin Theories & Historical Nomenclature
* **Meaning of "Pratihara"**: The term literally signifies *door-keeper*. The dynasty traced its epic lineage to **Lakshmana**, the younger brother of Lord Rama, who acted as a faithful door-keeper and protector.
* **The "Gurjara" Epithet**: Refers primarily to the territorial homeland **Gurjaratra** (गुर्जरात्रा भूमि — the Marwar-Didwana region of western Rajasthan).
* **Literary References**:
  * Banabhatta's *Harshacharita* mentions the king of Gurjara.
  * Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)** visited Bhinmal in 641 CE, referring to the kingdom as **Ku-che-lo** (*Gurjara*) and its capital Bhinmal as **Pi-lo-mo-lo** (*Bhillamala*).
  * Arab travelers (Sulaiman, Al-Masudi) referred to the kingdom as **Al-Juzr** (अल-जुज्र) and its king as **Baura** (बौरा, a corruption of *Pratihara* or *Adivaraha*).

---

### 2. The Mandore Branch of Gurjara-Pratiharas (Oldest Branch)
* **Founder**: **Harishchandra** (titled *Rohilliddhi* — skilled in Vedic learning and warfare) in the 6th century CE.
* **Dual Lineage**: Harishchandra had two wives:
  1. A Brahmana wife, whose offspring were known as *Pratihara Brahmanas*.
  2. A Kshatriya wife named **Bhadra** (रानी भद्रा), whose four sons—**Bhogabhata**, **Kakka**, **Rajjil**, and **Dadda**—conquered Mandore and founded the royal ruling line.
* **Rajjil**: Established Mandore as the fortified dynastic seat; performed the *Rajasuya Yajna*.
* **Nagabhata I (Mandore Branch)**: Transferred the royal headquarters temporarily to **Merta** (Medantaka).
* **Bauka (बाउक)**: Inscribed the **Mandore Inscription of 837 CE** on the ramparts of Mandore fort, which supplies the earliest complete genealogy of the dynasty from Harishchandra.
* **Kakkuka (कक्कुक)**:
  * Inscribed the famous **Ghatiyala Inscription of 861 CE** (near Jodhpur).
  * Eradicated the lawless **Abhira** plunderers who had terrorized caravan routes; established a thriving public commercial mart (*हट्ट*) with free shops to rehabilitate Jain and Hindu merchants.
  * Constructed the *Jayastambha* (pillar of victory) at Ghatiyala and founded the temple of Mahavira.
* **End of Mandore Branch**: In 1395 CE, the last Pratihara ruler of Mandore, **Inda Pratihara**, ceded Mandore in dowry to **Rao Chunda Rathore** to forge a joint defense against the Tughlaq forces of Delhi.

---

### 3. The Bhinmal-Kannauj Imperial Branch

#### A. Nagabhata I (c. 730–756 CE) — The Imperial Founder
* **Establishment**: Founded the imperial branch with **Bhinmal** (Jalore) as his initial capital; later expanded to Avanti (Ujjain).
* **The Defeat of Arab Invasions (Mlechchhas)**:
  * When the Umayyad Arab armies under Junaid and Tamin conquered Sindh and advanced across Gujarat and Rajputana, Nagabhata I organized a confederacy of Rajput chieftains and decisively crushed the Arab forces.
  * The **Gwalior Prashasti of Mihir Bhoja** praises him as **Narayan** incarnate, delivering the earth from the oppressive *Mlechchhas* (Arabs).
  * Conferred the title **Naga-Avaloka** (नागावलोक).

#### B. Vatsaraja (c. 775–800 CE) — Genesis of the Tripartite Struggle
* **Titles**: Titled **Ranahastin** (रणहस्तिन — The Elephant of the Battlefield) and **Jaya-Varaha**.
* **The Tripartite Struggle (त्रिपक्षीय संघर्ष)**:
  * Initiated the historic 200-year conflict between the **Pratiharas of Rajasthan**, the **Palas of Bengal**, and the **Rashtrakutas of the Deccan** for control over **Kannauj** (the imperial symbol of northern Indian sovereignty).
  * Vatsaraja marched east, defeated the Pala king **Dharmapala** in the Doab, and captured the two white royal umbrellas of Bengal.
  * However, he was subsequently ambushed and defeated by Rashtrakuta king **Dhruva I** in central India, forcing him to retreat to the deserts of Marwar.
* **Cultural & Literary Splendor**:
  * Jain scholar **Udyotana Suri** authored the famous Prakrit epic **Kuvalayamala** (कुवलयमाला) at Jalore in 778 CE during Vatsaraja's reign (which provides the earliest textual reference to *Maru-Bhasha* / Rajasthani language).
  * Acharya **Jinasena** completed the *Harivamsa Purana* (783 CE) under Vatsaraja's patronage.
  * Constructed the magnificent Sun Temple and Mahavira Temple at **Osian** (Jodhpur).

#### C. Nagabhata II (c. 800–833 CE) — Imperial Ascendancy
* Defeated Pala king **Dharmapala** at the historic **Battle of Munger** (Bihar).
* Captured Kannauj and made it the **permanent imperial capital** of the Gurjara-Pratihara Empire.
* Assumed imperial sovereign titles: **Paramabhattaraka**, **Maharajadhiraja**, and **Parameshvara**.
* Rebuilt the sacred **Somnath Temple** in Gujarat using red sandstone after it had been desecrated by Arab sea raiders.
* According to the *Prabhavakacharita*, he voluntarily ended his life by taking *Jala-Samadhi* in the sacred Ganga river in 833 CE.

#### D. Mihir Bhoja (c. 836–885 CE) — The Golden Zenith
* **Imperial Stature**: The **greatest and most powerful ruler** of the Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty.
* **Titles**:
  1. **Adivaraha** (आदिवराह) — Proclaimed on his silver *Dramma* coins and the Gwalior Prashasti, denoting his devotion to Lord Vishnu.
  2. **Prabhasa** (प्रभास) — Inscribed on his Daulatpura copper plate.
* **Military Campaigns**:
  * Crushed Pala ruler **Devapala** and re-established undisputed authority over Bengal and Bihar.
  * Contained the Rashtrakuta king **Krishna II** south of the Narmada river.
* **Arab Chronicles of Sulaiman (851 CE)**:
  * Arab merchant **Sulaiman** visited India during Bhoja’s reign and recorded that the king of *Jurz* (Gurjara) possessed the finest cavalry in India, maintained absolute internal security, and was the **greatest enemy of the Arab/Muslim faith** (*"He has a great hatred for Islam and is the greatest barrier to our advance"*).
* **The Gwalior Prashasti**: Authored during his reign by Baladitya; provides the definitive poetic genealogy and military conquests of the dynasty.

#### E. Mahendrapala I (c. 885–910 CE) & Royal Court Laureate Rajasekhara
* **Imperial Titles**: Conferred the titles *Maharajadhiraja*, *Nirbhayaraja*, *Nirbhayanarendra*, and *Raghukula Tilak*.
* **Court Poet Laureate: Rajasekhara (राजशेखर)**:
  * The celebrated Sanskrit dramatist and literary theorist was the court poet and preceptor (*Guru*) of Mahendrapala I and his son Mahipala.
  * **Master Literary Works of Rajasekhara**:
    1. **Karpuramanjari** (कर्पूरमंजरी) — Written entirely in Sauraseni Prakrit at the request of his scholarly Kshatriya wife, Avantisundari.
    2. **Kavyamimamsa** (काव्यमीमांसा) — Practical handbook of poetic theory and geography of ancient India.
    3. **Viddhasalabhanjika** (विद्धशालभंजिका) — Four-act drama.
    4. **Balaramayana** (बालरामायण) — Ten-act epic drama.
    5. **Balabharata / Prachandapandava** (बालभारत).
    6. **Bhuvanakosha** (भुवनकोश) & *Harakeli* references.

#### F. Mahipala I (c. 912–944 CE) & Decline of the Empire
* Arab geographer **Al-Masudi** visited Kannauj in 915–916 CE; praised the vastness of the empire, its four massive armies facing the four directions, and referred to the ruler as *Baura*.
* In 916 CE, Rashtrakuta king **Indra III** invaded northern India, sacked and burned Kannauj, inflicting a mortal blow on Pratihara imperial prestige.
* **Final Fall (1018 CE)**: Mahmud of Ghazni attacked Kannauj; the weak Pratihara king **Rajyapala** fled without a fight. The last nominal ruler was **Yashapala** (c. 1036 CE), after which the Gahadavalas seized Kannauj.

---

### 4. Gurjara-Pratihara Art & Architecture: The Maha-Maru Style (महा-मारू शैली)
* **Temple Architecture**: The Gurjara-Pratiharas developed the indigenous **Maha-Maru style** (7th to 10th century CE), characterized by high carved plinths (*Jagati*), cruciform plan, intricate sculpted outer niches (*Rathikas*), and curvilinear spires (*Rekha-Shikhara*).
* **Key Masterpieces in Rajasthan**:
  * **Osian Temple Complex (Jodhpur)**: The "Khajuraho of Marwar" — Harihara Temples, Surya Temple, Sachiya Mata Temple, and the early Mahavira Jain Temple.
  * **Kiradu Temples (Barmer)**: Someshwara Temple (11th century climax of Solanki-Maru synthesis).
  * **Harshat Mata Temple (Abhaneri, Dausa)**: 8th-century tiered masterpiece located next to Chand Baori.
  * **Kumbhashyam / Kalika Mata Temple (Chittorgarh)**: Originally constructed as a Sun temple in the 8th century by Pratihara architects.
  * **Ghatiyala Pillar & Shrine (Jodhpur)**: 9th-century Kakkuka architectural complex.

## 2.2 The Chauhans of Shakambhari & Ajmer

The Chauhans (Chahamanas) rose from imperial feudatories of the Gurjara-Pratiharas to become the sovereign rulers of north-western India and defenders of Delhi against the Ghurid invasions.

### 1. Foundational Epoch: Shakambhari (Sambhar)
* **Vasudeva Chauhan (551 CE)**:
  * The **historical founder (आदि पुरुष / मूल पुरुष)** of the Chauhan dynasty.
  * According to the **Bijolia Inscription (1170 CE)**, he founded the kingdom around **Shakambhari** (Sambhar Lake) in Vikram Samvat 608 (551 CE) and constructed the salt lake.
  * Initial capital: **Ahichchhatrapura** (modern Nagaur).
  * Established the shrine of family tutelary deity **Shakambhari Mata** at Sambhar.
* **Guvaka I (c. 820 CE)**: Feudatory of Pratihara Nagabhata II; constructed the famous **Harshnath Temple** in Sikar. His sister Kalavati was married to King Kanyakubja.
* **Guvaka II & Chandraraja**: Chandraraja's queen was **Rudrani** (titled *Atmaprabha*), an ardent Shaivite yogini who famously lit **1,000 lamps daily** in worship of Lord Shiva at Pushkar lake.
* **Vakpatiraja I & Simharaja (c. 956 CE)**: Simharaja decisively threw off the Gurjara-Pratihara suzerainty, took the title **Maharajadhiraja**, and established the Chauhans as fully sovereign rulers.

---

### 2. The Era of Ajmer: Ajayraja to Vigraharaja IV

#### A. Ajayraja II (1113 CE) — Foundation of Ajmer
* **Foundation of Ajaymeru**: In **1113 CE**, Ajayraja founded the city of **Ajmer** (Ajaymeru) and built the strategic hill fortress **Ajaymeru Durg** (later known as **Taragarh** / *Garh Beetli*).
* **Numismatic Issue**: Issued silver and copper coins bearing the legend **Sri Ajayadeva** (श्री अजयदेव); his queen **Somaladevi** (सोमलवती) also issued coins in her own name, a unique milestone in Rajasthani numismatics.
* Crushed the invading Turushkas (Ghaznavid Turks) near Pushkar.

#### B. Arnoraja / Ana Ji (c. 1133–1153 CE)
* Defeated the Ghaznavid Turkish invaders in a bloodbath; to purify the blood-stained land, he diverted the Chandra river and constructed the sacred **Ana Sagar Lake (आना सागर झील)** at Ajmer.
* Built the historic **Varaha Temple** at Pushkar.
* Court scholars: **Devabodha** and **Dharmaghosha**.
* Murdered by his own son, **Jagaddeva** (the first patricide in Chauhan history).

#### C. Vigraharaja IV / Bisaldeo (1153–1163 CE) — The Golden Age
Historian **Dr. Dasharatha Sharma** described the reign of Vigraharaja IV as the **"Golden Age of the Chauhans of Ajmer"**.

* **Literary Accomplishments**:
  * Vigraharaja was an accomplished Sanskrit poet; he authored the monumental Sanskrit drama **Harkeli** (हरकेलि नाटक), based on Bharavi’s *Kiratarjuniya* (dialogue between Arjuna and Lord Shiva). Portions of this drama were engraved on the stone slabs of his Sanskrit College.
  * His court poet **Somadeva** authored the Sanskrit romantic drama **Lalita Vigraharaja** (ललित विग्रहराज), describing Vigraharaja’s love for Princess Deshaldevi of Indrapuri.
* **The Sanskrit College & Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra**:
  * Vigraharaja constructed the premier academy **Saraswati Kanthabharana Sanskrit College** in Ajmer.
  * In **1194–1199 CE**, Qutb-ud-din Aibak demolished the college and, using its original pillars, converted it into a mosque known as **Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra** (ढाई दिन का झोंपड़ा).
  * *Percy Brown* noted that it was converted in two and a half days; John Marshall identified that the name stems from the 2.5-day annual Urs of Sufi saint **Punjab Shah**.
  * Slabs of Vigraharaja's *Harkeli* drama are still embedded in the walls of this structure (and on Raja Ram Mohan Roy’s tomb in Bristol, UK, where identical verses were inscribed).

### 3. Prithviraja III (c. 1177–1192 CE) — The Last Hindu Emperor

```text
[BORN: 1166 CE at Patan (Gujarat) | FATHER: Someshwara | MOTHER: Karpuri Devi | ACCESSION: 1177 CE (Age 11)]
```

#### 1. Regencies & Suppression of Internal Rivals
* **The Regency of Queen Mother Karpuri Devi**: Prithviraja ascended the throne at age 11. His mother **Karpuri Devi** (daughter of Kalachuri king Tejala) acted as regent, assisted by Prime Minister **Kadambavasa** (Kaimasa) and Commander-in-Chief **Bhuvanamalla**.
* **Suppression of Nagarjuna**: Crushed the rebellion of his cousin Nagarjuna (son of Vigraharaja IV) at Gurgaon.
* **The Bhadanaka Conquest (1182 CE)**: Extirpated the turbulent **Bhadanaka** tribe in the Bharatpur-Mathura region.

#### 2. The Battle of Mahoba (1182 CE)
* Clashed with the Chandela king **Paramardideva** of Jejakabhukti (Bundelkhand).
* Prithviraja’s commander invaded Mahoba; the legendary Chandela warrior brothers **Alha and Udal** (आल्हा एवं ऊदल) fought heroically to the death.
* Prithviraja captured Mahoba and appointed **Panjun Rai** as governor.

#### 3. Imperial Rivalry with Jayachandra of Kannauj & The Sanyogita Episode
* The Gahadavala ruler of Kannauj, **Jayachandra**, sought imperial supremacy (*Rajsuya Yajna*).
* **Sanyogita Swayamvara**: According to Chand Bardai's *Prithviraj Raso* and Abul Fazl's *Ain-i-Akbari*, Prithviraja abducted Jayachandra’s daughter **Sanyogita** from her swayamvara in Kannauj after Jayachandra placed a statue of Prithviraja as a doorkeeper, igniting permanent enmity between the two premier Hindu monarchs.

#### 4. The Battles of Tarain (1191 & 1192 CE)

* **First Battle of Tarain (1191 CE)**:
  * Sparked by Ghori's capture of the frontier fort of **Tabarhindah** (Bhatinda).
  * Prithviraja marched with a massive army; **Govind Rai**, the ruler of Delhi, shattered Ghori’s teeth with a lance; a young Khalji soldier carried the bleeding Ghori from the field; the fleeing Ghurid army was routed.
  * **Critical Strategic Error**: Prithviraja besieged Bhatinda for 13 months instead of pursuing and annihilating the fleeing invader in the Punjab.
* **Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE)**:
  * Ghori returned with 120,000 elite horse archers.
  * **Tactical Feint**: Ghori signed a temporary truce, then launched a pre-dawn surprise attack while Rajput forces were performing morning ablutions.
  * Ghori divided his cavalry into four squadrons of 10,000 mounted archers, employing feigned retreats (*टुलघुमा जैसी चाल*) to draw the disciplined Rajput center into disarray.
  * Prithviraja was captured near **Sirsa** (Saraswati river) and executed.
  * *Hasan Nizami's Taj-ul-Ma'asir* records that Prithviraja ruled as a vassal for a brief period (confirmed by joint coins bearing *Prithviraja* on one side and *Sri Muhammad Sam* on the other) before being executed for conspiracy.
  * *Chand Bardai’s Raso Account*: Popular legend recounts that Prithviraja was blinded, taken to Ghazni, and killed Ghori using his sound-aiming archery skill (*शब्धभेदी बाण*) upon the couplet:  
    > *"चार बांस चौबीस गज, अंगुल अष्ट प्रमाण। ता ऊपर सुल्तान है, मत चूको चौहान॥"*  
    *(Modern historians dismiss this, as Ghori died in 1206 CE at Damyak on the Indus).*

#### 5. Court Laureates & Intellectual Circle of Prithviraja III
* **Jayanaka (जयानक)**: Kashmiri court scholar who authored **Prithviraja Vijaya** (पृथ्वीराज विजय) in Sanskrit, the most historically accurate biographical text on Prithviraja.
* **Chand Bardai (चंदबरदाई)**: Court bard and close companion who authored **Prithviraj Raso** (पृथ्वीराज रासो) in Pingal/Braj (completed by his son **Jalhan**).
* **Vidyapati Gauda**, **Vagishwara Janardana**, **Vishvarupa**, and **Prithvibhata**.

## 2.3 The Chauhans of Ranthambore (1194–1301 CE)

```text
[FOUNDER: Govindaraja (1194 CE) | CAPITAL: Ranthambore Fort | GREATEST RULER: Hammiradeva (1282–1301 CE)]
```

### 1. Dynastic Foundations & Succession
* **Govindaraja (1194 CE)**: Son of Prithviraja III; after Qutb-ud-din Aibak captured Ajmer, Govindaraja was granted the feudatory fief of **Ranthambore**, where he founded the Ranthambore Chauhan branch.
* **Valhana, Prahladana, & Viranarayana**: Defended Ranthambore against early Iltutmish sieges.
* **Vagbhata (वाग्भट)**: Recaptured Ranthambore from Razia Sultan's forces; built Baharwand and established stable defenses.
* **Jaitrasimha (जयसिंह)**: Ruled for 32 years; abdicated in favor of his third son, Hammiradeva.

---

### 2. Hammiradeva Chauhan (1282–1301 CE) — "The Inflexible Sovereign"
Hammir is immortalized in Rajasthani bardic history for his unbending chivalric obstinacy (*हम्मीर हठ*):
> *"सिंह सवन, सत्पुरुष वचन, कदली फलत इक बार। तिरिया तेल, हम्मीर हठ, चढ़ै न दूजी बार॥"*

#### A. Digvijaya Policy & Imperial Splendor
* Ascended the throne in 1282 CE; launched a *Digvijaya* campaign, extracting tributes from Arjun of Bhimarasa, the Paramaras of Malwa, and Bhoja II; performed the Vedic **Koti-Yajna** (कोटियज्ञ) under royal priest Vishwarupa.
* Constructed the magnificent **32-Pillared Chhatri** (32 खंभों की छतरी — *न्याय की छतरी*) at Ranthambore in memory of his father Jaitrasimha’s 32-year reign.
* Constructed the **Padmala Pond** (पद्मला तालाब) inside Ranthambore Fort.

#### B. Defeat of Jalaluddin Khalji (1290–1292 CE)
* In 1290 and 1292 CE, Sultan **Jalaluddin Khalji** invaded Ranthambore. He captured the outer key-fort **Jhain** (झाईन दुर्ग — "रंथंभौर की कुंजी"), renaming it *Shahr-i-Nau*.
* However, Jalaluddin failed to breach Ranthambore's impregnable citadel and retreated, rationalizing:  
  > *"I do not consider ten such forts worth a single hair on the head of a Muslim."*

#### C. The Catalyst for Alauddin Khalji's Invasion (1299–1301 CE)
* **The Rebel Mongol Refugees**: In 1299 CE, after the Gujarat plunder, Mongol neo-Muslim commanders **Muhammad Shah** (महमूद शाह) and **Kabhru** mutinied against Alauddin's generals Ulugh Khan and Nusrat Khan over the division of booty (*Khums*).
* Hammir granted unconditional political asylum (*शरणागत की रक्षा*) to Muhammad Shah and his family inside Ranthambore, refusing Alauddin's ultimatum to surrender them.

#### D. The Siege of Ranthambore & The First Jauhar of Rajasthan (1301 CE)
* **Initial Campaign (1299 CE)**: Alauddin sent Ulugh Khan and **Nusrat Khan**. At the pass of Jhain, Chauhan generals **Bhim Singh** and **Dharma Singh** intercepted the Delhi army. Nusrat Khan was struck and killed by a missile from a siege catapult (*मग़रबी*). Bhim Singh was subsequently ambushed and killed.
* **Direct Imperial Assault by Alauddin (1301 CE)**: Alauddin personally marched to Ranthambore and besieged the fort for months without success.
* **Treachery of Ranmal & Ratipala**: Alauddin invited Hammir's prime minister **Ranmal** (रणमल) and general **Ratipala** (रतिपाल) for peace parleys, suborning them with the promise of the fort's kingship. Ranmal poisoned the fort’s water cisterns with cow carcasses and diverted food convoys.
* **The Historic Jauhar (11 July 1301 CE)**:
  * Faced with starvation, the Rajputs decided on the final *Sāka*.
  * Queen **Ranga Devi** (रंगा देवी) and Hammir's daughter **Devalde / Padmala** led the women in the **First Jauhar of Rajasthan** (classified by chronicles as **Jal Jauhar** / water self-immolation in Padmala pond).
  * The Rajput warriors, clad in saffron robes (*केसरिया*), opened the gates; Hammir fought furiously and fell in battle alongside the loyal Mongol rebel Muhammad Shah.
* **Amir Khusrau's Chronicle**: The contemporary court chronicler **Amir Khusrau**, who accompanied Alauddin, recorded in his *Khazain-ul-Futuh*:  
  > *"The fortress of infidelity became the home of Islam."* (*"कुफ्र का गढ़ इस्लाम का घर हो गया"*).
* **Fate of the Traitors**: Alauddin immediately executed Ranmal and Ratipala, declaring: *"Those who were disloyal to their own benevolent master can never be loyal to me."*

#### E. Literary Canons on Hammir
1. **Hammira Mahakavya** (हम्मीर महाकाव्य) — By Jain scholar **Nayachandra Suri**.
2. **Hammira Raso** (हम्मीर रासो) — By **Jodharaja** (18th century) and **Sarngadhara** (14th century).
3. **Hammira Hatth** (हम्मीर हठ) — By **Chandrashekhara**.
4. **Hammira Bandhana** (हम्मीर बंधन) — By **Amritkailash**.

## 2.4 The Chauhans of Jalore & Siwana (Songara Branch)

```text
[FOUNDER: Kirtipala (1181 CE) | CANONICAL RULER: Kanhadadeva Chauhan (1305–1311 CE) | FORT: Suvarnagiri (Jalore)]
```

### 1. Origins of the Songara Chauhans
* Founded in **1181 CE** by **Kirtipala Chauhan** (son of Alhana of Nadol).
* The chronicler Muhnot Nainsi praised him as **"कीतू एक महान राजपूत"** (*Kitu, a Great Rajput*).
* Seat of power: **Jalore Fort** (built on *Suvarnagiri* hill by Pratihara Nagabhata I along the Sukri river); hence the branch was known as **Songara Chauhans** (सोनगरा चौहान).

---

### 2. The Siege of Siwana (1308 CE) — The Key to Jalore
* **Fort of Siwana**: Located on the Chhappan hills (Balotra district); built in the 10th century by **Veer Narayan Panwar**; known as **"जालौर दुर्ग की कुंजी"** (The Key to Jalore) and refuge fort of Marwar kings.
* **The Defense**: Ruled by Kanhadadeva’s brave nephew, **Sataldeo** (सातळदेव) and **Som**.
* **The Treachery of Bhayla**: Alauddin’s army under **Kamal-ud-din Gurg** besieged the fort. The traitor **Bhayla Pawar** (भायला पंवार) contaminated the *Bhandelao pond* (भांडेलाव तालाब) with cow blood.
* **The Sāka of 1308**: Sataldeo and Som fell fighting after performing *Kesariya*; Queen **Maina Devi** led the *Jauhar*. Alauddin renamed Siwana as **Khairabad** (खैराबाद).

---

### 3. The Epic Resistance of Kanhadadeva Chauhan (1305–1311 CE)

#### A. Genesis of the Conflict
1. **Refusal of Passage (1299 CE)**: When Alauddin dispatched his army under Ulugh Khan to plunder the Somnath temple in Gujarat, Kanhadadeva refused permission to cross Jalore territory, forcing the imperial army to take an alternate route.
2. **Rescue of the Somnath Shivalinga**: During the imperial army’s return from Gujarat, Kanhadadeva’s commanders intercepted the Delhi army at Sakrana, rescued the fragments of the Somnath Shivalinga, and consecrated them in five shrines across Marwar.
3. **The Viramdeva-Feroza Episode**: Padmanabha’s *Kanhadade Prabandha* records that Alauddin's daughter **Princess Feroza** fell in love with Kanhadadeva's handsome son, **Kunwar Viramdeva**, who was stationed at the Delhi court. When Alauddin proposed marriage, Viramdeva refused to marry a Muslim princess, declaring:  
   > *"मामो लाजै भाटियां, कुल लाजै चहुआण। जे मैं परणू तुरकणी, तो पश्चिम ऊगै भाण॥"*  
   Viramdeva fled Delhi secretly, prompting Alauddin to dispatch the imperial army to destroy Jalore.

#### B. The Final Siege & Sāka of Jalore (1311 CE)
* Alauddin dispatched an imperial army under general **Kamal-ud-din Gurg**.
* **Treachery of Bika Dahiya**: Unable to breach the massive walls, the Turks bribed a Rajput soldier named **Bika Dahiya** (बीका दहिया), who revealed a secret unfortified section where the wall was built with loose earth and mud.
* **The "Rai" Market Episode**: The Turks bought massive quantities of mustard (*राई*) at exorbitant rates overnight to identify the weak earthen wall section through dampness; hence the famous Rajasthani proverb:  
  > *"राई रा भाव तो रात ही बिका"* (*The rates of mustard expired with the night*).
* Bika Dahiya’s patriotic wife, **Hira Devi**, killed her traitorous husband upon discovering his betrayal and rushed to inform Kanhadadeva, but the Turkish army had already entered.
* **The Sāka of 1311 CE**: Kanhadadeva and Viramdeva died sword in hand; Queen **Jethan Devi** led the mass Jauhar.
* Alauddin seized the fort, slaughtered the inhabitants, constructed the **Alai Mosque / Topkhana Mosque** inside Jalore fort, and renamed the city **Jabalipura / Jalalabad** (जलालाबाद).

#### C. Literary Chroniclers
* **Kanhadade Prabandha** (कान्हड़दे प्रबंध) — Composed in Old Rajasthani / Gujarati by **Padmanabha** (1455 CE) under the patronage of Akheraj Songara; celebrated as the ultimate historical epic on the Jalore resistance.
* **Viram Feroza ri Baat** (वीरम फिरोजा री बात) — Also authored by Padmanabha.

## 2.6 Top 25 Deadliest Examination Traps & Examiner Pitfalls

```text

```

1. **The Pratihara Founder Distinction**: Do not confuse the founder of the **Mandore Branch** (**Harishchandra**, 6th century) with the founder of the imperial **Bhinmal-Kannauj Branch** (**Nagabhata I**, c. 730 CE).
2. **Arab Traveler Coordinates**: **Sulaiman** visited during the reign of **Mihir Bhoja** (851 CE), whereas **Al-Masudi** visited during the reign of **Mahipala I** (915 CE).
3. **The Kuvalayamala Date & Location**: Composed by Udyotana Suri in **778 CE at Jalore** during the reign of **Vatsaraja Pratihara**, NOT Mihir Bhoja.
4. **The Patricide in Chauhan Dynasty**: The first patricide in Chauhan history was **Jagaddeva** (who murdered his father Arnoraja), NOT Prithviraja III.
5. **The Saraswati Kanthabharana Founders**:
   * Saraswati Kanthabharana School at Ajmer was built by **Vigraharaja IV**.
   * Saraswati Kanthabharana treatise on grammar was written by Paramara king **Bhoja of Dhar**.
6. **The Inscription of Delhi Conquest**: The capture of Delhi from the Tomaras by Vigraharaja IV is epigraphically documented in the **Bijolia Inscription (1170 CE)** and Delhi-Topra Pillar.
7. **The Two Authors of Kirti Stambha vs. Hammira Mahakavya**:
   * *Kirti Stambha Prashasti*: Atri and Mahesh.
   * *Hammira Mahakavya*: Nayachandra Suri.
   * *Prithviraja Vijaya*: Jayanaka (Kashmiri scholar).
8. **The Title "Vishama Ghati Panchanan"**: Belongs to **Rana Hammir of Mewar** (1326 CE), NOT Hammiradeva Chauhan of Ranthambore (1301 CE).
9. **Tarain First Battle Tactical Leader**: In 1191 CE, the commander who struck and wounded Muhammad Ghori was **Govind Rai (Governor of Delhi)**, NOT Prithviraja himself.
10. **The Renaming of Conquered Forts by Alauddin**:
   * Chittor $\rightarrow$ **Khizrabad** (after son Khizr Khan)
   * Siwana $\rightarrow$ **Khairabad**
   * Jalore $\rightarrow$ **Jalalabad**
11. **The First Jauhar of Rajasthan**: Took place at **Ranthambore on 11 July 1301 CE** (led by Queen Ranga Devi), NOT Chittorgarh (which occurred in 1303 CE).
12. **The "Water Jauhar" (जल जौहर)**: Devalde / Padmala committed self-immolation in **Padmala Pond** at Ranthambore; Chittor's Jauhar was fire-based (*अग्नि जौहर*).
13. **Nusrat Khan's Death**: Alauddin's famous general **Nusrat Khan** was killed at the siege of **Ranthambore** (1299 CE), NOT Chittor or Jalore.
14. **The 32-Pillared Chhatris in Rajasthan**:
   * At Ranthambore: Built by **Hammiradeva** in memory of father Jaitrasimha's 32-year reign (*Nyaya ki Chhatri*).
   * At Mandalgarh (Bhilwara): Chhatri of **Jagannath Kachhwaha**.
15. **The Traitors of Ranthambore vs. Jalore**:
   * Ranthambore: **Ranmal and Ratipala**.
   * Jalore: **Bika Dahiya** (slain by his wife Hira Devi).
   * Siwana: **Bhayla Pawar**.
16. **The Poet Laureate Rajasekhara's Rulers**: Court scholar of **Mahendrapala I** and **Mahipala I**, NOT Mihir Bhoja.
17. **The Inscription Confirming Chauhans as Brahmins**: The **Bijolia Inscription (1170 CE)** explicitly terms them *Vatsa-Gotra Bhuta Vipra*.
18. **The Title "Kavi Bandhava"**: Bestowed exclusively upon **Vigraharaja IV (Bisaldeo)** by contemporary poets and by Jayanaka in *Prithviraja Vijaya*.
19. **The Coin Inscription of Queen Somaladevi**: Issued in Ajmer by the queen of **Ajayraja II**, NOT Arnoraja or Someshwara.
20. **The Key Fort of Ranthambore**: The satellite fort that served as the key to Ranthambore was **Jhain** (झाईन दुर्ग), renamed *Shahr-i-Nau*.
21. **The Key Fort of Jalore**: The satellite fort that served as the key to Jalore was **Siwana** (सिवाना दुर्ग).
22. **The Battle of Mahoba (1182 CE)**: Prithviraja III fought against Chandela king **Paramardideva**; the hero brothers who died were **Alha and Udal**.
23. **The Temple Purified by Ana Sagar**: Arnoraja constructed Ana Sagar to wash the ground stained with the blood of **Turushkas (Turks)**, and built the **Varaha Temple** at Pushkar.
24. **The Mongol Refugees at Ranthambore**: The primary rebel general given shelter by Hammir was **Muhammad Shah (Mir Muhammad Shah)** and Kabhru.
25. **The Language of Karpuramanjari**: Written by Rajasekhara in pure **Sauraseni Prakrit**, NOT classical Sanskrit.

### Part D: Model 10-Marker Analytical Answer (RPSC RAS Mains — 100-Word Budget)

**Q1. Critically examine the military, tactical, and political causes behind the defeat of Prithviraja Chauhan III in the Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE). What were its historical consequences for India?**  
*Model Answer*:  
The Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE) remains the definitive political turning point in medieval Indian history, transforming northern India from regional Rajput hegemony into the centralized Delhi Sultanate.

**1. Military & Tactical Causes of Defeat**:  
* **Flawed Strategic Complacency**: Following his 1191 victory, Prithviraja failed to pursue and destroy Ghori’s shattered army, spending 13 months besieging Bhatinda while Ghori systematically reorganized in Ghazni.  
* **Cavalry Tactics Divergence**: Rajput forces relied on heavy elephant corps and frontal sword cavalry charges. Ghori deployed 120,000 light mounted archers operating in four flexible divisions of 10,000, using feigned retreats to disrupt the rigid Rajput formation.  
* **Deceptive Tactics**: Ghori signed a deceptive truce negotiation and launched a pre-dawn surprise attack while the Chauhan camp was engaged in morning ablutions.

**2. Political & Diplomatic Failures**:  
* **Diplomatic Isolation**: Prithviraja’s aggressive *Digvijaya* policy had alienated every neighboring Hindu sovereign—the Chandelas of Mahoba, the Chalukyas of Gujarat, and especially Gahadavala Jayachandra of Kannauj—leaving him without allied reinforcements.  
* **Absence of Premier Generals**: Top general Skanda was engaged in another military campaign, depriving the army of veteran tactical coordination.

**3. Historical Consequences**:  
* **Collapse of the Chauhan Empire**: Ajmer was captured and plundered; Govindaraja was relegated to Ranthambore as a tributary.  
* **Foundation of Muslim Rule**: Paved the way for the immediate fall of Delhi, Kanauj, and Varanasi, directly establishing the Delhi Sultanate under Qutb-ud-din Aibak.

*Conclusion*: Prithviraja’s personal valor was undermined by medieval chivalric chivalry, strategic inertia, and tactical inferiority against Central Asian mobile horse-archery warfare. *(254 words)*
