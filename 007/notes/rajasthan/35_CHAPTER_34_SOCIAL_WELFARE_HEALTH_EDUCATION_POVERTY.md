## 34.1 The Social Welfare Paradigm of Rajasthan: Rights-Based Transformation

Rajasthan has emerged as a national pioneer in rights-based social welfare legislation and universal entitlements. Historically challenged by extreme arid geography, low literacy rates—particularly female literacy—and acute poverty across southern tribal and western desert tracts, the State has radically altered its developmental trajectory over the past two decades.

Through an aggressive policy of universal healthcare, statutory income guarantees, targeted subsidies, and direct social assistance, Rajasthan has created a social safety net that shields vulnerable populations from multidimensional shocks.

```
      │                               │                               │

└── Guaranteed Public Services  └── Annapurna Rasoi (₹8 Meal)   └── Devnarayan & Kalibai Scooty
```

### The Milestone Enactments of 2023
In 2023, Rajasthan enacted two pioneering statutes that made national legislative history:
1. **The Rajasthan Minimum Guaranteed Income Act, 2023**: The first statute in India to confer a legally enforceable right to 125 days of wage employment and an inflation-indexed minimum pension.
2. **The Rajasthan Right to Health Act, 2023**: The first statute in India guaranteeing every resident access to emergency medical care without upfront out-of-pocket payment.

## 34.2 Public Health & Healthcare Missions: Universal Health Coverage (UHC)

### 1. Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya Yojana (MAA Yojana)
- **Evolutionary Trajectory**: Began as the *Bhamashah Swasthya Bima Yojana (BSBY)* in 2015, transitioned to the *Mukhyamantri Chiranjeevi Swasthya Bima Yojana* on **1 May 2021**, and subsequently restructured into the **Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya (MAA) Yojana** integrated with the Central *Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB-PMJAY)*.
- **Coverage & Entitlement**:
  - Provides cashless health insurance cover up to **₹25 Lakh to ₹50 Lakh** per family per year for secondary and tertiary care hospitalization across empaneled public and private hospitals.
  - Covers 1,800+ specialized medical, surgical, and organ-transplant packages.
  - **Free Categories**: NFSA families, SECC beneficiaries, small and marginal farmers, contractual workers, and COVID-19 ex-gratia families. Other families can enroll by paying a nominal 50% premium (~₹850/year).
  - Linked to the **Jan Aadhaar Platform** for biometric and cashless paperless verification.

### 2. Mukhyamantri Nishulk Dawa Yojana (MNDY - Free Medicine Scheme)
- **Historic Launch Date**: **2 October 2011** (Gandhi Jayanti).
- **Institutional Engine**: **Rajasthan Medical Services Corporation Ltd (RMSCL)**, established in 2011 as a dedicated public procurement and logistics agency for drugs, surgical items, and diagnostics.
- **Essential Drugs List (EDL)**: Covers **over 1,800+ medicines, 900+ surgical items, and 180+ sutures** provided completely free of charge at all government hospitals (Sub-Centres, PHCs, CHCs, District Hospitals, Medical College Hospitals).
- **Supply Chain Automation**: Managed through the central **e-Aushadhi** software platform tracking batch expirations, QC testing, and real-time inventory from central warehouses to hospital dispensaries.
- **National Recognition**: Evaluated by the World Health Organization (WHO) and Planning Commission/NITI Aayog; served as the benchmark blueprint for the National Health Mission's (NHM) Free Drugs Service Initiative across India.

### 3. Mukhyamantri Nishulk Janch Yojana (MNJY - Free Diagnostic Scheme)
- **Historic Launch Date**: **7 April 2013** (World Health Day), rolled out in three phases.
- **Diagnostic Scope**: Provides **up to 90 to 133 clinical, biochemical, microbiological, and radiological diagnostic tests** (including blood pathology, digital X-rays, ECGs, CT scans, and MRIs in medical college hospitals) free of cost to all indoor and outdoor patients visiting public health facilities.
- **Laboratory Automation**: Tracked real-time via the **e-Upkaran** and **e-Lab** management portals.

### 4. Nirogi Rajasthan Abhiyan
- **Launch Date**: **18 December 2019**; Government of Rajasthan declared **17 December** as **"Rajasthan Nirogi Divas"**.
- **Core Philosophy**: Preventive and promotive health campaign addressing communicable and non-communicable diseases, lifestyle ailments, drug abuse, adulteration, and maternal-child nutrition.
- **Swasthya Mitras**: Appointment of **two voluntary community health mobilizers (one male, one female)** in every revenue village and urban ward to act as health sentinels without remuneration.
- **Nirogi Rajasthan Fund**: Created with an initial revolving corpus of **₹100 Crore** (augmented across budgets).

### 5. The Rajasthan Right to Health Act, 2023
- **First in India**: Enacted in March 2023, making Rajasthan the only Indian state with a statutory Right to Health.
- **Core Mandate**:
  - Guarantees every resident of Rajasthan the right to emergency medical treatment and care without prepayment of fees at any healthcare institution (public or private).
  - In emergency cases (accidents, cardiac arrests, snakebites, obstetric emergencies), private hospitals cannot deny treatment or demand upfront fees. If the patient is unable to pay, the State Government reimburses the hospital.
  - Establishes State and District Health Authorities to adjudicate patient grievances and enforce medical accountability.

### 6. Medical Infrastructure & Higher Medical Education Expansion
- **"One District, One Medical College" Initiative**: Under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) for establishment of new medical colleges attached with existing district/referral hospitals, medical colleges have been approved and operationalized across all 33 original administrative districts.
- **Apex Medical Institutions**:
  - **SMS Medical College, Jaipur**: Premier medical hub; construction of the **24-storey IPD Tower** with a rooftop helipad, creating India's tallest public healthcare infrastructure.
  - **AIIMS Jodhpur**: Established in **2012** under PMSSY Phase-I.
  - **Rajasthan University of Health Sciences (RUHS), Jaipur**: Established under the *RUHS Act, 2005* as the affiliating university for all state medical, dental, nursing, and pharmacy colleges.

### Forensic Breakdown of Installments: Mukhyamantri Rajshri Yojana
Provides a total of **₹50,000** for institutional girl child births occurring on or after 1 June 2016 across six specific lifecycle milestones:
1. **1st Installment**: **₹2,500** at birth in a government/accredited hospital.
2. **2nd Installment**: **₹2,500** on completing 1 year of age and all primary vaccinations.
3. **3rd Installment**: **₹4,000** on admission to Class 1 in any government school.
4. **4th Installment**: **₹5,000** on admission to Class 6 in a government school.
5. **5th Installment**: **₹11,000** on admission to Class 10 in a government school.
6. **6th Installment**: **₹25,000** on passing Class 12 from a government school.

### Indira Gandhi Matritva Poshan Yojana (IGMPY)
- **Historic Distinction**: While the Central *Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY)* covers the *first child*, IGMPY uniquely targets the **second child** to improve nutritional parameters and counter stunting/wasting.
- **Pilot Phase**: Launched on **19 November 2020** across 5 southern tribal/backward districts (Pratapgarh, Dungarpur, Banswara, Udaipur, and Baran).
- **State-wide Rollout**: Expanded to all 33 districts in the 2022–23 State Budget.
- **Incentive**: **₹6,000 paid in 5 conditional cash installments** upon antenatal checks, institutional delivery, child vaccination, and family planning adoption.

### Major School Education Initiatives
1. **Mahatma Gandhi Government Schools (English Medium - MGGS)**:
   - Launched in **2019** on the 150th Birth Anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi.
   - Converts existing government schools into English-medium institutions following the RBSE/NCERT English curriculum to eliminate the socio-economic divide between private and government school students.
   - Admission through transparent lottery; over 3,000+ MGGS operational across primary, upper primary, and senior secondary levels.
2. **Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan (SMSA)**:
   - Integrated Centrally Sponsored Scheme subsuming Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA), Rashtriya Madhyamik Shiksha Abhiyan (RMSA), and Teacher Education (TE) into a single continuum from pre-school to Class 12.
3. **Mukhyamantri Bal Gopal Yojana**:
   - Provides free powdered milk (hot milk) to students of Classes 1 to 8 in government schools and madrasas.
   - Quantity: 150 ml (Classes 1–5) and 200 ml (Classes 6–8), served on designated school days to counter protein-energy malnutrition.
4. **Mukhyamantri Nishulk Uniform Vitran Yojana**:
   - Provides two sets of free uniform fabric to all government school students from Classes 1 to 8 along with **₹200 stitching allowance** credited directly to the student's Jan Aadhaar bank account.
5. **Shiksha Setu & DIKSHA-Rise Portal**:
   - Digital e-learning infrastructure providing curriculum-mapped e-textbooks, video lectures, and diagnostic self-assessments.

## 34.5 Poverty Alleviation, Food Security & Guaranteed Social Protection

### 1. Rajasthan Minimum Guaranteed Income Act, 2023
Enacted in July 2023, this landmark legislation created a tripartite statutory entitlement for all residents of Rajasthan:

```
                  RAJASTHAN MINIMUM GUARANTEED INCOME ACT, 2023
                                        │
      ┌─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
      │                                                                   │
STATUTORY WAGE EMPLOYMENT                                       STATUTORY SOCIAL PENSION
      │                                                                   │
├── **125 Days Guaranteed Work**                                ├── **Minimum ₹1,000/month Pension**
├── Rural: 100 days MGNREGS + 25 state days                     ├── For Elderly, Widows, Divyangjan
├── Urban: 125 days under Indira Gandhi                         └── **Statutory 15% Annual Escalation**
│   Urban Employment Scheme (IRGY-Urban)                            ├── 5% automatic in July
└── Unemployment allowance if not provided in 15 days               └── 10% automatic in January
```

- **Statutory Escalation Clause**: Unlike executive notifications that change arbitrarily with governments, the Act legally mandates an annual **15% compounded increase** on the base pension of ₹1,000 (broken into **5% on 1st July** and **10% on 1st January**), raising current baseline pensions to **₹1,150+ per month**.

### 2. Social Security Pension Roster (Samajik Suraksha Pension)
- **Mukhyamantri Vridhjan Samman Pension Yojana**:
  - Eligibility: Women aged **55+ years** and Men aged **58+ years** with annual household income under ₹48,000 (no income limit for BPL/Antyodaya).
  - Amount: Minimum **₹1,150/month** (up to 75 years) and **₹1,250 to ₹1,500/month** (above 75 years).
- **Mukhyamantri Ekal Nari Samman Pension Yojana**:
  - Eligibility: Destitute widows, divorced, or abandoned women aged **18+ years** with annual income under ₹48,000.
  - Slabs: 18–55 years: ₹1,150/mo; 55–60 years: ₹1,250/mo; 60–75 years: ₹1,250/mo; 75+ years: ₹1,500/mo.
- **Mukhyamantri Vishesh Yogyajan Samman Pension Yojana**:
  - Eligibility: Persons with Disabilities (Divyangjan) with **40% or higher certified disability** and annual income under ₹60,000.
  - Slabs: Under 55/58 years: ₹1,150/mo; 55/58 to 75 years: ₹1,250/mo; 75+ years: ₹1,500/mo; leprosy/silicosis victims: ₹1,500 to ₹2,500/mo.

### 3. Palanhar Yojana: Institutional Care in a Family Setting
- **Historic Genesis**: Launched on **8 February 2005** under the Social Justice & Empowerment Department (SJED).
- **Core Concept**: Rather than placing orphan and destitute children in juvenile homes or orphanages, the scheme pays a monthly financial grant to an immediate relative or foster caretaker (*Palanhar*) who raises the child and ensures regular school enrollment.
- **Eligible Categories**:
  - Orphan children (*Anath*).
  - Children of parents sentenced to life imprisonment or capital punishment.
  - Children of destitute widows, divorced, or remarried mothers.
  - Children of parents affected by Leprosy, HIV/AIDS, or Silicosis.
- **Financial Assistance Slabs**:
  - **Children aged 0 to 6 years**: **₹1,500 per month** (non-orphan categories: ₹750/mo).
  - **Children aged 6 to 18 years**: **₹2,500 per month** (non-orphan categories: ₹1,500/mo).
  - **Annual Grant**: **₹2,000 per year** per child for clothes, shoes, and school uniforms.
  - Mandatory condition: The child must be enrolled in an Anganwadi (age 2–6) or recognized school (age 6+), with minimum 75% attendance.

### 4. Shree Annapurna Rasoi Yojana (Subsidized Food Security)
- **Evolutionary Background**: Originally launched on **20 August 2020** as the *Indira Rasoi Yojana* with the philosophical motto *"कोई भूखा न सोए" (No one sleeps hungry)*, subsequently reformed and upgraded as the **Shree Annapurna Rasoi Yojana**.
- **Beneficiary Cost**: A complete, hygienic, hot cooked meal provided to any citizen for **₹8 per thali**.
- **Enhanced Government Subsidy**:
  - The State Government subsidy per plate was augmented from ₹17 to **₹22 per thali** (total plate cost = ₹30).
- **Nutritional Enrichment (600 Grams Thali)**:
  - Enhanced from 450 grams to **600 grams per thali**, incorporating:
    - **100 grams of Dal (lentils)**
    - **100 grams of Vegetables**
    - **300 grams of Chapati / Coarse grain roti (Bajra/Millets - Shree Anna)**
    - **100 grams of Rice / Khichdi alongside pickles**.
- **Operations & Smart Monitoring**: Over 1,000+ permanent and mobile kitchen vans operated primarily through women Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and registered non-profits, with real-time biometric and face-recognition monitoring to prevent fraudulent invoicing.

### 1. Rajasthan Policy on Pneumoconiosis & Silicosis (2019)
- **Historic Precedence**: Rajasthan became the **first state in India** to notify a comprehensive, rights-based **Policy on Pneumoconiosis (Silicosis)** on **3 October 2019**.
- **Context**: Thousands of sandstone, marble, quartz, and soapstone mining and grinding workers across Jodhpur, Nagaur, Karauli, Dholpur, Bharatpur, and Bhilwara suffer from fatal lung fibrosis caused by silica dust inhalation.
- **Financial Package**:
  - **Rehabilitation Grant**: **₹3 Lakh** paid immediately upon clinical certification by a District Pneumoconiosis Medical Board.
  - **Mortality Assistance**: **₹2 Lakh** paid to the legal nominee upon the patient's death.
  - **Funeral Assistance**: **₹10,000** cash grant for last rites.
  - **Widow Pension**: Monthly pension of **₹1,500** under Ekal Nari Pension.
  - **Child Support**: All surviving children automatically covered under the **Palanhar Yojana** (₹1,500/mo up to age 6; ₹2,500/mo up to age 18).

### 2. Mukhyamantri Anuprati Coaching Yojana
- Launched to prevent brilliant underprivileged youths from being excluded from elite civil services, engineering, and medical careers due to coaching fees in hubs like Kota and Jaipur.
- Covers students whose family annual income is **below ₹8 Lakh** (or children of government employees up to Pay Matrix Level-11).
- Empanels top coaching institutes; state bears 100% tuition fees.
- Students studying outside their home towns receive an additional **₹40,000 per year** for lodging and boarding expenses.
- Reserved seats: SC (16%), ST (12%), OBC (21%), MBC (5%), EWS (10%), Minorities (remaining). Over 30,000 seats allocated annually.

### 3. Scooty Schemes for Girls' Mobility & Education
- **Devnarayan Chhatravriti evam Scooty Yojana**: Implemented by the Higher Education Department for More Backward Classes (MBC - Gujjar, Raika, Rebari, Gadariya, Lohar, Banjara). Eligible for girls securing 50%+ marks in Class 12 pursuing regular higher education.
- **Kalibai Bheel Medhavi Chhatra Scooty Yojana**: Named after the 13-year-old tribal martyr Kalibai of Rastapal (Dungarpur). Unified umbrella scheme for meritorious girls from ST, SC, OBC, EWS, and General categories scoring 65%+ in RBSE or 75%+ in CBSE Class 12 examinations. Includes electric scooties.

## 34.8 RPSC RAS 2-Marker, 5-Marker & 10-Marker Model Answers

### Q1 (2 Marks): What is the core entitlement guaranteed under the Rajasthan Minimum Guaranteed Income Act, 2023?
> **Answer**: The Act statutorily guarantees:
> 1. **125 Days Wage Employment** per household per year (rural via MGNREGS + state 25 days; urban via Indira Gandhi Urban Employment Scheme).
> 2. **Guaranteed Social Pension** of minimum ₹1,000/month for elderly, widows, and persons with disabilities, backed by a statutory **15% annual compounded escalation** (5% in July, 10% in January).

### Q2 (2 Marks): State the four components of financial compensation under Rajasthan's Silicosis Policy, 2019.
> **Answer**:
> 1. **₹3,00,000** one-time grant upon medical certification.
> 2. **₹2,00,000** survivor relief paid to legal heirs upon patient's death.
> 3. **₹10,000** immediate funeral assistance for last rites.
> 4. **₹1,500/month** pension to surviving widow plus automatic child coverage under Palanhar Yojana.

### Q3 (5 Marks): Explain the operational architecture and nationwide significance of the Mukhyamantri Nishulk Dawa Yojana (MNDY).
> **Answer**:
> 1. **Launch & Agency**: Launched on **2 October 2011**; implemented by the **Rajasthan Medical Services Corporation Ltd (RMSCL)** as an autonomous centralized procurement agency.
> 2. **Universal Coverage**: Free supply of essential medicines across all tiers of public healthcare (PHCs to medical college hospitals) for both outdoor (OPD) and indoor (IPD) patients.
> 3. **Supply Chain Digitalization**: End-to-end drug logistics managed via the **e-Aushadhi** software portal, tracking quality testing, batch expiration, and minimum stock levels across 34 district drug warehouses.
> 4. **Scope**: Expanded to cover **over 1,800 medicines, 900+ surgical items, and 180+ sutures**.
> 5. **National Impact**: Replicated as the benchmark model for the Government of India's *Free Drugs Service Initiative* under the National Health Mission (NHM).

### Q4 (5 Marks): Outline the financial installments and eligibility conditions under the Mukhyamantri Rajshri Yojana.
> **Answer**:
> 1. **Objective**: To foster positive attitudes toward the girl child, eradicate female feticide, and promote institutional birth and girl child education up to Class 12.
> 2. **Eligibility**: Girl children born in institutional health facilities in Rajasthan on or after **1 June 2016** (applicable up to 2 daughters per family).
> 3. **Installment Structure (Total ₹50,000)**:
>    - *Birth*: ₹2,500 at hospital birth.
>    - *Age 1*: ₹2,500 upon complete primary vaccination.
>    - *Class 1*: ₹4,000 upon admission in a government school.
>    - *Class 6*: ₹5,000 upon continuing government education.
>    - *Class 10*: ₹11,000 upon admission in Class 10.
>    - *Class 12*: ₹25,000 upon passing Class 12 from a government school.

### Q5 (10 Marks): Critically evaluate Rajasthan's performance in maternal and child health indicators and female literacy, highlighting policy interventions and persistent socio-cultural bottlenecks.
> **Answer**:
> **1. The Demographic & Human Development Context**:
> - Historically, Rajasthan has lagged on human development indices, characterized by an alarming **Gender Literacy Gap of 27.07%** (Male: 79.19%, Female: 52.12% - 2nd lowest in India, Census 2011).
> - Rural female literacy languished at **45.80%**, with southern tribal and western desert districts like Jalore (38.47%) and Sirohi (39.73%) falling below national safety thresholds.
>
> **2. Transformative Policy Interventions**:
> - **Incentivizing Education & Mobility**: The **Mukhyamantri Rajshri Yojana** (₹50,000 lifecycle grant) and Scooty schemes (**Kalibai Bheel & Devnarayan Scooty Yojana**) have successfully driven female secondary school retention and reduced child marriage incidence.
> - **Universal Institutional Deliveries**: Integration of *Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)*, *Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK)*, and the **Indira Gandhi Matritva Poshan Yojana (IGMPY)** (₹6,000 cash grant for second child) has raised institutional deliveries above 95% (NFHS-5).
> - **Public School Transformation**: Opening of **Mahatma Gandhi English Medium Schools (MGGS)** and **Mukhyamantri Bal Gopal Yojana** (free milk) has incentivized girl child attendance in government schools.
>
> **3. Persistent Socio-Cultural Bottlenecks**:
> - **Deep-Rooted Patriarchal Customs**: Female feticide remnants, preference for male offspring, and customs like *Aata-Saata* (reciprocal sister-exchange marriages) and *Natra* perpetuate female disempowerment.
> - **Severe Anemia & Malnutrition**: NFHS-5 reveals that over **54% of women in reproductive age (15–49) and over 71% of children aged 6–59 months** suffer from anemia, driven by low dietary diversity and early marriages.
> - **Geographic Remoteness in Arid/Tribal Tracts**: In dispersed settlements (*Dhanis*) across Jaisalmer, Barmer, and the Banswara hill tracts, access to Secondary/Senior Secondary schools remains a physical barrier for adolescent girls after puberty.
>
> **4. Policy Roadmap (Viksit Rajasthan @2047)**:
> - Universalization of fortified ration packets (Shree Anna millets) via Anganwadi centers to combat stunting and wasting.
> - Transitioning from basic school access to specialized STEM vocational training and digital literacy for rural adolescent girls.
