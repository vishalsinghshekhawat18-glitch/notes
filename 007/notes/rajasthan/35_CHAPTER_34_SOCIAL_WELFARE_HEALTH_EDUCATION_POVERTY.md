# CHAPTER 34: Social Sector Dynamics, Healthcare, Education, Poverty & Social Protection

**RPSC RAS study edition | Verification cut-off: 17 October 2026**

> **How to use this chapter.** Stable legal concepts and historical baselines are separated from changeable benefit rates. Where a scheme's design has changed, the latest located official Rajasthan source is preferred. A budget estimate is not an expenditure outcome; an announcement is not proof of full implementation; and a Census 2011 statistic must never be presented as a current measured rate.

## 1. Rajasthan's Social-Sector Architecture

Rajasthan's social policy combines statutory rights, public-service delivery, insurance/assurance, cash transfers, nutrition, school support and targeted assistance for vulnerable groups. These instruments do different jobs:

| Instrument | Main purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory entitlement | Creates a legal claim subject to the Act and applicable rules | Rajasthan Minimum Guaranteed Income Act, 2023; Rajasthan Right to Health Act, 2022 |
| Health assurance / insurance | Pays for eligible listed healthcare packages for enrolled families | Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya Yojana (MAA) |
| In-kind public provision | Reduces the cost of essential services | Free medicines, free diagnostics, PM POSHAN, school milk |
| Conditional / milestone-linked cash support | Links assistance to defined life-cycle or eligibility milestones | Lado Protsahan Yojana, maternity support |
| Social assistance | Supports eligible people facing age, disability, widowhood or child-care risks | Social security pensions, Palanhar Yojana |
| Targeted rehabilitation | Responds to a specific occupational or social vulnerability | Silicosis rehabilitation assistance, Anuprati coaching support |

**Exam principle:** Read every scheme through five questions: *who is eligible; what benefit is provided; how much and how often; which agency implements it; and what is the date/vintage of the rule or figure?*

## 2. Public Health and Healthcare Missions

### 2.1 Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya Yojana (MAA)

- Rajasthan's health-assurance architecture evolved from Bhamashah Swasthya Bima Yojana to the Chiranjeevi scheme and was subsequently branded/restructured as **Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya Yojana**.
- The Rajasthan Medical & Health Department's scheme documentation describes a **₹25 lakh family coverage/wallet**, delivered through insurance and trust arrangements. It is not accurate to describe the current state entitlement as an unqualified “₹25 lakh to ₹50 lakh” range.
- Eligibility is tied to the applicable state categories, enrolment/Jan Aadhaar records and scheme conditions. State-funded categories have included NFSA families, eligible SECC/PM-JAY families, small and marginal farmers, contractual workers, EWS families and specified COVID-19 ex-gratia beneficiaries. The current enrolment rules and exclusions must be checked against the active notification.
- The **Economic Review 2025–26** reports that 132 packages were added for surgery, mental healthcare, AYUSH and geriatric care; interstate portability for incoming beneficiaries began in April 2025 and outgoing portability from December 2025. For 2025–26 up to December, it reports 1,532,780 treated patients, 6,275,311 claims submitted and 4,015,705 paid claims. These are period-specific administrative counts, not annual totals for the whole financial year.
- **Do not confuse** MAA with the separate Rajasthan Government Health Scheme (RGHS), which is designed for specified state government employee/pensioner and dependent categories.

Sources: [Rajasthan Health Department – MAA and other schemes](https://rajswasthya.rajasthan.gov.in/schemes.php?id=1); [Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf); [MAA scheme procurement document](https://rajswasthya.rajasthan.gov.in/admin/upload/tender_file/2021-25/Bid%20Document-Document%201731503624.pdf).

### 2.2 Mukhyamantri Nishulk Dawa / Nishulk Nirogi Rajasthan (Dawa)

- **Launch:** Mukhyamantri Nishulk Dawa Yojana began on **2 October 2011**.
- **Institutional agency:** Rajasthan Medical Services Corporation Limited (RMSCL) was formed on **4 May 2011** to centralise procurement and supply of medicines, surgical items and related material.
- **Coverage:** Essential items are supplied without charge to eligible patients at state public health facilities, according to the level of facility and the applicable essential-drug list. The supply-chain system includes quality testing, stock management and district/medical-college drug stores.
- **Figure handling:** The 2025–26 Economic Review describes a list of **1,238 medicines, 428 surgical items and 156 sutures**. The department's scheme page separately describes an institution-level Essential Drug List/NRD inventory with a combined total of **1,829 items** across category and facility-level lists. These are different presentations/vintages; do not add them together or paraphrase “1,829 items” as 1,829 medicines.
- The scheme's important governance features are pooled procurement, standardisation, quality testing and last-mile availability. A free-medicine entitlement is only meaningful when stock availability, procurement quality and facility-level distribution work together.

Source: [Rajasthan Medical & Health Department – Free Drug Scheme](https://rajswasthya.rajasthan.gov.in/schemes.php?id=1); [Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf).

### 2.3 Mukhyamantri Nishulk Janch / Nirogi Rajasthan (Diagnostics)

- The free diagnostic initiative was launched on **7 April 2013**. It reduces out-of-pocket expenditure for patients using public health institutions.
- The exact panel of laboratory tests and imaging services can differ by facility tier, package, equipment availability and current departmental orders. An undated single number such as “90–133 tests everywhere” should not be memorised as a universal entitlement.
- Use the current Health Department service list when a question asks for the exact number of tests at a specific facility level.

### 2.4 Rajasthan Right to Health Act

- **Correct legal title:** *Rajasthan Right to Health Act, 2022*. It was passed by the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly in 2023; calling it simply the “Rajasthan Right to Health Act, 2023” loses the statute's formal title.
- The statute addresses patient rights, healthcare information, free services at public health institutions according to the prescribed level/rules, grievance redressal and specified emergency treatment without prepayment.
- Its emergency-care provision refers to defined situations—including accidental emergencies and snake/animal bites, plus other emergencies designated by the State Health Authority—and applies to qualified facilities/providers, including private providers, subject to prescribed circumstances and reimbursement/fee rules.
- **Critical qualification:** It is inaccurate to reduce the Act to an unlimited promise of every treatment, at every private hospital, free of charge. The precise obligation depends on the statutory wording, designated services, the applicable rules/orders and whether the case falls within the defined emergency provisions.
- The Bill text hosted by the Rajasthan Legislative Assembly is useful for locating the Act's wording; for a live legal dispute or a question specifically about commencement/rules, verify the latest Gazette notification and notified rules rather than relying on a coaching summary.

Source: [Rajasthan Legislative Assembly – Rajasthan Right to Health Bill/Act text](https://assembly.rajasthan.gov.in/BillInformationSystem/BillReport/Bill212022.pdf).

### 2.5 Preventive Health and Medical Infrastructure

- **Nirogi Rajasthan Abhiyan:** A broad preventive/promotive health initiative launched in December 2019, covering communicable and non-communicable diseases, lifestyle risks, nutrition and health awareness. Avoid repeating unsupported claims about universal appointment of two unpaid “Swasthya Mitras” per village/ward without the governing order.
- **Medical colleges:** “One district, one medical college” is a policy objective used in national/state medical-education planning. Distinguish *announced/approved*, *under construction*, *partially operational* and *fully operational* colleges. Do not infer that a medical college is operational in every current district from the policy slogan; district boundaries have also changed since Census 2011.
- **AIIMS Jodhpur:** An All India Institute of Medical Sciences established under the Pradhan Mantri Swasthya Suraksha Yojana framework.
- **RUHS Jaipur:** Rajasthan University of Health Sciences, established under the RUHS Act, 2005, performs affiliating and academic roles for specified health-sciences institutions.
- SMS Medical College, Jaipur is a major tertiary medical education institution. Avoid unsupported superlatives such as “India's tallest public healthcare infrastructure” unless backed by an official project source and a clear comparator.

## 3. Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health

### 3.1 Lado Protsahan Yojana: Current flagship girl-child support

- Implemented from **1 August 2024**.
- The **Economic Review 2025–26** reports assistance of **₹1.50 lakh per girl child in seven milestone-linked instalments**. This is the current scheme figure to prioritise over the older Rajshri schedule of ₹50,000 in six instalments.
- The review records 3.27 lakh girl children benefited and expenditure of ₹217.63 crore in 2025–26 up to December 2025. These are administrative progress figures for that reporting period.
- Eligibility and release conditions are governed by scheme guidelines; the benefit should not be described as an unconditional lump-sum cash payment on birth.

Source: [Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf).

### 3.2 Mukhyamantri Rajshri Yojana (historical/current-rule caution)

The earlier Rajshri design provided up to **₹50,000 in six instalments** tied to institutional birth, infant vaccination/age and school stages. This is a valuable historical scheme fact, but the chapter must not present it as the current headline girl-child entitlement without explaining the later Lado Protsahan scheme and the latest applicable rules. If a question explicitly asks about Rajshri, answer in the historical scheme frame and consult current departmental guidance for its present operational relationship with Lado.

### 3.3 Maternity benefit schemes

- **Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY):** A central maternity-benefit scheme; amount and eligibility vary by pregnancy/child category and the applicable version of guidelines.
- The 2026–27 Economic Review reports state additions to first-child maternity support from **1 April 2024** and enhanced assistance for disabled pregnant women from **1 September 2024**. The Review's implementation figures and current PMMVY/State guidelines should be consulted for eligibility; do not merge separate components into a single unconditional ₹6,000 entitlement.
- **Mukhya Mantri Matratva Poshan Yojana (MMMPY):** The current Economic Review describes **₹6,000 for the birth of the second child**, paid in **three instalments**, to improve maternal and child nutrition. This corrects the uploaded chapter's five-instalment description. The original state pilot began on 19 November 2020 in five districts (Pratapgarh, Dungarpur, Banswara, Udaipur and Baran); later budget documents describe expansion. Use the latest scheme guidelines for present eligibility and coverage.
- **Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY):** Centrally sponsored conditional cash assistance to promote institutional delivery; amounts vary by state classification and rural/urban category.
- **Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK):** In public facilities, provides specified free maternity/newborn services, including delivery/C-section, drugs, diagnostics, diet, blood and transport subject to scheme conditions.

Source: [Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf); [Rajasthan Economic Review 2021–22 – IGMPY background](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2021-2022/economicreviewE.pdf).

### 3.4 Nutrition in Anganwadis and schools

- **ICDS / Anganwadi services:** Provide supplementary nutrition, preschool education and linked health/referral services for young children and eligible pregnant/lactating women.
- **Mukhyamantri Amrit Aahar (Anganwadi milk distribution):** The 2025–26/2026–27 Economic Review describes this as implemented at Anganwadi centres from **14 December 2024** for children aged 3–6 years.
- **Pannadhay Bal Gopal Yojana:** The 2025–26 Economic Review describes hot milk prepared from skimmed milk powder for schoolchildren, for **six days a week**: 150 ml for pre-primary/Classes 1–5 and 200 ml for Classes 6–8, with prescribed milk-powder and sugar quantities. Do not confuse this school scheme with Amrit Aahar at Anganwadi centres.
- **PM POSHAN:** Supports nutrition among eligible children from pre-primary to Class 8 in government and other eligible schools/institutions. For 2025–26 up to December, the Review lists 68,480 schools/institutions and 57.01 lakh students; these numbers are time-bound, not permanent invariants.

Source: [Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf); [Rajasthan CMO – Pannadhay Bal Gopal update, 24 September 2026](https://cmo.rajasthan.gov.in/pressreleasedetail/251837).

## 5. Minimum Guaranteed Income and Employment Entitlements

### 5.1 Rajasthan Minimum Guaranteed Income Act, 2023

The Act established a statutory framework for a minimum guaranteed income through guaranteed wage employment or social-security pension for eligible people, with implementation depending on notified schemes, eligibility, rules and departmental responsibility.

**As originally enacted/provided in the 2023 Act:**

- Urban residents were given a statutory entitlement to at least **125 days of guaranteed employment** in a financial year under the state framework.
- Rural employment was framed as an **additional 25 days** after completion of the maximum days prescribed under the then-applicable MGNREGA framework.
- Work was to be provided within 15 days of application; the Act provides for unemployment allowance if work is not provided within that period, with rates and process to be prescribed.
- Eligible older people, widows/single women and people with disabilities were covered through a guaranteed social-security-pension framework.
- The statute specifies a 15% annual pension increase over the base rate in two instalments: 5% in July and 10% in January, subject to its terms, including a minimum 12-month period from the date the individual's pension is sanctioned before an increase is allowed.

**Crucial 2026 update:** The national **VB–G RAM G Act, 2025** came into force from **1 July 2026**, repealing MGNREGA from that date and providing 125 days of guaranteed wage employment to eligible rural households. Therefore, it is no longer safe to teach the old rural formula “100 days under MGNREGA + 25 days by Rajasthan” as the current operating arrangement. The state Act's original cross-reference and the post-July-2026 national transition must be read with current implementing notifications; do not assume the two guarantees stack to 150 days unless a valid official rule expressly says so.

Sources: [Rajasthan Legislative Assembly – Rajasthan Minimum Guaranteed Income Bill, 2023](https://assembly.rajasthan.gov.in/BillInformationSystem/BillReport/Bill262023.pdf); [PIB – commencement of VB–G RAM G Act from 1 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2279995&lang=1&reg=48); [PIB – 125-day rural guarantee and transition](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2259703&lang=2&reg=3).

### 5.2 Social-security pension amounts: use the latest dated baseline

- The 2025–26 Economic Review reports **₹1,250 per month** for several current social-security pension categories, including Indira Gandhi National Old Age Pension and the Mukhya Mantri Vishesh Yogyajan Samman Pension. It also reports ₹2,500 per month for leprosy-cured persons and ₹1,500 per month for silicosis-certified persons under the specific disability pension provisions.
- These are category-specific programme rates—not proof that every pensioner gets the same amount. Eligibility conditions, central/state funding shares, age cut-offs, disability criteria and category-specific rates differ.
- Older notes listing ₹1,150 per month reflect an earlier rate vintage. Do not use them as the current statewide baseline without an explanatory date.
- The Minimum Guaranteed Income Act's base rate/escalation clause and the published operational pension rate need to be read together with current rules/orders. Do not calculate a new entitlement purely by compounding a statutory formula unless the operative notification confirms the calculation.

Source: [Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26, Social Security section](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf); [Rajasthan Economic Review 2024–25 for older pension-rate vintage](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2025-2026/EconomicReviewE.pdf).

### 5.3 Palanhar Yojana

- A family/community-based support scheme for eligible children in difficult circumstances, designed to help children remain in a family setting rather than relying exclusively on institutional care.
- Eligible categories include orphans and specified children whose parents are incarcerated, widowed/remarried/abandoned or divorced mothers, or parents affected by specified conditions/disabilities, as provided in scheme rules.
- **Rates reported in the 2025–26 Economic Review:** orphan children aged 0–6: **₹1,500/month**; orphan children aged 6–18 who meet schooling conditions: **₹2,500/month**. Other eligible categories receive **₹750/month** for ages 0–6 and **₹1,500/month** for ages 6–18. The Review also reports a **₹2,000 annual lump sum** for clothing/essentials.
- Eligibility categories, school/Anganwadi requirements and beneficiary verification should be checked in current rules; do not assert a universal 75% attendance clause without the relevant scheme order.
- The Review records 6.09 lakh children benefited and ₹760 crore spent in 2025–26 up to December 2025.

Source: [Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf).

### 5.4 Shree Annapurna Rasoi Yojana

- The programme evolved from Indira Rasoi and provides cooked meals to beneficiaries at a subsidised price.
- Official portal terms: **₹8 per thali from the beneficiary** and **₹22 state subsidy per thali**. The official scheme page reports a 600-gram meal with 300 g chapati, 100 g dal, 100 g vegetables and 100 g rice or millet khichdi, plus pickle.
- The current official portal also states an annual provision of ₹250 crore and an indicative target of around 2.52 lakh beneficiaries per day; targets are not the same as actual daily utilisation.
- Do not add claims about universal facial recognition or biometric invoicing unless an official operating document verifies that mechanism.

Source: [Official Shree Annapurna Rasoi portal](https://shreeannapurnarasoi.rajasthan.gov.in/); [Rajasthan Government scheme information](https://department.rajasthan.gov.in/scheme/detail/747).

## 6. Targeted Social Welfare

### 6.1 Rajasthan Pneumoconiosis (Silicosis) Policy, 2019

- The 2019 policy addresses diagnosis, prevention, control, rehabilitation and social assistance for pneumoconiosis/silicosis, particularly among workers exposed to silica dust in mining, quarrying, cutting and grinding.
- Published official policy information lists **₹3 lakh on certification**, **₹2 lakh to family members after the patient's death**, and a **₹1,500 monthly pension for a silicosis patient**, with widow/family benefits subject to scheme terms. The state Health Department also describes ₹10,000 funeral assistance.
- Silicosis assistance is tied to certification by the competent medical board and the current process on the state portal. Do not say that every child is “automatically covered” under Palanhar without satisfying that scheme's eligibility and documentation rules.

Sources: [Rajasthan SJE – Silicosis Policy 2019 benefit schedule](https://sje.rajasthan.gov.in/siteadmin/Uploads/202109111338281207.pdf); [Rajasthan Health Department – Silicosis programme](https://rajswasthya.rajasthan.gov.in/dmhspro.php); [Rajasthan Silicosis Portal](https://rajsilicosis.rajasthan.gov.in/).

### 6.2 Mukhyamantri Anuprati Coaching Yojana

- Supports eligible students from specified socially/economically disadvantaged categories for selected competitive examinations through empanelled coaching arrangements.
- Eligibility, income ceilings, eligible examination lists, seat numbers, coaching-fee caps and maintenance allowance are controlled by the current annual advertisement/guidelines. The SJE portal publishes scheme-specific 2025 guidelines, merit lists and income-certificate formats.
- The uploaded chapter's claim of a uniform ₹40,000 hostel grant “for all” should not be memorised. Where a maintenance/housing allowance exists, its eligibility depends on the scheme's current guidelines and whether the student qualifies under specified circumstances.

Source: [Rajasthan Social Justice & Empowerment Department – Anuprati scheme documents](https://sje.rajasthan.gov.in/ShowSection.aspx?LinkArea=4).

### 6.3 Scooty and student-incentive schemes

- **Devnarayan Girls Scooty and Incentive Scheme** and **Kali Bai Bheel Medhavi Chhatra Scooty Yojana** are merit-linked programmes with separate rules, categories, authorities and annual selection lists.
- There are separate lists for different departments/categories and, in some editions, specific denotified/nomadic/semi-nomadic communities. It is misleading to describe Kali Bai Bheel as a single all-category umbrella with one universal marks threshold.
- For a current question, use the academic year's notification and distinguish: eligibility group, qualifying examination/marks, regular higher-education enrolment, selection quota, and physical scooty versus other incentive.

Source: [Rajasthan Higher & Technical Education scholarship portal](https://hte.rajasthan.gov.in/HTE/30992).

### 6.4 Lado, Kanyadan and menstrual-health support

- **Lado Protsahan Yojana:** Current flagship girl-child milestone support—₹1.50 lakh in seven instalments, according to the Economic Review 2025–26.
- **Mukhyamantri Kanyadan Yojana:** The Economic Review 2024–25 documented different base grants and education-linked top-ups for eligible economically weaker families; exact benefit depends on category and current order. Do not memorise one single ₹31,000–₹51,000 range as applying identically to all groups.
- **Kalibai Bheel Udaan Yojana:** Free sanitary napkin distribution to eligible girls/women through specified institutional channels. Scheme eligibility, distribution channels and implementation can change and should be checked against current WCD/Health guidance.

Sources: [Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf); [Economic Review 2024–25](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2025-2026/EconomicReviewE.pdf).

## 7. Nutrition and Human Development Indicators

- **NFHS-5 (2019–21)** reported anaemia affecting approximately **54.4% of women aged 15–49** and **71.5% of children aged 6–59 months** in Rajasthan. These are survey-period indicators, not 2026 measurements.
- High anaemia rates, maternal nutrition, child growth, educational retention, early marriage and geographic distance to services are linked but distinct development problems. Policy assessment should distinguish nutrition inputs (ration, milk, supplements) from outcome measures (anaemia, stunting, wasting, mortality and learning outcomes).
- **Lado Protsahan**, maternity schemes, ICDS/Anganwadi services, PM POSHAN and public-health programmes address different points in the life cycle; they are complementary, not interchangeable.

Source: [NFHS-5 India fact-sheets and reports](https://rchiips.org/nfhs/factsheet_NFHS-5.shtml).

## 8. Critical Exam Traps: Corrected, Current and Dated

1. **Formal statute name:** Rajasthan Right to Health Act, **2022**; passage took place in 2023. Do not change the formal title to “Act, 2023.”
2. **MAA cover:** State scheme documentation describes **₹25 lakh per family**; do not write “₹25–50 lakh” without a newer official order.
3. **Free medicine figures:** Distinguish the Economic Review's **1,238 medicines + 428 surgical items + 156 sutures** from department webpage summaries; do not call 1,829 combined EDL/NRD items “medicines.”
4. **Girl-child support:** Current Lado Protsahan Yojana reports **₹1.50 lakh in seven instalments**. Rajshri's ₹50,000/six-instalment design is the older scheme framework.
5. **Second-child maternity support:** The latest Economic Review describes MMMPY as **₹6,000 in three instalments**, not five.
6. **School milk vs Anganwadi milk:** Pannadhay Bal Gopal is the school programme; Mukhyamantri Amrit Aahar is the Anganwadi programme. Do not merge the two.
7. **Social pensions:** The 2025–26 Economic Review reports **₹1,250/month** for several categories; ₹1,150 is an earlier rate vintage. Individual categories vary.
8. **Rural job guarantee:** From **1 July 2026**, VB–G RAM G replaced MGNREGA and provides 125 days. Do not present “100 + 25 state days” as the current central statutory structure.
9. **Minimum Guaranteed Income Act:** Original 2023 rural wording depended on completion of the then-MGNREGA day limit; urban guarantee and the rural transition should be treated separately.
10. **Palanhar:** Orphans: ₹1,500 per month (0–6), ₹2,500 (6–18); other eligible categories have lower rates, with a ₹2,000 annual lump sum as reported in the 2025–26 Review.
11. **Annapurna Rasoi:** ₹8 beneficiary contribution + ₹22 state subsidy; official meal weight is 600 g. Do not confuse plate price, subsidy and total notional cost.
12. **Census literacy:** Rajasthan 66.11% total, 79.19% male, 52.12% female are **Census 2011** values, not current 2026 rates.
13. **District literacy:** Rankings in the old 33-district census table refer to 2011 boundaries.
14. **Silicosis:** ₹3 lakh on certification, ₹2 lakh after death and ₹1,500/month patient pension appear in the official policy summary; eligibility hinges on competent certification.
15. **Anuprati and scooty schemes:** Current annual guidelines control income limits, examination coverage, category and allowance; do not turn a specific annual condition into a universal rule.

## 9. RPSC Practice Questions and Model Answers

### 9.1 Two-mark questions

**Q1. What is the current reported coverage under Rajasthan's MAA scheme?**

**Model answer:** Rajasthan Health Department documentation describes a ₹25 lakh family coverage/wallet under Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya Yojana, subject to the scheme's eligibility and package conditions.

**Q2. Distinguish the Right to Health Act's emergency provision from universal free treatment.**

**Model answer:** The Rajasthan Right to Health Act, 2022 provides specified resident rights and emergency care without prepayment in defined situations at qualified facilities; it is not an unlimited guarantee of every private treatment free of charge.

**Q3. State the current amount reported under Lado Protsahan Yojana.**

**Model answer:** The Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26 reports ₹1.50 lakh per girl child in seven milestone-linked instalments.

**Q4. What are the Palanhar monthly rates reported in the 2025–26 Economic Review for orphan children?**

**Model answer:** ₹1,500 for ages 0–6 and ₹2,500 for ages 6–18, subject to eligibility and care/school conditions.

**Q5. What changed in rural employment guarantees on 1 July 2026?**

**Model answer:** The VB–G RAM G Act, 2025 came into force and repealed MGNREGA, providing 125 days of guaranteed wage employment for eligible rural households.

### 9.2 Five-mark questions

**Q1. Explain how Rajasthan's public-health architecture reduces household out-of-pocket expenditure.**

**Model answer framework:**
- MAA provides cashless hospital cover for eligible families and specified packages.
- Nishulk Dawa/Nirogi Rajasthan supplies essential medicines in government facilities.
- Free diagnostic services reduce laboratory/imaging expenses at eligible public facilities.
- JSSK/JSY and maternal-child nutrition programmes reduce costs and encourage institutional care.
- Effectiveness depends on enrolment, package clarity, stock/diagnostic availability, facility quality and grievance redressal.

**Q2. Summarise the Rajasthan Minimum Guaranteed Income Act, 2023 and the key 2026 transition issue.**

**Model answer framework:**
- Creates statutory architecture for guaranteed employment or eligible social-security pension.
- Original state framework provided additional rural employment days beyond the then-central guarantee and 125 urban days.
- It provided an unemployment allowance if work was not made available within the specified 15-day period, subject to prescribed rules.
- Pension provisions include a 15% annual increase in two instalments (5% July, 10% January) from FY 2024–25, subject to the Act's conditions.
- From 1 July 2026 the VB–G RAM G Act replaced MGNREGA and independently created a 125-day rural guarantee; the interaction with the older state provision must be read from current notifications, not an assumed “100+25” arithmetic.

**Q3. Discuss the significance of Palanhar and silicosis assistance in targeted social protection.**

**Model answer framework:**
- Palanhar provides financial support through a family caretaker to eligible children in difficult circumstances.
- Age/category-based rates are intended to support nutrition, schooling and family care.
- Silicosis assistance targets a serious occupational disease prevalent in silica-dust work.
- Certification by the competent medical board is central to eligibility.
- Pensions, family relief and child-support convergence help mitigate the long-term effects of disability or death but depend on rules and documentation.

### 9.3 Ten-mark questions

**Q1. “Rajasthan's social-sector model is moving from fragmented subsidies toward a life-cycle architecture of rights and services.” Critically examine.**

**Suggested structure:**
1. **Introduction:** Explain how statutory entitlements, health assurance, nutrition, school support and pensions address risks at different life stages.
2. **Health:** MAA, free medicines and diagnostics, Right to Health framework and maternal-child services.
3. **Education and nutrition:** Samagra Shiksha, PM POSHAN, school milk, Anganwadi nutrition and Lado Protsahan.
4. **Social protection:** Minimum Guaranteed Income framework, pensions, Palanhar and silicosis relief.
5. **Implementation challenges:** Exclusion due to documents/digital access, outdated beneficiary data, quality and stock availability, delayed payments, complex eligibility and fragmented grievance systems.
6. **Way forward:** Make eligibility and entitlements transparent; integrate but do not conflate scheme databases; publish outcome measures; strengthen facility-level delivery and independent audits.

**Q2. Evaluate Rajasthan's healthcare rights and assurance framework.**

**Suggested structure:**
- Differentiate the Right to Health Act (legal rights and defined obligations) from MAA (cashless insurance/assurance for eligible packages) and public-health programmes (free drugs/diagnostics).
- Describe how each reduces different components of out-of-pocket spending.
- Explain why “₹25 lakh coverage” does not mean all treatment in all facilities is automatically cashless; eligibility, package, empanelment and claims rules matter.
- Examine gaps: quality of care, referral pathways, drug stock-outs, diagnostic reach, grievance redressal and provider reimbursement.
- Conclude with transparent rules, portable records, regular audits and public reporting.

**Q3. Analyse the importance of reliable data vintages in social-sector governance and RPSC preparation.**

**Suggested structure:**
- Census 2011 remains a historical population/literacy baseline, not a current annual estimate.
- NFHS-5 estimates describe 2019–21 survey conditions, not 2026 values.
- Economic Reviews report progress for specified periods (for example, up to December of a financial year).
- Annual benefit circulars can change pension rates, student eligibility and scheme instalments.
- Mixing old and current figures causes false comparisons and erroneous exam answers.
- Use date-stamped sources, definition notes, current orders and separate columns for historical baseline/current official reporting.
