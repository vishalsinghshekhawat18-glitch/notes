## 1. Rajasthan's industrial economy: structure and measurement

### 1.1 Industry's place in the state economy

The industrial sector includes **mining and quarrying, manufacturing, electricity/gas/water and other utilities, and construction**. Do not equate “industry” with manufacturing alone.

According to the *Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26*, the **2025–26 Advance Estimate (AE)** for Gross State Value Added (GSVA) at basic current prices places the industry sector at **₹4,54,486 crore**, or **26.55% of total GSVA**. At constant 2011–12 prices, the source chapter reports industry GSVA of ₹2,47,872 crore and a 28.21% share. These figures should be cited with the 2025–26 AE label; they are estimates, not final audited outcomes.

| Indicator | 2025–26 AE / published current series | Exam-use note |
|---|---:|---|
| Total GSVA, current prices | About ₹17.12 lakh crore | GSVA is not GSDP |
| Industry GSVA, current prices | ₹4,54,486 crore | Includes construction and utilities as well as manufacturing and mining |
| Industry share in total GSVA | 26.55% | Current-price share |
| Industry GSVA, constant 2011–12 prices | ₹2,47,872 crore | Keep the same base year when comparing real growth |
| Industry share at constant prices | 28.21% | A different price basis can produce a different share |

**Sub-sectoral Composition of Industry GSVA (2025–26 AE, Current Prices):**

| Sub-Sector | Share of Total State GSVA (%) | Share Within Industry Sector (%) | Absolute GSVA (₹ Crore) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Manufacturing (विनिर्माण)** | **10.76%** | **40.52%** | ₹1,84,166 Cr |

## 2. Institutional framework for industrial development

### 2.1 Rajasthan State Industrial Development and Investment Corporation (RIICO)

- **Origin:** Incorporated on 28 March 1969 as the Rajasthan State Industrial and Mineral Development Corporation (RSIMDC).
- **Restructuring:** On 1 January 1980, RSIMDC was bifurcated into RIICO and the Rajasthan State Mineral Development Corporation (RSMDC). Do not incorrectly describe this as a direct 1980 transfer to RSMML without a source explaining the later institutional history.
- **Mandate:** Develops industrial infrastructure and areas; facilitates industrial land, roads, power, water, drainage and related infrastructure; also provides certain forms of project finance.
- **Current official profile:** RIICO reports **472 industrial areas developed**. This supersedes the draft's older “390+” count. Counts can change, so quote the source date when a precise number matters.
- **Head office:** Udyog Bhawan, Tilak Marg, Jaipur.

[Official RIICO profile](https://riico.rajasthan.gov.in/AboutUsDept.aspx)

### 2.2 Rajasthan Financial Corporation (RFC)

RFC is a state-level financial institution established under the **State Financial Corporations Act, 1951**. Its broad role is to provide term finance and related assistance to eligible industrial and service enterprises. Exact loan ceilings, sanctioned amounts and eligible categories are scheme- and date-dependent; do not memorise the draft's claimed ₹20-crore ceiling or cumulative disbursement without checking the current RFC lending rules.

### 2.3 Bureau of Investment Promotion (BIP)

- Established in **1991** as Rajasthan's nodal investment-promotion agency.
- Supports investors, coordinates with government departments, and facilitates single-window/One Stop Shop clearances.
- Its published service focus includes investment proposals above **₹10 crore**.
- The MSME Act's state-level nodal agency role is also assigned to BIP; District Industries and Commerce Centres (DICCs) function at district level.

[Official BIP page](https://rising.rajasthan.gov.in/about-bip)

### 2.4 RajNivesh / single-window clearance

RajNivesh provides an online channel for approvals, registrations, applications and tracking. The Economic Review 2025–26 reports **181 services across 19 departments** integrated into the portal. During calendar year 2025, it records 1,925 applications involving proposed investment of ₹42,693.70 crore approved through the portal. These are administrative throughput figures, not necessarily completed investment or realised capital expenditure.

### 2.5 Rajasthan State Industrial Development and Investment / Small Industries support: avoid institutional conflation

RIICO, RFC, BIP, DICCs and the Rajasthan State Industrial Development/marketing entities perform different functions. For exam answers, separate them by verb:

- **RIICO:** industrial land and infrastructure.
- **RFC:** finance for eligible projects.
- **BIP:** investment facilitation and One Stop Shop.
- **DICC:** district-level enterprise assistance.
- **RSIC / “Rajasthico”:** state-level small-industry marketing, raw-material and related support functions; use the current official corporation description for a specific claim.

The draft incorrectly lists **Kota** among four RSIC inland container depots. The Rajasthan government’s RSIC RTI schedule lists depots at **Jaipur (Sanganer), Bhiwadi, Bhilwara and Jodhpur (Basni)**. Use this list rather than the draft's Kota entry.

[RSIC RTI schedule](https://environment.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/industries/pdf/rsicl/rti/RTIScheduleIV.pdf)

## 3. Investment and MSME policy architecture

### 3.1 Rajasthan Investment Promotion Scheme (RIPS) 2024

RIPS 2024 was notified by the Finance Department on **8 October 2024**. The policy is designed to attract investment and promote the state priorities of **green growth, export promotion and capability development**. It has a tiered design: standard incentives, add-on incentives linked to state priorities, and customised packages.

The policy is operative until **31 March 2029**, or until a new policy is notified, whichever occurs first.

For eligible enterprises, the asset-creation incentive menu can include a choice between:
1. **Investment subsidy:** reimbursement of up to 75% of eligible State tax due and deposited for a specified period;
2. **Capital subsidy:** rates vary by investment category, location and eligibility; large/mega/ultra-mega packages include staged support over a number of years; or
3. **Turnover-linked incentive:** a percentage of net sales turnover, with rate and caps determined by the package.

Additional incentives may be available for employment generation, skilling/training, IP creation, green growth, export promotion, or designated thrust sectors. Exemptions/reimbursements can include electricity duty, stamp duty, land-conversion charges and mandi/market fees, but the exact benefit, period, caps and eligibility **vary by enterprise category and chosen package**.

**Do not memorise the original draft's blanket statement** that every unit receives 75% SGST reimbursement plus 100% exemption from all listed charges for seven years. RIPS is a conditional menu of benefits, not one universal package.

[Official RIPS 2024 policy PDF](https://istart.rajasthan.gov.in/public/Policies/2024/rips-2024.pdf) · [RIICO policy listing](https://riico.rajasthan.gov.in/PoliciesGuidelines.aspx)

### 3.2 Rajasthan MSME Facilitation Act, 2019 — five-year exemption

The *Rajasthan Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (Facilitation of Establishment and Operation) Act, 2019* creates a facilitation route for eligible new MSMEs through a **Declaration of Intent** and an **Acknowledgement Certificate**.

- The original Act provided a **three-year** exemption period.
- The **Rajasthan MSME (Amendment) Act, 2023 (Act No. 10 of 2023)** substituted “five years” for “three years” in **Sections 6 and 7**.
- The benefit is for eligible new enterprises and concerns specified approvals/inspections under applicable State law, subject to statutory conditions and exclusions.
- It does **not** mean that an enterprise may disregard worker safety, public health, prohibitions on hazardous/illegal activity, or all substantive legal duties. The Act and its procedures also set conditions for later obtaining required approvals and permit revocation in cases such as wilful misstatement or loss of MSME eligibility.
- The official portal FAQ is internally inconsistent: it states that exemption applies for five years but elsewhere describes the certificate's validity as three years. Where the FAQ conflicts with the Gazette amendment, cite the statutory amendment and verify the current certificate implementation procedure before advising an actual applicant.

**RPSC trap:** the 2023 amendment changed Sections **6 and 7**, not Section 4. The legal change was an extension from three to five years.

Sources: [2023 Amendment Act, official Gazette translation](https://rajudyogmitra.rajasthan.gov.in/Documents/MSMEAct2023.pdf); [Raj Udyog Mitra official portal and FAQ](https://rajudyogmitra.rajasthan.gov.in/).

### 3.3 Mukhyamantri Laghu Udyog Protsahan Yojana (MLUPY)

The scheme supports eligible new enterprises and the expansion, modernisation or diversification of existing enterprises in manufacturing, services and trade through loans via participating financial institutions.

The Government of Rajasthan's Economic Review materials report the following interest-subsidy tiers:

| Loan amount | Reported interest-subsidy rate |
|---|---:|
| Up to ₹25 lakh | 8% |
| Above ₹25 lakh up to ₹5 crore | 6% |
| Above ₹5 crore up to ₹10 crore | 5% |

These are scheme-reported tiers, subject to detailed guidelines, lending institution conditions, eligibility and the effective scheme notification. A headline “interest subsidy” should not be confused with an unconditional promise that the borrower pays no interest.

[Government of Rajasthan Economic Review / scheme summary](https://jankalyanfile.rajasthan.gov.in/Content/UploadFolder/DepartmentMaster/166/2025/Feb/30409/166fc88acc1-df17-45f3-bdfc-a9054819229d.pdf)

### 3.4 Rajasthan Handicrafts Policy 2022

The 2022 policy focuses on the competitiveness and continuity of craft-based enterprises: market access, design/technology improvement, promotion of artisan clusters and assistance associated with intellectual property/GI registration. Do not present unverified claims such as “100% reimbursement for every new GI registration” or a guaranteed annual “National Crafts Week” as universal statutory entitlements without the relevant notification.

### 3.5 Industrial and export policy updates

RIPS 2024 and the **Rajasthan Export Promotion Policy 2024** are central current policy references. Older schemes remain relevant only to the extent they are still operational and not replaced or modified. For exam notes, give every scheme fact a date and distinguish:
- announced target;
- notified policy;
- operational scheme;
- sanctioned assistance; and
- actual disbursement.

## 4. Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC) and industrial geography

The Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC) is a major industrial/logistics development initiative associated with the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor and the Delhi–Mumbai freight and economic linkage. Rajasthan's central location, proximity to the National Capital Region, road/rail links and existing industrial clusters give it strategic advantages.

Projects frequently associated with Rajasthan's DMIC planning include:
- **Khushkhera–Bhiwadi–Neemrana (KBNIR)** in the NCR-facing industrial belt; and
- **Jodhpur–Pali–Marwar (JPMIA)** in western Rajasthan.

**Status discipline:** treat these as named planning/project areas unless a current government source proves the specific phase is complete or operational. The original draft's exact claims—548 km, 36.4% and “largest share among six states,” 22 districts and 58% of state area, 165/154 sq km, and time savings “48 hours to under 14 hours”—were not retained as verified facts because an authoritative current source substantiating those exact measures was not established in this audit.

Industrial corridors can generate manufacturing, logistics, warehousing, ancillary services and employment; outcomes depend on land acquisition, water availability, environmental capacity, rail/road connections and the execution status of individual nodes.

## 5. Petroleum and petrochemicals: Pachpadra / HRRL

### 5.1 HPCL Rajasthan Refinery Limited (HRRL)

- **Location:** Pachpadra, Balotra district, Rajasthan.
- **Joint-venture equity:** Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited (HPCL) **74%**; Government of Rajasthan **26%**.
- **Nameplate refinery capacity:** **9 million metric tonnes per annum (MMTPA)**.
- **Petrochemical capacity:** **2.4 MMTPA**.
- **Revised project cost approved in April 2026:** approximately **₹79,459 crore**.
- The project integrates refining with petrochemical production and is intended to anchor downstream plastic, packaging, chemical and ancillary industries.

The Government of India announced that the complex would be dedicated to the nation on **21 April 2026**. An April 2026 Cabinet release stated a Scheduled Commercial Operation Date (SCOD) of **1 July 2026**. In the sources checked for this revision, a later official statement confirming the precise commercial ramp-up status as of October 2026 was not established. For exam purposes, distinguish the date of dedication from the SCOD and from proof of sustained commercial operation.

Sources: [PIB — dedication announcement, 20 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2253676&lang=2&reg=48); [PIB — revised project cost and SCOD, 8 April 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2250055&lang=2&reg=3).

### 5.2 Oil and gas geography

Rajasthan is an important onshore oil and gas province, with major petroleum activity concentrated in the Barmer–Sanchore area and gas resources associated with the Jaisalmer basin. Older sources identify multiple petroliferous basins across western and south-eastern Rajasthan, but basin boundaries, exploratory status and producing fields must be kept distinct.

The draft's ranking statement—“second-largest crude-producing state overall after offshore Bombay High”—compares a state with an offshore field and should not be memorised as a like-for-like state ranking. Retain the better-supported statement that Rajasthan is a significant **onshore** crude producer; attach a specific national rank or production share only to a dated PPAC/official production table.

## 6. Minerals and mining: resource base, revenues and policy

### 6.1 Mineral endowment

Rajasthan is one of India's leading mineral-resource states. The Department of Mines and Geology's current mineral-potential page refers to **81 varieties of minerals**, of which **57 are commercially exploited**. The Economic Review 2025–26 uses the wording “nearly 82 varieties” and 57 in commercial production; this minor source-version difference is one reason to quote the named source and year rather than suggest that the count is immutable.

Mineral strengths include:
- lead and zinc;
- limestone, including cement-grade limestone;
- gypsum;
- marble, granite, sandstone and other dimensional stones;
- feldspar, ball clay, calcite, phosphorite/rock phosphate, wollastonite and related industrial minerals;
- copper, lignite, crude oil and natural gas.

The state has historically held a leading or distinctive national position in several minerals, but labels such as “sole producer” should be attached to the precise mineral and product category—ore, concentrate, refined product or mineral output—and the official source year.

### 6.2 Mining governance and revenue

- Major-mineral leases are granted through e-auction processes.
- The **Rajasthan Mineral Policy 2024** emphasises responsible mineral development, investment, zero-waste/efficient mining, environmental safeguards, digital monitoring and improved transparency.
- The Economic Review 2025–26 reports a **₹12,980-crore revenue target** for the Mines and Geology Department for 2025–26 and receipts of **₹6,857.97 crore up to December 2025**. The latter is a year-to-date figure, not a full-year outcome.
- That review reports **34 major mineral blocks auctioned in 2024–25**, the highest number among states according to the Review.

For the number of leases or licences, always retain the report-year label: these counts change as leases are granted, surrendered, converted or renewed.

Sources: [Rajasthan Department of Mines and Geology — Mineral Potential](https://mines.rajasthan.gov.in/dmgcms/page?menuName=%2Fq%2Frwh%2FKECGUMOi0%3B455611%3BBE9jQMBWH9CvuLj); [Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf).

## 7. Export performance and trade facilitation

### 7.1 Export value, policy target and product mix

The **Rajasthan Export Promotion Policy 2024** records merchandise exports of **₹83,704.24 crore in FY 2023–24**. Its stated target is **₹1.5 lakh crore in merchandise exports by 2029**—not by 2028.

The policy identifies the leading product groups and reports this 2023–24 mix:

| Product group | Share shown in the Export Promotion Policy |
|---|---:|
| Engineering goods | **16.60%** |
| Gems and jewellery | **11.18%** |
| Ferrous and non-ferrous metals | **9.80%** |
| Textiles | **8.80%** |
| Handicrafts | **7.90%** |
| Agro and food products | 6.40% |
| Chemicals and allied products | 4.50% |

**Exam trap:** in the 2023–24 policy dataset, engineering goods are the largest listed product group. The source draft's 13.8% textiles, 13.1% gems and 11.2% non-ferrous metal shares do not match the notified policy's published mix. The values refer to merchandise exports, not export “volume” in tonnes and not all services exports.

### 7.2 Rajasthan Export Promotion Policy 2024 — support instruments

The policy aims to widen export participation, especially by MSMEs, crafts enterprises and new exporters. Instruments include assistance for:
- export documentation, quality and management certification;
- eligible marketing expenses for approved international trade fairs;
- product testing/certification; and
- technology upgrading for export units.

The Economic Review 2025–26 reports the following policy implementation through **December 2025**:
- documentation/certification assistance: 50% of annual cost, capped at ₹5 lakh per unit; assistance of ₹22.36 lakh to 25 applicants;
- marketing assistance: 75% of eligible expenses, capped at ₹3 lakh per year and subject to the policy's frequency rules; ₹182.68 lakh granted to 75 applicants;
- product testing: 75% of expenses, capped at ₹20,000 per shipment and ₹3 lakh per year; ₹10.02 lakh to 11 applicants;
- technology-upgradation assistance: 75% reimbursement subject to a ₹50-lakh cap; 15 approvals totalling ₹624.96 lakh.

These are scheme-specific limits and dated implementation totals, not automatic benefits for every exporter.

Sources: [Rajasthan Export Promotion Policy 2024](https://istart.rajasthan.gov.in/public/Policies/2024/rajasthan-export-promotion-policy-2024.pdf); [Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26](https://finance.rajasthan.gov.in/docs/budget/statebudget/2026-2027/Economicreviewe.pdf).

### 7.3 Niryatak Bano and inland logistics

“Niryatak Bano” is an exporter-handholding initiative intended to help enterprises, artisans and producers navigate export registration and market access. For a current answer, pair it with the notified Export Promotion Policy 2024 rather than treating the campaign as the sole export framework. The campaign's launch date should be checked against a dated notification before memorisation.

Rajasthan's landlocked geography makes inland container depots, air cargo, freight corridors and links to ports such as Mundra and Kandla important to export competitiveness. The RSIC RTI schedule identifies ICD facilities at **Jaipur (Sanganer), Bhiwadi, Bhilwara and Jodhpur (Basni)**. The draft's inclusion of Kota in that four-location list is not supported by that official schedule.

### 7.4 Export constraints and policy analysis

Key constraints include logistics and freight cost, access to working capital/export credit, product testing and certification expenses, standards compliance, global-market discovery, small-scale production and limited direct-export experience. Policy responses should be assessed by measuring actual support disbursed, new exporters, market diversification, product value addition and export growth—not simply the number of announcements.

## 8. Industrial clusters and status discipline

Rajasthan's established industrial strengths include:
- automobiles and engineering around the NCR-facing belt, including Neemrana and Bhiwadi;
- textiles and synthetic yarn around Bhilwara and other textile centres;
- gems, jewellery, coloured gemstones and handicrafts around Jaipur;
- cement and limestone-based industries across mineral-rich districts;
- marble, granite, sandstone, Kota stone and related processing;
- petrochemicals around Pachpadra/HRRL; and
- renewable-energy-linked industries and components.

### Important correction: PM-MITRA Park

The original chapter states that a PM Mega Integrated Textile Region and Apparel (PM-MITRA) Park is at Kakani/Jodhpur. The Centre's approved PM-MITRA sites are **Virudhunagar (Tamil Nadu), Warangal (Telangana), Navsari (Gujarat), Kalaburagi (Karnataka), Dhar (Madhya Pradesh), Lucknow (Uttar Pradesh) and Amravati (Maharashtra)**. Rajasthan was not among the seven final approved locations. Rajasthan does have other textile parks/industrial areas, but these should not be renamed as PM-MITRA parks.

Source: [PIB — PM-MITRA site list, 27 March 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2245979&lang=1&reg=1).

**Use this distinction for all industrial parks:** proposed site ≠ selected site ≠ approved project ≠ land acquired ≠ under construction ≠ operational facility.

## 10. Practice questions and model answer frameworks

### 10.1 Two-mark questions

**Q1. When was RIICO formed, and how did it originate?**  
**Model answer:** RIICO emerged from the bifurcation of RSIMDC on 1 January 1980; RSIMDC had been incorporated on 28 March 1969.

**Q2. What did the Rajasthan MSME (Amendment) Act, 2023 change?**  
**Model answer:** It substituted “five years” for “three years” in Sections 6 and 7 of the Rajasthan MSME Facilitation Act, 2019.

**Q3. What is Rajasthan's export target under the Export Promotion Policy 2024?**  
**Model answer:** The policy targets merchandise exports of ₹1.5 lakh crore by 2029.

**Q4. State the ownership and capacity of HRRL at Pachpadra.**  
**Model answer:** HRRL is a 74:26 joint venture between HPCL and the Government of Rajasthan, with a 9-MMTPA refinery and 2.4-MMTPA petrochemical capacity.

### 10.2 Five-mark questions

**Q1. Explain how RIPS 2024 supports industrial investment in Rajasthan.**  
**Model answer framework:**
1. It aims at green growth, export promotion and capability development.
2. It offers tiered packages, including eligible SGST reimbursement, capital subsidy or turnover-linked incentive options.
3. Add-on assistance may cover employment, training, IP creation, green initiatives and exports.
4. Selected exemptions may reduce the cost of stamp duty, land conversion, electricity duty and mandi/market fees.
5. Benefits depend on the enterprise category, location, investment, selected package, caps and policy conditions.

**Q2. Describe the role of RIICO, RFC and BIP in industrial development.**  
**Model answer framework:** RIICO supplies industrial land and infrastructure; RFC provides eligible project finance; BIP promotes investments and coordinates facilitation/One Stop Shop clearances. RajNivesh is the online portal for applications, approvals and tracking, while DICCs provide district-level support to enterprises.

**Q3. Explain the main challenges and policy responses in Rajasthan's export sector.**  
**Model answer framework:** Challenges include distance from seaports, freight costs, finance, certification, market discovery and scale. The Export Promotion Policy 2024 supports documentation/certification, marketing, product testing and technology upgradation. Evaluate results through realised exports, new direct exporters, product diversification and actual support delivered.

### 10.3 Ten-mark questions

**Q1. “The effectiveness of industrial policy depends on implementation as much as on the subsidy announced.” Critically examine Rajasthan's industrial and MSME policy framework.**

**Suggested structure:**
- **Introduction:** Explain industry’s 26.55% share of current-price GSVA in the 2025–26 AE and the need for jobs, productivity and regional balance.
- **Instruments:** RIICO industrial infrastructure; RFC finance; BIP/RajNivesh facilitation; RIPS 2024 incentives; MSME Act facilitation; MLUPY interest subsidy; skill, technology and market access support.
- **Expected outcomes:** Lower set-up friction, investment, supplier development, formalisation, export readiness and employment.
- **Limitations/risks:** Fiscal cost of incentives, uneven regional uptake, water/energy constraints, credit access, compliance/safety needs, and the risk of counting proposals/MoUs as completed investment.
- **Way forward:** Publish time-bound implementation dashboards; measure disbursement and jobs rather than announcements alone; strengthen MSME credit and testing access; link incentives to verifiable outcomes; improve transport, reliable utilities and environmentally sustainable production.
- **Conclusion:** A transparent, predictable and implementation-tested regime can turn investment intentions into productive, geographically balanced industry.

**Q2. Examine the strategic opportunities presented by DMIC, mineral-based industries and HRRL for Rajasthan, while discussing the risks.**

**Suggested structure:**
- **Opportunities:** Freight/market access, engineering and auto clusters, stone/cement/metals, mining-linked value addition, petrochemicals, logistics and downstream manufacturing.
- **Complementarities:** Combine industrial areas, export assistance, skills, supplier networks and freight infrastructure.
- **Risks:** Land acquisition, water scarcity, industrial pollution, mine reclamation, commodity cycles, and the danger of treating proposed corridor nodes as already operational.
- **Policy requirements:** Reliable infrastructure, environmental enforcement, worker skills, local vendor development, waste minimisation and export-market diversification.
- **Conclusion:** Resource endowment offers a platform, but higher local value addition and sustainable infrastructure determine whether it generates resilient development.
