## 2. Land utilisation and agrarian geography

### 2.1 Land-use profile

The Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26 reports a total **reporting area of 343.43 lakh hectares** for 2024–25 (provisional). A reporting area is an agricultural-statistics concept based on land records; do not assume it must equal the state's geographical area expressed in hectares.

| Land-use category | Share of reporting area | Approximate area (lakh ha) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Net sown area | **53.02%** | 182.09 |
| Culturable waste land | 10.11% | 34.72 |
| Forest area (land-use classification) | 8.30% | 28.51 |
| Barren and unculturable land | **6.85%** | **about 23.53** |
| Land put to non-agricultural uses | 5.96% | 20.47 |
| Other fallow land | 5.62% | 19.30 |
| Current fallow | 5.22% | 17.93 |
| Permanent pastures and other grazing land | 4.81% | 16.52 |
| Miscellaneous tree crops and groves | 0.11% | 0.38 |
| **Total (shares)** | **100.00%** | **343.43 reporting area** |

**Correction to the source:** the original placed barren and unculturable land at 22.50 lakh ha / 6.55%. The latest state Economic Review chart gives **6.85%**, which implies about 23.53 lakh ha. The printed rounded category-area figures may not sum exactly to the total because of rounding/underlying tabulation; use the official total and share series.

### 2.2 Concepts that must not be confused

- **Net sown area:** area sown at least once during the agricultural year.
- **Gross cropped area:** total area sown across all crop seasons; an area sown twice counts twice.
- **Cropping intensity:** gross cropped area ÷ net sown area × 100.
- **Current fallow:** land left uncultivated during the current agricultural year.
- **Other fallow:** fallow land reported beyond current fallow, commonly across the preceding years specified by the statistical definition.
- **Culturable waste:** land capable of cultivation but not cultivated under the applicable reporting definition.
- **Forest land-use category is not identical to forest cover:** forest cover from the Forest Survey of India is a separate measure and should not be substituted into this table.

### 2.3 What the pattern means for Rajasthan

Rajasthan has a high dependence on monsoon timing and distribution. The policy response is not simply to expand irrigated acreage; it includes water-use efficiency, drought-resilient crops, rainwater harvesting, watershed development, soil-health management, agroforestry, storage and risk-sharing.

### Quick regional pattern

- **Western arid zones:** bajra, moth, guar, sesame; cumin and mustard in rabi where conditions permit.
- **Irrigated north-west:** cotton and guar in kharif; wheat, mustard and gram in rabi.
- **Eastern plains:** bajra, guar, groundnut and other crops shaped by rainfall and local soils.
- **Southern and south-eastern zones:** maize, pulses, paddy in suitable pockets, soybean, jowar and wheat.
- **RPSC trap:** use the official zone nomenclature and zone code; do not infer that every district in a zone grows every listed crop or that a crop is exclusive to one zone.

### 5.2 Seasonal logic

- **Kharif:** sowing broadly follows the monsoon; examples include bajra, maize, groundnut, cotton, soybean, guar and pulses.
- **Rabi:** follows the monsoon season, often relying on residual soil moisture and irrigation; examples include wheat, mustard, barley and gram.
- **Cash crops and spices:** guar, cumin, coriander and fenugreek are important regional crops, but their national production ranks must be stated with a year and reliable source.
- **Output versus productivity:** production can increase because of more area, better yield, or both. Productivity is normally expressed as kilograms per hectare; production is a total quantity.

### 5.3 Latest state-review production signals

The Economic Review 2025–26 contains estimates for 2025–26 as well as comparisons with 2024–25. For instance, it estimates kharif oilseed production at **41.23 lakh tonnes** versus **38.23 lakh tonnes** in 2024–25 (+7.85%), and rabi oilseed production at **59.23 lakh tonnes** versus **55.50 lakh tonnes** (+6.72%). These are estimates in a state review, not final post-harvest actuals.

## 6. Animal husbandry: output, census and breeds

### 6.1 Economic contribution

Livestock is not merely supplementary to crop farming in Rajasthan. It provides regular cash flow through milk and other products, and diversifies risk when crop yields are affected by low or irregular rainfall.

- The Economic Review 2025–26 reports livestock GVA of **₹2.17 lakh crore at current prices in 2025–26**, greater than the crop sector's GVA.
- The livestock-product gross value of output (GVO) composition at current prices in 2025–26 was:

| Product group | Share |
|---|---:|
| Milk | **79.60%** |
| Meat, including poultry | 7.46% |
| Other livestock products (including wool, dung and hides in the statistical grouping) | 12.27% |
| Eggs | 0.67% |
| **Total** | **100.00%** |

**Do not confuse:** GVA measures value added; GVO measures the gross value of output. Product shares in livestock GVO are not shares of total state GSVA.

## 7. Agricultural marketing and market infrastructure

### 7.1 Regulated markets and e-NAM

The Rajasthan Agricultural Produce Markets Act, 1961 provides the legal framework for regulated agricultural markets in the state. Regulated markets aim to improve market organisation, transparency, weighing and trading conditions; actual outcomes depend on implementation, market competition, facilities and farmer access.

- **Rajasthan State Agricultural Marketing Board (RSAMB):** state-level institution for agricultural marketing infrastructure and related development.
- **e-NAM (National Agriculture Market):** electronic trading and market-integration platform connecting participating mandis.
- The e-NAM state-wise unified-license page lists **173 Rajasthan mandis** in the portal table retrieved for this audit. This is a portal snapshot; integration totals can change and should be rechecked before memorising them for a later exam.

Avoid writing “140+” or “173” without adding the reference date or identifying the portal. Market-yard counts also vary with the definition—main mandi, sub-market yard, notified market or e-NAM-integrated mandi—and should not be interchanged.

**Source:** [e-NAM, state-wise unified licences and mandi information](https://www.enam.gov.in/web/state-unified-license/no-of-unified-licenses).

## 9. Rural livelihoods, women’s institutions and employment guarantee

### 9.1 Major change after 1 July 2026: rural employment law

The original source described the **MGNREGA 100-day guarantee plus 25 state-funded days** as the current Rajasthan model. That explanation is now outdated.

- The **Viksit Bharat—Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025**, commonly styled **VB–G RAM G Act, 2025**, received assent in December 2025 and replaced the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005.
- The Government of India notified commencement across rural areas from **1 July 2026**. From that date the 2005 Act stands repealed.
- The new Act provides a **statutory guarantee of 125 days of wage employment per rural household per financial year** for households whose adult members volunteer for unskilled manual work.

**The distinction matters:** under the previous framework, the central statutory guarantee was 100 days and Rajasthan had described an additional 25-day state guarantee. Under the new central Act, 125 days is the statutory national guarantee. Do not teach the old “100 + 25” formula as the current legal basis.

The original also claimed **200 days** for Sahariya, Kathodi and persons with disabilities. That is not stated as a general guarantee in the new central Act. Treat the earlier special provision as historical unless a current Rajasthan order or scheme establishes its continuation under the new framework.

**Sources:** [India Code: VB–G RAM G Act, 2025](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/handle/123456789/22478?view_type=browse); [PIB: commencement from 1 July 2026 and repeal of MGNREGA](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2259691&lang=1&reg=3); [PIB FAQ, 11 May 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/FaqDetails.aspx?ModuleId=4&NoteId=158511&lang=1&reg=3).

### 9.2 Rajivika and self-help-group structure

**Rajivika** is the Rajasthan Grameen Aajeevika Vikas Parishad, the state's rural-livelihoods institution for mobilising and supporting rural women through Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and federations.

A common institutional ladder is:

**SHG → Village Organisation (VO) → Cluster-Level Federation (CLF)**

This structure helps with savings, credit linkage, community institutions, collective enterprise, skill development, market linkage and livelihood diversification. Figures for the number of groups, members or loans should be dated to the official annual report or dashboard.

### 9.3 Rajasthan Mahila Nidhi

- Inaugurated on **26 August 2022**.
- Designed to make credit more accessible to women SHGs and members, and to support daily needs and self-employment.
- Government support includes interest subvention under specific loan products.
- Avoid memorising a universal loan limit or turnaround time unless it appears in current operating rules; lending terms can differ by product, eligibility and verification.

### 9.4 Watershed and water harvesting

Rajasthan's dryland context makes water harvesting, watershed works, farm ponds, check dams, anicuts, percolation tanks and restoration of traditional systems important. **Johads, tankas and khadins** are regionally adapted traditional water-management systems; modern watershed programmes can converge public investment with community maintenance.

## 10. The most important exam traps

1. **Data vintage:** operational holdings (76.55 lakh; average 2.73 ha) are from **Agriculture Census 2015–16**, not 2026.
2. **Land use:** 2024–25 provisional reporting area is **343.43 lakh ha**; net sown area is **53.02%**. Barren and unculturable land is **6.85%**, not 6.55%.
3. **Female operational holdings:** the 7.75-lakh figure is a 2015–16 operational-holding count; it is not synonymous with female ownership/title.
4. **Zone nomenclature:** use all ten official zone codes I-A, I-B, I-C, II-A, II-B, III-A, III-B, IV-A, IV-B and V. Learn the district/tehsil coverage from the current Economic Review, not a 50-district coaching table.
5. **Crop ranks:** 2023–24 state-review table ranks Rajasthan first in rapeseed-mustard, bajra, total oilseeds, coarse cereals and guar. It ranks second in groundnut and third in gram, total pulses and soybean.
6. **Crop-rank trap:** “coarse cereals” is not interchangeable with “nutri-cereals.” The source’s blanket claim that Rajasthan ranks first in nutri-cereals is unsupported by the cited comparison table.
7. **Livestock census:** use 20th Livestock Census (2019) for species population; the census is a DAHD-led national exercise, not conducted by the Board of Revenue at Ajmer.
8. **Output composition:** in 2025–26 livestock GVO, milk accounted for **79.60%**.
9. **Kisan Samman state support:** the Economic Review 2025–26 records annual state assistance increased to **₹3,000**, not ₹2,000.
10. **Animal insurance:** current budget documents highlight **Mukhyamantri Mangla Pashu Bima Yojana**; do not confuse its terms with earlier Mukhyamantri Kamdhenu Bima announcements.
11. **Employment guarantee:** from **1 July 2026**, VB–G RAM G Act, 2025 replaced MGNREGA and provides a 125-day statutory guarantee. “100 central + 25 state-funded” is the old framework description.
12. **Scheme amounts:** always attach a financial year, eligibility, whether amount is per person/animal/litre/year, and whether it is a target, budget provision, sanction or actual disbursement.
13. **e-NAM count:** the portal lists **173 Rajasthan mandis** in the snapshot reviewed; the count is dynamic.
14. **Rank vs share:** state rank, national share, area, production and productivity are different statistics and should not be mixed.
15. **GVA vs GVO:** value added is not the same as gross value of output; livestock product shares are not shares of total Rajasthan GSVA.

### 11.1 Two-mark answers

**Q1. What was Rajasthan's total reporting area and net sown-area share in 2024–25?**  
**Answer:** The provisional reporting area was 343.43 lakh hectares; net sown area accounted for 53.02%.

**Q2. What is the source vintage for Rajasthan's 76.55-lakh operational-holding figure?**

**Q3. Name five crops/groups in which Rajasthan ranked first in production in the state's 2023–24 comparative table.**  
**Answer:** Rapeseed and mustard, bajra, total oilseeds, coarse cereals and guar.

**Q4. What was the share of milk in livestock-product GVO at current prices in 2025–26?**  
**Answer:** 79.60%, according to the Rajasthan Economic Review 2025–26.

**Q5. What replaced MGNREGA from 1 July 2026?**  
**Answer:** The VB–G RAM G Act, 2025, which provides a statutory 125-day rural household wage-employment guarantee.

**Q6. What is the annual assistance under Rajasthan's Chief Minister Kisan Samman Nidhi according to the Economic Review 2025–26?**  
**Answer:** ₹3,000 per eligible farmer annually, as reported after the increase announced in the 2025–26 Budget.

### 11.2 Five-mark questions

**Q1. Explain why livestock is important to Rajasthan's rural economy.**

- Livestock provides income diversification where rainfall and crop yields are uncertain.
- The 2025–26 Economic Review reports livestock GVA at current prices of ₹2.17 lakh crore, higher than crop-sector GVA.
- Milk formed 79.60% of livestock-product GVO at current prices in 2025–26.
- Livestock supports household cash flow, nutrition, employment and value chains in dairy, wool, meat and other products.
- Constraints include fodder and water scarcity, animal disease, market access and declining populations of some draught/pastoral species.

**Q2. Describe the main structural changes in Rajasthan's operational landholdings.**

- Operational holdings rose from 68.88 lakh in 2010–11 to 76.55 lakh in 2015–16.
- Total operated area fell from 211.36 to 208.73 lakh hectares.
- Average size declined from 3.07 to 2.73 hectares.
- Marginal holdings formed the largest group at 40.12%; marginal and small holdings together were 62.02%.
- This strengthens the case for FPOs, cooperative input purchases, custom-hiring centres, micro-irrigation and targeted credit and extension.

**Q3. Explain the significance of the ten agro-climatic zones.**

- They reflect rainfall, soils, topography and cropping patterns across a large, climatically diverse state.
- The western zones are generally arid or semi-arid; north-western irrigated plains support crops such as cotton and wheat.
- Eastern and southern/eastern zones support different mixes of bajra, groundnut, maize, pulses, soybean and wheat.
- Zone-based planning can tailor seeds, irrigation, extension, insurance, watershed management and crop diversification.
- District and tehsil coverage should follow the latest state Economic Review because administrative labels may change.

**Q4. Explain the distinction between the old MGNREGA framework and the employment guarantee now in force.**

- MGNREGA, 2005 was replaced by the VB–G RAM G Act, 2025 from 1 July 2026.
- The new Act provides a statutory guarantee of 125 days per rural household per financial year.
- Under the earlier framework the central guarantee was 100 days, and Rajasthan described an additional 25-day state guarantee.
- The legal basis for 125 days is now the central Act; it should not be described merely as 100 central plus 25 state.
- Any claimed additional state entitlement for a special group must be verified against a current state order.

### 11.3 Ten-mark answer frameworks

**Q1. “Livestock is a stabilising pillar of Rajasthan's agrarian economy.” Critically examine.**

**Introduction:** Rajasthan's semi-arid and arid conditions make dependence on crops alone risky. Livestock complements crop farming and supports rural incomes.

**Economic evidence:**
- Livestock GVA at current prices was reported at ₹2.17 lakh crore in 2025–26.
- Livestock GVA exceeded the crop sector's GVA in the state review.
- Milk accounted for 79.60% of livestock-product GVO.

**Why it matters:**
- Diversifies household income and smooths cash flow during crop stress.
- Supports milk cooperatives, small producers, wool, meat and other value chains.
- Contributes to nutrition, employment and the local rural economy.

**Constraints:**
- Fodder and water shortages during drought; degraded pastures.
- Animal-disease risks, weak veterinary access in remote locations and market-price volatility.
- Decline in camel, donkey and sheep counts in the 2012–19 census comparison.

**Way forward:**
- Improve animal health services, vaccination and mobile veterinary access.
- Build fodder banks and improve common grazing land.
- Strengthen dairy procurement, chilling, producer organisations and value addition.
- Align livestock insurance and credit with current scheme rules, including Mangla Pashu Bima and Gopal Credit Card where eligible.

**Conclusion:** Livestock can buffer climate risk, but resilience depends on better feed, water, veterinary systems, insurance, markets and sustainable herd practices.

**Q2. Analyse landholding fragmentation in Rajasthan and suggest policy responses.**

**Empirical trend:** Agriculture Census 2015–16 shows more holdings but less total operated area; average size decreased from 3.07 to 2.73 ha. Marginal and small holdings together made up 62.02% of operational holdings.

**Implications:** smaller plots may make machinery ownership, irrigation investment and market bargaining more expensive; household labour may be under-employed; female operational holdings are rising but title, credit and extension barriers may persist.

**Policy response:** promote FPOs and producer groups; expand custom-hiring centres; enable shared equipment and water systems; improve warehouse receipts and pledge finance; support high-value horticulture where agro-climatically suitable; provide extension for climate-resilient seeds, soil health, livestock integration and micro-irrigation.

**Qualification:** smaller holdings do not automatically imply lower productivity in every setting. Outcomes depend on crop choice, irrigation, labour, technology, market access and collective organisation.

**Q3. Explain the implications of the VB–G RAM G Act, 2025 for rural development and Rajasthan.**

**Introduction:** The Act marks a change in India's statutory rural-employment framework and has applied from 1 July 2026.

**Key change:** replaces MGNREGA, 2005 and guarantees 125 days of wage employment per rural household annually, subject to statutory conditions.

**Potential rural-development links:** water security, rural infrastructure, livelihood-related assets, climate resilience, local planning and stronger monitoring are central features in the national framework.

**Rajasthan relevance:** drought-prone districts can benefit from appropriately planned water harvesting, watershed works and productive rural assets, while the state must align planning, staffing, payment processes and social accountability with the new statute and rules.

**Implementation questions:** budget-sharing rules, local planning capacity, availability and quality of works, wage timeliness, inclusion, digital access, grievance redress and independent social audit.

**Conclusion:** the shift should be evaluated not only through days generated but also through wage payment, work availability, inclusion, asset durability and local water/ livelihood outcomes.
