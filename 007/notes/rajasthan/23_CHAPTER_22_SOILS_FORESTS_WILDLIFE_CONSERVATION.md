## 1. Soils of Rajasthan: Scientific Taxonomy & Morphological Profiles

Soil is the foundational pedological skin of Rajasthan's agricultural economy. Due to acute variations in geological parent rock, precipitation gradients (from $<10\text{ cm}$ in Jaisalmer to $>100\text{ cm}$ in Jhalawar), temperature extremes, and paleo-drainage history, Rajasthan exhibits a complex soil mosaic.

### B. Traditional & ICAR Soil Classification of Rajasthan

1. **Sandy Desert Soil (*रेतीली बलुई मृदा*)**:
   - Covers over **60% of the state's geographical area** (Jaisalmer, Bikaner, Barmer, Balotra, Phalodi, Jodhpur).
   - High porosity, high permeability, coarse sand grains ($>85\%$ quartz), severe nitrogen and humus deficiency, but possesses adequate levels of **phosphates and calcium**.
2. **Red-Loam Soil (*लाल दोमट मृदा*)**:
   - Found predominantly in **Dungarpur, Banswara, Udaipur, and Salumbar**.
   - Derived from the weathering of ancient Archaean granites and schists; rich in **iron oxide** (which imparts its vibrant red coloration); deficient in nitrogen, phosphorus, and organic matter.
   - Soil texture is light and moisture-retaining, making it the **ideal pedological medium for Maize (*मक्का*) and Paddy**.
3. **Deep Black Regur Soil (*काली मृदा*)**:
   - Confined to the **Hadoti Plateau (Kota, Bundi, Baran, Jhalawar)**.
   - Weathered from Cretaceous basaltic Deccan trap lava sheets; rich in potash, calcium, and iron; highly clayey.
   - Known as **"Self-Ploughing Soil" (*स्वतः जुताई वाली मृदा*)**: Expands and becomes sticky upon wetting; shrinks upon drying to form deep, gaping vertical cracks that naturally aerate the root zone.
4. **Alluvial / Loam Soil (*जलोढ़ / कछारी / दोमट मृदा*)**:
   - Formed by the depositional flood silt of the Chambal, Banas, and Banganga rivers across **Jaipur, Dausa, Alwar, Bharatpur, Dholpur, and Karauli**.
   - Most productive soil in Rajasthan; neutral pH (7.0 to 7.8); rich in potash and lime.
5. **Saline & Alkaline Soils (*लवणीय एवं क्षारीय मृदा — रेह, कल्लर, उसर*)**:
   - Extensive patches in **Sanchore, Balotra, Barmer, Nagaur, and Bikaner**.
   - Characterized by excessive accumulation of soluble sodium salts ($NaCl, Na_2SO_4$).
   - **Chemical Reclamation Invariants**:
     - **Alkaline Soils ($pH > 8.5$)**: Treated using **Gypsum (*जिप्सम — $CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$*)**.
     - **Acidic Soils ($pH < 6.5$)**: Treated using **Lime / Limestone (*चूना — $CaCO_3$*)**.
     - **Saline Soils with high alkalinity**: Treated using **Rock Phosphate / Iron Pyrites**.

### C. Soil Erosion & Degradation Mechanisms
Soil erosion in Rajasthan is famously termed the **"Crawling Death of Agriculture" (*कृषि की रेंगती हुई मृत्यु*)**.
1. **Wind Erosion (*वायु अपरदन*)**:
   - Dominates the Western Sandy Plains (Jaisalmer, Bikaner, Barmer, Churu).
   - Hot summer westerly winds peel away dry, unvegetated topsoil, causing desertification and sand-dune migration (*Dhrian*).
2. **Water Erosion (*जल अपरदन*)**:
   - **Sheet Erosion (*चादरी अपरदन*)**: Sheet-wash occurs on the deforested, undulating mountain slopes of **Sirohi, Rajsamand, and Udaipur**, where monsoon rain washes away the fertile topsoil layer invisibly.
   - **Gully Erosion (*अवनालिका अपरदन*)**: Deep vertical channel incision caused by rapid surface runoff in the soft alluvial plains of the **Chambal Basin** (Sawai Madhopur, Karauli, Dholpur, and Kota), carving out the notorious **Beehad (*बीहड़*) and Utkhat Bhumi (Badlands)**.

### D. Soil Health Card Scheme (*मृदा स्वास्थ्य कार्ड योजना*)
- **Historical Launch**: Officially launched on **19 February 2015** by Prime Minister Narendra Modi from **Suratgarh (Sriganganagar district, Rajasthan)**.
- **National Motto / Slogan**: *"स्वस्थ धरा, खेत हरा"* (Swasth Dhara, Khet Hara — Healthy Earth, Green Field).
- **The 12 Soil Health Parameters Tested**:
  1. **Primary Macro-nutrients (3)**: Nitrogen ($N$), Phosphorus ($P$), Potassium ($K$).
  2. **Secondary Macro-nutrients (1)**: Sulphur ($S$).
  3. **Micro-nutrients (5)**: Zinc ($Zn$), Iron ($Fe$), Copper ($Cu$), Manganese ($Mn$), Boron ($B$).
  4. **Physical Parameters (3)**: Soil Reaction ($pH$), Electrical Conductivity ($EC$ — measuring salinity), Organic Carbon ($OC$).

### A. Statutory & Policy Framework
- **National Forest Policy (1988)**: Mandates that at least **33% of total geographical area** (60% in mountainous zones and 20% in plains) must be maintained under forest cover.
- **Rajasthan State Forest Policy (2010)**:
  - Formulated in **February 2010**; Rajasthan was the **first state in India** to notify a standalone state forest policy aimed at raising forest cover to **20%**.
- **Updated Rajasthan Forest Policy 2023**:
  - Unveiled on **5 June 2023** (World Environment Day); reaffirms the target of achieving 20% green cover by 2040 through massive agro-forestry, community forestry, and eco-tourism promotion.

   - Extent: Approximately **12,176 sq km**.

   - Extent: Approximately **2,123 sq km**.
   - Legal status: Forests not covered under the reserved or protected categories; tree felling and grazing are permitted upon payment of state royalties/fees.

### 1. Tropical Dry Deciduous / Dhok Forests (*धोक वन — Anogeissus pendula*)
- **Dominance**: The **most widespread forest type in Rajasthan**, accounting for approximately **58% to 60%** of total forest area.
- **Elevation & Climate**: Found at elevations between 240 and 760 metres; 50 to 80 cm annual rainfall.
- **Geographic Belt**: Foothills of the Aravallis and Vindhyan scarps (Kota, Bundi, Sawai Madhopur, Chittorgarh, Rajsamand, Alwar).
- **Dominant Species**: **Dhok (*धोक / धोकड़ा — Anogeissus pendula*)**. Provides superior charcoal (*Koyla*) and strong timber.

---

### 2. Mixed Deciduous Forests (*मिश्रित पर्णपाती वन*)
- **Share**: Covers approximately **28%** of total forest area; 50 to 80 cm rainfall zone.
- **Geographic Belt**: Udaipur, Rajsamand, Chittorgarh, Sirohi, Bhilwara.
- **Diagnostic Floristic Species**:
  - **Salar (*सालार — Boswellia serrata*)**: Found on dry rocky ridges above 450 m; gum resin (*Guggal*); wood used for packing boxes and matches.
  - **Dhak / Palas (*ढाक / पलाश — Butea monosperma*)**: Renowned as the **"Flame of the Forest" (*जंगल की ज्वाला*)** due to its fiery red-orange flowers in spring.
  - **Khair (*खैर — Acacia catechu*)**: Heartwood is boiled to extract **Kattha (*कत्था*)**; traditionally extracted by the nomadic **Kathodi tribe** in Udaipur and Baran.
  - **Mahua (*महुआ — Madhuca indica*)**: Hailed as the **"Kalpvriksha of the Tribals" (*आदिवासियों का कल्पवृक्ष*)**; flowers fermented to brew indigenous liquor (*Hathkadh / Mahudi*); oil extracted from seeds (*Dolma*).
  - **Tendu (*तेंदू — Diospyros melanoxylon*)**: Known as **"Timru" (*टिमरू*)** in tribal tracts; leaves harvested to manufacture **Bidis**. Nationalized by the Rajasthan Government in **1974**.
  - **Bamboo (*बांस — Dendrocalamus strictus*)**: The world's tallest grass; abundant in Banswara (the "City of Hundred Islands") and Mount Abu.

---

### 3. Dry Teak Forests (*शुष्क सागवान वन — Tectona grandis*)
- **Share**: Covers approximately **7%** of total forest area; high rainfall zone (75 to 110 cm).
- **Geographic Belt**: Southernmost Rajasthan — **Banswara, Dungarpur, Pratapgarh, Jhalawar, and Baran**.
- **Distinction**: **Banswara** is recognized as the **"Home of Teak Forests" (*सागवान का आगार*)**. Vulnerable to severe winter frost (*Pala*).

---

### 4. Tropical Thorn Forests (*उष्णकटिबंधीय कंटीले वन / मरुद्भिद*)
- **Share**: Covers approximately **6% to 7%** of total forest area; hyper-arid and semi-arid tracts ($<30\text{ cm}$ rain).
- **Adaptations**: Classic **Xerophytic (*मरुद्भिद*)** adaptations: stunted gnarled trunks, deep taproots, leathery leaves reduced to thorns to inhibit transpiration.
- **Dominant Species**: **Khejri (*Prosopis cineraria*)**, **Rohida (*Tecomella undulata*)**, Kair (*Capparis decidua*), Ber, Phog (*Calligonum polygonoides*), Hingota, and Babool.
- **Nutritious Grasses**: **Sewan (*लसियूरस सिंडिकस*)**, *Dhaman*, *Karad*, and *Anjan*.

---

### 5. Sub-Tropical Semi-Evergreen Forests (*उपोष्ण कटिबंधीय पर्वतीय सदाबहार वन*)
- **Share**: The **rarest forest type**, occupying **less than 0.5% (~0.39%)** of the state's forest area.
- **Geographic Sanctuary**: Restricted exclusively to the **Mount Abu plateau (Sirohi)** at elevations exceeding **1,200 metres**, where rainfall surpasses **150 cm**.
- **Dominant Species**: Wild Mango, Jamun, Bamboo, Kara (*Strobilanthes callosus*), and the endangered endemic flowering plant **Ambertari (*Dikliptera abuensis*)** found nowhere else on Earth.

### A. State Tree: Khejri (*खेजड़ी — Prosopis cineraria*)
- **Notification**: Declared State Tree on **31 October 1983**.
- **Classical & Vernacular Nomenclature**:
  - Vedic / Sanskrit: **Shami (*शमी*)**.
  - Local Rajasthani: **Janti (*जांटी*)**.
  - Sindhi: *Chhokra*; Kannada: *Banni*; Tamil: *Parambu*.
  - Popular Epithet: **"Kalpvriksha of Rajasthan"** or **"Wonder Tree of Thar"**.
- **Anatomy & Utility**:
  - Fruit/Pod: **Sangri (*सांगरी*)** — Harvested green, sun-dried, and cooked as a delicacy in *Panchkuta* curry.
  - Foliage/Leaves: **Loom (*लूम*)** — Protein-rich fodder for camels, goats, and sheep.
  - Flower: **Meejhar (*मीझर*)**.
- **Fungal Threats**: Severely damaged by root-boring insects (*Acanthophorus serraticornis*) and fungal blight (*Phaeotrichoconis crotalariae*).
- **The Khejarli Sacrifice (1730 CE)**:
  - Occurred on **Bhadrapada Shukla Dashami, VS 1787 (September 1730)** at Khejarli village (Jodhpur).
  - **Amrita Devi Bishnoi**, along with her three daughters (Asu, Ratni, Bhagu) and **363 Bishnoi men and women**, sacrificed their lives hugging Khejri trees to protect them from being felled by royal axes ordered by Maharaja Abhay Singh.
  - Celebrated annually as the **Khejarli Mela** (the world's only tree fair).
  - **Amrita Devi National Wildlife Protection Award**: Instituted in 1994 (first recipient: Gangaram Bishnoi).

---

### B. State Flower: Rohida (*रोहिड़ा — Tecomella undulata*)
- **Notification**: Declared State Flower on **31 October 1983**.
- **Epithets**: **"Teak of Marwar" (*मारवाड़ का टीक*)** or **"Marushobha" (*मरुशोभा*)**.
- **Floral Biology**: Blooms during **March–April**; produces fiery bell-shaped blossoms in brilliant shades of yellow, saffron, and deep orange-red. Wood is termite-resistant and prized for fine artistic carving.

---

### C. State Bird: Godawan (*गोडावण — Ardeotis nigriceps*)
- **Notification**: Declared State Bird on **21 May 1981**.
- **Epithets**: **Great Indian Bustard (GIB)**, *Sohan Chidia*, *Hukna*, *Gurjan*, *Maldhok*, *Tigdar*.
- **Conservation Status**:
  - IUCN Red List: **Critically Endangered (CR)**.
  - CITES: Appendix I; Wildlife Protection Act 1972: Schedule I.
  - Global population stands at under 150 individuals, with **over 90% concentrated in Rajasthan**.
- **Primary Habitats in Rajasthan**:
  1. **Desert National Park (DNP)**: Jaisalmer and Barmer.
  2. **Sorsan**: Baran district.
  3. **Sokhliya**: Kekri / Ajmer district.
- **Threats & Interventions**:
  - Fatal collisions with high-voltage overhead power transmission lines connecting solar parks.
  - **Project Great Indian Bustard**: Launched on **5 June 2013** by Rajasthan (first state in India to launch a dedicated GIB conservation project).
  - Captive Breeding Facility established at **Sam (Jaisalmer)** in partnership with the Wildlife Institute of India (WII).

---

### D. State Animal (Domestic): Camel (*ऊंट — Camelus dromedarius*)
- **Notification**: Declared State Heritage Animal on **30 June 2014** (formal gazette notification: **19 September 2014**).
- **Statutory Protection**: *Rajasthan Camel (Prohibition of Slaughter and Regulation of Temporary Migration or Export) Act, 2015*.
- **Breeds & Institutions**:
  - **National Research Centre on Camel (NRCC)**: Located at **Jorbeer, Bikaner**.
  - Distinctive Breeds: **Bikaneri** (sturdy draft camel), **Jaisalmeri** (slender racing camel), **Nachna** (celebrated across India for speed and gait), **Gomath** (riding camel of Phalodi), **Mewari** (dairy camel).
  - Traditional Camel Protectors: **Rebari / Raika** community; camel protector deity: **Pabu Ji**.

---

### E. State Animal (Wild): Chinkara (*चिंकारा — Gazella bennettii*)
- **Notification**: Declared State Animal on **22 May 1981**.
- **Morphology**: Slender Indian gazelle; horns present in both males and females; can survive without drinking direct water for weeks by absorbing moisture from desert dew and vegetation. Nahargarh Biological Park is famed for Chinkara conservation.

### A. The 3 National Parks of Rajasthan

#### 1. Ranthambore National Park (*रणथंभौर राष्ट्रीय उद्यान — सवाई माधोपुर*)
- **Notification History**:
  - Established as Sawai Madhopur Wildlife Sanctuary: **1955**.
  - Included under Project Tiger: **1973** (1st Tiger Reserve in Rajasthan).
  - Declared **National Park on 1 November 1980** (Rajasthan's 1st National Park).
- **Dimensions & Geography**: Core National Park area: **282.03 sq km**.
- **Landmarks & Lakes**:
  - Perched atop a hill inside the park is the 10th-century **UNESCO World Heritage Ranthambore Fort** with the historic Trinetra Ganesha Temple.
  - Major picturesque water bodies: **Padam Talao, Rajbagh Talao, Malik Talao**. Jogi Mahal hunting lodge on the banks of Padam Talao.
- **Tiger Eminence**: "Land of the Royal Bengal Tiger"; world-famous for diurnal tiger sightings; home of the legendary tigress **Machhli (T-16)**, acclaimed as the "Queen of Ranthambore" and most photographed tigress in natural history.

#### 2. Keoladeo Ghana National Park (*केवलादेव घाना राष्ट्रीय उद्यान — भरतपुर*)
- **Notification History**:
  - Duck-shooting preserve of Bharatpur Maharajas: 1850s.
  - Declared Wildlife Sanctuary: **1956**.
  - Declared **National Park on 27 August 1981**.
  - Designated Rajasthan's first **Ramsar Wetland Site on 1 October 1981**.
  - Inscribed on the **UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985**.
- **Dimensions & Ecosystem**: Compact area of **28.73 sq km**; a man-made, managed wetland created by Maharaja Suraj Mal by constructing the **Ajan Dam (*अजान बांध*)** on the Gambhir and Banganga rivers.
- **The "Bird Paradise of the World"**:
  - Serves as the premier winter breeding and feeding haven for over 370 species of aquatic birds.
  - Historic wintering home of the critically endangered **Siberian Crane (*साइबेरियन सारस*)**, which migrated 5,000 km across the Himalayas from Arctic Russia via the Ob river.
  - Internationally chronicled by the father of Indian ornithology, **Dr. Salim Ali** (*The Fall of a Sparrow*).
  - Water scarcity mitigated by the **Goverdhan Drain** and **Chambal-Dholpur-Bharatpur water project**.

#### 3. Mukundara Hills National Park (*मुकुंदरा हिल्स राष्ट्रीय उद्यान — कोटा, झालावाड़, चित्तौड़गढ़, बूंदी*)
- **Notification History**:
  - Formed in 2003 as Rajiv Gandhi National Park; renamed Mukundara Hills.
  - Officially notified as **National Park on 9 January 2012** (Rajasthan's 3rd National Park).
- **Area & Constituents**: Encompasses **199.55 sq km**; carved out by amalgamating three historical reserves: **Darrah Sanctuary, National Chambal Sanctuary, and Jawahar Sagar Sanctuary**.
- **Historical Monuments**: Houses the 8th-century **Abli Meeni ka Mahal (*अबली मीणी का महल*)** built by Maharao Mukund Singh Hada, the Badoli Shiva Temples, and Gagron Fort ramparts.

5. **Dholpur-Karauli Tiger Reserve (2023)**:
   - Approved by NTCA in **August 2023** as the **5th Tiger Reserve of Rajasthan and 54th Tiger Reserve of India**.
   - Spans the rugged Chambal ravines across Dholpur and Karauli, encompassing the **Kailadevi Wildlife Sanctuary**, Ram Sagar, and Van Vihar.

### C. The 2 Ramsar Wetland Sites of Rajasthan
1. **Keoladeo Ghana National Park (Bharatpur)**:
   - Designated on **1 October 1981** (along with Chilika Lake in Odisha, the first two Ramsar sites in India).
   - Inscribed on the **Montreux Record** in 1990 due to water scarcity and weed proliferation (*Paspalum distichum*).
2. **Sambhar Salt Lake (Tri-junction: Jaipur Rural, Didwana-Kuchaman, Ajmer)**:
   - Designated on **23 March 1990**.
   - Largest inland saline wetland in India; critical wintering station for hundreds of thousands of Northern Flamingos (*राजहंस*).

## 6. Top 25 Lethal Examination Traps & Ambiguities

1. **Most Widespread Soil Order in Rajasthan**: **Entisols** is the most widespread soil order in Rajasthan (present across all desert districts), NOT Aridisols!
2. **Vertisols Geography**: Vertisols (black shrink-swell clay) are found exclusively in the **Hadoti Plateau** (Kota, Bundi, Baran, Jhalawar) and small fringes of Chittorgarh, NOT in the Western Desert.
3. **Soil Health Card Launch Venue**: Launched on **19 February 2015 from Suratgarh (Sriganganagar)**, NOT from Jaipur or Delhi!
4. **Largest Legal Forest Category**: **Protected Forests (*संरक्षित वन*)** form the largest share (**56.49%**), NOT Reserved Forests (**37.05%**)!
5. **Actual Forest Cover vs Recorded Forest Area**:
   - Recorded Forest Area (RFA): **32,863 sq km (~9.60%)**.
   - Actual Forest Cover (ISFR): **16,655 sq km (~4.87%)**.
   - Tree Cover: **8,733 sq km (~2.55%)**.
   - Total Forest + Tree Cover: **25,388 sq km (~7.42%)**.
6. **Open Forests Form Largest Canopy Component**: Open Forest (10–40% canopy) accounts for **over 73%** of Rajasthan's actual forest cover; Very Dense Forest is barely **0.02% (78 sq km)**.
7. **District with Lowest Forest %**: **Jodhpur** has the lowest forest percentage (**0.48%**), while Churu has the lowest absolute forest area (77 sq km).
8. **Most Widespread Forest Species**: **Dhok (*Anogeissus pendula*)** covers **~58% to 60%** of Rajasthan's total forest area; Khejri dominates only in arid open scrub.
9. **Dry Teak Concentration**: **Banswara** is known as the "Home of Teak Forests" (*सागवान का आगार*); dry teak forests require high rainfall ($>75\text{ cm}$) and are vulnerable to frost.
10. **Tendu Nationalization Year**: Tendu leaf trade was nationalized by the Rajasthan Government in **1974**.
11. **State Tree Notification Date**: Khejri was notified on **31 October 1983** (the exact same day as Rohida, the State Flower).
12. **State Bird & Animal Notification Dates**: Godawan (Bird) and Chinkara (Wild Animal) were notified in **May 1981**.
13. **Camel Declaration Dates**: Declared State Heritage Animal on **30 June 2014**; official notification issued on **19 September 2014**.
14. **Smallest Sanctuary vs Largest Sanctuary**:
    - Largest Sanctuary: **Desert National Park (3,162 sq km)** in Jaisalmer-Barmer.
    - Smallest Sanctuary: **Sariska 'A' (3.01 sq km)** in Alwar (earlier Sajjangarh at 5.19 sq km).
15. **National Park Sequence**:
    1. Ranthambore (1980)
    2. Keoladeo Ghana (1981)
    3. Mukundara Hills (2012)
16. **Complete 5 Tiger Reserves Order**:
    1. Ranthambore (1973)
    2. Sariska (1978)
    3. Mukundara Hills (2013)
    4. Ramgarh Vishdhari (2022 — 52nd in India)
    5. Dholpur-Karauli (2023 — 54th in India)
17. **Ramgarh Vishdhari Tiger Reserve Location**: Located in **Bundi district** along the Mez River; serves as a vital corridor connecting Ranthambore and Mukundara.
18. **Montreux Record Wetland**: **Keoladeo Ghana** is the only wetland in Rajasthan placed on the Ramsar **Montreux Record** (due to water supply degradation).
19. **Desert National Park is NOT a National Park**: Despite its statutory name "National Park", **Desert National Park (DNP) is legally a Wildlife Sanctuary**, NOT a declared National Park!
20. **Flying Squirrel Sanctuary**: The Flying Squirrel (*Petaurista philippensis*) is found exclusively in **Sita Mata Wildlife Sanctuary** (Pratapgarh).
21. **Chemical Treatment of Alkaline vs Acidic Soil**:
    - Alkaline Soil: **Gypsum** ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$).
    - Acidic Soil: **Lime** ($CaCO_3$).
22. **Project Great Indian Bustard**: Launched on **5 June 2013**; Rajasthan was the **first state in India** to launch a dedicated project for the bustard.
23. **First Lion Safari of Rajasthan**: Located at **Nahargarh Biological Park (Jaipur)**.
24. **Snake Sanctuary of Rajasthan**: **Shergarh Wildlife Sanctuary (Baran)** is popularly called the "Sanctuary of Snakes" (*सांपों की शरणस्थली*).
25. **Dikliptera abuensis**: A rare endemic flowering medicinal plant found exclusively on **Mount Abu**.

## 7. Model Examination Questions & Benchmark Frameworks

### 2-Marker Benchmark Responses (15 Words)

#### Q1. Differentiate between Entisols and Aridisols in Rajasthan.
**Model Answer**:  
**Entisols** are immature, widely distributed sandy soils with weak A-C horizons. **Aridisols** are mature arid soils possessing diagnostic calcic or cambic horizons ($CaCO_3$ nodules).

#### Q2. What is the legal distinction between Reserved Forests and Protected Forests?
**Model Answer**:  
**Reserved Forests (37.05%)**: Complete state monopoly, all grazing/felling banned. **Protected Forests (56.49%)**: Controlled grazing/felling permitted with permits.

#### Q3. State the location and national standing of the Ramgarh Vishdhari Tiger Reserve.
**Model Answer**:  
Located in **Bundi district** along the Mez River; notified in **May 2022** as Rajasthan's 4th and India's **52nd Tiger Reserve**.

#### Q4. What is the ecological significance of Sita Mata Wildlife Sanctuary?
**Model Answer**:  
Located in Pratapgarh; renowned for the nocturnal **Flying Squirrel**, Four-horned Antelope (Chowsingha), rich medicinal flora, and origin of Jakham River.

### 5-Marker Benchmark Responses (50 Words)

#### Q5. Analyze the spatial distribution and silvicultural characteristics of Dhok forests in Rajasthan.
**Model Answer**:  
1. **Spatial Profile**: Dhok (*Anogeissus pendula*) is the **most widespread forest type in Rajasthan**, covering **~58% to 60%** of total forest area. Found at 240–760 m elevation along Aravalli and Vindhyan foothills in Kota, Bundi, Sawai Madhopur, Chittorgarh, and Alwar.
2. **Characteristics**: Highly drought-resistant; thrives in dry shallow soils with 50–80 cm rainfall. Yields dense, high-calorific firewood, charcoal, and durable agricultural timber, but suffers from over-lopping by pastoralists.

#### Q6. Enumerate the primary threats facing the Godawan (Great Indian Bustard) and outline government conservation measures.
**Model Answer**:  
1. **Threats**: Extreme habitat fragmentation in the Thar, fatal overhead collisions with high-voltage renewable power lines, predation of ground clutches by feral dogs and wild boars; IUCN status is **Critically Endangered**.
2. **Conservation Measures**:
   - Launched **Project Great Indian Bustard (2013)** (1st in India).
   - Established a dedicated captive breeding centre at **Sam (Jaisalmer)** with WII.
   - Installation of bird-diverters on power transmission corridors.
   - Protected enclosures in Desert National Park, Sorsan, and Sokhliya.

1. **Statutory Protected Area Framework**:
   - Rajasthan encompasses **3 National Parks** (Ranthambore, Keoladeo Ghana, Mukundara Hills), **5 Tiger Reserves**, **2 Ramsar Wetland Sites**, and **36+ Conservation Reserves**, protecting diverse biomes from the hyper-arid Thar to the humid Vindhyan scarplands.

2. **Evolution of Tiger Reserves in Rajasthan**:
   - **Ranthambore (1973)**: Pioneer reserve under Kailash Sankhala ("Tiger Man of India"); core source population for northern India.
   - **Sariska (1978)**: Recovered from 2004–05 poaching crisis via world's first helicopter tiger translocations in 2008.
   - **Mukundara Hills (2013)**: Established as a 417 sq km core to decongest Ranthambore.
   - **Ramgarh Vishdhari (2022 — 52nd in India)**: Bundi; critical stepping-stone connecting Ranthambore with Mukundara.
   - **Dholpur-Karauli (2023 — 54th in India)**: Spans the rugged ravines of Chambal and Kailadevi sanctuary.

3. **Ecological Imperative of Contiguous Wildlife Corridors**:
   - **Genetic Viability**: Prevents dangerous inbreeding depression by facilitating natural gene flow between isolated tiger metapopulations.
   - **Territorial Dispersal**: Ranthambore's tiger density exceeds carrying capacity ($>75$ tigers), causing fatal territorial infighting. Contiguous corridors along the Chambal and Mez rivers allow sub-adult tigers to disperse naturally into Ramgarh Vishdhari, Mukundara, and Dholpur-Karauli.
   - **Mitigation of Human-Wildlife Conflict**: Reduces accidental tiger straying into human-inhabited agro-pastoral tracts.

4. **Conservation Challenges & Future Roadmap**:
   - **Linear Infrastructure Severance**: National highways and railway lines bisect corridors. Requires mandatory **eco-bridges, animal underpasses, and acoustic barriers**.
   - **Voluntary Village Relocation**: Accelerating fair compensation ($₹15\text{ lakh}$ or land) to create inviolate core breeding zones.
   - **Involvement of Local Communities**: Incentivizing eco-development committees (EDCs) through nature tourism revenue, transforming local villagers from reluctant neighbors into frontline guardians of Rajasthan's biodiversity.
