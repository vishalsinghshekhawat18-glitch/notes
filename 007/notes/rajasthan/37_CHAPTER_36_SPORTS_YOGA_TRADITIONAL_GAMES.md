## 36.1 Institutional Sports Architecture: Rajasthan State Sports Council (RSSC)

The promotion, development, and regulatory governance of sports in Rajasthan is steered by a centralized statutory and institutional machinery designed to discover grassroots talent and train elite athletes for international representation.

```

      │                              │                              │

└── Specialized Academies      └── DSP/RAS Direct Appointment └── Traditional Sports
```

### 1. Rajasthan State Sports Council (RSSC - राजस्थान राज्य क्रीड़ा परिषद)
- **Genesis & Establishment**: Constituted on **1 January 1957** under the Department of Youth Affairs & Sports, Government of Rajasthan.
- **Headquarters**: **Sawai Mansingh (SMS) Stadium, Jaipur**.
- **First President**: **V.G. Patel**.
- **Core Mandates**:
  - Apex coordinating body for sports federations and district sports associations in Rajasthan.
  - Organizing state coaching camps, administering state sports awards (*Maharana Pratap* and *Guru Vashishta* awards).
  - Maintenance and management of state-owned sports complexes, stadiums, synthetic tracks, and indoor arenas.
  - Distribution of sports grants and equipment to rural taluks and educational institutions.

### 2. Major Stadiums & International Sports Infrastructure
- **Sawai Mansingh (SMS) Stadium, Jaipur**: Multi-purpose international sporting complex featuring an international cricket stadium, FIFA-standard football ground, 8-lane synthetic athletics track, Olympic-size swimming pool, and an air-conditioned indoor stadium.
- **Barkatullah Khan Stadium, Jodhpur**: Major international cricket venue in western Rajasthan.
- **Maharana Pratap Khel Gaon, Udaipur**: Integrated multi-sport complex catering to southern Rajasthan.
- **Anil Agarwal International Cricket Stadium (Chop, Jaipur)**: Under construction at Chop village on the Jaipur-Delhi highway; planned for **75,000 capacity**, making it the **second largest cricket stadium in India** (after Narendra Modi Stadium, Ahmedabad) and the **third largest in the world**.

### 1. Out-of-Turn Government Appointments (Service Cadres)
Under the **Rajasthan Out-of-Turn Appointment to Sports Medal Winners Rules**:
- Olympic and Paralympic medal winners are offered direct appointments to elite state services: **Rajasthan Administrative Service (RAS)**, **Rajasthan Police Service (RPS)**, or **Deputy Superintendent of Police (DSP)**.
- Asian and Commonwealth medal winners are recruited directly into Sub-Inspector of Police, Forest Range Officer, or Grade-I physical training cadres.
- **2% Reservation for Outstanding Sportspersons**: Mandatory 2% horizontal reservation across all direct recruitments in state government departments and public sector undertakings.

### 2. State Sports Awards (Rajya Khel Puraskar)
1. **Maharana Pratap Award (महाराणा प्रताप पुरस्कार)**:
   - **Instituted**: **1982–83**.
   - **Nature**: The highest sports award in Rajasthan conferred upon outstanding players demonstrating exemplary performance at national and international levels.
   - **Prize**: Upgraded to **₹5,00,000 (₹5 Lakh)** cash prize, a bronze trophy depicting Maharana Pratap on his steed Chetak, a blazer, and a citation scroll.
2. **Guru Vashishta Award (गुरु वशिष्ठ पुरस्कार)**:
   - **Instituted**: **1985–86**.
   - **Nature**: The highest sports coaching award in Rajasthan conferred upon elite coaches and mentors who train athletes achieving international and national laurels.
   - **Prize**: Upgraded to **₹5,00,000 (₹5 Lakh)** cash prize, a bronze statue of Sage Vashishta, a ceremonial blazer, and a citation.

## 36.4 Rajiv Gandhi Rural & Urban Olympic Games: Mass Movement

- **Historic Launch Date**: **29 August 2022** (National Sports Day, birth anniversary of Major Dhyan Chand), flagged off from **Pal / Kankani village (Jodhpur)**.
- **Guinness World Record**: Registered as the **world's largest mass participation sporting tournament**, drawing over **30 lakh participants in 2022** and expanding to over **58 lakh participants** across rural and urban editions.
- **Four-Tier Hierarchy**:
  1. *Gram Panchayat / Ward Level*
  2. *Block / Municipality Level*
  3. *District Level*
  4. *State Level (SMS Stadium, Jaipur)*.
- **Sporting Disciplines Contested**:
  - *Rural Events*: Kabaddi, Shooting Ball (Boys), Volleyball, Tennis-Ball Cricket, Kho-Kho (Girls), and Hockey.
  - *Urban Additions*: Athletics (100m, 200m, 400m), Football, and Basketball.
- **Socio-Developmental Objectives**:
  - Grassroots scouting of rural sports prodigies.
  - Fostering communal and inter-caste harmony across villages.
  - Promoting anti-drug awareness (*Nasha Mukti Abhiyan*) and healthy aging (no age restrictions; senior citizens played alongside youth).

## 36.5 Science of Yoga: Philosophy, Asanas, Pranayama & Shatkarma

Yoga represents an integral component of RPSC RAS Paper 3 (Unit III). Grounded in the classical Indian philosophical tradition of **Maharshi Patanjali's Yoga Sutras**, it is systematically taught across state physical education curricula and universities.

### 1. The Eight Limbs of Classical Yoga (Ashtanga Yoga)
- **1. Yama (यम - 5 Universal Restraints)**:
  - *Ahimsa* (Non-violence in thought, word, and deed).
  - *Satya* (Truthfulness).
  - *Asteya* (Non-stealing / absence of covetousness).
  - *Brahmacharya* (Continence / moderation in sensual desires).
  - *Aparigraha* (Non-possessiveness / non-accumulation of excess wealth).
- **2. Niyama (नियम - 5 Personal Observances)**:
  - *Saucha* (Internal and external purity/cleanliness).
  - *Santosha* (Contentment with what is attained).
  - *Tapas* (Austerity, spiritual endurance, discipline).
  - *Svadhyaya* (Self-study and reflection upon sacred texts).
  - *Ishvara Pranidhana* (Surrender to the Supreme Divine Consciousness).
- **3. Asana (आसन)**: *"स्थिरसुखमासनम्"* (*Sthira-sukham-asanam*)—A physical posture that is firm, stable, yet relaxed and comfortable. Categorized into meditative postures (*Padmasana*, *Siddhasana*), cultural/corrective postures (*Bhujangasana*, *Paschimottanasana*, *Sarvangasana*), and relaxing postures (*Shavasana*, *Makarasana*).
- **4. Pranayama (प्राणायाम)**: Conscious regulation, expansion, and cessation of the respiratory rhythm. Three phases: *Puraka* (inhalation), *Kumbhaka* (retention—internal *Antar* or external *Bahir*), and *Rechaka* (exhalation). Core forms include *Nadi Shodhana / Anulom Vilom*, *Bhastrika*, *Ujjayi*, *Sheetali*, *Sheetkari*, and *Bhramari*.
- **5. Pratyahara (प्रत्याहार)**: Intentional decoupling and withdrawal of sensory organs from external temptations.
- **6. Dharana (धारणा)**: Fixing consciousness upon a single focal point (navel center, heart lotus, eyebrow center).
- **7. Dhyana (ध्यान)**: Continuous, uninterrupted flow of cognition toward the focal object without mental distraction.
- **8. Samadhi (समाधि)**: Ultimate transcendence where the individual meditator, the act of meditation, and the object of meditation merge into unitary consciousness.

### 2. Shatkarma: The Six Yogic Purification Kriyas (Hatha Yoga Pradipika)
Before practicing advanced pranayama, the classical texts mandate somatic purification (*Shatkarma*) to balance the three doshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha):
1. **Neti (नेति)**: Cleansing of nasal passages using warm saline water (*Jala Neti*) or waxed thread/rubber catheter (*Sutra Neti*).
2. **Dhauti (धौति)**: Cleansing of the digestive tract and stomach using a long muslin cloth strip (*Vastra Dhauti*) or salty water regurgitation (*Vamana Dhauti / Kunjal Kriya*).
3. **Nauli (नौली)**: Isolation and horizontal rolling churning of the abdominal rectus muscles to kindle digestive fire (*Jatharagni*).
4. **Basti (बस्ति)**: Yogic colonic irrigation utilizing water suction into the lower bowels.
5. **Kapalabhati (कपालभाति)**: Rapid, forceful involuntary exhalations through diaphragmatic contractions with passive inhalation, purifying the frontal brain sinuses and respiratory tract.
6. **Trataka (त्राटक)**: Steady, unblinking gazing upon a flame or small point until tears flow, calming the optic nerves and awakening focus.

### Detailed Forensic Profiles of Iconic Sportspersons

#### 1. Devendra Jhajharia (देवेन्द्र झाझड़िया)
- **Origin**: Born in Rungpura village, **Churu district**. Lost his left hand at age eight after accidentally touching a live 11 kV electric wire.
- **Historic Achievements**:
  - **First Indian to win TWO Gold Medals at the Paralympic Games**:
    1. **Athens 2004 Paralympics**: Won Gold in Men's Javelin Throw (F46) with a World Record throw of **62.15 meters**.
    2. **Rio 2016 Paralympics**: Won Gold with another World Record throw of **63.97 meters**.
    3. **Tokyo 2020 Paralympics**: Won Silver Medal with a personal best of **64.35 meters**.
  - **National Honors**: Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (2017 - first para-athlete to receive it), Padma Shri (2012), and **Padma Bhushan (2022 - first para-athlete in Indian history to be conferred the Padma Bhushan)**.
  - **Administrative Leadership**: Elected unopposed as the **President of the Paralympic Committee of India (PCI)** in March 2024.

#### 2. Avani Lekhara (अवनि लेखरा)
- **Origin**: Born in **Jaipur**. Paralyzed waist down (paraplegia) following a traumatic automobile road accident in 2012 at age 11.
- **Historic Achievements**:
  - **First Indian woman to win a Paralympic Gold Medal**: At **Tokyo 2020 Paralympics**, clinched Gold in Women's 10m Air Rifle Standing (SH1) with a world record-equalling score of **249.6 points**.
  - Clinched **Bronze Medal** in Women's 50m Rifle 3 Positions (SH1) at Tokyo 2020, becoming the first Indian woman to win multiple medals in a single Paralympic edition.
  - **Paris 2024 Paralympics Defense**: Created history by successfully defending her **Gold Medal** in 10m Air Rifle Standing (SH1) with a Paralympic record score of **249.7 points**, cementing her status as India's greatest female Paralympian.
  - **Honors**: Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (2021), Padma Shri (2022), appointed Brand Ambassador for Rajasthan's *Beti Bachao, Beti Padhao* campaign and Assistant Conservator of Forests (ACF).

#### 3. Colonel Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore (कर्नल राज्यवर्धन सिंह राठौड़)
- **Origin**: Born in **Jaisalmer**; distinguished Indian Army shooting prodigy (Grenadiers regiment).
- **Historic Achievement**: Won the **Silver Medal in Men's Double Trap Shooting at the Athens 2004 Olympic Games**—the **first individual Olympic silver medal won by independent India**.
- **Honors**: Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna (2004), Padma Shri (2005), Ati Vishisht Seva Medal (AVSM). Later served as Union Minister of State for Information & Broadcasting and Youth Affairs & Sports; currently a senior Cabinet Minister in Rajasthan Government.

#### 4. Limba Ram (लिम्बा राम)
- **Origin**: Born into a poor tribal Bhil family at Saradeet village, **Udaipur district**.
- **Historic Achievements**:
  - Discovered during a grassroots tribal archery scouting camp organized by Sports Authority of India (SAI) in 1987.
  - Represented India at **three consecutive Olympic Games** (Seoul 1988, Barcelona 1992, Atlanta 1996).
  - At the **1992 Asian Archery Championships in Beijing**, clinched the **Gold Medal** in 30-meter event, equalling the then World Record score of **357 out of 360 points**.
  - Conferred the **Arjuna Award (1991)** and **Padma Shri (2012)**. Served as National Archery Coach.

#### 5. Bajrang Lal Takhar (बजरंग लाल ताखर)
- **Origin**: Born in Banur village, **Sikar district** (Shekhawati belt); served in the Indian Army.
- **Historic Achievement**: Clinched the **Gold Medal in Men's Single Sculls Rowing at the Guangzhou 2010 Asian Games**, becoming the **first Indian rower to win an individual Asian Games Gold**. Previously won Silver at Doha 2006.
- **Honors**: Arjuna Award (2008), Padma Shri (2013).

#### 6. Sriram Singh Shekhawat (श्रीराम सिंह शेखावत)
- **Origin**: Born in Badhal village, **Jaipur district**; legendary middle-distance runner trained by iconic coach Ilyas Babar.
- **Historic Achievement**: Clinched multiple Asian Games Golds (Tehran 1974, Bangkok 1978 in 800m). At the **Montreal 1976 Summer Olympics**, finished 7th in the Men's 800m final with a blistering time of **1 minute 45.77 seconds**, which stood as the **unbroken Asian Record for 42 years** (until 2018).
- **Honors**: Arjuna Award (1973), Padma Shri.

## 36.8 RPSC RAS 2-Marker, 5-Marker & 10-Marker Model Answers

### Q1 (2 Marks): What is the official State Game of Rajasthan and when was it declared?
> **Answer**: The official State Game of Rajasthan is **Basketball (बास्केटबॉल)**, officially designated in **1948**. The state boasts an illustrious basketball pedigree, producing legendary Arjuna Awardees like Khushi Ram, Hanuman Singh, and Ajmer Singh.

### Q2 (2 Marks): Distinguish between the Maharana Pratap Award and the Guru Vashishta Award.
> **Answer**:
> - **Maharana Pratap Award (1982–83)**: Highest state sports award given to outstanding **athletes/players** for international and national excellence; carries a cash prize of **₹5,00,000**.
> - **Guru Vashishta Award (1985–86)**: Highest state sports award given to distinguished **coaches and mentors** who train medal-winning athletes; carries a cash prize of **₹5,00,000**.

### Q3 (5 Marks): Summarize the historic sporting achievements of Avani Lekhara in the Paralympic Games.
> **Answer**:
> 1. **Tokyo 2020 Milestone**: Became the **first Indian woman to win a Paralympic Gold Medal** by clinching the title in Women's 10m Air Rifle Standing (SH1) with a world record-equalling score of **249.6 points**.
> 2. **Multi-Medal Record**: Clinched a **Bronze Medal** in Women's 50m Rifle 3 Positions (SH1) at the same Tokyo Games, becoming the first Indian woman to win multiple medals in a single Paralympics.
> 3. **Paris 2024 Title Defense**: Successfully defended her **Gold Medal** in 10m Air Rifle Standing (SH1) with a new Paralympic record of **249.7 points**, cementing her position as India's greatest female Paralympian.
> 4. **National Laurels**: Conferred the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (2021) and Padma Shri (2022).

### Q4 (5 Marks): Describe the eight limbs of Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga, categorizing them into Bahiranga and Antaranga.
> **Answer**:
> Enunciated by Maharshi Patanjali in the *Yoga Sutras*, the eight limbs are categorized into:
> - **Bahiranga Yoga (External Disciplines)**:
>   1. *Yama*: Social ethics and self-restraints (Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha).
>   2. *Niyama*: Personal self-purification and observances (Saucha, Santosha, Tapas, Svadhyaya, Ishvara Pranidhana).
>   3. *Asana*: Stable, comfortable physical postures (*Sthira-sukham-asanam*).
>   4. *Pranayama*: Regulation and mastery of vital respiration (Puraka, Kumbhaka, Rechaka).
>   5. *Pratyahara*: Sensory withdrawal from external distractions (bridge to inner limbs).
> - **Antaranga Yoga (Internal Disciplines)**:
>   6. *Dharana*: Focused, single-pointed concentration on an internal or external object.
>   7. *Dhyana*: Sustained, uninterrupted stream of meditative awareness.
>   8. *Samadhi*: Pure transcendental absorption where the meditator, object, and process merge.

### Q5 (10 Marks): Evaluate the strategic sports policies, out-of-turn recruitment schemes, and grassroot initiatives (Rajiv Gandhi Olympics) implemented in Rajasthan, analyzing their impact on producing world-class athletes.
> **Answer**:
> **1. The Modern Paradigm of Sports in Rajasthan**:
> - Historically, Rajasthan's athletic achievements were isolated successes of individual military or police personnel. Over the past decade, the State has engineered a structural paradigm shift, transforming sports into a viable professional career through institutional backing and life-changing financial security.
>
> **2. Financial & Career Safety Nets**:
> - **Substantial Cash Incentives**: Awarding **₹3 Crore for Olympic/Paralympic Gold**, ₹2 Crore for Silver, and ₹1 Crore for Bronze represents one of the most generous incentive structures globally.
> - **Out-of-Turn Direct Appointments**: The *Out-of-Turn Rules* offer direct recruitment into elite state services (RAS, RPS, DSP) for international medalists. Icons like Devendra Jhajharia and Avani Lekhara were secured in high state offices without having to compete in routine recruitment exams.
> - **2% Horizontal Reservation**: Mandating 2% reservation for outstanding sportspersons across all government recruitments ensures career security for national-level contenders.
>
> **3. Grassroot Scouting: Rajiv Gandhi Olympics**:
> - Launched on **29 August 2022**, this movement drew over **58 lakh participants** across rural and urban panchayats and wards.
> - It revived traditional indigenous sports like Kabaddi, Kho-Kho, and Shooting Ball, creating a direct pipeline for state scouts to identify raw athletic talent in remote villages.
>
> **4. Infrastructure & Academy Ecosystem**:
> - Establishment of specialized residential academies catering to regional and physical advantages: Archery in tribal Dungarpur/Udaipur, Boys Basketball in Jaisalmer, Cycling in Bikaner, and Kabaddi in Jhunjhunu.
> - Expansion of world-class facilities: Synthetic tracks at SMS Stadium, Barkatullah Khan Stadium upgrade, and the upcoming 75,000-seater Chop International Cricket Stadium.
>
> **5. Enduring Challenges & The Road Ahead**:
> - **Coaching Quality**: Acute shortage of certified NIS coaches and international sports scientists at the block level.
> - **Dietary Support**: Grassroots athletes in drought-prone or tribal pockets often suffer from protein deficits, necessitating specialized nutritional funding at school levels.
> - **Governance & Politicization**: Factionalism in state sports associations occasionally hampers fair athlete selection, requiring strict enforcement of the National Sports Development Code.
