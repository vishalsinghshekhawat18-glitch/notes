## 36.1 Rajasthan's sports-administration architecture

### Rajasthan State Sports Council (RSSC)

The Rajasthan State Sports Council is the apex state-level body for promotion and development of sports under the Department of Youth Affairs and Sports, Government of Rajasthan.

- **Establishment:** The Council's official website dates its establishment to **1958**, not 1 January 1957.
- **Legal form:** A registered organisation under the Rajasthan Societies Registration Act, 1958; it is not correctly described simply as a statutory authority created by a separate sports statute.
- **Headquarters:** Sawai Man Singh (SMS) Stadium, Jaipur.
- **Main functions:** Grassroots talent identification, coaching camps, district sports development, competitions, training facilities, sports awards, athlete support and management of Council facilities.
- **First president:** The source claim “V. G. Patel” has not been independently confirmed in the current official Council history and is omitted from the memorisation list.

The Council's official history records its **first residential sports training camp at Mount Abu in 1959**. It also reports a history of specialised camps for athletes from tribal areas.  
Official reference: [RSSC — About Us](https://rssc.rajasthan.gov.in/content/raj/sports/rajasthan-state-sports-council/en/overview/about-us.html)

### Major facilities and stadiums

| Facility | Location | Exam-useful description |
|---|---|---|
| Sawai Man Singh (SMS) Stadium | Jaipur | Major state sports complex and the RSSC headquarters |
| Barkatullah Khan Stadium | Jodhpur | Important cricket stadium in western Rajasthan |

**Proposed Chop cricket stadium:** A stadium project at Chop near Jaipur has been described in public reporting as a large proposed facility, with a planned capacity often stated as 75,000. The evidence reviewed for this chapter does not establish that it is an operational international venue or justify memorising claims such as “second-largest in India” or “third-largest in the world.” Treat project location, capacity and completion status as **date-sensitive** and check an official Rajasthan Cricket Association/state update.

## 36.2 State awards, cash incentives and athlete recruitment

### State sports awards

The RSSC official history lists the following institutional dates:

- **Maharana Pratap Award:** Conferred since **1982–83** for outstanding sportspersons.
- **Guru Vashishtha Award:** Instituted in **1985–86** to recognise coaches who have produced athletes of national or international standing.

The Council lists separate award rules for 2025. The original chapter's stated **₹5 lakh** prize amount may reflect the recent award framework, but the current rule PDF should be consulted for the relevant award year, eligibility, documentation and prize components before the amount is memorised. Award years and award rules are more securely established than a cash figure copied without the rule text.

Official references: [RSSC About Us](https://rssc.rajasthan.gov.in/content/raj/sports/rajasthan-state-sports-council/en/overview/about-us.html) and [RSSC Downloads & Forms](https://rssc.in/downloads_forms.htm).

### Cash awards for major international medals

Rajasthan has publicly announced large cash awards for international medal winners. One well-documented example is the 2021 Tokyo Paralympics: the State announced **₹3 crore for Avani Lekhara's gold, ₹2 crore for Devendra Jhajharia's silver, and ₹1 crore for Sundar Singh Gurjar's bronze**. Later public reporting also records ₹1 crore for a Commonwealth Games gold and ₹30 lakh for a bronze in 2026.

| Event group | Commonly reported award schedule | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Olympic / Paralympic Games | Gold ₹3 crore; Silver ₹2 crore; Bronze ₹1 crore | Consistent with the State's publicly announced 2021 Paralympic payouts |
| Asian / Commonwealth Games and corresponding para events | Gold ₹1 crore; Silver commonly reported as ₹60 lakh; Bronze ₹30 lakh | Confirm the exact current government order and event class before applying the figure to a specific medal |
| World Cups / World Championships | Do **not** automatically apply the Olympic schedule | A distinct event-specific rule or government sanction may apply |

These are **policy-dependent amounts**, not a constitutional entitlement applicable regardless of event, domicile, medal class or current notification. Check the latest order for the specific competition and medal before treating the amount as a final answer.

### Out-of-turn appointment rules: what they do and do not say

Rajasthan has a specific **Out-of-Turn Appointment to Sports Medal Winners Rules, 2017**. These rules are eligibility-based and define the sports achievements and posts covered; they do not establish an unrestricted right to choose any state service.

Illustrative posts listed in the rules include:

- **State services:** Deputy Superintendent of Police, Deputy Superintendent Jail, Assistant Conservator of Forests, Assistant Excise Officer (Preventive), Lecturer in Physical Education / Coach, and Deputy Commandant.
- **Subordinate services:** Physical Training Instructor grades, Sub-Inspector / Constable, Assistant Jailor / Warder, Forest Range Officer / Forester / Forest Guard and certain other listed posts.

**Critical correction:** The rules reviewed do **not** list the Rajasthan Administrative Service (RAS) as a guaranteed direct appointment for Olympic medal winners. Do not teach “Olympic medal = direct RAS appointment.” Appointment depends on the applicable rules, eligible medal, time limits, available or deemed-created posts, physical/medical requirements and government approval.

The 2017 rules state that the relevant achievements must be after **1 January 2016**, and an eligible medal winner must apply within **three years** of winning the medal. Because amendments can occur, verify the latest consolidated rules before answering a question asking about the current recruitment position.

Official reference: [Rajasthan Out-of-Turn Appointment Rules, 2017 (PDF)](https://yas.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/sports/rssc/pdfs/out-of-turn-form-%26-rules/out-of-turn-2017-English.pdf).

### Reservation for outstanding sportspersons

A **2% horizontal reservation** is recorded in Rajasthan service rules for outstanding sportspersons in the applicable direct-recruitment vacancies. The general-service rules specify **2% of the year's direct-recruitment vacancies outside the purview of the Commission**, subject to eligibility and the rule's definition of outstanding sportsperson. The reservation is adjusted horizontally in the candidate's relevant category.

Therefore, the source statement that the 2% reservation applies uniformly to “all direct recruitments across every state department and PSU” is too broad. Apply the specific service rules and recruitment advertisement.

Official reference: [Rajasthan Ministerial / General Service Rules, as amended (PDF)](https://gad.rajasthan.gov.in/writereaddata/ActsRules/202410011128565958289RMGSSR_1979_26092024.pdf).

### “Basketball is Rajasthan's state game since 1948”

The source makes this an absolute official designation. No primary government order supporting a 1948 declaration was located in the evidence reviewed. The date is also chronologically suspect because the modern State of Rajasthan was formed through the 1948–49 integration process and was not yet established in its later form at the start of 1948.

**Exam treatment:** Do not present “officially declared in 1948” as a verified fact unless an authoritative notification or a reliable government publication is produced. Basketball has a strong sporting history in Rajasthan, but that does not by itself prove an official state-game designation or its date.

## 36.4 Rajiv Gandhi Rural Olympic Games

- **Launch:** **29 August 2022**, National Sports Day, at Pal village in the Luni area of Jodhpur district.
- **Registration / scale:** Contemporary reporting put registrations at approximately **29.8–30 lakh players** from around **11,341 gram panchayats** for the first rural edition.
- **Core sports:** Kabaddi, tennis-ball cricket, volleyball, hockey, shooting ball and kho-kho were listed for the inaugural edition.
- **Progression:** Gram-panchayat level → block level → district level → state level.
- **Objectives:** Expand mass participation, identify rural talent, encourage physical activity and build social cohesion across age groups.

**Record-book correction:** The 2022 event was reported as recognised by the **World Book of Records**. That is not the same body as Guinness World Records. The source claim that it was a “Guinness World Record” is unsupported and should be removed. The “58 lakh participants” figure appears to combine editions or measures that are not explained consistently; it is not retained as one verified participant count.

References: [Indian Express, 30 August 2022](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/jaipur/rural-olympic-games-rajasthan-30-lakh-people-participate-8119899/) and [World Book of Records — Rajasthan Rural Olympics](https://worldbookofrecords.uk/records/record_detail/department-of-youth-affairs-and-sports--government-of-rajasthan-gets-included-by-world-book-of-records.916).

## 36.5 Yoga: philosophy, Ashtanga Yoga and Shatkarma

The Yoga section should be read as a philosophical and physical-education study note. It is not evidence that every listed practice is suitable for every person or that a health outcome is guaranteed.

### Patanjali's eight limbs (Ashtanga Yoga)

The eight limbs are conventionally grouped into five external disciplines and three internal disciplines.

| Limb | Sanskrit | Core meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Yama** | Ethical restraints |
| 2 | **Niyama** | Personal observances and discipline |
| 3 | **Asana** | Posture; traditionally described as steady and comfortable |
| 4 | **Pranayama** | Regulation of breath |
| 5 | **Pratyahara** | Withdrawal / regulation of sensory engagement |
| 6 | **Dharana** | Concentration on a chosen object or point |
| 7 | **Dhyana** | Sustained meditative attention |
| 8 | **Samadhi** | Deep meditative absorption |

**Bahiranga (external limbs):** Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama and Pratyahara.  
**Antaranga (internal limbs):** Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi.  
Pratyahara is often explained as the transition between external disciplines and the three internal limbs.

#### Five Yamas

1. **Ahimsa** — non-violence.
2. **Satya** — truthfulness.
3. **Asteya** — non-stealing.
4. **Brahmacharya** — disciplined conduct / restraint.
5. **Aparigraha** — non-possessiveness or non-hoarding.

#### Five Niyamas

1. **Saucha** — cleanliness / purity.
2. **Santosha** — contentment.
3. **Tapas** — disciplined effort or austerity.
4. **Svadhyaya** — self-study and reflective study of texts.
5. **Ishvara Pranidhana** — dedication or surrender to Ishvara.

#### Pranayama phases

- **Puraka:** inhalation.
- **Kumbhaka:** retention (internal or external).
- **Rechaka:** exhalation.

Commonly taught practices include Nadi Shodhana / Anulom-Vilom, Bhastrika, Ujjayi, Sheetali, Sheetkari and Bhramari. Names, techniques and classifications can vary between yoga traditions.

### Shatkarma: six purification practices in Hatha Yoga

The traditional six are:

1. **Neti** — nasal cleansing.
2. **Dhauti** — cleansing practices associated with the digestive tract.
3. **Nauli** — abdominal muscle isolation and churning.
4. **Basti** — a traditional colon-cleansing practice.
5. **Kapalabhati** — a cleansing practice classified among the six in *Hatha Yoga Pradipika*.
6. **Trataka** — steady gazing at an object or point.

**Important distinction:** Shatkarma belongs to Hatha Yoga traditions and should not be described as a universal prerequisite imposed by Patanjali's eight-limb Yoga. Traditional texts discuss their use in context; “all practitioners must perform all six” is an overstatement.

**Safety note:** Dhauti, Basti, Nauli and forceful respiratory practices can pose risks if performed incorrectly or by people with relevant medical conditions. This chapter is not a practical instruction manual. Do not attempt invasive or forceful cleansing techniques based only on written notes; seek qualified, appropriately trained guidance and follow clinical advice where relevant.

## 36.7 High-yield exam distinctions

1. **RSSC establishment:** the Council's official history says **1958**, not 1957.
2. **Awards:** Maharana Pratap Award (1982–83); Guru Vashishtha Award (1985–86).
3. **Out-of-turn appointment:** specific medal-winner rules and listed posts; not a blanket direct RAS appointment.
4. **Sports reservation:** 2% horizontal reservation within the applicable recruitment rules; scope is narrower than “all jobs in every department and PSU.”
5. **Academies:** boys' kabaddi is listed at Karauli and Churu; the Council's current list puts boys' cycling in Bikaner and girls' basketball in Jaipur.
6. **Rural Olympics:** launched on 29 August 2022; roughly 30 lakh registrations; six sports in the first edition; recognised by World Book of Records, not verified as a Guinness World Record.
7. **Sriram Singh:** 1:45.77 at Montreal 1976; it was an Indian national record until 2018, not a current Asian record.
8. **Takhar:** Guangzhou 2010 Asian Games gold in men's single sculls.
9. **Avani Lekhara:** Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 gold in women's 10 m air rifle standing SH1; Tokyo bronze in 50 m rifle 3 positions.
10. **Yoga:** five Bahiranga limbs; three Antaranga limbs. Pratyahara bridges outward-facing and inward-focused practice.
11. **Kapalabhati:** listed among the six Shatkarma in Hatha Yoga texts; classification differs across teaching traditions.
12. **State-game claim:** the “Basketball officially declared in 1948” assertion is not established by a verified primary source; do not memorise it as an official notification fact.
13. **Anil Agarwal stadium:** treat as proposed / date-sensitive until an authoritative update establishes current construction and operational status.

## 36.8 Practice questions and answer frameworks

These are **original practice questions**, not authenticated previous-year questions.

### 2-mark questions

**Q1. When was the Rajasthan State Sports Council established?**  
**Answer:** The RSSC's official website dates its establishment to **1958**; its headquarters is at Sawai Man Singh Stadium, Jaipur.

**Q2. Name the two major state sports awards and their institution years.**  
**Answer:** Maharana Pratap Award (1982–83) for sportspersons and Guru Vashishtha Award (1985–86) for coaches.

**Q3. What was Devendra Jhajharia's Paralympic medal record through Tokyo 2020?**  
**Answer:** Gold at Athens 2004 and Rio 2016, followed by silver at Tokyo 2020 in men's javelin F46.

**Q4. Name the three internal limbs of Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga.**  
**Answer:** Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi.

### 5-mark questions

**Q1. Explain the purpose and structure of the Rajiv Gandhi Rural Olympic Games.**  
**Answer framework:**
- Inaugurated on National Sports Day, 29 August 2022, at Pal in Jodhpur district.
- First edition registered about 29.8–30 lakh players.
- Six principal sports included kabaddi, tennis-ball cricket, volleyball, hockey, shooting ball and kho-kho.
- Competition moved from gram-panchayat to block, district and state level.
- The programme sought participation, talent discovery, fitness and social cohesion.

**Q2. Differentiate between out-of-turn appointment and sports reservation.**  
**Answer framework:**
- Out-of-turn appointment is a specific medal-winner route governed by designated rules, achievement criteria and listed posts.
- Sports reservation is a horizontal reservation in applicable direct-recruitment vacancies for candidates meeting the definition of outstanding sportsperson.
- Neither should be described as an unconditional guarantee of any post or service.
- Always check the latest amendment and the particular recruitment rules.

**Q3. Outline Patanjali's eight limbs of Yoga.**  
**Answer framework:**
- Bahiranga: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama and Pratyahara.
- Antaranga: Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi.
- Explain Yama as ethical restraints, Niyama as personal observances, Asana as posture and Pranayama as breath regulation.
- Pratyahara regulates sensory engagement; Dharana is concentration; Dhyana sustained meditation; Samadhi meditative absorption.

### 10-mark question

**“Assess how Rajasthan's sports administration can improve elite performance while widening grassroots participation.”**

**Answer framework:**
1. **Institutional base:** RSSC, district sports offices, academies, residential camps and athlete services.
2. **Elite pathways:** evidence-based coaching, sports science, nutrition, injury care, transparent selection and reliable competition exposure.
3. **Financial security:** dated and transparent cash incentives, athlete insurance and rule-based out-of-turn appointments.
4. **Grassroots inclusion:** rural competitions, school sport, women's participation, para-sport facilities and accessible coaching in underserved districts.
5. **Governance safeguards:** anti-doping education, published criteria, grievance redress and separation of selection decisions from political influence.
6. **Conclusion:** a high-performance system must pair medal incentives with broad participation, accountable administration and long-term athlete welfare.
