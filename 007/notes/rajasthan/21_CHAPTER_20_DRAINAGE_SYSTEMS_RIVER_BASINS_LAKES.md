## 1. The Great Indian Watershed & Hydrographic Architecture of Rajasthan

Rajasthan occupies **10.41% of India's total geographical area** (3,42,239 sq km) and supports approximately **5.67% of the nation's human population** and **10.6% of its livestock**. However, its share of the nation's total surface water resources is an alarming **1.16%** (~15.86 Billion Cubic Metres / BCM), and its share of dynamic replenishable groundwater is merely **1.72%**.

This severe hydrographic asymmetry makes the understanding of Rajasthan's drainage systems essential for state administration, irrigation planning, disaster management (droughts and localized flash floods), and public policy.

### A. Alignment of the Continental Water Divide (*Jal Vibhajak Rekha*)
The Aravalli Mountain Range functions as the **Continental Water Divide of Northern-Western India**. Running diagonally across Rajasthan from southwest to northeast, it bifurcates the state into two disparate hydrological regimes:
1. **The Western & South-Western Slope**: Directs precipitation runoff towards the **Arabian Sea** (via the Rann of Kutch and Gulf of Khambhat) or locks it into endorheic desert basins.
2. **The Eastern & South-Eastern Slope**: Directs runoff towards the **Bay of Bengal** via the Yamuna-Ganga river system.

#### Exact Trace of the Continental Divide Line across Rajasthan:
- Enters northeastern Rajasthan along the **Delhi Ridge – Khetri – Alwar** uplands.
- Passes through **Sambhar Lake** (south of the lake) and crosses **Ajmer** (Peeplu / Nag Pahar ridge).
- Traverses **Beawar** and runs atop the **Bhorat Plateau** (at an elevation of 1,225 m between Kumbhalgarh in Rajsamand and Gogunda in Udaipur).
- Descends south-southeast between the catchments of the **Sabarmati** and **Mahi** in southern Udaipur/Salumbar.
- Passes east of **Pratapgarh (Chhoti Sadri)** and leaves Rajasthan into Madhya Pradesh.

> **Crucial Geomorphological Invariant**: Any drop of rain falling to the **west** of the Bhorat Plateau crest flows via the Sei, Wakal, or Jawai rivers into the Arabian Sea. Any drop falling to the **east** of the crest flows via the Ayad/Bedach or Banas rivers into the Bay of Bengal!

### A. Luni River (*लव Flowvati / Sagarmati / Maruasya / आधी मीठी-आधी खारी*)

#### 1. Statutory Genesis & Nomenclature
- **Rigvedic / Ancient Name**: Mentioned as *Lavnavati* (saline river) in ancient Sanskrit scriptures.
- **Kalidasa's Epithet**: Renowned Sanskrit poet Kalidasa termed it **Antah-Salila** (*अन्तःसलिला* — subterranean water flowing with hidden purity).
- **Originating Names**: Originates at **Nag Pahar (Ajmer)** as the **Sagarmati**. Simultaneously, a holy stream named **Saraswati** originates from Pushkar Lake. The two streams converge at **Govindgarh** (Ajmer), from where the combined river is formally christened **Luni**.

#### 2. Hydrographic Profile & Route (50-District Lens)
- **Total Length**: **495 km** (approximately **330 to 350 km** within Rajasthan).
- **Districts Traversed (8 Districts)**:
  $$\text{Ajmer} \longrightarrow \text{Nagaur} \longrightarrow \text{Beawar} \longrightarrow \text{Jodhpur Rural} \longrightarrow \text{Pali} \longrightarrow \text{Balotra} \longrightarrow \text{Barmer} \longrightarrow \text{Sanchore}$$
- **Outflow / Terminal Sink**: Crosses the Rajasthan border at Sanchore and discharges into the marshes of the **Rann of Kutch (Gujarat)**.
- **The Balotra Salinity Phenomenon (*आधी मीठी - आधी खारी*)**:
  - Up to **Balotra (now a district, formerly in Barmer)**, the river water remains potable and sweet (*Meetha*).
  - Downstream of Balotra, the river water becomes violently saline (*Khara*).
  - **Scientific Explanation**:
    1. High evaporation rates in the hyper-arid plain leave crusts of soluble salts.
    2. Subterranean soils in the lower basin contain high concentrations of sodium chloride ($NaCl$) and gypsum deposited by the receding prehistoric Tethys Sea.
    3. Runoff passing through the deeply weathered Malani Rhyolite and granitic bedrocks leaches sodium ions into the unconfined shallow aquifer via capillary action.

```text
[Pushkar Lake]      ──(Saraswati)──┘                        ▲             │
```

#### 3. Complete Tributary Network of Luni

```text
                                     LUNI RIVER SYSTEM
                                             │
      Right-Bank Tributary (North)           │        Left-Bank Tributaries (Aravalli Origin)
      ────────────────────────────           │        ───────────────────────────────────────
      • JOJARI (जोजड़ी)                      │        • GUHIYA (गुहिया)
        - Origin: Pondlu village (Nagaur)    │        • BANDI (बांडी - Hemawas Dam)
        - Only right-bank tributary          │        • SUKRI (सुकड़ी - Bankli Dam)
        - Only non-Aravalli tributary        │        • MITHARI (मीठड़ी)
        - Flows North to South-West          │        • JAWAI (जवाई - Sumerpur Dam)
                                             │        • KHARI (खारी)
                                             │        • SAGI (सागी - Jaswantpura Hills)
```

1. **Jojari (*जोजड़ी*) — The Supreme Invariant Trap**:
   - **Origin**: Pondlu village, Nagaur district.
   - **Length**: ~83 km; joins Luni near Doli village (Jodhpur Rural / Balotra border).
   - **Exam Singularities**:
     - It is the **ONLY tributary of the Luni that joins from the right bank**.
     - It is the **ONLY tributary that does NOT originate from the Aravalli Range** (originates in the flat semi-arid plain of Nagaur).
     - It flows in a north-to-southwest trajectory, contrary to all other Aravalli tributaries that flow west/northwest.
2. **Bandi (*बांडी*)**:
   - **Origin**: Hemawas village hills, Pali district.
   - **Reservoir**: **Hemawas Dam** (Pali).
   - **Environmental Status**: Often dubbed the **"Chemical River" of Rajasthan**; notorious as the most polluted river in western Rajasthan due to untreated toxic effluents discharged from Pali's textile dyeing and printing industrial clusters.
3. **Guhiya (*गुहिया*)**:
   - Origin: Khariya Sodha hills, Pali; joins Bandi before Bandi joins Luni.
4. **Sukri (*सुकड़ी*)**:
   - **Origin**: Desuri pass / hills, Pali district.
   - **Course**: Flows through Pali and Jalore, joining Luni near Samdari (Balotra).
   - **Reservoir**: **Bankli Dam** (*बांकली बांध*) constructed at Bankli village in Jalore.
5. **Jawai (*जवाई*) — The Monarch Tributary**:
   - **Origin**: Goriyan village hills, Bali tehsil, Pali district.
   - **Course**: Flows through Pali, Jalore, and Balotra before meeting Luni near Sayla.
   - **Reservoir**: **Jawai Dam (*जवाई बांध*)** at Sumerpur (Pali):
     - Known as the **"Amrit Sarovar of Marwar" (*मारवाड़ का अमृत सरोवर*)**.
     - Commissioned in 1946 by Maharaja Umaid Singh of Jodhpur, completed in 1957 (Engineers: Edgar & Ferguson).
     - Largest reservoir by volume in Western Rajasthan; serves as the lifeline drinking source for Jodhpur and Pali cities.
     - **Sei Diversion Project**: When water levels deplete, water is imported via the **Sei Tunnel** (Rajasthan's first inter-basin water transfer tunnel, 6.7 km long) from the Sei River (Sabarmati catchment) into the Jawai Dam.
     - Surroundings declared the **Jawai Bandh Leopard Conservation Reserve**.
6. **Khari (*खारी*)**:
   - Origin: Shergaon hills, Sirohi district; joins Jawai near Sayla before Jawai joins Luni.
7. **Sagi (*सागी*)**:
   - Origin: Jaswantpura hills, Jalore district; joins Luni near Gandhav (Sanchore).

### B. Mahi River (*माही — वागड़ की गंगा / कांठल की गंगा / दक्षिण राजस्थान की स्वर्ण रेखा*)

#### 1. Mythological & Geographic Significance
- Known as the **"Kamdhenu of South Rajasthan"** and **"Ganga of the Tribals" (*आदिवासियों की गंगा*)**.
- **Unique World Feature**: It is the **only river in the world that crosses the Tropic of Cancer (23°30' N latitude) TWICE**. It cuts it first on its northward entry from MP into Banswara, loops around Pratapgarh-Dungarpur, and cuts it a second time on its southward exit from Dungarpur into Gujarat.
- Flows in the distinctive shape of an **Inverted 'U' (अंग्रेजी के उल्टे 'U' का आकार)** or a horseshoe.

```text
                         [Tropic of Cancer: 23°30' N] ── cuts 2nd time (Exit to Gujarat)
                                      ▲
                                      │
            Dungarpur ◄──────── Banswara-Dungarpur Border ◄──────── Pratapgarh
                │                                                        ▲
                │ (Turns Southward)                                      │ (Flows Northward)
                ▼                                                        │
          [Gujarat Entry]                                          [Entry: Khandu Village]
                │                                                        ▲
                ▼                                                        │
        [Gulf of Khambhat]                            [Origin: Amjhoru, Dhar (MP)]
                                                                         │
                                              [Tropic of Cancer: 23°30' N] ── cuts 1st time
```

#### 2. Hydrographic Profile & Course
- **Origin**: Amjhoru village, near Mehad Lake, Dhar district (Vindhyan Hills, Madhya Pradesh).
- **Entry Point into Rajasthan**: **Khandu village** (*खांदू गांव*), Banswara district.
- **Course**: Traverses Banswara, forms the natural geographical boundary between Banswara and Dungarpur, touches the border of Pratapgarh, loops south through Dungarpur, and enters Mahisagar district of Gujarat.
- **Terminal Sink**: Discharges into the **Gulf of Khambhat** (Arabian Sea).
- **Length**: Total length = **576 km**; Length inside Rajasthan = **171 km**.
- **Geomorphic Environs**:
  - **Chhappan ka Maidan**: The fertile alluvial floodplain formed by the 56 streams/drainage channels of the Mahi basin between Banswara and Pratapgarh.
  - **Kanthal Plain**: The coastal-like alluvial strip along the banks of Mahi in Pratapgarh (hence Mahi is called *Kanthal ki Ganga*).

#### 3. Major Hydraulic Projects on Mahi
1. **Mahi Bajaj Sagar Dam (*माही बजाज सागर बांध*)**:
   - Located at **Borkheda village**, 16 km north of Banswara.
   - Named after freedom fighter Jamnalal Bajaj.
   - Longest dam in Rajasthan (**3,109 metres** length).
   - Joint venture between Rajasthan (45% share) and Gujarat (55% share). However, **100% of power generated (140 MW) is allocated exclusively to Rajasthan**!
2. **Kagdi Pick-Up Dam**: Built immediately downstream in Banswara to regulate irrigation feeder canals (Bhikhabhai Sagwara Canal and Anandpuri Lift Canal).
3. **Kadana Dam (*कड़ाना बांध*)**: Built entirely in Mahisagar district of **Gujarat**, financed under the inter-state agreement to irrigate Gujarat tracts.

#### 4. The Tributary System of Mahi
1. **Som (*सोम*)**:
   - **Origin**: Bichhameda hills (*बीछामेड़ा की पहाड़ियां*), Babalwara, Kherwara tehsil, Udaipur district.
   - **Projects**: **Som-Kagdar Irrigation Project** (Udaipur) and **Som-Kamla-Amba Irrigation Project** (Dungarpur).
2. **Jakham (*जाखम*)**:
   - **Origin**: Bhawarmata hills (*भंवरमाता की पहाड़ी*), Chhoti Sadri tehsil, Pratapgarh district.
   - **Flow**: Passes through the dense teak forests of Sita Mata Wildlife Sanctuary.
   - **Jakham Dam (*जाखम बांध*)**: Located in Pratapgarh; with a height of **81 metres**, it is the **tallest dam in Rajasthan**.
3. **Anas (*अनास*)**:
   - Origin: Amber village, near Jhabua, MP; enters Rajasthan at Mellakhera village (Banswara); joins Mahi near Dungarpur border.
4. **Erav / Iru (*इराॅव / ईरू*)**:
   - Origin: Pratapgarh hills; **crucial trap**: It is the **only tributary that joins Mahi before it enters the Mahi Bajaj Sagar Dam reservoir**!
5. **Chap, Moran, and Haran**: Minor tribal streams of Banswara and Dungarpur.

#### 5. The Baneshwar Dham Triveni Sangam (*त्रिवेणी संगम*)
- **Location**: Nawata-para village (*नवाटापारा*), Aspur tehsil, Dungarpur district.
- **Convergence**: Three sacred rivers unite here: **Som + Mahi + Jakham**.
- **Religious Eminence**: Known as the **"Kumbh of the Tribals" (*आदिवासियों का कुंभ*)** or *Puskhar of Vagad*.
- Celebrated annually on **Magh Purnima**. Seat established by Sant Mavji; home to the world's only worshipped **Broken Shivalinga (*खंडित शिवलिंग*)**.

### C. Sabarmati River (*साबरमती*)
- **Origin**: Padarana hills (*पदारणा की पहाड़ियां*), near Jhadol tehsil, south-west of Udaipur.
- **Hydrological Paradox**: Though originating in Rajasthan, it is almost entirely celebrated as the **lifeline of Gujarat**.
  - Total length: **416 km**.
  - Length inside Rajasthan: Barely **44 to 45 km**!
  - Flows past Gandhinagar (Gujarat capital) and Ahmedabad, terminating in the Gulf of Khambhat.
- **Major Tributaries in Rajasthan**:
  - **Wakal (*वाकल*)**: Originates from Goram Ghat / Kotra hills, Udaipur.
  - **Sei (*सेई*)**: Originates from Padarana hills; source of the Sei diversion tunnel to Jawai Dam.
  - **Hathmati, Meshwo, Majam, and Vetrak**: Drain the southern spurs of Dungarpur and Udaipur into Gujarat.

---

### D. West Banas River (*पश्चिमी बनास*)
- **Origin**: Naya Sanwara village (*नया संवारा*), Pindwara tehsil, Sirohi district.
- **Course**: Drains the western slopes of Mount Abu and Sirohi, enters Banaskantha district of Gujarat, passes the commercial town of **Deesa**, and terminates in the **Little Rann of Kutch (कच्छ का छोटा रन)**.
- **Length**: Total length = **266 km** (~50 km in Rajasthan).
- **Major Tributary**: **Sukli / Sipu River** (*सुकली / सीपू* — not to be confused with Luni's Sukri).

### A. Chambal River (*चम्बल — चर्मण्वती / कामधेनु / नित्यवाही*)

#### 1. Identity & Hydrographic Superlatives
- **Perenniality**: The **only perennial (12-month continuous flow) river of Rajasthan** along with Mahi.
- **Epithets**: Mentioned as *Charmanvati* in the Mahabharata; revered as the *Kamdhenu of Rajasthan*.
- **Maximum Water Volume**: Carries the largest volume of surface runoff in the entire state.
- **Interstate Boundary**: Forms a natural interstate boundary of **241 km** between Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh (bordering Sawai Madhopur, Karauli, and Dholpur).

#### 2. Hydrographic Route & Physical Dimensions
- **Origin**: Janapav hills (*जानापाव पहाड़ी*), Vindhyan Range, near Mhow (Dr. Ambedkar Nagar), Indore district, Madhya Pradesh (elevation: 610 m).
- **Entry into Rajasthan**: Enters through a rocky gorge near **Chaurasigarh Fort (*चौरासीगढ़ दुर्ग*)**, Rawatbhata tehsil, Chittorgarh district.
- **Districts Traversed (6 Districts)**:
  $$\text{Chittorgarh (Rawatbhata)} \longrightarrow \text{Kota} \longrightarrow \text{Bundi} \longrightarrow \text{Sawai Madhopur} \longrightarrow \text{Karauli} \longrightarrow \text{Dholpur}$$
- **Terminal Confluence**: Discharges into the **Yamuna River** at **Muradganj** (near Chakarnagar / Panchnada), Etawah district, Uttar Pradesh.
- **Total Length**: 
  - Traditional metric: **965 km** (Length purely inside Rajasthan = **135 to 153 km**; Along MP-Rajasthan border = **241 km**; In MP = ~320 km; In UP = ~217 km).
  - Modern Survey of India measurement: **1,051 km**.

#### 3. Distinctive Geomorphological Features
1. **Chulia Waterfall (*चूलिया जलप्रपात*)**:
   - Located at **Bhainsrorgarh** (Chittorgarh), immediately downstream of the confluence where the Brahmani (Bamani) River meets Chambal.
   - Height: **18 metres** (highest waterfall in Rajasthan).
2. **Utkhat Bhumi (*उत्खात भूमि* — Badland Topography & Beehad Ravines)**:
   - Caused by accelerated vertical gully erosion (*अवनालिका अपरदन*) through soft alluvial mantles.
   - Creates labyrinthine, deep, vegetated ravines (*Beehad / खादर*) spanning Sawai Madhopur, Karauli, and Dholpur.
   - **Dang Region (*डांग क्षेत्र*)**: The rugged, dissected ravine plateau; **Karauli** is hailed as the **"Dang ki Rani" (*डांग की रानी*)**, while Dholpur is the "Dang ka Raja".
   - Special development programmes launched here: *Dang Area Development Programme* and *Kandhara Area Development Scheme*.
3. **Deep Gorges (*कंदराएं*)**:
   - The river carves magnificent narrow rocky canyons between Rawatbhata and Kota city (Garadia Mahadev canyon is often acclaimed as "The Grand Canyon of India").

#### 4. The Chambal Valley Multipurpose Hydel & Irrigation Project (1953–1972)
A 50:50 joint enterprise between **Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh**, executed in three systematic stages:

| Dam Name | Stage & Year | Exact Location | Reservoir & Structural Capacity | Hydroelectric Power Installed | Defining Role / Distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Gandhi Sagar Dam** (*गांधी सागर*) | Stage I (1960) | Bhanpura / Mandsaur district, **Madhya Pradesh** | Length: 514 m; Height: 62 m; Storage: 7,322 MCM | **115 MW** (5 units of 23 MW) | Largest storage reservoir of the Chambal cascade; located entirely in MP. |

1. **Kali Sindh (*काली सिंध*)**:
   - **Origin**: Bagli village, Dewas district, Madhya Pradesh.
   - **Entry into Rajasthan**: Binda village, Jhalawar district.
   - **Confluence with Chambal**: Nonaire village, Kota district.
   - **Historic Confluence (*Gagron Jal Durg*)**: At Gagron (Jhalawar), Kali Sindh merges with the **Ahu River**. Right on this rocky promontory stands the foundationless **UNESCO World Heritage Gagron Fort**.
2. **Ahu (*आहू*)**:
   - Origin: Susner, MP; enters Rajasthan near Chachurni (Jhalawar); merges with Kali Sindh at Gagron.
3. **Parwan (*परवन*)**:
   - Origin: Malwa Plateau, MP; enters Rajasthan near Manohar Thana (Jhalawar); traverses Baran (flows through the **Shergarh Wildlife Sanctuary**); joins Kali Sindh near Ramgarh (Baran).
4. **Parbati (*पार्वती*)**:

5. **Brahmani / Bamani (*ब्राह्मणी*)**:
   - Origin: Haripura village hills, Begun, Chittorgarh; merges with Chambal at **Bhainsrorgarh Fort** (forming the Chulia Falls).
6. **Mej (*मेज*)**:
   - Origin: Mandalgarh hills, Bhilwara; drains through Bundi; meets Chambal near Lakheri. Major tributaries: Kural, Mangali.
7. **Kuno (*कूनो*)**:
   - Origin: Shivpuri plateau, MP; enters Baran; rejoins MP and flows through Kuno National Park before terminating in Chambal.

### B. Banas River (*बनास — वन की आशा / वर्णाशा / वशिष्ठी*)

#### 1. Identity & Status
- **Title**: *Van Ki Asha* (Hope of the Forest) or *Varnasha*.
- **The Sovereign Invariant**: The **longest river flowing entirely and exclusively within Rajasthan**.
  - Total length: **480 km** (old metric) or **512 km** (modern satellite survey).
  - Its entire catchment area of ~47,060 sq km lies 100% inside the borders of Rajasthan.

#### 2. Hydrographic Route (50-District Lens)
- **Origin**: **Khamnor hills (*खमनोर की पहाड़ियां*)**, near Kumbhalgarh, Rajsamand district.
- **Districts Traversed (7 Districts)**:
  $$\text{Rajsamand} \longrightarrow \text{Chittorgarh} \longrightarrow \text{Bhilwara} \longrightarrow \text{Shahpura} \longrightarrow \text{Kekri} \longrightarrow \text{Tonk} \longrightarrow \text{Sawai Madhopur}$$
- **Terminal Confluence**: Discharges into the Chambal at **Rameswaram Ghat** (*रामेश्वरम धाम*), Padra village, Khandar tehsil, Sawai Madhopur district.

#### 3. The Three Great Triveni Sangams of the Banas Basin

```text
                                TRIVENI SANGAMS OF BANAS
                                           │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
1. BIGOD SANGAM (Bhilwara)        2. RAJMAHAL SANGAM (Tonk/Kekri)   3. RAMESWARAM GHAT (Sawai Madhopur)
   • Banas                           • Banas                           • Banas
   • Bedach (Ayad)                   • Khari                           • Chambal
   • Menal                           • Dai                             • Seep
```

1. **Bigod Sangam (*बीगोद संगम*)**: Located at Bigod (Bhilwara district); convergence of **Banas + Bedach + Menal**.
2. **Rajmahal Sangam (*राजमहल संगम*)**: Located at Rajmahal (on the border of Tonk and Kekri districts); convergence of **Banas + Khari + Dai**.
3. **Rameswaram Sangam (*रामेश्वरम संगम*)**: Located at Khandar (Sawai Madhopur); convergence of **Banas + Chambal + Seep**.

#### 4. Major Hydraulic Projects on Banas
1. **Bisalpur Dam (*बीसलपुर बांध*)**:
   - Located at Bisalpur village, Deoli tehsil, Tonk district.
   - Built in 1999 across a narrow gorge in the Banas River.
   - Gravity concrete dam (Length: 574 m; Height: 39.5 m; Crest capacity: 315.50 RL).
   - **Sovereign Role**: The **largest drinking water project in Rajasthan**; lifeline supplying potable drinking water to Jaipur city, Ajmer, Beawar, Dudu, Kekri, and Tonk.
2. **Isarda Dam (*ईसरदा बांध*)**:
   - Located at Isarda village, Sawai Madhopur district.
   - Constructed downstream of Bisalpur to impound surplus waters for drinking supply to Dausa, Sawai Madhopur, and Jaipur Rural.
3. **Matrikundiya Dam**: Located at Rashmi (Chittorgarh) on Banas; irrigates Mewar plains.
4. **Nand Samand Dam (*नंद समंद बांध*)**: Located in Rajsamand; hailed as the **"Lifeline of Rajsamand"**.

#### 5. Master Tributaries of the Banas
1. **Berach / Bedach (*बेड़च — पूर्व नाम: आयड़*)**:
   - **Origin**: **Gogunda hills (*गोगुंदा की पहाड़ियां*)**, Udaipur district.
   - **The Name-Change Paradox**:
     - Originates as the **Ayad River (*आयड़ नदी*)**.
     - Flows through Udaipur city, nourishing the ancient Chalcolithic site of **Ahar (आयड़ सभ्यता)**.
     - Dumps its water into **Udaisagar Lake** (constructed 1559–1565 by Maharana Udai Singh II).
     - **When it exits Udaisagar Lake, it is rechristened as the Bedach River (*बेड़च नदी*)**!
   - Meets Banas at Bigod (Bhilwara). Total length: 157 km.
   - Major tributary: **Gambhiri River** (origin: Jawad, MP; joins Bedach near Chittorgarh city; the historic Chittorgarh Fort is perched on the Mesa Plateau between the Bedach and Gambhiri rivers).
2. **Kothari (*कोठारी*)**:
   - **Origin**: **Diver hills (*दिवेर की पहाड़ियां*)**, Rajsamand district.
   - **Length**: ~145 km; traverses Rajsamand, Bhilwara, and Shahpura; meets Banas near Nandrai.
   - **Meja Dam (*मेजा बांध*)**: Built across Kothari in Bhilwara; historical drinking water source for the textile city ("Green Mount" park developed here).
   - **Archaeological Treasure**: The Mesolithic site of **Bagor** (oldest animal husbandry evidence in India) is situated directly on the bank of the Kothari River.
3. **Khari (*खारी*)**:
   - **Origin**: Bijral village hills (*बिजराल गांव की पहाड़ियां*), Rajsamand district.
   - **Course**: Traverses Rajsamand, Bhilwara, Shahpura, Kekri, and Tonk; meets Banas at Rajmahal. ( नारायण सागर बांध in Ajmer/Beawar is situated on a diversion of Khari).
4. **Dai (*डाई*)**: Originates from Nasirabad hills (Ajmer/Beawar border); joins Banas at Rajmahal.
5. **Morel (*मोरेल*)**: Originates from Dharla hills (Bassi, Jaipur Rural); traverses Dausa and Gangapur City; joins Banas in Sawai Madhopur. **Morel Dam** is located at Kaluwas (Dausa/Sawai Madhopur border). Major tributary: **Kalisil** (origin: Rajor, Karauli; near Kaila Devi Temple).
6. **Mashi (*माशी*)**: Originates from Kishangarh hills (Ajmer); joins Banas in Tonk.

### C. Banganga River (*बाणगंगा — अर्जुन की गंगा / ताला नदी / रुण्डित नदी*)
- **Mythological Origin**: Legend records that Arjuna shot an arrow (*Baan*) into the earth to quench the thirst of dying Bhishma Pitamaha, releasing this subterranean spring.
- **Physical Origin**: **Bairat hills (*बैराठ की पहाड़ियां*)**, Kotputli-Behror district (formerly Jaipur).
- **Districts Traversed**:
  $$\text{Kotputli-Behror} \longrightarrow \text{Jaipur Rural} \longrightarrow \text{Dausa} \longrightarrow \text{Bharatpur}$$
- **Hydrological Classification: The Severed / Beheaded River (*रुण्डित नदी / Rundhit Sarita*)**:
  - In hydrological science, a river that loses sufficient discharge to reach its master stream and terminates in an inland alluvial fan or swamp before its historic confluence is termed a **Severed / Beheaded River (*Rundhit Sarita*)**.
  - Banganga historically joined the **Yamuna River** at Fatehabad (Agra, UP) after a 240 km course.
  - Due to massive deforestation in the Bairat range, excessive siltation, and over-extraction via upstream check-dams, the river currently loses surface velocity in Bharatpur and terminates in the **Keoladeo Ghana National Park marshes**.
- **Hydraulic Landmark**: **Jamwa Ramgarh Dam (*जमवा रामगढ़ बांध*)** built in Jaipur Rural in 1897; served as the primary drinking water lifeline of Jaipur for nearly a century before drying up.

### D. Gambhir River (*गम्भीर — उटंगन नदी*)
- **Note of Caution**: Do NOT confuse the **Gambhir River (*गम्भीर*)** of eastern Rajasthan with the **Gambhiri River (*गम्भीरी*)** of Chittorgarh!
- **Origin**: Hindaun / Nadoti hills, Karauli district.
- **Course**: Karauli $\longrightarrow$ Sawai Madhopur $\longrightarrow$ Bharatpur $\longrightarrow$ enters Fatehabad (UP) and joins Yamuna.
- **Panchana Dam (*पांचना बांध*)**:
  - Located at Gudla village, Karauli district.
  - The **largest clay/earthen dam (*मिट्टी का सबसे बड़ा बांध*) in Rajasthan**, constructed with US financial aid.
  - Built at the hydrological confluence of **5 local rivulets**:
    1. **Ata (*अटा*)**
    2. **Machi (*माची*)**
    3. **Bhadrawati (*भद्रावती*)**
    4. **Barkheda (*बरखेड़ा*)**
    5. **Bhainsawat (*भैंसावट*)**
  - Supplies water downstream to the **Ajan Dam (*अजान बांध*)** in Bharatpur, which in turn feeds the wetland ecosystem of Keoladeo National Park.

Rajasthan has the **largest inland drainage system in India**. Over **60% of the state's territory** belongs to endorheic basins, where ephemeral streams fail to break through the encircling sand barriers or mountain shadows to reach the oceans.

### A. Ghaggar River (*घग्घर — सरस्वती / मृत नदी / नट नदी / सोतर / हकरा*)

#### 1. Identity & Paleo-Hydrology
- **Rigvedic Lineage**: Identified by geologists (Dr. K.S. Valdiya) and ISRO satellite imagery as the desiccated relict channel of the sacred **Saraswati River** and the **Drishadvati**.
- **Popular Epithets**: *Mrit Nadi* (Dead River), *Sotar*, *Nata Nadi*.
- **International Status**: The **only international river of Rajasthan**; during episodic extreme monsoonal surges, its floodwaters cross the international border into Pakistan.

#### 2. Hydrographic Route & Morphology
- **Origin**: Shivalik Hills, near Kalka / Dagshai, Solan district, Himachal Pradesh (elevation: 1,927 m).
- **Course in Northern Plains**: Traverses Punjab and Haryana; enters Rajasthan at **Talwara village**, Tibbi tehsil, Hanumangarh district.
- **Districts Traversed (3 Districts)**:
  $$\text{Hanumangarh} \longrightarrow \text{Sriganganagar} \longrightarrow \text{Anupgarh}$$
- **Terminal Sink**:
  - Under normal rainfall: Dries up in the sandy tracts near Bhatner Fort (Hanumangarh) or Suratgarh.
  - Under severe monsoonal floods: Reaches **Fort Abbas** (Bahawalpur, Punjab, Pakistan).
- **Vernacular Basin Terms**:
  - **Nali (*नाली*)**: The flat, fertile, silt-rich channel bed of Ghaggar inside Hanumangarh and Sriganganagar; famous for high-yield Basmati rice and wheat cultivation.
  - **Hakra (*हकरा*)**: The name by which the Ghaggar channel is known downstream of the international border in **Pakistan**.
- **Archaeological Stratigraphy**: The world-renowned Indus Valley Civilisation sites of **Kalibangan**, **Pilibangan**, and **Rangmahal** are situated directly on the ancient dried terrace of the Ghaggar River.
- **Talwara Lake (*तलवाड़ा झील*)**: Formed in a low natural depression along the Ghaggar course in Hanumangarh; recognized as the **lowest situated lake in Rajasthan**.

---

### B. Kantli River (*कांटली — तोरावाटी की जीवन रेखा*)
- **Origin**: **Khandela hills (*खंडेला की पहाड़ियां*)**, Sikar district.
- **Course**: Sikar $\longrightarrow$ Neem Ka Thana $\longrightarrow$ Jhunjhunu.
- **Terminal Sink**: Dries up in the undulating sand dunes near **Mandrella village**, on the border of Jhunjhunu and Churu districts.
- **Basin Name**: The catchment area of Kantli across Sikar, Neem Ka Thana, and Jhunjhunu was ruled by Tanwar Rajputs and is culturally designated as **Torawati (*तोरावाटी*)**.
- **Length & Status**: ~**100 km**; recognized as the **longest purely inland river originating and terminating entirely within Rajasthan**.
- **Archaeological Eminence**:
  - **Ganeshwar (*गणेश्वर सभ्यता*)**: Located at Neem Ka Thana on Kantli's bank; excavated by R.C. Agrawala; recognized as the **Mother of Copper Age Civilizations in India (*ताम्रयुगीन सभ्यताओं की जननी*)**.
  - **Sunari (*सुनारी सभ्यता*)**: Located in Jhunjhunu on Kantli's bank; ancient iron-smelting furnaces found here.

---

### C. Sabi River (*साबी*)
- **Origin**: **Sewar hills (*सेवर की पहाड़ियां*)**, Kotputli-Behror district (formerly Jaipur).
- **Course**: Kotputli-Behror $\longrightarrow$ Khairthal-Tijara $\longrightarrow$ crosses into Haryana (Gurugram district) $\longrightarrow$ terminates in the **Najafgarh drain (*नजफगढ़ झील / नाला*)** near Pataudi.
- **Archaeological Site**: **Jodhpura (*जोधपुरा सभ्यता*)** located on the bank of Sabi in Kotputli-Behror; excavated by R.C. Agrawala; famous for Iron Age smelting culture and Painted Grey Ware (PGW).

---

### D. Ruparel River (*रूपारेल — वराह नदी / लसवारी*)
- **Origin**: **Udainath hills (*उदयनाथ की पहाड़ियां*)**, Thanagazi tehsil, Alwar district.
- **Nomenclature**: Also referred to in local epigraphy as the **Varaha Nadi (*वराह नदी*)** and **Laswari (*लसवारी*)**.
- **Course**: Alwar $\longrightarrow$ enters Deeg and Bharatpur districts.
- **Hydraulic Management & Historic Disputes**:
  - Caused historic water-sharing conflicts between the princely states of Alwar and Bharatpur.
  - Maharaja Suraj Mal constructed the **Sikri Dam (*सीकरी बांध*)** to divert its waters into the **Moti Jheel Dam (*मोती झील*)** in Bharatpur.
  - **Moti Jheel** is acclaimed as the **"Lifeline of Bharatpur" (*भरतपुर की जीवन रेखा*)**. From Moti Jheel, the **Sujan Ganga Canal** carries water into the deep protective moat surrounding the impregnable mud-walled Lohagarh Fort.

---

### E. Mendha & Rupangarh Rivers (Sambhar Catchment)
- **Mendha River (*मेन्था / मेन्ढा*)**:
  - Origin: Manoharpur hills, Jaipur Rural.
  - Course: Flows westward through Jaipur Rural and Didwana-Kuchaman; enters **Sambhar Lake from the north**.
  - **Diagnostic Trap**: Brings the **highest concentration of soluble salts into Sambhar Lake** among all inflowing streams!
- **Rupangarh River (*रूपनगढ़*)**:
  - Origin: Salemabad hills, Kishangarh, Ajmer.
  - Course: Flows north; enters **Sambhar Lake from the south**.
  - Historical seat: The chief all-India pontifical seat of the **Nimbarka Sect (*निम्बार्क संप्रदाय की पीठ*)** is situated at Salemabad on the banks of Rupangarh.
- **Khari & Khandela**: Minor seasonal streams entering Sambhar Lake from the northwest and northeast.

---

### F. Kakney / Masurdi River (*काकनेय / काकनी / मसूरदी*)
- **Origin**: **Kotari village (*कोठारी गांव*)**, approximately 27 km south of Jaisalmer city.
- **Course**: Flows north for barely **17 km**; empties into and forms the ephemeral **Bhuj Lake (*बुझ झील*)**.
- **Length & Distinction**: At **17 km**, it is the **shortest named river in Rajasthan**. In seasons of rare intense rain, it overspills Bhuj Lake and terminates at Meetha Khadi.

## 5. Lakes of Rajasthan: Complete Limnological Taxonomy

Rajasthan's lakes represent a dual heritage: ancient tectonic/paleo-climatic brine depressions in the desert (*Saline Lakes*), and medieval monumental royal embankments (*Freshwater Lakes*) engineered for drought security, agricultural survival, and neoclassical leisure.

```text
         ┌────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┐

  [Remnants of Tethys Sea + Capillary Action]                              [Royal Embankments & Volcanic Calderas]
  • SAMBHAR (8.7% India's salt, Ramsar 1990)                               • JAISAMAND / DHEBAR (Salumbar, 7 Islands)

  • DIDWANA (Industrial salt, Sodium Sulphate)                             • PICHHOLA (Jag Mandir, Jag Niwas)

                                                                           • NAKKI (Mt. Abu, Highest, Caldera)
                                                                           • ANA SAGAR (Baradari by Shah Jahan)

```

### A. Saline Lakes (*खारे पानी की झीलें*)

#### 1. Geomorphological Origin of Desert Salinity
- **Tethys Sea Remnant Hypothesis**: The shallow depression left behind by the receding Mesozoic-Cenozoic Tethys Sea accumulated thick evaporite beds.
- **Capillary Action (*केशिकाकर्षण*)**: High ambient summer temperatures ($>45^\circ\text{C}$) induce tremendous evaporation. Subterranean moisture holding dissolved sodium, magnesium, and calcium salts is drawn upwards through microscopic soil pores via capillary action, depositing salt crusts on the playa floor.
- **Wind-Borne Marine Halite**: Strong south-westerly summer monsoon winds transport microscopic airborne salt particles from the Rann of Kutch and Arabian Sea coastline into inland basins.

### B. Freshwater Lakes (*मीठे पानी की झीलें*)

#### 1. Jaisamand Lake (*जयसमंद झील — ढेबर झील*)
- **Builder & Epoch**: Built between **1685 and 1691 CE** by **Maharana Jai Singh** of Mewar by damming the **Gomati River** (along with the Jhamri, Ruparel, and Bhagar rivulets).
- **Location**: **Salumbar district** (historically in Udaipur).
- **Status**: The **largest artificial (man-made) freshwater lake in Rajasthan** and the second largest in India (after Govind Ballabh Pant Sagar).
- **Dimensions**: Length: 15 km; Width: 1.5 to 8 km; Circumference: ~88 km.
- **The Seven Islands (*सात टापू*)**:
  - The lake houses **seven natural inhabited islands** populated by Bhil and Mina tribes.
  - **Largest Island**: **Baba ka Bhagra** (*बाबा का भागड़ा / बाबा का मगरा*).
  - **Smallest Island**: **Pyari (*प्यारी*)**.
  - A luxury resort island is named *Baba ka Island*.
- **Canal Infrastructure**: In 1950, two irrigation feeder canals were carved out from the southern embankment:
  1. **Shyampura Canal (*श्यामपुरा नहर*)**
  2. **Bhatbhat Canal (*भाटभट नहर*)**
- **Architectural Splendor**: The massive eastern embankment (*Pal*) is embellished with **six exquisite white marble Chhatris (cenotaphs)**, each fronted by a carved elephant statue with an upturned trunk, and an ancient Shiva temple (*Narmadeshwar Mahadev*).
- **Jaisamand Wildlife Sanctuary**: Surrounds the lake; celebrated for leopards, chital, and aquatic birds, historically known as the "Water Sanctuary" (*जलचरों की बस्ती*).

---

#### 2. Rajsamand Lake (*राजसमंद झील — कांकरोली*)
- **Builder & Epoch**: Built between **1662 and 1676 CE** by **Maharana Raj Singh I** by constructing an embankment across the **Gomati River** at Kankroli, Rajsamand.
- **Purpose**: Conceived as a grand **Famine Relief Project (*अकाल राहत कार्य*)** to provide livelihood and food security to thousands of starving subjects during devastating droughts.
- **The Nine-Step Embankment (*नौचौकी की पाल*)**:
  - The massive northern embankment is termed *Nauchowki ki Pal* due to its flights of marble steps descending to the water in descending tiers of nine.
- **Raj Prashasti (*राज प्रशस्ति*) — The Epigraphic Monument of the World**:
  - Embedded into the niches of the Nauchowki embankment are **25 monumental slabs of polished black marble**.
  - Engraved on them is the **Raj Prashasti**, authored by Telugu Dravidian Brahmin **Ranchhod Bhatt Telang**.
  - Consists of **24 cantos (*सर्ग*) and 1,106 Sanskrit verses**.
  - Recognized as the **largest stone inscription in the world**. It documents the complete history of Mewar from Bappa Rawal down to Maharana Raj Singh, including details of the 1615 Mughal-Mewar Treaty and the engineering expenditure of the dam.
- **Dwarikadhish Temple**: The sacred Vaishnava Vallabh-sect seat of **Dwarikadhish Ji** at Kankroli overlooks the lake. The shrine of **Ghevar Mata** (the virtuous lady who placed the foundation stone without being widowed) is situated on the embankment.

---

#### 3. Pichhola Lake (*पिछोला झील — उदयपुर*)
- **Builder & Epoch**: Constructed in the late **14th century (c. 1388 CE)** during the reign of **Maharana Lakha** by a wealthy nomadic grain merchant (Banjara) named **Pichhu Banjara / Chhitarmal Banjara** to allow his pack bullocks to cross the muddy Kotra and Sisarma streams.
- **Subsequent Expansion**: Strengthened and enlarged by Maharana Udai Singh II in 1559 CE after founding Udaipur city.
- **Inflowing Streams**: **Sisarma (*सीसारमा*)** and **Bujhada (*बुझड़ा*)** nullahs.
- **Palatial Islands & Monuments**:
  1. **Jag Mandir (*जग मंदिर*)**:
     - Commenced in 1620 by Maharana Karan Singh, completed in 1651 by Maharana Jagat Singh I.
     - Provided sovereign asylum to Mughal prince **Khurram (later Emperor Shah Jahan)** during his 1623–24 rebellion against Emperor Jahangir. The marble dome architecture and pietra dura work directly inspired the design of the **Taj Mahal**.
     - Provided refuge to British families fleeing Neemuch during the 1857 Mutiny under Maharana Swaroop Singh.
  2. **Jag Niwas (*जग निवास — Lake Palace*)**:
     - Built in 1746 CE by **Maharana Jagat Singh II** as a summer pleasure palace; now a luxury heritage hotel managed by the Taj Group.
  3. **Mohan Mandir & Arsi Vilas**: Small islands built by Maharana Jagat Singh and Maharana Ari Singh (Arsi Vilas houses an ammunition depot and bird nesting sanctuary).
  4. **Natni ka Chabutra (*नटनी का चबूतरा*)**: A memorial platform raised on the western bank opposite the City Palace to commemorate the tragic legendary rope-dancer *Galgi*.

---

#### 4. Fateh Sagar Lake (*फतेह सागर झील — उदयपुर*)
- **Builder & Epoch**:
  - Originally excavated in **1678 CE by Maharana Jai Singh**.
  - Destroyed by severe flood breaches, it was completely reconstructed in **1889 CE by Maharana Fateh Singh**.
  - The foundation stone of the reconstructed embankment was laid by the **Duke of Connaught** (son of Queen Victoria); hence the lake's primary bund is officially christened the **Connaught Dam (*कनॉट बांध*)**.
- **Hydraulic Interlinking (*Swaroop Sagar Canal*)**:
  - Linked to Lake Pichhola via the narrow **Swaroop Sagar Canal (*रंगसागर / स्वरूप सागर*)**. When Pichhola overflows, surplus waters automatically spill through sluice gates into Fateh Sagar.
  - Linked to the downstream **Badi Lake** and **Chhota Madar / Bada Madar** reservoirs.
- **Islands & Scientific Institutions**:
  1. **Nehru Park (*नेहरू गार्डन*)**: An island developed into an oval public botanical park with boat connectivity.
  2. **Udaipur Solar Observatory (USO)**:
     - Established on an isolated island in Fateh Sagar in 1975 by renowned astrophysicist **Dr. Arvind Bhatnagar**.
     - Modeled on the Big Bear Solar Observatory in California; surrounded by water which reduces thermal ground turbulence, providing the finest solar imaging clarity in Asia.
     - Houses the advanced **MAST (Multi-Application Solar Telescope)**.

---

#### 5. Nakki Lake (*नक्की झील — माउंट आबू, सिरोही*)
- **Elevation & Character**: Perched at an altitude of approximately **1,200 metres (3,937 ft)** above sea level at Mount Abu; recognized as the **highest and deepest lake in Rajasthan**.
- **The Dual Origin Debate**:
  - **Mythological Legend**: Believed to have been scooped out by Hindu gods using their fingernails (*Nakh / नाखून*) to escape the demon Bashkali.
  - **Geomorphological Fact**: Recognized by geologists as a volcanic **Caldera / Crater Lake (*ज्वालामुखी क्रेटर झील*)** or a tectonic fault depression formed during the Tertiary uplifting of the Abu granitic horst block.
- **The Freezing Superlative**: It is the **only lake in Rajasthan that freezes solid** during sub-zero December–January winter spells.
- **Tribal Sacredness**: Sacred pilgrimage water for the **Garasia tribe**, who immerse the mortal ashes of their ancestors in Nakki Lake during their ancestral festivals.
- **Diagnostic Sculpted Granitic Rocks**:
  - **Toad Rock**: Resembles a giant toad poised to leap into the water.
  - **Nun Rock**: Resembles a veiled praying Christian nun.
  - **Camel Rock**: Resembles a kneeling camel.
  - **Nandi Rock**: Resembles Lord Shiva's bull.

---

#### 6. Ana Sagar Lake (*आना सागर झील — अजमेर*)
- **Builder & Epoch**: Built between **1135 and 1137 CE** by Chauhan king **Arnoraja (Ana Ji)**, the grandfather of Prithviraja Chauhan III.
- **Historic Purpose**: Conceived to cleanse the valley of the blood shed during a bloody massacre repelling an invading Ghaznavid Turkish army; dammed the waters of the seasonal **Luni / Chandra stream** between Taragarh and Nag Pahar.
- **Mughal Architectural Additions**:
  1. **Daulat Bagh (*दौलत बाग — वर्तमान सुभाष उद्यान*)**: Laid out along the embankment by Mughal Emperor **Jahangir** in 1615–1618 (Sir Thomas Roe, the first British ambassador from King James I, held frequent diplomatic audiences with Jahangir here).
  2. **Baradari (*बारहदरी*)**: In **1637 CE, Emperor Shah Jahan** erected **five exquisite pavilions of polished white Makrana marble** along the paved bund (*Pal*).

---

#### 7. Pushkar Lake (*पुष्कर झील — तीर्थराज / आदि तीर्थ / पंचम तीर्थ*)
- **Sovereign Status**: Reared as the **"King of Pilgrimages" (*तीर्थराज*)**, *Aditirtha*, or *Konkan Tirtha*; the most sacred Hindu wetland in Western India.
- **Form**: A natural crescent-shaped (*अर्धचंद्राकार*) semi-caldera aquifer fed by subterranean springs from the Aravalli hills.
- **The 52 Sacred Ghats**:
  - Encircled by **52 historical bathing ghats** (Varaha Ghat, Brahma Ghat, Gau Ghat, Badri Ghat).
  - **Gandhi Ghat (formerly Queen Mary Zenana Ghat)**: Built in 1911 for Queen Mary's visit; in 1948, a portion of **Mahatma Gandhi's mortal ashes** was immersed here.
- **The Jagatpita Brahma Temple**: Houses the world's most preeminent temple dedicated to Lord Brahma (enshrined by Adi Shankaracharya; present marble structure erected by Gokul Chand Parekh in 1809 CE).
- **Pushkar Fair**: Celebrated annually from **Kartik Shukla Ekadashi to Kartik Purnima**; famous for colorful camel trading, folk dances, and spiritual **Deepdan (*दीपदान*)** ceremonies.
- **Ostracization of Cleanliness**: Cleaned under Canadian aid in 1997–98 under the *Pushkar Sarovar Cleanliness Project*.

## 7. Top 25 Lethal Examination Traps & Ambiguities

1. **Jojari Tributary Trap**: Jojari is the **ONLY right-bank tributary of the Luni** and the **ONLY one that does not originate from the Aravallis** (it originates in the flat plains of Pondlu, Nagaur). All other Luni tributaries originate on the western slopes of the Aravalli Range and join from the left bank.
2. **Balotra Salinity Boundary**: Luni's water is NOT saline from its source; it remains sweet, fresh, and potable for over 200 km until it crosses **Balotra**, where subterranean saline soil, capillary halite action, and industrial effluents render it intensely brackish.
3. **Longest River: Overall vs In Rajasthan**:
   - Longest river *flowing through* Rajasthan: **Chambal** (965 km total).
   - Longest river *flowing entirely and exclusively within Rajasthan*: **Banas** (480–512 km).
4. **Perennial Rivers of Rajasthan**: Rajasthan has only **TWO perennial ( बारहमासी ) rivers**: the **Chambal** and the **Mahi**. All other rivers (including Banas, Luni, and Banganga) are seasonal/ephemeral (*बरसाती*).
5. **Tropic of Cancer Intersection**: **Mahi** is the ONLY river globally to intersect the Tropic of Cancer ($23^\circ 30'\text{ N}$) **twice** — once entering Rajasthan from MP (flowing north), and once exiting Rajasthan into Gujarat (flowing south).
6. **Mahi Bajaj Sagar Power Sharing Trap**: In the Mahi project, cost is shared 45% (Rajasthan) : 55% (Gujarat). However, **100% of the hydroelectric power generated (140 MW) belongs exclusively to Rajasthan**!
7. **Tallest Dam vs Longest Dam in Rajasthan**:
   - Tallest Dam: **Jakham Dam** (81 metres, Pratapgarh).
   - Longest Dam: **Mahi Bajaj Sagar Dam** (3,109 metres, Banswara).
   - Largest Dam by Water Storage: **Rana Pratap Sagar Dam** (Rawatbhata, Chittorgarh).
8. **Kota Barrage: Zero Hydropower**: Out of the 4 dams on the Chambal River, **Kota Barrage produces ZERO MW of hydroelectric power**; it is exclusively an irrigation diversion structure. The other three (Gandhi Sagar, Rana Pratap Sagar, Jawahar Sagar) generate power.
9. **Chambal Dam Locations (The MP Trap)**: **Gandhi Sagar Dam is NOT in Rajasthan**! It is located in Mandsaur district of Madhya Pradesh. The remaining three dams are located in Rajasthan.
10. **Chulia Waterfall Height & Location**: The Chulia waterfall is **18 metres high** and is located at **Bhainsrorgarh (Chittorgarh)**, NOT in Kota or Bundi!
11. **Dang ki Rani vs Dang ka Raja**:
    - *Dang ki Rani*: **Karauli**.
    - *Dang ka Raja*: **Dholpur**.
12. **The Two Distinct Gambhir Rivers**:
    - **Gambhir River (*गम्भीर*)**: Originates in Karauli, feeds Panchana Dam, terminates in Yamuna.
    - **Gambhiri River (*गम्भीरी*)**: Originates in MP, joins the Bedach River near Chittorgarh city.
13. **Panchana Dam Composition**: Panchana Dam is the **largest earthen / clay dam (*मिट्टी का बांध*) in Rajasthan**, formed by 5 rivulets: Ata, Machi, Bhadrawati, Barkheda, and Bhainsawat.
14. **Ayad to Bedach Name Transition**: The river is called **Ayad** from its origin at Gogunda until it pours into **Udaisagar Lake**. Downstream of Udaisagar, its official geographic name becomes the **Bedach**.
15. **Rundhit Sarita Classification**: **Banganga** is scientifically termed a *Rundhit Sarita* (severed river) because it no longer reaches its parent river (Yamuna), instead drying up in the alluvial wetlands of Bharatpur.
16. **Longest Inland River: Purely Rajasthan vs Total**:
    - Longest inland river originating and terminating within Rajasthan: **Kantli River** (~100 km).
    - Longest inland river overall passing through Rajasthan: **Ghaggar River** (~465 km).
17. **Talwara Lake vs Nakki Lake (Elevation Extremes)**:
    - Lowest elevation lake in Rajasthan: **Talwara Lake** (Hanumangarh, bed of Ghaggar).
    - Highest elevation lake in Rajasthan: **Nakki Lake** (Mount Abu, 1,200 m).
18. **Ghaggar Terminology: Nali vs Hakra**:
    - The bed inside Rajasthan is termed **Nali**.
    - The bed across the border in Pakistan is termed **Hakra**.
19. **Sambhar Lake Salt Percentage**: Sambhar produces **8.7% of India's total salt production** (NOT 80%! A classic multiple-choice numerical trap where candidates confuse 8.7% of national production with high local figures).
20. **Pachpadra vs Didwana Salt Grades**:
    - **Pachpadra**: 98% pure sodium chloride ($NaCl$), supreme edible culinary quality.
    - **Didwana**: High sodium sulphate ($Na_2SO_4$), **non-edible**, strictly industrial.
21. **Kolayat Lake Taboo**: Members of the **Charan community** abstain from visiting or bathing in Kolayat Lake because legend records that Karni Mata's adopted son, Lakhan, drowned in it on Kartik Purnima.
22. **Lake Pichhola Originator**: Lake Pichhola was NOT built by Maharana Udai Singh or Maharana Lakha; it was built by a nomadic grain merchant, **Chhitarmal / Pichhu Banjara**, in the late 14th century. Udai Singh only expanded its bund later.
23. **Connaught Dam Real Name**: The Duke of Connaught laid the foundation stone of **Fateh Sagar Lake's** rebuilt dam, hence its main bund is called the *Connaught Dam*.
24. **World's Largest Stone Inscription Location**: The **Raj Prashasti** is engraved on 25 black marble slabs at **Rajsamand Lake** (on the *Nauchowki ki Pal*), NOT at Jaisamand Lake!
25. **Jaisamand's Modern District**: Following the 2023 administrative reorganizations, **Jaisamand Lake is now located in Salumbar district**, NOT in Udaipur!

## 8. Model Examination Questions & Benchmark Frameworks

### 2-Marker Benchmark Responses (15 Words)

#### Q1. What is a *Rundhit Sarita*? Give an example from Rajasthan.
**Model Answer**:  
A river that fails to reach its master stream due to decreased discharge, terminating in its own alluvial silt before confluence. Example: **Banganga River** in eastern Rajasthan.

#### Q2. Name the five rivulets that converge at Panchana Dam.
**Model Answer**:  
**Ata, Machi, Bhadrawati, Barkheda, and Bhainsawat**. Located in Karauli, it is Rajasthan's largest earthen dam.

#### Q3. Why does the Luni River turn saline downstream of Balotra?
**Model Answer**:  
Due to high evaporation, subterranean halite deposits from the ancient Tethys Sea, and capillary leaching of sodium chloride from weathered granitic soils.

#### Q4. What is the geological significance of the Raj Prashasti?
**Model Answer**:  
Engraved on 25 black marble slabs at Rajsamand Lake by Ranchhod Bhatt; with 1,106 Sanskrit verses, it is the **world's largest stone inscription**.

### 5-Marker Benchmark Responses (50 Words)

#### Q5. Delineate the geomorphological alignment of the Great Indian Continental Divide across Rajasthan.
**Model Answer**:  
The Aravalli Range acts as the Great Continental Divide, separating the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal drainages:
1. **Trace**: Runs from Delhi Ridge through Khetri, Alwar, Sambhar Lake, Ajmer (Nag Pahar), Beawar, and the Bhorat Plateau (1,225 m).
2. **Hydrological Bifurcation**: 
   - Western slopes feed the **Arabian Sea** (~17.4% area via Luni, Mahi, Sabarmati, West Banas).
   - Eastern slopes feed the **Bay of Bengal** (~22.4% area via Chambal, Banas, Gambhir).
   - Rain falling on the western edge of Bhorat flows into the Arabian Sea; rain falling on the eastern edge flows to the Bay of Bengal.

#### Q6. Analyze the hydro-electric and irrigation architecture of the Chambal Valley Project.
**Model Answer**:  
The Chambal Project is a 50:50 joint venture between Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh executed in three stages:
1. **Four Cascading Dams**:
   - **Gandhi Sagar** (MP, 1960): 115 MW; largest storage reservoir.
   - **Rana Pratap Sagar** (Chittorgarh, 1970): 172 MW; largest storage dam in Rajasthan; feeds RAPS nuclear plant.
   - **Jawahar Sagar** (Kota/Bundi, 1972): 99 MW; peak-load hydel station.
   - **Kota Barrage** (Kota, 1960): 0 MW; pure irrigation diversion feeding Right and Left Main Canals.
2. **Power & Water Equity**: Total hydel output of 386 MW is shared equally (Rajasthan gets 193 MW); irrigates over 5 lakh hectares in Hadoti.

### 10-Marker Analytical Essay (100 Words / 200 Words)

#### Q7. Provide a comprehensive comparative evaluation of the Inland Drainage System of Rajasthan. Analyze its geomorphological origins, spatial distribution, ecological challenges, and archaeological significance.

**Model Answer Architecture**:

```text

         ▼                  ▼                   ▼                   ▼                  ▼
```

1. **Geomorphological Origins & Extent**:
   - Comprises **~60.2% of Rajasthan's total geographical area**, making it the largest endorheic system in India.
   - Caused by the hyper-arid climate, low hydraulic gradients, extreme evaporation, and towering longitudinal/barkhan sand dunes that block river flow, preventing surface runoff from reaching the sea.
   - Represents the desiccated remnants of the Vedic Saraswati and Drishadvati river systems.

2. **Spatial Distribution & Major Rivers**:
   - **Ghaggar River**: Only international river; traverses Hanumangarh, Sriganganagar, and Anupgarh in its fertile *Nali* bed; floodwaters historically reach Fort Abbas (*Hakra*) in Pakistan.
   - **Kantli River**: 100 km long river of Torawati (Sikar, Neem Ka Thana, Jhunjhunu); longest purely inland river originating and terminating within Rajasthan.
   - **Sabi River**: Originates at Sewar (Kotputli); drains into Haryana's Najafgarh wetland.
   - **Ruparel River**: Originates at Udainath (Alwar); feeds Moti Jheel ("Lifeline of Bharatpur").
   - **Sambhar Catchment**: Inflowing streams Mendha, Rupangarh, Khari, and Khandela feed India's largest inland saline lake.

3. **Archaeological Stratigraphy**:
   - These dry channels hosted India's greatest prehistoric cultures:
     - **Kalibangan & Rangmahal** on the Ghaggar (Indus Valley Civilization).
     - **Ganeshwar** on the Kantli (Copper Age mother culture).
     - **Jodhpura** on the Sabi (Painted Grey Ware and Iron Age furnaces).

4. **Contemporary Ecological Challenges & Management**:
   - **Siltation & Course Choking**: Severe deforestation in Aravalli headwaters has accelerated sediment yield, choking river channels and causing localized flash floods during erratic monsoon bursts.
   - **Pollution of Terminal Playas**: Heavy industrial and municipal discharge into ephemeral streams (such as textile dyes in Bandi and chemical salts in Mendha) degrades critical Ramsar habitats like Sambhar Lake (evidenced by the 2019 Avian Botulism disaster).
   - **Sustainable Interventions**: Requires extensive catchment afforestation, desilting of traditional check-dams (*Johads*), and integrated watershed management under the State Water Policy.
