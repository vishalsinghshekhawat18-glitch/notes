## 1. Secular & Residential Architecture: The Royal Palaces (*Raj-Mahal*)

Royal palaces in Rajasthan evolved from fortified medieval keeps (such as the early citadel rooms of Chittor and Kumbhalgarh) into expansive, luxurious, multi-courtyard complexes during the Pax Mughalica and British eras. They synthesized Rajput traditional spatial planning (*Zanana* and *Mardana* segregation, *Jharokhas*, *Chhatris*, and *Bari*) with Mughal elements (fluted arches, cusped vaults, mirrored mosaics, and water cascades).

### 1.1 City Palace, Udaipur (उदयपुर का राजमहल)
- **Foundational Epoch**: Commenced in **1559 CE by Maharana Udai Singh II** along the eastern embankment of Lake Pichola; successively expanded over 400 years by 22 successive Maharanas without breaking architectural harmony.
- **James Fergusson's Verdict**: The British architectural historian James Fergusson famously characterized it as the **"Windsor of Rajasthan"** (*राजस्थान का विंडसर महल*) due to its colossal scale, dramatic lakeside cliff setting, and granite-marble masonry.
- **Architectural Anatomy & Key Pavilions**:
  1. **Badi Mahal (Garden Palace)**: Constructed on a natural 90-foot hillock enclosed within the upper palace, featuring 104 carved marble pillars, cisterns, and lush fruit trees.
  2. **Chandra Mahal & Dilkhush Mahal**: Intricately adorned with Belgian glass mosaics, mirrored walls, and traditional Mewari miniature murals.
  3. **Moti Mahal & Sheesh Mahal**: Built by Maharana Karan Singh (1620–1628), showcasing early Mughal architectural integration.
  4. **Toran Pol & Badi Pol**: Grand entrance portals; outside Badi Pol sits the historic *Hathi Agad* (arena for elephant duels).
  5. **Mor Chowk (Peacock Courtyard)**: Executed during Maharana Sajjan Singh’s reign; adorned with three world-renowned relief peacocks crafted from over 5,000 brilliant green, blue, and gold glass tesserae representing summer, monsoon, and winter.

### 1.2 City Palace & Hawa Mahal, Jaipur

#### City Palace Complex (Chandra Mahal)
- **Genesis & Layout**: Commissioned in **1727 CE by Sawai Jai Singh II** and designed by chief architect **Vidyadhar Bhattacharya** according to Vastu Purusha Mandala principles. Occupies one-seventh of the walled city of Jaipur.
- **Chandra Mahal (Moon Palace)**: The monumental seven-storeyed sanctum sanctorum of the Kachhwaha dynasty:
  - *Ground Floor*: Pitam Niwas / Sukh Niwas.
  - *Second Floor*: Rang Mandir.
  - *Third Floor*: Shobha Niwas (Mica and mirror decoration).
  - *Fourth Floor*: Chhavi Niwas (Blue and white monochrome Chinese porcelain patterns).
  - *Fifth Floor*: Shri Niwas.
  - *Sixth Floor*: Mukut Mandir (Crowned apex displaying the royal *Pachranga* flag).
- **Mubarak Mahal**: Built in the late 19th century by **Sawai Madho Singh II** as a royal reception pavilion (*Welcome Palace*); designed by British architect **Sir Samuel Swinton Jacob** in an ornate Indo-Saracenic synthesis combining Mughal, Rajput, and European classical styles.
- **Pritam Niwas Chowk (Courtyard of the Beloved)**: Inner courtyard renowned for its four vibrantly painted gateway arches dedicated to the four seasons and Hindu deities:
  1. *Peacock Gate* (North-East, dedicated to Lord Vishnu / Autumn).
  2. *Lotus Gate* (South-West, dedicated to Shiva-Parvati / Summer).
  3. *Green / Waves Gate* (North-West, dedicated to Lord Ganesha / Monsoon).
  4. *Rose Gate* (South-East, dedicated to Devi / Winter).
- **The Gangajalis (Guinness World Record)**: Two colossal sterling silver vessels displayed in the *Diwan-i-Khas*, commissioned by Sawai Madho Singh II in 1901 to carry 4,000 liters of holy Ganges water for his voyage to London for King Edward VII's coronation. They are certified as the largest single-piece silver vessels in the world (each weighing 340 kg).

#### Hawa Mahal (हवामहल) — "Palace of the Breeze"
- **Date & Patron**: Erected in **1799 CE by Sawai Pratap Singh**; envisioned as an extension of the City Palace *Zanana*.
- **Master Architect**: **Ustad Lal Chand Ustad** (उस्ताद लाल चंद).
- **Iconography**: Shaped in the silhouette of the divine crown (*Mukut*) of **Lord Krishna**, whom Sawai Pratap Singh devoutly worshipped under his pen name *Brijnidhi*.
- **Architectural Mechanics**:
  - Soars **five storeys high (87 feet)** without any traditional deep foundation pit; constructed entirely on a slender 8-inch base wall using red and pink sandstone.
  - Features **953 intricately carved latticed stone windows (*Jharokhas*)** and tiny casements (*Jalis*).
  - Utilizes the **Venturi effect**: air passing through narrow perforations accelerates and undergoes adiabatic cooling, ensuring a continuous chilled breeze during peak summer.
  - Built explicitly so royal secluded ladies (*Pardanashin Zanana*) could discreetly observe bustling street pageants and religious processions (*Teej*, *Gangaur*) along Badi Chaupar without being viewed by the public.

## 2. Aristocratic & Mercantile Architecture: The Havelis of Rajasthan

Havelis (*हवेलियाँ*) were private residential mansions built by wealthy prime ministers, royal treasurers (*Diwans*), feudal thakurs, and prominent Marwari merchant families (*Seth-Sahukars*). They reached their artistic zenith during the 18th, 19th, and early 20th centuries, functioning as secure urban fortresses, business offices (*Gaddis*), and social showpieces.

### 2.1 The Golden Havelis of Jaisalmer

#### 1. Patwon ki Haveli (पटवों की हवेली) — The Crown Jewel
- **Founder & Date**: Commissioned by **Guman Chand Patwa** (a wealthy merchant of brocade, jewelry, and opium) in **1805 CE**.
- **Structural Blueprint**: A colossal cluster of **five separate storeys/mansions** built over a span of 50 years for his five sons.
- **Architectural Excellence**:
  - The first and grandest haveli constructed in Jaisalmer.
  - Carved entirely from golden-yellow Jurassic limestone; famous for over **60 unique open balconies (*Jharokhas*)**, perforated floral lattices, and slender stone balustrades carved so thinly that stone resembles spun lace.

#### 2. Salim Singh ki Haveli (सालिम सिंह की हवेली)
- **Founder**: Commissioned in **1815 CE by Salim Singh Mohta**, the eccentric and ruthless prime minister (*Diwan*) of Jaisalmer State.
- **Unique Silhouette**:
  - Built with a narrow stone base that fans outward dramatically on the fifth and sixth storeys, resembling the prow of a sailing ship — hence titled **Jahaz Mahal** (*जहाज महल*).
  - The upper cantilevered floor features 38 intricately sculpted balconies crowned with dancing peacock brackets (*Moti Mahal*). Originally possessed two wooden/glass top storeys, which were dismantled by order of the Maharawal due to royal jealousy.

#### 3. Nathmal ki Haveli (नथमल की हवेली)
- **Patron**: Built in **1885 CE** as the official residence for Diwan Mohata Nathmal.
- **Twin Master Sculptors**: Carved simultaneously by two Muslim architect brothers, **Hathi (हाथी)** and **Lalu (लालू)**. One brother carved the right half and the other carved the left half independently. Though symmetrically unified from a distance, minute examination reveals that no two architectural panels or jali screens are identical.
- **Unique Embellishments**: Two life-sized stone elephants (*Hathi*) flank the main arched entry gate; features early carvings of steam engines, bicycles, and wristwatches based on the brothers' descriptions of modern inventions they had never seen firsthand.

### 2.2 The Painted Havelis of Shekhawati — "Open-Air Art Gallery"
The arid triangle of Shekhawati (spanning Sikar, Jhunjhunu, and Churu districts) possesses the world's highest concentration of painted frescoed mansions, earning it global acclaim as the **"Open-Air Art Gallery of Rajasthan"**.

#### The Fresco Techniques: Arayash / Ala-Gila / Mora-Kashi
To withstand scorching summer heat and desert dust storms, Shekhawati artists (*Chiteras*) perfected traditional mural plastering methods:
1. **Fresco Buono (Ala-Gila / Arayash / गीला पलस्तर)**:
   - Pigments ground from natural minerals (lapis lazuli for blue, cinnabar for red, yellow ochre, copper sulfate for green, and soot for black) were applied directly onto **fresh, wet lime plaster**.
   - As the slaked lime plaster (*Chuna*) dried, calcium hydroxide reacted with atmospheric carbon dioxide to form insoluble calcium carbonate, binding the pigments permanently into the wall fabric itself.
   - The surface was buffed vigorously with smooth agate stones (*Ghutai*) until it achieved a mirror-like marble sheen.
2. **Fresco Secco**: Pigments applied onto dried lime wash using organic binders (glue or gum), used for faster, less durable exterior embellishments.

#### The Thematic Evolution of Shekhawati Murals
- **Phase 1 (18th – Mid 19th Century)**: Strictly religious, mythological, and local folk themes: Krishna Leela, Raslila, Ramayana episodes, Dhola-Maru, Pabuji, and equestrian hunts.
- **Phase 2 (Late 19th – Early 20th Century)**: British colonial impact: Portraits of Queen Victoria, British soldiers with muskets, steam locomotives, phonographs, motorcars, hot air balloons, and telephones painted alongside traditional deities.

### 2.3 Other Notable Havelis of Rajasthan
- **Rampuria Havelis, Bikaner**: Built by merchant Baldeo Das Rampuria using fine red **Dulmera sandstone**; celebrated for their fusion of traditional Jharokhas with Victorian European floral relief brackets.
- **Bade Devtaji ki Haveli & Jhala Ji ki Haveli, Kota**: Acclaimed for exceptional wall paintings executed in the classical **Kota School of Painting** (hunting expeditions, royal durbars).
- **Pungaliya Haveli & Bade Miyan ki Haveli, Jodhpur**: Ornate red sandstone lattice screens with characteristic Marwari arches.
- **Bagore ki Haveli, Udaipur**: Perched at Gangaur Ghat on Lake Pichola; built by Mewar Prime Minister **Amarchand Barwa**; houses the world's largest turban and a preserved royal puppet theater.

## 3. Hydraulic Stepwell Architecture: Baoris & Kunds of Rajasthan

In the arid and semi-arid terrain of Rajasthan, water architecture transcended utilitarian storage to become sacred community monuments. Stepwells (*Baori* / *Bawdi* / *Vapi*) combined deep subterranean wells with descending stepped corridors, pillared pavilions, and stone idols of water and fertility deities.

### 3.1 Chand Baori, Abhaneri (चाँद बावड़ी, आभानेरी)
- **Location**: Abhaneri village, Bandikui, **Dausa district**.
- **Epoch & Builder**: Constructed in the **8th–9th century CE** by **Raja Chand (Nikumbha Rajput ruler)** of ancient *Abha-Nagari* (City of Brightness).
- **Architectural Anatomy**:
  - The **deepest and most geometrically complex stepwell in the world**: drops over **100 feet (13 storeys)** into the earth.
  - Comprises precisely **3,500 crisscrossing steps** arranged in perfectly symmetrical double-diamond flight patterns.
  - The steps are engineered with optical illusion precision: an individual ascending the steps cannot traverse the exact same staircase configuration taken while descending.
  - The northern face contains multi-tiered arched pavilions (*Chhatris*) housing carved statues of Mahishasuramardini and Lord Ganesha, serving as the royal rest chambers.
  - Overlooks the adjoining 8th-century **Harshat Mata Temple** (a masterpiece of Gurjara-Pratihara temple art).

### 3.2 Raniji ki Baori, Bundi (रानीजी की बावड़ी) — "The Queen of Stepwells"
- **Location**: Bundi, officially celebrated as the **"City of Stepwells"** (*बावड़ियों का शहर*).
- **Founding Monarch**: Built in **1699 CE by Rani Nathavati** (consort of Rao Raja Aniruddha Singh and mother of Maharao Budh Singh).
- **Dimensions & Artistic Grandeur**:
  - Drops **150 feet** deep; entered through a magnificent freestanding stone ceremonial archway (*Torana Dwar*).
  - Flanked by four soaring sculpted pillars terminating in bracketed capitals with life-sized sculpted elephants (*Gaj-Lakshmi*).
  - Contains intricate niche shrines displaying the ten incarnations (*Dashavatara*) of Lord Vishnu along its subterranean stairs.
  - Acknowledged as the most ornate and architecturally flawless stepwell in south-eastern Rajasthan.

## 4. Memorial & Commemorative Architecture: The Chhatris (Cenotaphs)

Chhatris (*छतरियाँ*) are elevated, domed commemorative cenotaphs erected over the cremation sites of kings, queens, feudal nobility, war heroes, and ascetic saints. Rooted in ancient Hindu funerary stambhas, they developed under Rajput patronage into sublime open-pillared pavilions adorned with carved domes, decorative finials (*Amalaka & Kalasha*), and central equestrian or Shiva lingam steles.


### 4.1 Master Individual Chhatris of Rajasthan

#### 1. 84-Pillared Chhatri, Bundi (चौरासी खंभों की छतरी)
- **Patron & Date**: Commissioned in **1683 CE by Rao Raja Aniruddha Singh** in memory of his beloved foster brother **Dhaabhai Deva (धाभाई देवा)**.
- **Structural Blueprint**:
  - Two-storeyed pavilion supported by **precisely 84 carved stone pillars**.
  - Lower floor contains a central sacred **Shiva Lingam** surrounded by carvings of animals, dancers, and musicians.
  - The upper floor features 16 central pillars supporting a grand fluted dome flanked by four corner chhatris.

#### 2. Moosi Maharani ki Chhatri, Alwar (मूसी महारानी की छतरी)
- **Patron & Date**: Constructed in **1815 CE by Maharaja Vinay Singh** in memory of his predecessor **Maharaja Bakhtawar Singh** and his consort **Moosi Maharani**, who performed Sati.
- **Architectural Synthesis**:
  - Supported by **80 carved pillars**.
  - **Bichromatic Masonry**: The lower storey is carved from rugged red sandstone, while the upper storey is crafted from pure white Makrana marble with Bengal-style vaulted cornices (*Bangaldar Chhatri*).
  - Ceiling contains murals depicting the Ramayana and Mahabharata.

#### 3. Jaswant Thada, Jodhpur (जसवंत थड़ा) — "The Taj Mahal of Marwar"
- **Patron & Date**: Erected in **1899 CE by Maharaja Sardar Singh** to commemorate his father, **Maharaja Jaswant Singh II**.
- **Architectural Marvel**:
  - Carved entirely from paper-thin, polished sheets of **Makrana white marble**.
  - The marble slabs are carved so thinly that sunlight penetrates the solid walls, casting a translucent golden-white inner luminescence.
  - Encloses memorial portraits of Jodhpur rulers, intricately landscaped gardens, and a sacred cremation lake.

### 5.1 The Earliest Inscribed Temple: Sheetleshwar Mahadev, Jhalrapatan (689 CE)
- Located at **Jhalrapatan (Jhalawar)** on the banks of the Chandrabhaga river.
- Bearing an epigraph of **689 CE (V.S. 746)** from the reign of king Durgagana, it is officially recognized as the **oldest dated temple in Rajasthan** (*राजस्थान का प्रथम तिथि-अंकित मंदिर*). Built by royal architect Voppa.

---

### 5.2 The Gurjara-Pratihara / Maha-Maru Style (8th–11th Century CE)

#### 1. Osian Temple Complex, Jodhpur Rural (ओसियां)
- Celebrated as the **"Khajuraho / Bhuvaneshwar of Rajasthan"**; served as the prime socio-religious capital of the Gurjara-Pratiharas.
- Contains 16 Hindu and Jain temples dating between the 8th and 11th centuries:
  - **Mahavira Temple**: Erected during the reign of **Pratihara king Vatsaraja (778–794 CE)**; universally acknowledged as the **oldest surviving Jain temple in western India**.
  - **Sachiya Mata Temple**: Consecrated to Mahishasuramardini; revered as the family deity (*Kuldevi*) of the **Oswal Jain community**.
  - **Surya Temples**: Three distinct sun temples boasting elaborately carved sanctum doorframes (*Dwar-Sakhas*) depicting Navagrahas and flying Gandharvas.

#### 2. Kiradu Temple Complex, Barmer (किराडू के मंदिर)
- Located at the foot of Hathma village hills in Barmer; ancient epigraphic name: **Kiratkoot (किराटकूट)**.
- Famous as the **"Khajuraho of Rajasthan"** (*राजस्थान का खजुराहो*) due to its masterfully sculpted human figures, sensuous erotic reliefs, and celestial musicians (*Apsaras*).
- Comprises five temples (four dedicated to Shiva and one to Vishnu), with the **Someshwara Temple (सोमेश्वर मंदिर)** being the largest and most architecturally complete. Built in the late Gurjara-Pratihara / early Solanki transitional style (11th–12th century).

#### 3. Other Maha-Maru Shrines
- **Harshat Mata Temple, Abhaneri (Dausa)**: 8th-century sanctuary dedicated to the Goddess of Joy; exquisite bas-reliefs of dance, music, and divine love.
- **Harshnath Temple, Sikar**: Built in **973 CE by Chauhan ruler Vigraharaja II**; designed by ascetic Allata; dedicated to Lord Shiva atop Harsh Hill.
- **Baroli Temple Complex, Rawatbhata (Chittorgarh)**: 9th-century Panchayatana temples dedicated to Ghateshwar Mahadev on the Chambal river; acclaimed by James Tod for flawless proportions.

#### 2. Ranakpur Jain Temple, Pali (रणकपुर का चौमुखा जैन मंदिर)
- **Geographic Setting**: Situated in the dense Aravali gorge of Sadri, Desuri tehsil, **Pali district**, along the banks of the **Maghai river**.
- **Historical Era**: Commenced in **1439 CE** during the glorious reign of **Maharana Kumbha of Mewar**.
- **The Pious Merchant**: Commissioned and funded by the wealthy Jain Porwal merchant **Dharanashah (धरणशाह / धरणा शाह)** after receiving a divine vision of a celestial aerial vehicle (*Nalini-Gulma Vimana*). Maharana Kumbha gifted the surrounding land and village.
- **The Master Architect**: **Depaka (देपाका / दीपा)**, whose genius transformed white Makrana marble into a sprawling three-dimensional sacred labyrinth.
- **Dedication**: Dedicated to the 1st Jain Tirthankara, **Bhagwan Adinatha (Rishabhadeva)**.
- **The Chaumukha Configuration**: Features a 6-foot four-faced white marble idol of Adinatha facing all four cardinal directions, allowing worshippers to glimpse the Tirthankara from any quadrant of the sanctum.
- **Architectural Mechanics & The "Forest of Pillars"**:
  - Contains precisely **1,444 intricately sculpted marble pillars** (*स्तंभों का वन*).
  - **Sovereign Engineering Feat**: Despite 1,444 pillars, **no single pillar obstructs the direct line of sight** between a devotee standing anywhere in the peripheral colonnades and the central idol.
  - **Unique Carvings**: Every single pillar exhibits a distinct decorative motif; no two pillars are carved identically.
  - Features 84 secondary shrines (*Devakulikas*), 29 halls (*Mandapas*), and 4 grand entrance halls.
- **Classic Epigraphic Epithets**:
  - *Dharani-Vihara* (धरण विहार — named after Dharanashah).
  - *Trailokya Dipaka* (त्रैलोक्य दीपक — "Illuminator of the Three Realms").
  - *Khabhon ka Ajaibghar* (Museum of Pillars).

## 6. The 25 Deadly Prelims Traps: Palaces, Havelis, Baoris, Chhatris & Temples

1. **The "Windsor of Rajasthan" Attribution**: The City Palace of **Udaipur** was called the "Windsor of Rajasthan" by British architectural historian **James Fergusson**, NOT James Tod.
2. **Hawa Mahal Architect vs Founder**: Founded by **Sawai Pratap Singh (1799)**; designed by architect **Ustad Lal Chand Ustad**. It has **no regular foundation** and sits directly on a narrow stone base.
3. **The Five Storeys of Hawa Mahal Sequence**: Bottom to top: (1) Sharad Mandir, (2) Ratan Mandir, (3) Vichitra Mandir, (4) Prakash Mandir, (5) Hawa Mandir. If RPSC asks which floor gives the monument its name, it is the **topmost 5th floor (Hawa Mandir)**.
4. **Sterling Silver Gangajalis Location**: The world's largest silver vessels are housed in the **Diwan-i-Khas of City Palace, Jaipur**, commissioned by **Sawai Madho Singh II** for his 1901 voyage to England.
5. **Umaid Bhawan Palace Builder & Alternate Name**: Built by **Maharaja Umaid Singh** between 1929 and 1943 as a famine relief project (*Akal Rahat Karya*); called **Chittar Palace** because it is built from local golden *Chittar* sandstone; designed by British architect **Henry Vaughan Lanchester**.
6. **The First vs Grandest Haveli in Jaisalmer**: **Patwon ki Haveli** was the first haveli built in Jaisalmer (1805 CE, Guman Chand Patwa, 5 storeys for 5 sons). **Salim Singh ki Haveli** is shaped like a ship (*Jahaz Mahal*).
7. **The Non-Identical Symmetry of Nathmal ki Haveli**: Carved by two brothers, **Hathi and Lalu**, who carved left and right halves simultaneously without looking at each other's designs, resulting in intentional, microscopic asymmetries.
8. **The Ala-Gila / Arayash Plaster Formula**: *Ala-Gila* is the technique of applying pigments onto **wet lime plaster** (*Fresco Buono*), buffed with agate stone (*Ghutai*) to create a marble shine. It originated in Jaipur and spread across Shekhawati.
9. **Haveli with 1,111 Windows and Doors**: **Surana Haveli** in **Churu**, famously known as the *"Hawa Mahal of Churu"*.
10. **The Golden City of Shekhawati**: **Nawalgarh** is titled the "Golden City of Shekhawati" due to its dense cluster of painted havelis (Poddar, Aath Haveli, Chokhani).
11. **Deepest Stepwell in the World**: **Chand Baori** in **Abhaneri (Dausa)**, 13 storeys, 100 feet deep, with **3,500 symmetrical steps** built by Nikumbha king Raja Chand (8th–9th century).
12. **The "City of Stepwells"**: **Bundi** is officially titled *"बावड़ियों का शहर"* (City of Stepwells). Its most famous stepwell is **Raniji ki Baori**, built in 1699 by Rani Nathavati.
13. **Subterranean Multi-Storey Stepwells**: **Neemrana Baori** in Kotputli-Behror has **9 storeys**; **Chand Baori** has **13 storeys**.
14. **The Exception to Gaitore Royal Cremations**: All Kachhwaha rulers of Jaipur from Sawai Jai Singh II onwards are cremated at **Gaitore**, EXCEPT **Sawai Ishwari Singh**, whose chhatri is located inside the City Palace compound near **Talkatora** (due to his suicide over Maratha debt).
15. **The "Taj Mahal of Marwar"**: **Jaswant Thada** in **Jodhpur**, built in 1899 by **Maharaja Sardar Singh** for his father Maharaja Jaswant Singh II, using translucent white Makrana marble.
16. **84-Pillared vs 80-Pillared Chhatri**:
    - **84-Pillared Chhatri**: Located in **Bundi**, built in 1683 by Rao Raja Aniruddha Singh for foster brother **Dhaabhai Deva**.
    - **80-Pillared Chhatri**: Located in **Alwar**, built in 1815 by Maharaja Vinay Singh for **Bakhtawar Singh & Moosi Maharani**.
17. **32-Pillared Chhatri Dual Confusion**:
    - **Ranthambore Fort**: Built by Hammiradeva for his father **Jaitrasimha's 32-year reign** (*Nyaya ki Chhatri*).
    - **Mandalgarh (Bhilwara)**: Built for Mughal commander **Jagannath Kachhwaha**.
18. **Oldest Inscribed Dated Temple in Rajasthan**: **Sheetleshwar Mahadev Temple** at **Jhalrapatan (Jhalawar)**, dated **689 CE (V.S. 746)** from King Durgagana's reign.
19. **Oldest Surviving Jain Temple in Western India**: **Mahavira Temple at Osian** (Jodhpur Rural), built during the reign of Pratihara king **Vatsaraja** (8th century CE).
20. **The "Khajuraho of Rajasthan"**: **Kiradu Temples** in **Barmer** (specifically the Someshwara temple). Do NOT confuse with:
    - *"Mini Khajuraho / Khajuraho of Hadoti"*: **Bhanddevara Temple** (Ramgarh, Baran).
    - *"Khajuraho of Mewar"*: **Ambika Mata Temple** (Jagat, Udaipur).
21. **Vimal Vasahi vs Luna Vasahi Architects & Patrons**:
    - **Vimal Vasahi (1031 CE)**: Patron = Vimal Shah; Architect = **Kirti Dhara**; Dedicated to Adinatha.
    - **Luna Vasahi (1230 CE)**: Patron = Vastupala & Tejapala; Architect = **Shobhana Deva**; Dedicated to Neminatha; Contains *Derani-Jethani Gokhla*.
22. **The 1,444 Pillars of Ranakpur**: Ranakpur Jain Temple has **1,444 pillars**, built in **1439 CE** during Maharana Kumbha's reign by merchant **Dharanashah** and architect **Depaka**. It is dedicated to **Adinatha (Rishabhadeva)**.
23. **"Sapno se bana Mandir" (Temple Built of Dreams)**: The **Jagdish Temple** in Udaipur, built in **1651 CE by Maharana Jagat Singh I** (Architect: Barna), dedicated to Lord Vishnu in the Panchayatana style.
24. **Eklingji Temple Dedication & Sect**: Dedicated to Lord Shiva's **Lakulisha sect** (Pashupata); four-faced black marble Shivalinga founded by Bappa Rawal in 734 CE.
25. **The Bearded Hanuman Shrine**: **Salasar Balaji** in **Churu district** contains the only idol in Rajasthan representing Lord Hanuman with a **beard and mustache** (*दाढ़ी-मूँछ वाले हनुमान जी*).

## 7. RPSC RAS Mains High-Yield Model Answers

### 7.1 2-Markers (15–20 Words)

#### Q1: What is the architectural significance of Raniji ki Baori in Bundi?
**Answer**:
Built in 1699 CE by Rani Nathavati, this 150-foot deep stepwell features a grand *Torana Dwar*, high-relief *Gaj-Lakshmi* pillar brackets, and sculpted *Dashavatara* niches, representing the apex of Hadoti water architecture.

---

#### Q2: Name the master architect and the patron of Ranakpur Jain Temple.
**Answer**:
Commissioned and funded in 1439 CE by the Jain Porwal merchant **Dharanashah** under the royal patronage of **Maharana Kumbha**; engineered by master architect **Depaka**.

---

#### Q3: What is the "Ala-Gila" or "Arayash" technique?
**Answer**:
A traditional Rajasthani *Fresco Buono* plaster method where mineral pigments are applied directly onto wet slaked lime plaster and buffed with agate stone, binding colors permanently with a marble-like luster.

---

#### Q4: Why is Jaswant Thada termed the "Taj Mahal of Marwar"?
**Answer**:
Erected in Jodhpur (1899 CE) by Maharaja Sardar Singh for Jaswant Singh II, it is constructed from paper-thin, polished Makrana white marble that glows translucently under sunlight.

---

#### Q5: State the key architectural feature of the Dilwara Luna Vasahi temple.
**Answer**:
Built in 1230 CE by Vastupala-Tejapala (architect Shobhana Deva) dedicated to Neminatha; renowned for its delicate marble pendant dome and the intricately carved *Derani-Jethani ka Gokhla*.

---

### 7.2 5-Markers (50 Words)

#### Q6: Analyze the functional and bioclimatic architecture of Hawa Mahal in Jaipur.
**Answer**:
Erected in 1799 CE by Sawai Pratap Singh and designed by Ustad Lal Chand, Hawa Mahal is a 5-storeyed, 87-foot monument without deep foundations, shaped like Lord Krishna’s crown.
1. **Bioclimatic Engineering**: Its 953 stone *Jharokhas* and latticed *Jalis* exploit the **Venturi effect**, cooling incoming desert wind through adiabatic pressure drops.
2. **Socio-Cultural Function**: Permitted royal *Pardanashin* women to observe city festivals and processions on Badi Chaupar without violating purdah seclusion.

---

#### Q7: Delineate the distinct architectural and decorative characteristics of Shekhawati havelis.
**Answer**:
Developed by Marwari merchants during the 18th to early 20th centuries across Sikar, Jhunjhunu, and Churu, Shekhawati havelis represent an "Open-Air Art Gallery":
1. **Spatial Plan**: Dual-courtyard layout separating outer commercial spaces (*Mardana*) from inner domestic spaces (*Zanana*).
2. **Fresco Buono**: Exterior and interior walls plastered using *Arayash/Ala-Gila* techniques with natural mineral pigments.
3. **Thematic Evolution**: Shifted from Krishna Leela and folk epics (Dhola-Maru) to colonial innovations (railway trains, motorcars, and airplanes).

---

#### Q8: Write a concise note on the Gurjara-Pratihara (Maha-Maru) temple style with two examples.
**Answer**:
Flourishing between the 8th and 11th centuries CE in Rajasthan, the **Maha-Maru style** is distinguished by elevated carved plinths (*Jagati*), clustered curvilinear *Shikharas*, ornate doorframes (*Dwar-Sakhas*) depicting river goddesses (Ganga-Yamuna), and rich pediments:
1. **Mahavira Temple, Osian (Jodhpur Rural)**: Consecrated under Pratihara king Vatsaraja (8th century); oldest surviving Jain shrine in western India.
2. **Kiradu Temples (Barmer)**: Someshwara Shiva temple, celebrated as the "Khajuraho of Rajasthan" for its exquisite sculptures.
