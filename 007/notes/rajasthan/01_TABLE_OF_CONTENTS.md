## Detailed Part Breakdown & Curricular Blueprint

### Part I: Prehistoric Sites, Epigraphy & Medieval Rajput Dynasties
* **Chapter 01: Prehistoric Civilizations, Archaeological Sites & Epigraphic Heritage**
  * Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic sites in Rajasthan: Bagor (Kothari river, Mesolithic animal husbandry proof), Tilwara (Luni basin).
  * Chalcolithic & Bronze Age Civilizations:
    * Ahar-Banas Culture (Dhulkot, Tambavati Nagari, terracotta bulls, black-and-red ware, baking ovens).
    * Ganeshwar (Kantli river, Sikar/Neem Ka Thana, Mother of Copper Cultures, 99% pure copper tools).
    * Kalibangan (Ghaggar river, Hanumangarh, ploughed field, fire altars, grid plan, earthquake evidence).
    * Balathal (Vallabhnagar, Udaipur, woven cloth, copper knives), Gilund (Rajsamand).
  * Iron Age & Historic Civilizations: Bairat (Viratnagar, Ashokan Bhabru edict, Buddhist chaityas, Greek coins), Sunari, Rairh (Tatanagar of Rajasthan), Nagari (Madhyamika), Nagar (Karkot Nagar).
  * Epigraphic Heritage & Inscriptions: Ghosundi Inscription (early Vaishnavism / Bhagavat cult), Bijolia Inscription (Chauhan genealogy, Gotra, ancient place names: Jabalipura, Shakambhari, Mandalgarh), Ranakpur Prashasti, Kumbhalgarh Prashasti, Kiradu Inscriptions.
* **Chapter 02: Early Medieval Dynasties: Gurjara-Pratiharas & Chauhans**
  * Gurjara-Pratiharas: Origin theories, Mandore branch (Harishchandra, Rajjil, Nagabhata I), Bhinmal-Kannauj branch (Vatsaraja and the Tripartite Struggle, Nagabhata II, Mihir Bhoja, Mahendrapala I - court poet Rajasekhara, Mahipala); Administrative titles and temple patronage.
  * Chauhans of Shakambhari & Ajmer: Vasudeva (551 CE, Sambhar salt lake creation), Ajayraja (Ajmer foundation 1113 CE, Ajaymeru fort), Arnoraja (Ana Sagar lake), Vigraharaja IV / Bisaldeo (Harkeli drama, Sanskrit college / Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra, golden age), Prithviraja III (Battles of Tarain 1191 & 1192 CE, digvijaya policy, Chand Bardai).
  * Chauhans of Ranthambore: Govindaraja (1194 CE), Hammiradeva (1282–1301 CE, "Hammir Hatth", resistance against Jalaluddin & Alauddin Khalji, 1301 CE siege, first Jauhar of Rajasthan).
  * Chauhans of Jalore: Kirtipala (Kitu ek mahan Rajput), Kanhadadeva (1308 Siwana siege / Sataldeo, 1311 Jalore siege / Alauddin Khalji, Padmanabha's *Kanhadade Prabandha*).
* **Chapter 03: The Guhils & Sisodias of Mewar**
  * Guhil foundations: Guhaditya (566 CE), Bappa Rawal (734 CE, captured Chittor from Man Mori, Nagda capital, Eklingji temple), Jaitra Singh (Battle of Bhutala 1227 CE against Iltutmish, shifted capital to Chittor).
  * Rawal Ratan Singh & 1303 CE Chittor Siege (Alauddin Khalji, Rani Padmini's Jauhar, Khizr Khan).
  * Rana Hammir (1326 CE, Rana branch/Sisodia foundation, "Vishama Ghati Panchanan").
  * Maharana Kumbha (1433–1468 CE): Battles of Sarangpur (1437 CE), Treaty of Champaner (1456 CE), construction of 32 of 84 forts (Shyamaldas's *Vir Vinod*), Vijay Stambha (architects: Jaita, Napa, Poma, Punja), literary works (*Sangitaraja*, *Sangitamimamsa*, *Rasikapriya* commentary).
  * Maharana Sanga (1509–1528 CE): Unification of Rajput confederacy, battles of Khatoli, Dholpur, Gagron, Bayana (Feb 1527), and Battle of Khanwa (March 1527, Babur, cannonry, tulghuma tactics, wounded warrior).
  * Maharana Pratap (1572–1597 CE): Coronation at Gogunda, Mughal diplomatic missions (Jalal Khan, Man Singh, Bhagwan Das, Todar Mal), Battle of Haldighati (June 1576, commanders, Hakim Khan Sur, Jhala Manna, Chetak), Guerrilla warfare, Battle of Dewair (1582, "Marathon of Mewar"), Chavand capital, architectural & artistic renaissance.
  * Maharana Amar Singh I (Mughal-Mewar Treaty 1615 CE) & Maharana Raj Singh (Battle of Debari, Rajsamand Lake, Charumati episode, patron of Srinathji temple Nathdwara).
* **Chapter 04: The Rathores of Marwar & Bikaner**
  * Marwar: Rao Siha (founder), Rao Chunda (Mandore capital), Rao Jodha (1459 CE Jodhpur foundation, Mehrangarh fort), Rao Maldeo (1531–1562 CE, "Hashmat Vala Raja", battle of Paheba, battle of Giri-Sumel 1544 against Sher Shah Suri).
  * Rao Chandrasen (1562–1581 CE, "The Forgotten Hero", "Pratap of Marwar", Nagaur Court 1570 boycott, Bhadrajun & Siwana resistance).
  * Mota Raja Udai Singh, Maharaja Jaswant Singh I (Battle of Dharmat 1658, court scholars, *Bhasha Bhushan*), Durgadas Rathore and the 30-Year Rathore-Mughal War for Maharaja Ajit Singh.
  * Bikaner: Rao Bika (1465/1488 CE Bikaner foundation), Rao Jaitasi (Battle of Rati-Ghati against Kamran), Rai Singh ("Karna of Rajputana", Junagarh fort, Prithviraj Rathore and *Veli Krishan Rukmani Ri*), Anup Singh (Anup Sanskrit Library).
* **Chapter 05: The Kachhwahas of Amber-Jaipur & Allied Dynasties**
  * Kachhwahas of Amber: Dulherai (1137 CE), Bharmal (1562 Sambhar alliance with Akbar), Bhagwan Das, Man Singh I (Mughal mansabdar of 7000, Bengal/Bihar/Kabul campaigns, Shila Devi temple, Amer palaces).
  * Mirza Raja Jai Singh (served 3 Mughal emperors, Treaty of Purandar 1665 with Shivaji, Bihari Lal's *Bihari Satsai*).
  * Sawai Jai Singh II (1700–1743 CE): Foundation of Jaipur (1727 CE, Vidyadhar Bhattacharya), Jantar Mantar observatories (Jaipur, Delhi, Mathura, Ujjain, Varanasi), Ashvamedha Yajna, Hurda Conference (1734 CE).
  * Sawai Pratap Singh (Hawa Mahal 1799, Ustad Lal Chand, Gandharva Baisi), Sawai Ram Singh II (Pink City coloring, Maharaja College).
  * Allied Dynasties: Bhatis of Jaisalmer (Jaisal 1156 CE, Sonar Kila, 2.5 Sākas), Hadas of Bundi and Kota (Rao Deva, Surjan Hada, Shatrushal, Zalim Singh Jhala), Jats of Bharatpur (Badan Singh, Maharaja Suraj Mal - "Plato of the Jat Tribe", Lohagarh Fort).

### Part II: Colonial Impact, Resistance & Modern Awakening
* **Chapter 06: British Treaties of 1818 & The 1857 Revolution in Rajasthan**
  * Subordinate Alliance Treaties of 1818: Causes (Maratha-Pindari devastation, succession disputes), Lord Hastings & Charles Metcalfe, treaty provisions, states sequence (Karauli 1st, Sirohi last 1823), Political Agents.
  * 1857 Revolution: 6 British Cantonments (Naseerabad, Neemuch, Deoli, Beawar, Erinpura, Kherwara - note Beawar & Kherwara did not participate), Naseerabad outbreak (28 May 1857, 15th Bengal Native Infantry), Neemuch outbreak (3 June 1857, Mohammad Ali Beg), Erinpura & "Chalo Dilli, Maro Firangi" (21 August 1857).
  * Auwa's Resistance: Thakur Kushal Singh, Battle of Bithoda (8 Sept 1857), Battle of Chelawas / "Battle of Black and White" (18 Sept 1857, Monk Mason killed), Brigadier Holmes' attack on Auwa (Jan 1858), Sugali Mata idol.
  * Kotah Revolt: Mass rebellion without military cantonment, Jaidayal & Mehrab Khan, execution of Major Burton, captivity of Maharao Ram Singh II.
* **Chapter 07: Peasant & Agrarian Movements**
  * Bijolia Peasant Movement (1897–1941, Mewar): 84 types of taxes (Lāg-Bāg), Chanwari Tax (Rao Krishna Singh, 1903), Talwar Bandhai Tax (Rao Prithvi Singh, 1906); Phase 1 (1897–1915, Sadhu Sitaram Das, Nanji-Thakari Patel); Phase 2 (1916–1923, Vijay Singh Pathik / Bhoop Singh, Uparmal Panch Board 1917, *Pratap* newspaper by Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi); Phase 3 (1923–1941, Manikya Lal Verma, Jamnalal Bajaj, agreement with Sir T. Raghavachari).
  * Bengu Peasant Movement (1921–1925): Ramnarayan Chaudhary, Rupa Ji and Kripa Ji Dhakar martyrdom (Govindpura firing by Trench).
  * Bundi (Barad) Peasant Movement: Nanak Ji Bhil martyrdom (Dabi firing 1923, Jhanda Geet).
  * Nimuchana Massacre (Alwar, 14 May 1925): Wild boar problem and tax hike, Gandhi described as "Dyerism Double Distilled".
  * Shekhawati Peasant Movement: Panchpana landlords, Katrathal Women's Conference (1934, Kishori Devi, 10,000 peasant women).
* **Chapter 08: Tribal Awakening Movements**
  * Bhagat Movement: Govind Guru (Banjara family, Vedsa village), establishment of Samp Sabha (1883), 10 rules, Mangarh Dham Massacre (17 Nov 1913, Mewar Bhil Corps firing, 1500 Bhils martyred, "Jallianwala Bagh of Rajasthan").
  * Eki Movement (Matrikundiya, Rashmi, Chittor 1921): Motilal Tejawat ("Bavji"), 21-point demand charter ("Mewar Ki Pukar"), Neemra firing (1922).
  * Meena Movement: Criminal Tribes Act (1871) and Jairam Pesha Act (1930), daily police roll-call requirement, Meena Kshatriya Sabha (1933), Muni Magan Sagar's Meena Mahasabha (1944 Neem Ka Thana), abolition of Jairam Pesha (1952).
* **Chapter 09: Prajamandal Movements & Modern Awakening**
  * Foundation and chronological matrix: Marwar Prajamandal (1934, Jai Narayan Vyas, Bhanwarlal Saraf), Mewar Prajamandal (1938, Manikya Lal Verma, Balwant Singh Mehta), Jaipur Prajamandal (1931 Kapur Chand Patni / 1936 Jamnalal Bajaj), Bikaner Prajamandal (1936 Magharam Vaidya in Calcutta), Kota Prajamandal (1939 Nayanuram Sharma), Sirohi (Gokulbhai Bhatt).
  * Gentlemen's Agreement (1942, Jaipur): Mirza Ismail and Hiralal Shastri, non-participation in Quit India Movement; dissent and Azad Morcha (Baba Harish Chandra).
  * Women's Role: Anjana Devi Chaudhary, Ratna Shastri, Narayani Devi Verma, Kalibai (Rastapal sacrifice 1947), Nagendra Bala.
  * Role of Press: *Tyagbhoomi*, *Aagiwan*, *Tarun Rajasthan*, *Naveen Rajasthan*, *Rajasthan Kesari*.
* **Chapter 10: The Unification of Rajasthan (1948–1956)**
  * The 7 Stages Complete Matrix:
    1. Matsya Union (18 March 1948): Alwar, Bharatpur, Dholpur, Karauli + Neemrana; Capital: Alwar; Rajpramukh: Udaybhan Singh (Dholpur); PM: Shobharam Kumawat; Named by K.M. Munshi.
    2. Former Rajasthan / Rajasthan Union (25 March 1948): 9 states (Banswara, Bundi, Dungarpur, Jhalawar, Kota, Pratapgarh, Shahpura, Tonk, Kishangarh) + Kushalgarh; Capital: Kota; Rajpramukh: Bhim Singh (Kota); PM: Gokul Lal Asawa.
    3. United Rajasthan (18 April 1948): Rajasthan Union + Udaipur (Mewar); Capital: Udaipur; Rajpramukh: Bhupal Singh; PM: Manikya Lal Verma; Inaugurated by Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru.
    4. Greater Rajasthan (30 March 1948 - Rajasthan Day): United Rajasthan + Jaipur, Jodhpur, Bikaner, Jaisalmer; Capital: Jaipur; Maharajpramukh: Bhupal Singh; Rajpramukh: Man Singh II; PM: Hiralal Shastri; Patel Committee (P. Satyanarayan Rao Committee) recommendations on high court, capital, education.
    5. United Greater Rajasthan (15 May 1949): Greater Rajasthan + Matsya Union; Dr. Shankar Rao Deo Committee recommendations.
    6. Rajasthan Union (26 January 1950): Inclusion of Sirohi (excluding Delwara & Abu); Name officially became "Rajasthan".
    7. Reorganized Rajasthan (1 November 1956): Fazal Ali State Reorganization Commission; Inclusion of Abu-Delwara, Ajmer-Merwara, Sunel Tappa of MP; Sironj sub-district transferred to MP; Abolition of Rajpramukh post, first Governor: Gurmukh Nihal Singh.

### Part III: Art, Architecture, Performing Arts & Cultural Heritage
* **Chapter 11: Fort Architecture of Rajasthan**
  * Classification (Kautilya & Shukraniti: Giri, Jal, Dhanvan, Vana, Sainya, Parikha, Paridha).
  * UNESCO 6 Hill Forts of Rajasthan (Inscribed 2013: Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Ranthambore, Gagron, Amber, Jaisalmer).
  * Architectural Anatomy & Bastions:
    * Chittorgarh: Largest living fort, 7 gates (Padan Pol to Ram Pol), Vijay Stambha, Kirti Stambha, 3 Sākas (1303, 1535, 1568).
    * Kumbhalgarh: 36 km wall (second longest in world), Katargarh (Eye of Mewar), birthplace of Maharana Pratap.
    * Gagron: Water fort (Jal Durg) at confluence of Ahu and Kali Sindh, Achaldas Khichi ri Vachanika, Jauhar 1423.
    * Mehrangarh: Mayuradhwaja Durg, Chintamani, Chamunda Mata temple, cannons (Kilkila, Shambhuban, Gajni Khan).
    * Junagarh: "Zameen Ka Geona", Suraj Pol, Anup Mahal, Karan Mahal.
* **Chapter 12: Palaces, Havelis, Baoris, Chhatris & Temples**
  * Havelis: Patwon ki Haveli (5-storey stone carving), Salim Singh ki Haveli (Jahaz Mahal), Nathmal ki Haveli (architect brothers Hathi and Lalu); Shekhawati Havelis (fresco wall paintings, gold leaf work).
  * Baoris (Stepwells): Chand Baori (Abhaneri, 3,500 steps, Harshat Mata temple), Raniji ki Baori (Bundi, "City of Stepwells"), Neemrana Baori.
  * Chhatris (Cenotaphs): 84-Pillared Chhatri (Bundi), Moosi Maharani Chhatri (Alwar), Bada Bagh (Jaisalmer), Gaitore (Jaipur kings), Jaswant Thada ("Taj Mahal of Marwar").
  * Temple Architecture: Gurjara-Pratihara / Maha-Maru style (Osian temples: Sachiya Mata, Surya temple; Kiradu temples: "Khajuraho of Rajasthan"; Harshat Mata Abhaneri); Jain Temples (Dilwara 5 temples: Vimal Vasahi, Luna Vasahi; Ranakpur: 1444 pillars, Dharanashah, architect Depaka).
* **Chapter 13: Schools of Rajasthani Painting**
  * Classification (Anand Coomaraswamy's *Rajput Painting* 1916).
  * Mewar School: Udaipur sub-style (Sahibdin, Manohar, *Arsha Ramayana*), Nathdwara sub-style (Pichhwai painting on cloth, women artists: Kamala & Ilaichi), Chavand sub-style (Nasiruddin, *Dhak* ragamala).
  * Marwar School: Jodhpur style (yellow/red palette, spiral clouds), Kishangarh style (Golden Age under Savant Singh / Nagari Das, master painter Nihal Chand, *Bani Thani* - "Indian Mona Lisa"), Bikaner style (Usta and Mathewarn art, Muslim artists, signatures on paintings).
  * Dhundhar School: Amer style, Jaipur style (Suratkhana, large portraits, Sahibram, Mohammad Shah), Alwar style (elephant tusk paintings, miniature courtesan portraits), Shekhawati fresco paintings.
  * Hadoti School: Bundi style (bird and animal representation, lush vegetation, *Chitrashala* by Ummed Singh), Kota style (hunting scenes with women hunting, Vallabh sect influence).
* **Chapter 14: Folk Performing Arts**
  * Folk Dances:
    * Professional & Commercial: Terah Taali (Kamad sect women for Ramdevji, 13 cymbals/manjiras, Mangi Bai), Bhawai (balance brass pots on head, Dayaram, Rup Singh), Kalbelia (UNESCO 2010, Gulabo Bai, Pungi instrument), Chari dance (Gujjar women, brass pot with burning cottonseeds, Falqu Bai).
    * Tribal Dances: Bhil dances (Gair, Neja, Yudh, Dwichakri, Ghumra); Garasia dances (Valar - without instrument, Lur, Mandal, Moria, Walo); Sahariya dances (Shikari, Chhail); Kanjod dances (Chakri, Dhakad).
    * Regional: Ghoomar (State dance, "Soul of Rajasthani dance"), Agni dance (Jasnathi sect at Katariyasar, dance on burning embers shouting "Fateh-Fateh"), Dhol dance (Jalore, Thākna style, sponsored by Jai Narayan Vyas), Bam dance (Bharatpur-Alwar, big drum Bam), Kacchi Ghodi (Shekhawati wooden horse dance).
  * Folk Theatre (Khyal): Turra-Kalangi (Tukangir & Shah Ali, Shiva-Shakti dialogue, non-scripted, Chanderi origin), Kuchamani Khyal (Lachhiram), Shekhawati Khyal (Nanu Ram, Duliyarana), Tamasha (Jaipur, Banshidhar Bhatt, Phoolji Bhatt), Rammat (Bikaner-Jaisalmer, Patta culture, Maharaj Tej Kavi's *Swatantra Bavani* gifted to Gandhi), Gavari / Rai (Bhil mythological drama of Shiva & Bhasmasur, 40 days in Bhadrapada).
  * Musical Instruments:
    * Tata (Stringed): Sarangi (King of instruments), Rawanhattha (coconut shell, played during Pabuji ki Phad), Kamayacha (Manganiyars), Jantar (played with Devnarayanji ki Phad), Tandura / Ektara.
    * Sushira (Wind): Algoza (State instrument, twin flutes), Pungi, Shehnai, Bankiya, Bhungal, Mashak.
    * Avanaddha (Membranophone): Mridang / Pakhawaj, Dhol, Chang, Khanjari, Daph, Damru, Mandal.
    * Ghana (Idiophone): Manjira, Khartal (Siddique Khan Manganiyar), Thali, Chimta, Jhalar.
* **Chapter 15: Folk Deities, Saints & Religious Movements**
  * The Panchpir:
    1. Pabu Ji: Born Kolumand (Phalodi), camel protector (brought camels/sandhis to Marwar), Rebaris worship him, Pabuji ki Phad (longest phad, Rawanhattha, Nayak Bhopas), Pabuji ra Pavade (Mathe instrument), died fighting Jind Rao Khichi for Deval Charni's cows.
    2. Ramdev Ji: Born Runducha/Pokhran, Tanwar Rajput, Runicha fair (Bhadrapada Shukla 2 to 11), Samadhi at Ramdevra, founded Kamadiya sect, authored *Chaubis Vaniya*, Terah Taali dance, communal harmony, white horse (Leela).
    3. Goga Ji: Born Dadrewa (Churu), snake protector deity, fought Mahmud of Ghazni ("Jahir Peer"), Top: Dhurmedi (Nohar, Hanumangarh, built like mosque by Firoz Shah Tughlaq with Bismillah inscribed), Shirshmedi (Dadrewa), Goga Navami.
    4. Harbhuji: Sankhla Rajput, vehicle is cart (Chhakada Gadi), blessed Rao Jodha with Katari to regain Mandore, temple at Bengti (Phalodi).
    5. Mehaji Manglia: Temple at Bapini (Jodhpur/Phalodi), horse: Kirad Kabra, Janmashtami fair.
  * Other Folk Deities: Tejaji (Born Karnal, Nagaur, cow protector, snakebite at Sursura, fair at Parbatsar), Devnarayan Ji (Gurjar deity, incarnation of Vishnu, Phad read with Jantar, neem leaves offering), Mallinath Ji (Tilwara, Barmer cattle fair), Veer Kallaji (Four-armed deity of 1568 Chittor battle).
  * Lok Deviyan: Karni Mata (Deshnok, white rats / Kabas), Shila Devi (Amer, brought from Jasore by Man Singh I), Jeen Mata (Rewasa, Sikar, longest song / Chirja), Tanot Mata (Soldiers' goddess, Jaisalmer), Sheetla Mata (Chaksu, donkey vehicle, cold food / Basoda).
  * Saints & Panths: Jambhoji (1451 Pipasar, founded Bishnoi sect with 29 rules at Samrathal Dhora, animal/tree protection), Jasnathji (Katariyasar, 36 rules, Gorakhnath disciple), Dadu Dayal ("Rajasthan's Kabir", Naraina/Jaipur headquarters, 52 pillars/disciples, Sundardas, Rajjabji), Mirabai (Rathore of Merta, married Bhojraj of Mewar, Bhakti poetry), Saint Mavji (Baneshwar Dham founder, Chopra texts).
* **Chapter 16: Fairs, Festivals, Customs, Ornaments, Costumes & Literature**
  * Fairs & Festivals: Pushkar Fair (Kartik Purnima), Gangaur (Chaitra Shukla 3, Shiva-Parvati worship, 18 days), Teej (Shravan Shukla 3 - Hariyali Teej in Jaipur; Kajli Teej in Bundi - Bhadrapada Krishna 3), Sheetla Ashtami (Chaitra Krishna 8), Baneshwar Fair (Tribal Kumbh, Som-Mahi-Anas confluence, Magh Purnima).
  * Customs & Sanskars: 16 Sanskars; Marriage customs (Bindoli, Samela, Toran, Barāt, Dapa, Natra); Mourning rituals (Mosar/Nukta, Badhar).
  * Traditional Ornaments (Head: Rakhdi, Borla, Sheeshfool; Neck: Timaniya, Champakali, Hansli, Thussi; Nose: Nath, Besri; Hand: Chuda, Hathphool, Damna; Foot: Pajeb, Jhanjhar, Kadla).
  * Rajasthani Literature & Dialects: Dingal (Western Rajasthani bardic poetry) vs. Pingal (Eastern Rajasthani mixed with Braj); Major works: *Prithviraj Raso* (Chand Bardai), *Vansh Bhaskar* (Suryamal Misran), *Kanhadade Prabandha* (Padmanabha), *Batān ri Phulwari* (Vijaydan Detha), *Dharti Dhoran Ri* & *Pāthal aur Peethal* (Kanhaiyalal Sethia); 5 Major Dialects: Marwari, Dhundhari, Haroti, Mewati, Malvi.
* **Chapter 17: Traditional Handicrafts & GI Tags of Rajasthan**
  * Crafts: Thewa Art (Gold foil on colored glass, Pratapgarh, Soni family, Padma Shri awards), Blue Pottery (Jaipur, glazed pottery introduced from Turko-Persia by Man Singh I / patronized by Ram Singh II, Kripal Singh Shekhawat), Usta Art (Camel hide gold embossing, Bikaner, Hisamuddin Usta), Tarkashi (Silver wire inlay, Nathdwara).
  * Textiles & Printing: Sanganeri Print (Natural dyes on white base), Bagru Print (Dabu resist print, red & black), Akola Print (Dabu print of Chittor), Bandhej / Tie & Dye (Jaipur, Jodhpur), Lehariya and Mothada, Kota Doria (Masuria weaving).
  * All GI Tags of Rajasthan (Pokhran Pottery, Blue Pottery, Sanganeri, Bagru, Kota Doria, Thewa, Kathputli, Bikaner Usta, Kashidakari, Nathdwara Pichhwai).

### Part IV: Morphotectonic Divisions, Drainage & Environment
* **Chapter 18: Morphotectonic Divisions of Rajasthan**
  * 4 Master Divisions:
    1. Western Sandy Plain (61.11% area, 40% population, arid & semi-arid): Great Desert (Marusthali), Sand dune typology (Barkhan / crescent dunes, Seif, Longitudinal, Transverse), Reg, Hamada, Semi-Arid Transitional Plain (Rajasthan Bagar): Luni Basin (Godwar), Shekhawati Internal Drainage, Nagauri Upland (Kobar Patti / Fluorosis belt), Ghaggar Plain.
    2. Aravalli Mountain Region (9% area, 10% population, oldest fold mountains, pre-Cambrian): North Aravalli (Raghunathgarh 1055m, Babai, Bairat), Central Aravalli (Taragarh, Goramji 934m, Moramji), South Aravalli (Abu block, Guru Shikhar 1722m, Ser 1597m, Dilwara 1442m, Jarga 1431m, Achalgarh 1380m, Kumbhalgarh 1224m); Major Passes (Ghats): Desuri, Hathi, Someshwar, Jhilwa.
    3. Eastern Plains (23% area, 39% population, fertile alluvium): Banas Basin (Mewar Plain & Malpura-Karauli Plain), Chhappan Basin / Mahi Plain (56 villages/streams between Banswara-Pratapgarh), Chambal Basin (Badland topography / Bīhad / Ravines).
    4. South-Eastern Hadoti Plateau (6.89% area, 11% population, Deccan lava & Vindhyan scarpland): Vindhyan Scarpland (Sandstone), Deccan Lava Plateau (Black regur soil), Shahbad Upland (horseshoe hill), Mukundara Hills.
* **Chapter 19: Drainage Systems of Rajasthan**
  * Hydrographic Divide: Aravalli Range acting as the Great Indian Water Divide (50 cm isohyet).
  * Three Drainage Classifications:
    1. Arabian Sea Drainage (17%): Luni River (Origin: Nag Pahar Ajmer, sweet water till Balotra, tributaries: Sukri, Bandi, Jawai, Guhiya, Lilri, Jojari - only right-bank tributary not from Aravalli), Mahi River (Origin: Amjhoru MP, crosses Tropic of Cancer twice, "Ganga of Kanthal", tributaries: Som, Jakham, Anas), Sabarmati, West Banas.
    2. Bay of Bengal Drainage (23%): Chambal River (Origin: Janapav MP, only perennial river, waterfalls: Chulia 18m, dams: Gandhi Sagar MP, Rana Pratap Sagar Chittorgarh, Jawahar Sagar, Kota Barrage), Banas River ("Varnasha" / Hope of Forest, Origin: Khamnor hills Rajsamand, confluence at Rameswaram Ghat Sawai Madhopur, tributaries: Berach, Bedach, Kothari, Khari, Morel), Gambhir, Banganga (Arjun ki Ganga).
    3. Inland Drainage System (60% - largest): Ghaggar River ("Saraswati / Dead River", Origin: Shivalik HP, enters Tibbi Hanumangarh, flood reaches Fort Abbas Pakistan), Kantli River (Origin: Khandela Sikar, inland flow in Torawati), Sabi (Origin: Sewar hills, enters Haryana), Ruparel (Origin: Udainath hills Alwar), Mendha, Rupangarh.
  * Lakes: Salt Lakes (Sambhar - 8.7% of India's salt, Didwana, Pachpadra - 98% NaCl, Lunkaransar); Freshwater Lakes (Jaisamand / Dhebar Lake - largest artificial lake, 7 islands: Babaka Bhagra, Pyari; Rajsamand - Raj Prashasti in 25 marble slabs by Ranchhod Bhatt; Pichhola - Jag Niwas & Jag Mandir, Natni ka Chabutra; Fateh Sagar; Nakki Lake - highest lake, toad rock, nun rock).
* **Chapter 20: Water Infrastructure & Major Canals**
  * Indira Gandhi Canal Project (IGNP): Origin: Harike Barrage (confluence of Satluj & Beas), Feeder canal (204 km: 170 km in Punjab/Haryana, 34 km in Rajasthan), Main canal (445 km till Mohangarh Jaisalmer, tail at Ghadra Road Barmer); 7 Lift Canals (Bikaner-Lunkaransar/Kanwarsain, Gandheli-Sahawa, Gajner, Kolayat, Phalodi, Pokhran, Jodhpur) and 9 Branches; Benefits and problems (waterlogging, Sem problem, salinity).
  * Eastern Rajasthan Canal Project (ERCP / Modified PKC Link): Interlinking Parbati, Kalisindh, and Chambal to address drinking/irrigation water across 21 eastern districts.
  * Narmada Canal Project: Origin: Sardar Sarovar Dam Gujarat, enters Silu (Sanchore/Jalore), total 458 km, first project in India where **drip/sprinkler irrigation is mandatory**.
  * Bisalpur Dam: On Banas river in Tonk, lifeline drinking water for Jaipur, Ajmer, Tonk.
  * Traditional Water Harvesting Systems: Tanka, Khadin (Paliwal Brahmins of Jaisalmer), Baori, Jhalra, Nadi, Kui/Beri, Tobha, Paar system.
* **Chapter 21: Climate, Agro-Climatic Zones & Soils**
  * Climate Attributes: Extreme continental climate, erratic rainfall (10 cm in Jaisalmer to 100 cm in Jhalawar - wettest district; Mount Abu - wettest place 150 cm); Isohyets: 25 cm separates Marusthali from Bagar, 50 cm divides Western arid from Eastern humid.
  * Climatological Classifications:
    * Koeppen: BWhw (Arid hot desert), BShw (Semi-arid steppe), Cwg (Sub-humid monsoon with dry winter), Aw (Tropical wet & dry - Hadoti, Banswara).
    * Thornthwaite (CA'w, DA'w, DB'w, EA'd) and Trewartha.
  * The 10 Agro-Climatic Zones of Rajasthan: I-A (Arid Western), I-B (Irrigated North-Western Plain), I-C (Hyper Arid Partially Irrigated), II-A (Internal Drainage Dry Zone), II-B (Transitional Plain of Luni Basin), III-A (Semi-Arid Eastern Plain), III-B (Flood Prone Eastern Plain), IV-A (Sub-Humid Southern Plain & Aravalli Hills), IV-B (Humid Southern Plain), V (Humid South-Eastern Plain).
  * Soils: ICAR classification (Desert soils, Red-yellow, Black/Regur, Alluvial, Brown); Modern USDA Taxonomy: Aridisols (Western arid), Entisols (All across desert), Alfisols (Eastern plains), Inceptisols (Aravalli & sub-humid), Vertisols (Black clayey Hadoti plateau).
* **Chapter 22: Forest Cover, Wildlife & Biodiversity Conservation**
  * Forest Profile (ISFR 2023 Analysis): Total Recorded Forest Area (RFA: 32,863 sq km ~ 9.60% of state); Legal Classification: Reserved Forests (37%), Protected Forests (56%), Unclassed Forests (7%); Actual Forest Cover (16,654 sq km ~ 4.87%); Top forest districts (Udaipur, Alwar, Pratapgarh); Lowest (Churu, Hanumangarh).
  * State Symbols: State Tree: Khejri (*Prosopis cineraria*, Kalpvriksha, 1983); State Flower: Rohida (*Tecomella undulata*, 1983); State Animal (Wild): Chinkara (*Gazella bennettii*, 1981); State Animal (Domestic): Camel (2014); State Bird: Godawan / Great Indian Bustard (*Ardeotis nigriceps*, 1981, Critically Endangered).
  * Protected Area Network:
    * National Parks (3): Ranthambore (1980), Keoladeo Ghana (1981, UNESCO World Heritage, Ramsar Site), Mukundara Hills (2012).
    * Tiger Reserves (5): Ranthambore (1973), Sariska (1978), Mukundara Hills (2013), Ramgarh Vishdhari (Bundi, 2022 - 52nd in India), Dholpur-Karauli (2023 - 54th in India).
    * Ramsar Wetland Sites (2): Keoladeo Ghana (1981) and Sambhar Lake (1990).
    * Conservation Reserves: Bisalpur, Joda Beed, Gogelao, Beed Jhunjhunu, Mansa Mata, Rotu, Jawai Bandh (Leopards), Shahbad Jungle, Hamirgadh.
* **Chapter 23: Mineral Wealth, Hydrocarbons & Renewable Energy**
  * Mineral Profile ("Museum of Minerals"): Rajasthan produces 81 varieties (57 commercially exploited); Monopoly / Sole Producer in India: Lead & Zinc (Zawar, Rampura Agucha - world's richest zinc mine, Rajpura Dariba), Wollastonite (100%), Jasper (100%), Selenite (100%), Garnet (Tamra/Raktmani), Calcite, Gypsum (Jamsar Bikaner).
  * Non-Metallic & Building Stones: Makrana Marble (White calcitic), Yellow Sandstone (Jaisalmer), Red Sandstone (Dholpur/Karauli), Kota Stone, Granite (Jalore - Granite City).
  * Hydrocarbon Architecture: 4 Petroliferous Basins across 14 districts:
    1. Barmer-Sanchore Basin (Mangala - first commercial oil field 2004, Bhagyam, Aishwarya, Saraswati, Raageshwari).
    2. Jaisalmer Basin (Natural gas: Tanot, Dandewala, Gamnewala, Manhera Tibba).
    3. Bikaner-Nagaur Basin (Heavy oil: Poonam, Baghewala).
    4. Rajasthan Shelf / Vindhyan Basin (Kota, Jhalawar, Baran).
    * Pachpadra Oil Refinery (HPCL Rajasthan Refinery Ltd - HRRL: 74:26 JV, 9 MMTPA capacity, BS-VI petrochemical complex).
  * Renewable Energy Leadership:
    * Solar Energy: Rajasthan ranks #1 in India; Bhadla Solar Park (Phalodi, 2245 MW - world's largest contiguous solar park), Fatehgarh, Nokh; State Solar Policy targets.
    * Wind Energy: Jaisalmer ("Wind City"), Pratapgarh, Phalodi.

### Part V: Political & Administrative Architecture of Rajasthan
* **Chapter 24: Constitutional Executive & Legislature of Rajasthan**
  * Governor (Articles 153–161): Constitutional position, appointment, powers, discretionary powers (Art 163); Historical state controversies (dissolution of assembly, reservation of bills under Art 200); Key Governors (Gurmukh Nihal Singh - 1st & longest, Sampurnanand - President's Rule 1967, Raghukul Tilak, Margaret Alva, Kalyan Singh, Kalraj Mishra).
  * Chief Minister & Council of Ministers (Articles 163, 164, 167): Cabinet structure, maximum size (15% limit under 91st Amendment = 30 ministers in Rajasthan including CM; minimum 12 ministers); Role in policy framing.
  * Rajasthan Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha): Unicameral legislature (200 seats; SC reserved: 34, ST reserved: 25); Historical trajectory (1st Assembly 1952 with 160 seats, Speaker: Narottam Lal Joshi, Leader of Opposition: Jaswant Singh); Legislative procedure, committees (Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committees A & B, Public Undertakings).
* **Chapter 25: State Judicial Architecture**
  * Rajasthan High Court (Article 214–231): Established 29 August 1949 at Jodhpur by Maharaja Sawai Man Singh; First Chief Justice: Justice Kamal Kant Verma; Satyanarayan Rao Committee recommendations (1958) regarding Principal Bench at Jodhpur and permanent bench at Jaipur (restored 1977); Territorial jurisdiction, writ jurisdiction (Art 226).
  * Subordinate Judiciary: District and Sessions Courts, Civil Courts, Fast Track Courts, Family Courts, Lok Adalats, Gram Nyayalayas (Gram Nyayalayas Act 2008 - Bassi Jaipur first in state).
* **Chapter 26: Constitutional & Statutory Bodies**
  * Rajasthan Public Service Commission (RPSC): Constitutional mandate (Articles 315–323); Established 20 August 1949 (effective 22 Dec 1949) at Jaipur, shifted to Ajmer on Satyanarayan Rao Committee recommendations; First Chairman: Sir S.K. Ghosh; Composition (Chairman + up to 7 members); Tenure (6 years or 62 years); Removal process (Presidential order via Supreme Court inquiry under Art 317).
  * State Election Commission (Articles 243K & 243ZA): Single-member commission established July 1994; Conducts elections for Panchayati Raj Institutions and Urban Local Bodies; First State Election Commissioner: Amar Singh Rathore; Tenure & removal like High Court Judge.
  * Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission (SHRC): Constituted January 1999 (operational March 2000 under Protection of Human Rights Act 1993); Composition (Chairperson: former Chief Justice/Judge of High Court + 2 members); First Chairperson: Justice Kanta Bhatnagar; Investigating powers, recommendations non-binding.
  * Rajasthan State Information Commission (SIC): Constituted 18 April 2006 under RTI Act 2005; Composition (Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 ICs); First CIC: M.D. Kaurani.
  * Lokayukta and Up-Lokayukta: Established under Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act 1973 (effective 3 Feb 1973); First Lokayukta: Justice I.D. Dua; Jurisdiction and strict exclusions (Chief Minister, High Court Judges, RPSC Chairman/Members, Sarpanch/Panch, Assembly Speaker are excluded).
* **Chapter 27: State Administration & Revenue Hierarchy**
  * Government Secretariat (Sachivalaya): History, administrative head: Chief Secretary; Powers and role (Principal Advisor to CM, Head of Civil Services, Secretary to Cabinet); First Chief Secretary: K. Radhakrishnan; First Woman CS: Kushal Singh.
  * Directorate vs. Secretariat (Staff agency vs. Line agency relationship).
  * Divisional Commissioners: Link between state HQ and districts; Abolished in 1962 (Mohanlal Sukhadia) and revived in 1987 (Harideo Joshi).
  * District Administration: The District Collector (Multi-dimensional roles: District Magistrate, District Collector of Revenue, District Development Officer, Returning Officer, Disaster Head); Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO/SDM), Tehsildar, Naib Tehsildar, Revenue Inspector (Girdawar), Patwari (foundational land record official).
* **Chapter 28: Democratic Decentralization & Urban Governance**
  * Panchayati Raj in Rajasthan: Nagaur launch on 2 October 1959 by Pt. Nehru (Baghdari village); Balwant Rai Mehta 3-tier architecture; Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act 1994 (aligned with 73rd Amendment); 3-tier structure (Gram Panchayat - Sarpanch, Panchayat Samiti - Pradhan/BDO, Zila Parishad - Zila Pramukh/CEO); 50% reservation for women; PESA Act 1996 implementation in TSP districts (Banswara, Dungarpur, Pratapgarh, parts of Udaipur, Sirohi).
  * Urban Local Bodies (74th Amendment): Rajasthan Municipalities Act 2009; Municipal Corporations (Nagar Nigam - Mayor/Commissioner), Municipal Councils (Nagar Parishad), Municipal Boards (Nagar Palika).
* **Chapter 29: Administrative Reforms & Good Governance Initiatives**
  * Rajasthan Guaranteed Delivery of Public Services Act, 2011: Time-bound delivery of notified public services, statutory first and second appellate authority, daily monetary penalty for delay.
  * Rajasthan Right to Hearing Act, 2012: Mandatory right of citizens to be heard on public grievances within 15 days, Public Grievance Redressal Officers (PGRO) at Panchayat, Tehsil, and District levels.
  * Citizen Charters, Jan Soochna Portal (Section 4(2) RTI proactive disclosure), Rajasthan Sampark Portal (CM Helpline 181), Social Audit Directorate.
