## 1. Administrative structure: the four connected levels

Rajasthan's state administration converts political direction and law into departmental policy, district-level coordination, and public services. The structure is not a single rigid command chain: a parallel distinction exists between **administrative supervision**, **statutory revenue-court jurisdiction**, **police command**, and **election administration**.

```text
STATE GOVERNMENT — Jaipur
  Political executive: Chief Minister and Council of Ministers
  Administrative coordination: Chief Secretary and Secretariat departments
                         │
                         ▼
DIVISION — 7 divisions (current arrangement)
  Divisional Commissioner; regional coordination and supervision
                         │
                         ▼
DISTRICT — 41 districts (current arrangement)
  District Collector / District Magistrate / District Election Officer
  District-level departments and local-government institutions
                         │
                         ▼
SUB-DISTRICT AND VILLAGE
  Sub-division / SDO-SDM → tehsil / Tehsildar → revenue inspector
  → Patwar circle / Patwari → revenue village and individual field records
```

**Current facts to memorise:** the Government of Rajasthan lists **41 districts**. The RAJ MASTERS dashboard, consulted on 17 October 2026, lists **7 divisions, 328 sub-divisions, 426 tehsils, 290 sub-tehsils, 2,815 ILR circles, 11,336 Patwar circles and 51,840 villages**. The latter figures are live administrative counts and may change as boundaries or units are altered; do not treat them as permanent constitutional numbers. Sources: [Rajasthan State Portal](https://rajasthan.gov.in/) and [RAJ MASTERS](https://rajmasters.rajasthan.gov.in/).

## 2. State Secretariat and Directorate

### 2.1 Constitutional basis

- **Article 166(1):** State executive action is expressed in the name of the Governor.
- **Article 166(2):** Authentication of orders and instruments is carried out as prescribed by rules.
- **Article 166(3):** Rules govern transaction of state-government business and allocation of business among Ministers.

The Secretariat is the central policy and coordination machinery; directorates and field departments typically carry out operational and technical implementation. This is a useful **administrative-science distinction**, not an absolute rule that every Secretariat officer is a generalist or every directorate is purely technical.

### 2.2 Secretariat: common functions

The Secretariat supports Ministers and the Cabinet by preparing policy proposals, legislation and cabinet notes; coordinating between departments; preparing and monitoring budgets; answering legislative questions; overseeing personnel matters; and reviewing implementation. Departmental arrangements differ, and a particular function may be assigned by the Rules of Business or a government order.

### 2.3 Directorate / line department

Directorates, commissionerates and departmental field formations undertake execution, technical regulation, inspections, programme delivery and supervision of district or subordinate offices. The exact relationship between a Secretariat department and its directorate is department-specific.

### 2.4 Simplified file hierarchy

A useful study model is:

**ACS / Principal Secretary / Secretary → Special or Additional Secretary → Joint Secretary → Deputy Secretary → Assistant Secretary / Section Officer → supporting establishment.**

This is not a universal, compulsory ladder in every department. Designations and reporting lines depend on a department's sanctioned structure and allocation of business.

## 3. Chief Secretary (मुख्य सचिव)

### 3.1 Status and functions

The Chief Secretary is generally the senior-most coordinating civil servant in the State Government. The office is important because the Chief Secretary:

1. advises the Chief Minister on administrative coordination and implementation;
2. coordinates inter-departmental issues and monitors major government decisions;
3. supports Cabinet business, subject to the State's Rules of Business;
4. leads or coordinates state-level administrative response during major emergencies;
5. acts as an important channel between state departments and the Union Government; and
6. participates in senior personnel and administrative-reform processes as authorised by rules and government orders.

Avoid describing the Chief Secretary as the statutory disciplinary head of *all* IAS, IPS, IFS and state-service officers: cadre control and disciplinary powers are distributed among competent authorities, cadre rules and department heads.

### 3.2 Appointment and tenure

The Chief Secretary is a senior civil-service posting made by the State Government. The post is **not a constitutional office with a separate appointment article or a fixed constitutional term**. The Chief Minister has decisive practical influence over the choice, but “appointed by the Governor” and “holds office legally at the personal pleasure of the Chief Minister” are misleading descriptions of the service arrangement. Superannuation and any extension are governed by applicable service rules and approvals; an extension is not automatic and should not be memorised as a standard six-month entitlement.

### 3.3 Current Chief Secretary

The Government of Rajasthan's Chief Minister Office page records **V. Srinivas** as Chief Secretary, with joining recorded on **17 November 2025** (order date 16 November 2025). Treat this as a dated administrative fact, not a permanent item. Source: [Chief Minister Office — V. Srinivas service history](https://cmo.rajasthan.gov.in/pages/sm/department-page/372483/2584).

### 3.4 Historical facts: handle with care

Exam guides commonly associate K. Radhakrishnan with the early Chief Secretary office in Rajasthan, Bhagwat Singh Mehta with a long tenure, Kushal Singh with the first woman Chief Secretary, and Niranjan Arya with the first Dalit Chief Secretary. Exact dates and “longest-serving” superlatives should be included in a final exam fact sheet only after cross-checking an official personnel-history record. The original chapter's claim that B.S. Mehta served for “over seven years” from May 1958 to October 1964 is arithmetically inconsistent: that interval is about six years and five months.

The institutional history of the Chief Secretary in colonial India is often simplified in exam literature. Do not treat the statement “created in 1799 by Lord Wellesley; first Chief Secretary of India G.H. Barlow” as a safe, unqualified constitutional fact without a robust historical source.

## 4. Divisional administration (संभागीय प्रशासन)

### 4.1 Historical sequence

A useful conventional chronology for Rajasthan is:

- **1949:** five original divisions are commonly listed — Jaipur, Jodhpur, Bikaner, Udaipur and Kota.
- **1962:** the divisional commissioner system was abolished in the state through the relevant legal reorganisation.
- **31 January 1987:** the system was restored through amendment of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act; Ajmer became an additional division.
- **2005:** Bharatpur Division was created.
- **August 2023:** Sikar, Pali and Banswara divisions were created, bringing the count temporarily to ten.
- **December 2024:** the state reversed that expansion and the current count returned to **seven divisions**.

The current seven names are **Ajmer, Bharatpur, Bikaner, Jaipur, Jodhpur, Kota and Udaipur**. The 2023 divisions of Sikar, Pali and Banswara should therefore not be stated as current divisions. The districts named Sikar, Pali and Banswara remain districts; it was their division status that changed. Current count: [RAJ MASTERS](https://rajmasters.rajasthan.gov.in/); seven-division confirmation and districts: [RISL/e-Mitra RFP](https://risl.rajasthan.gov.in/tenders/pdf/RFPEmitraLSP2025BeforePrebid20250807250548..pdf).

### 4.2 Legal basis and functions

The Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956 provides that the State Government may create, abolish or alter divisions, districts, subdivisions, tehsils and sub-tehsils by Gazette notification. Section 17 provides for appointment of a Commissioner in each division by the State Government. The Act also sets out the revenue-administration subordination structure. Source: [Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956](https://landrevenue.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/landrevenue/revenuedepartment/Rules/THE%20RAJASTHAN%20LAND%20REVENUE%20ACT%2C%201956.pdf).

Divisional Commissioners coordinate and inspect district administration, review programme implementation, and exercise powers conferred by applicable statutes and government orders. Their administrative role should be distinguished from the **Revenue Appellate Authority (RAA)**, a separate statutory office established under Section 20-A of the Land Revenue Act. The RAA hears such appeals, revisions and references as the law assigns to it; it is not simply an assistant or subordinate “wing” of the Commissioner.

## 5. District administration and the Collector

### 5.1 The office

The Collector's office developed from the colonial land-revenue administration, conventionally associated with Warren Hastings' reforms of 1772. Its functions have expanded, and modern district administration combines several legally distinct roles. The Collector's precise powers derive from applicable legislation, rules, notifications and specific appointments.

### 5.2 Collector as revenue officer

Under the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, the State Government appoints a Collector for each district; the Collector is also the district Land Records Officer under that Act. Functions may include:

- collection of land revenue and other dues recoverable under law;
- supervision of prescribed land-record and settlement processes;
- administration of specified government lands;
- coordination of revenue officers; and
- land acquisition or compensation functions when assigned under the applicable acquisition law.

The Collector is not automatically the original court for every tenancy or land dispute. The forum and appellate route depend on the relevant Act, the nature of the claim, the Schedule and the order being challenged.

### 5.3 Collector as District Magistrate

Under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), Executive Magistrates exercise specified preventive and public-order powers. **Section 163 BNSS** provides for an order in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger; it is the successor provision commonly compared with Section 144 of the former Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. Calling every Section 163 order a “curfew” is imprecise.

Distinctions to retain:

- **Sections 126–129 BNSS** concern specified security proceedings for keeping the peace or good behaviour.
- The BNSS provisions on dispersal of unlawful assemblies occur separately (including Sections 148 onward), not in Sections 126–129.
- Preventive detention requires a separate statutory basis and cannot be presented as a general, inherent power of every District Magistrate.
- The District Magistrate coordinates district public order; routine police command lies within the police hierarchy, headed districtally by the Superintendent of Police and ultimately controlled under the State's police/legal framework.
- Jail management and parole decisions are governed by prison statutes, rules and competent-authority arrangements; they are not blanket, automatic powers attached to every Collector posting.

The legal text is available through [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) and the Rajasthan Gazette/statutory framework. For exam answers, quote the section and the power precisely rather than using the colloquial word “curfew” as the legal definition.

### 5.4 Elections: distinguish the roles

A Collector may be appointed **District Election Officer (DEO)** for the district under the Representation of the People Act framework. A **Returning Officer (RO)** is appointed for a particular election/constituency by the competent election authority. Therefore the Collector is not automatically the Returning Officer for all Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies in the district. Similarly, an SDO may be designated Electoral Registration Officer (ERO) or RO only when appointed to that role.

Local-body elections are under the State Election Commission, not the Election Commission of India. This division of responsibility is a frequent exam distinction.

### 5.5 Development and disaster response

The Collector coordinates district-level schemes and interdepartmental delivery, but is not universally the formal **District Development Officer (DDO)**. In the Panchayati Raj structure, the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of the Zila Parishad has important executive and development functions. The Collector chairs the District Disaster Management Authority under the Disaster Management Act, 2005, subject to the Act and applicable arrangements.

## 6. Sub-divisional administration: SDO / SDM

The Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO) often performs the role of Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) where appointed/designated under the applicable laws. Officers may be drawn from the Rajasthan Administrative Service or be IAS officers on field assignments. The post links district coordination with tehsil administration.

Common functions may include:

- revenue administration and adjudication within the jurisdiction specifically conferred by revenue law;
- exercise of Executive Magistrate powers where appointed and empowered under the BNSS;
- coordination and inspection of public services and development programmes; and
- election duties when designated by the relevant election authority.

Do not state that every agricultural-tenancy suit originates in the SDO's court. The Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955 and its schedules allocate jurisdiction by type of suit/application. Likewise, not every SDO is automatically the ERO or RO for each Assembly constituency.

## 7. Revenue hierarchy and statutory appeal routes

### 7.1 A useful schematic (not a universal appeal ladder)

```text
State Government — administrative control over non-judicial revenue matters
                     │
Board of Revenue, Ajmer — apex revenue court for matters provided by law
                     │
Commissioner / Revenue Appellate Authority / Collector / SDO / Tehsildar
(as separate statutory roles; appeal routes depend on the order and enactment)
                     │
Patwari / Girdawar (ILR) / Tehsil land-record machinery
```

The Rajasthan Land Revenue Act differentiates **administrative subordination** from **judicial control**. Section 23 vests control of non-judicial revenue matters (other than settlement matters) in the State Government and control of judicial matters and settlement matters in the Board, subject to the Act. Section 24 sets out the subordination of revenue officers, including divisional, district and sub-divisional relationships. Section 20-A establishes Revenue Appellate Authorities. Sections 75–76 and the relevant provisions of the Rajasthan Tenancy Act provide particular appeal routes. Source: [Rajasthan Land Revenue Act — official text](https://landrevenue.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/landrevenue/revenuedepartment/Rules/THE%20RAJASTHAN%20LAND%20REVENUE%20ACT%2C%201956.pdf) and [Board of Revenue — important Acts and sections](https://landrevenue.rajasthan.gov.in/content/landrevenuenew/en/board-of-revenue-for-raj-dep/documents/acts/acts.html).

### 7.2 Board of Revenue, Ajmer

- **Headquarters:** Ajmer.
- **Legal foundation:** Sections 4–10 of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956.
- **Composition:** a Chairman and **not fewer than three nor more than twenty other members** under Section 4(2); the maximum is not twenty members including the Chairman.
- **Appointment framework:** qualifications, selection method and service conditions are prescribed by the State Government; appointments are notified as provided by the Act. Members hold office during the pleasure of the Governor under Section 5.
- **Jurisdiction:** Section 8 calls it the highest revenue court of appeal, revision and reference in Rajasthan, subject to the Rajasthan Tenancy Act and other applicable law. A dispute about whether jurisdiction belongs to a civil or revenue court remains subject to the High Court's final authority as provided by the Act.
- **Benches:** the Act permits the Chairman or a member to sit singly, and permits a Bench of two or more members. A special appeal from a single-member decision is conditional under the statutory text; do not simplify it to an unconditional appeal in every case.

The Board's legal role must not be confused with the preparation of the Agricultural Census or Livestock Census. Those are separate sectoral statistical programmes; the source chapter's claim that the Board organises both censuses as a core statutory responsibility is not supported by the Act.

### 7.3 Tehsildar, Naib-Tehsildar and revenue inspectors

The Tehsildar is a key tehsil-level revenue officer responsible for collecting lawful dues, supervising revenue records and performing other duties specified in the Land Revenue Act and rules. A Naib-Tehsildar performs assigned functions and may exercise powers conferred by law or delegated through notification. A Tehsildar should not automatically be described as the Sub-Registrar or as an Executive Magistrate unless the relevant legal appointment/designation applies.

A Revenue Inspector / Girdawar / Kanungo supervises and checks Patwari work and land-record processes under the applicable rules. The number of Patwar circles covered by one inspector is an administrative deployment figure, not a permanent constitutional rule. Attestation of mutations and final checks must be attributed to the authority empowered by the Land Records Rules—not simply to the Girdawar in every case.

### 7.4 Patwari and basic land records

The Patwari maintains field-level land and crop records under supervision. These records matter greatly, but do not make every entry conclusive evidence of title.

- **Jamabandi / record of rights:** records relevant rights, holdings, rent/revenue and prescribed particulars. The Rajasthan Land Revenue (Land Records) Rules, 1957 describe the preparation of a new Jamabandi on a **four-year/quadrennial cycle** in the prescribed programme, with villages scheduled over time. This contradicts the original chapter's blanket five-year rule. Current digital processes and local orders should be checked before quoting a particular village's update cycle.
- **Khasra Girdawari:** field inspection and crop record for the agricultural seasons, including crop, area, irrigation status and relevant damage/possession details. The official rules schedule Kharif and Rabi work; do not universalise the original's exact “1 October / 1 February / 1 May” trio without a current departmental instruction.
- **Shajra / cadastral map:** depicts village/field parcels and mapped features used in survey and land-record work.
- **Mutation / Namantaran:** records a change in revenue entries after events such as inheritance or transfer. A mutation is a fiscal/revenue entry; by itself it does not create or extinguish title. The underlying transaction, succession or adjudication remains legally relevant.
- **Apna Khata / e-Dharti:** Rajasthan's digital land-record services enable users to access prescribed online land-record information; a web printout should not be treated as a substitute for every certified record or legal title document in every proceeding.

Primary source: [Rajasthan Land Revenue (Land Records) Rules, 1957](https://landrevenue.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/landrevenue/revenuedepartment/Rules/Rajasthan%20Land%20Revenue%20(Land%20Records)%20Rules%201957.pdf).

## 8. Secretariat versus Directorate: comparison

| Dimension | Secretariat | Directorate / field department |
|---|---|---|
| Main orientation | Policy support, coordination and government business | Implementation, technical administration and service delivery |
| Political relationship | Direct support to Ministers and Cabinet through allocated business | Implements policy through departmental chain; accountable under relevant Secretariat department |
| Common work | Cabinet notes, legislation, budget, inter-departmental decisions, Assembly business | Technical instructions, inspections, programme delivery, operational reporting |
| Staff profile | Often general administration and policy officers plus specialist staff | Often subject-matter specialists, engineers, inspectors and field cadres |
| Location | Primarily Jaipur Secretariat | Headquarters and field units may be located throughout the State |

This distinction is an analytical model; actual structures vary by department and by allocation-of-business rules.

## 9. High-yield corrections and exam traps

1. **Current administrative map:** Rajasthan has **41 districts and 7 divisions**, not 50 districts and 10 divisions. The Sikar, Pali and Banswara divisions created in 2023 were cancelled in the December 2024 reorganisation; the districts of those names remain.
2. **District counts can change by notification:** use a dated official source for any new district, tehsil or sub-division statistic.
3. **State Government creates divisions and appoints Commissioners:** Section 16 allows the State Government to create, abolish or alter territorial units by Gazette notification; Section 17 concerns Commissioners.
4. **Chief Secretary is a senior administrative posting, not a separately enumerated constitutional office.** Do not write that the Governor appoints the CS in the same constitutional sense as a Governor or High Court judge.
5. **Chief Secretary's current name is time-sensitive:** official CMO history records V. Srinivas joining in November 2025; verify again before the exam.
6. **Collector is not automatically every RO:** District Election Officer, Returning Officer and Electoral Registration Officer are distinct appointments under election law.
7. **Collector is not a universal DDO:** rural development responsibilities also belong to the Zila Parishad CEO and other departmental authorities.
8. **Section 163 BNSS:** urgent orders to prevent danger/nuisance; popularly called Section 144-type prohibitory orders, but “curfew” is not the statutory name.
9. **BNSS sections:** preventive-security proceedings under Sections 126–129 are not the same as dispersal of unlawful assemblies (Sections 148 onward).
10. **No blanket detention power:** preventive detention needs a specific statute and competent statutory authority.
11. **No blanket control of prison/parole:** prison and parole powers follow their own statutes, rules and delegation.
12. **RAA is a statutory office:** not merely an assistant attached to the Divisional Commissioner.
13. **Revenue hierarchy is not one straight line for every lawsuit:** routes vary under the Land Revenue Act, Tenancy Act and other special laws.
14. **Board of Revenue composition:** Chairman plus 3–20 other members under Section 4(2), rather than twenty in total.
15. **Board of Revenue is the highest revenue court subject to statutory provisions:** Section 8 does not displace the High Court's role in matters within its jurisdiction.
16. **Section 23 vs Section 24:** statutory control of judicial revenue matters/settlement and administrative subordination are related but distinct.
17. **Jamabandi cycle:** rules describe quadrennial preparation, not a universal five-year cycle.
18. **Girdawari calendar:** don't treat 1 February and 1 May as universal current dates without a current order.
19. **Mutation does not create title:** it updates revenue entries; title depends on underlying law and rights.
20. **Patwari is important but not omnipotent:** entries can be corrected, challenged, and adjudicated through prescribed processes.
21. **Tehsildar ≠ automatically Sub-Registrar:** the latter role depends on designation under registration law.
22. **Tehsildar ≠ automatically Executive Magistrate:** BNSS powers require appointment/designation and statutory authority.
23. **Board of Revenue does not own all statistical operations:** Agricultural and Livestock Censuses are separate sectoral programmes.
24. **Historical superlatives need evidence:** “longest-serving Chief Secretary” and disputed dates should not be memorised from a single secondary source.

## 10. Practice questions and answer frameworks

### 2-mark questions

**Q1. What is the current number of districts and divisions in Rajasthan?**  
**Answer:** Rajasthan currently has **41 districts and 7 administrative divisions**. The three divisions created in 2023—Sikar, Pali and Banswara—were cancelled in the December 2024 reorganisation.

**Q2. Differentiate the Collector's role as District Magistrate and as revenue officer.**  
**Answer:** As District Magistrate, the Collector exercises specified public-order and Executive Magistrate functions under applicable law. As Collector, the officer performs revenue and land-record functions under the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act and related laws.

**Q3. Is the District Collector always the Returning Officer?**  
**Answer:** No. The Collector may be appointed District Election Officer; a Returning Officer is appointed for a specified constituency/election by the competent election authority.

**Q4. What is the difference between Jamabandi and Khasra Girdawari?**  
**Answer:** Jamabandi is a record-of-rights/landholding record prepared under the Land Records Rules; Girdawari is a crop/field inspection record. Neither should be treated as an automatic, conclusive certificate of title.

### 5-mark questions

**Q5. Explain why the revenue-administration hierarchy is not a single universal appeal ladder.**  
**Answer framework:** (i) The Land Revenue Act and Tenancy Act govern different matters; (ii) the competent original revenue court depends on the suit and statutory schedule; (iii) the Act assigns first and subsequent appeals to different authorities depending on the original order; (iv) the RAA is a distinct statutory office; (v) the Board of Revenue is the highest revenue appellate/revisional forum for matters assigned by law, subject to the High Court's jurisdiction.

**Q6. Explain the current division reorganisation in Rajasthan.**  
**Answer framework:** Rajasthan had five original divisions in the commonly cited 1949 arrangement, abolition in 1962, restoration in 1987 with Ajmer, addition of Bharatpur in 2005, and a temporary rise to ten divisions after 2023. The three new divisions were cancelled at the end of 2024, returning the current number to seven. The present names are Ajmer, Bharatpur, Bikaner, Jaipur, Jodhpur, Kota and Udaipur.

### 10-mark analytical framework

**Q7. “The District Collector remains a central coordinating authority, but modern district governance depends on role clarity and lawful delegation.” Discuss with reference to Rajasthan.**

1. **Introduction:** The Collector links state policy, statutory revenue administration, public-order coordination, elections and disaster response.
2. **Roles:** Distinguish Collector, District Magistrate, District Election Officer and DDMA Chairperson; acknowledge that separate statutes and appointments confer these roles.
3. **Inter-agency coordination:** Explain relations with SP, CEO Zila Parishad, district line departments and local governments without portraying one officer as the legal superior of every unit.
4. **Challenges:** Heavy scheme-monitoring load, urbanisation, land disputes, interdepartmental fragmentation, digital grievance monitoring and disaster response.
5. **Reforms:** Delegate routine matters; publish clear service standards; strengthen land-record audit and dispute resolution; use data systems without weakening field verification; improve coordination between elected institutions and administrative officers.
6. **Conclusion:** The Collector's role is best understood as a multi-statutory coordinating office—not as an unrestricted, all-purpose authority.
