## 1. Ideological Genesis, Evolution & Institutional Foundations

### 1.1 The Concept of "Praja Mandal" (People's Council)
The Prajamandal (प्रजामंडल — People's Association / Council) movement represented the revolutionary shift of Rajasthan's freedom struggle from localized economic-peasant protests into an organized, constitutional campaign for democratic governance:

```

                    │      THE PRAJAMANDAL REVOLUTIONARY DUAL MANDATE        │
                    └───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                                │

         │                                                                             │
┌────────┴─────────────────────────────┐                     ┌─────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
│  Responsible Governance (उत्तरदायी शासन) │                 │  Civil Liberties & Anti-Feudal Rights            │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤                     ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Demand for constitutional democracy│                     │ • Complete eradication of forced labor (Begar)   │
│   under the aegis of reigning princes│                       and arbitrary feudal cesses (*Lāg-Bāg*).         │
│   (*Nareshon ke Sanrakshan mein*).   │                     │ • Freedom of press, freedom of speech, right to  │
│ • Establishment of representative    │                       peaceful assembly, and basic human rights.       │
│   legislatures and elected ministries│                     │ • Integration of princely states with free India.│
└──────────────────────────────────────┘                     └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Precursor Nationalist Organizations
Prior to the formal launch of Prajamandals, several pioneering socio-political organizations laid the ideological groundwork across Rajputana:
1. **Sarvahitkarini Sabha (सर्वहितकारिणी सभा, 1907 CE)**:
   - Established at **Churu** by **Swami Gopal Das** and **Kanhaiyalal Dhoond**.
   - Known as the *"Congress of Bikaner"*; established **Kabir Pathshala** (for Dalit untouchable children) and **Putri Pathshala** (for girls' education).
2. **Marwar Seva Sangh (1920 CE)**: Founded at Jodhpur by **Jai Narayan Vyas**.
3. **Marwar Hitkarini Sabha (1921 / 1924 CE)**: Revived by Anand Raj Surana and Jai Narayan Vyas to fight state export of female cattle (*Gau-Niryat*) and weight-metric corruption.
4. **Rajasthan Seva Sangh (1919 CE)**: Founded at Wardha by Vijay Singh Pathik, Ramnarayan Chaudhary, and Haribhai Kinkar; shifted to Ajmer in 1920.

## 3. Deep-Dive Curricular Analysis: Major Prajamandals

### 3.1 The Jaipur Prajamandal & The Gentlemen's Agreement (1942)
- **Establishment (1931 & 1936–1938)**:
  - First established in 1931 by **Kapur Chand Patni**, making it the first Prajamandal in Rajasthan. Remained dormant until reorganized in 1936–1937 under the patronship of national leader **Seth Jamnalal Bajaj** and **Hiralal Shastri**.
  - **First Annual Session (8–9 May 1938)**: Held at Jaipur under the presidency of Jamnalal Bajaj; attended by **Kasturba Gandhi**.
- **The Historic Gentlemen's Agreement (सितंबर 1942)**:
  - During the Quit India Movement (August 1942), Jaipur Prime Minister **Sir Mirza Ismail** held secret negotiations with Jaipur Prajamandal President **Hiralal Shastri**.
  - **The Accord**:
    1. Jaipur Prajamandal pledged **NOT to launch Quit India satyagraha** against the Jaipur state government.
    2. In return, Jaipur state agreed **not to assist the British war effort** with state resources, permitted peaceful anti-British propaganda, and promised to grant responsible government incrementally.

### 3.2 The Marwar (Jodhpur) Prajamandal & Jai Narayan Vyas
- **Founding (1934 CE)**: Established in 1934 by **Jai Narayan Vyas**, **Bhanwarlal Saraf** (first President), and Abhaymal Jain.
- **Marwar Lok Parishad (1938 CE)**: Re-activated under **Ranchhod Das Gattani** to mobilize youth, peasants, and civil servants.
- **The Martyrdom of Balmukund Bissa (19 June 1942)**:
  - **Identity**: Known as the **"Jallianwala / Bhagat Singh of Rajasthan"**, born at Peelwa (Didwana); established Jawahar Khadi Bhandar in Jodhpur.
  - **The Fast**: Imprisoned during anti-fascist protests; initiated an indefinite hunger strike against unhygienic jail food and torture in Jodhpur Central Jail.
  - **Martyrdom**: Collapsed due to medical negligence and force-feeding pneumonia on **19 June 1942**. Over 1 lakh citizens marched in his funeral procession.
- **Women Leaders**: Smt. Mahimadevi Kinkar, Krishna Kumari, Rama Devi.

### 3.3 The Mewar Prajamandal & The 1945 Udaipur All-India Conference
- **Founding (24 April 1938 CE)**:
  - Founded at Udaipur by **Manikya Lal Verma** at the residence of **Balwant Singh Mehta** (*Sahitya Kuteer*).
  - First President: **Balwant Singh Mehta**; Vice-President: **Bhuralal Baya**; General Secretary: **Manikya Lal Verma**.
  - **State Ban & Devgarh Assault**: Mewar Prime Minister Dharmanarayan declared the organization unlawful within a week; Verma was expelled from Mewar. When Verma attempted to re-enter via Devgarh border, state police thrashed him unconscious.
- **First Annual Session (November 1941 CE)**:
  - Held at Udaipur, inaugurated by Congress President **Acharya J.B. Kripalani**; attended by **Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit**.
- **The Seventh All-India States' Peoples' Conference (31 Dec 1945 – 1 Jan 1946)**:
  - Held at **Salotia Ground, Udaipur**; the **first time in Indian history** that the national annual session of the AISPC was convened within a princely state!
  - Presided over by **Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru**; attended by Jammu & Kashmir leader **Sheikh Abdullah**.

### 3.4 The Bikaner Prajamandal & The Birbal Day Sacrifice
- **Founding in Calcutta (4 October 1936 CE)**:
  - Founded in **Calcutta** (at Machhua Bazar) by **Magharam Vaidya** and Laxmi Das Swami to evade Maharaja Ganga Singh’s secret police.
- **Bikaner Conspiracy Case (1932 CE)**:
  - During the Second Round Table Conference in London, activists distributed the explosive booklet **"Bikaner: A True Picture" (बीकानेर एक दिग्दर्शन)** exposing Ganga Singh’s autocracy.
  - Ganga Singh arrested Swami Gopal Das, Chandanmal Bahad, and Satyanarayan Saraf in the infamous *Bikaner Conspiracy Case*.
- **The Martyrdom of Birbal Singh Dhaliwal (1946 CE)**:
  - On **30 June 1946**, at Raisinghnagar (Sri Ganganagar), a peaceful Prajamandal procession led by **Birbal Singh Dhaliwal** was fired upon by state police.
  - Birbal took three bullets in his chest while protecting the tricolor flag, dying with the flag aloft on 1 July 1946.
  - On **17 July 1946**, the Bikaner Prajamandal observed **"Birbal Day" (बीरबल दिवस)** across the state.
  - The Indira Gandhi Canal project honored his sacrifice by naming the **Birbal Shakha** after him.

### 3.5 The Dungarpur Prajamandal & The Rastapal Martyrdom (1947)
- **Founding (1944 CE)**: Established by **Bhogilal Pandya (भोगीलाल पांड्या)** — revered as the **"Gandhi of Vagad" (वागड़ के गांधी)**, alongside Harideo Joshi and Gauri Shankar Upadhyaya.
- **The Rastapal Incident (19 June 1947 / रास्तापाल कांड)**:
  - The Dungarpur state police and district magistrate marched to **Rastapal village** to shut down a nationalist primary school operated by the Seva Sangh.
  - Headmaster **Nana Bhai Khant (नाना भाई खांट)** refused to hand over the keys and was beaten to death on the spot.
  - The police tied teacher **Sengabhai (सेंगाभाई)** to the back of their police truck with thick ropes and drove off, dragging him over jagged rocks.
  - **The Supreme Sacrifice of Kalibai**:
    - A 13-year-old Bhil schoolgirl, **Kalibai (कालीबाई)**, grazing cattle nearby, witnessed her teacher being dragged to death.
    - Armed only with her grassroots agricultural sickle (*Danti*), she bravely dashed through armed constables and slashed the rope, freeing Sengabhai.
    - The police opened fire, shooting Kalibai with multiple rifle rounds. She succumbed to her wounds on **21 June 1947**.
  - **Memorial**: A statue of Veer Balika Kalibai stands at the banks of **Gaib Sagar Lake (गैब सागर)** in Dungarpur. Rajasthan Government institutes the prestigious *Kalibai Bheel Medhavi Chhatra Scooty Yojana* in her honor.

### 3.6 Jaisalmer Prajamandal & The Fiery Ordeal of Sagar Mal Gopa
- **Founding (1945 CE)**: Established at **Jodhpur** by **Mithalal Vyas** because Maharawal Jawahar Singh banned all political organizations in Jaisalmer.
- **Sagar Mal Gopa (1900–1946 CE) — The Lion of the Thar**:
  - Exiled from Jaisalmer for authoring revolutionary nationalist pamphlets:
    1. **Jaisalmer Ka Gundaraj (जैसलमेर का गुंडाराज — The Mafia Rule of Jaisalmer)**
    2. **Azadi Ke Diwane (आजादी के दीवाने)**
    3. **Raghunath Singh Ka Muqadma (रघुनाथ सिंह का मुकदमा)**
  - Arrested treacherously on 25 May 1941 when visiting his ailing father under false safe-conduct assurances.
  - **The Jailhouse Murder (3–4 April 1946)**:
    - Subjected to horrific physical torture for five years in Jaisalmer Central Jail under Jailor **Guman Singh**.
    - On the night of 3 April 1946, Guman Singh poured kerosene over Gopa’s body and burned him alive inside his cell. Gopa succumbed on 4 April 1946.
    - Following national outrage, the state appointed the **Gopal Swaroop Pathak Commission** to whitewash the murder as suicide.

## 7. The 25 Deadliest Examination Traps (Forensic Autopsy)

1. **Trap 1: The First Prajamandal in Rajasthan**:
   - *Trap*: Selecting Mewar or Marwar.
   - *Fact*: **Jaipur Prajamandal (1931 CE)** founded by **Kapur Chand Patni** was the first.

2. **Trap 2: Prajamandals Founded Outside Rajasthan**:
   - *Trap*: Assuming all Prajamandals were established inside their respective states.
   - *Fact*: Four Prajamandals had external origins:
     1. **Bikaner Prajamandal (1936)**: Founded in **Calcutta** by Magharam Vaidya.
     2. **Bharatpur Prajamandal (1938)**: Founded in **Rewari (Haryana)** by Kishen Lal Joshi.
     3. **Sirohi Prajamandal (1934)**: Founded in **Bombay** by Vridhi Shankar Trivedi.
     4. **Jaisalmer Prajamandal (1945)**: Founded in **Jodhpur** by Mithalal Vyas.

3. **Trap 3: Parties to the Gentlemen's Agreement (1942)**:
   - *Trap*: Concluded between Jamnalal Bajaj and the Maharaja.
   - *Fact*: Signed between Jaipur Prime Minister **Sir Mirza Ismail** and Prajamandal President **Hiralal Shastri**.

4. **Trap 4: Founder of Azad Morcha**:
   - *Trap*: Hiralal Shastri.

### 8.2 Section B: 5-Mark Analytical Blueprints (50 Words)

#### Q8: Analyze the dual mandate and accomplishments of the Prajamandal movement in Rajasthan.
**Answer**:
The Prajamandals bridged local socio-economic issues with pan-Indian freedom goals:
- **Dual Mandate**: Agitated for the establishment of responsible government under princely auspices while demanding complete civil liberties (free press, speech, abolition of Begar).
- **Accomplishments**:
  - Mobilized women, youth, and peasantry into political consciousness, ending centuries of feudal inertia.
  - Successfully eroded monarchical absolutism, training a democratic political leadership that paved the way for Rajasthan's smooth integration into the Indian Union (1948–1956).

#### Q9: Detail the martyrdom of Sagar Mal Gopa and its impact on the democratic awakening in Jaisalmer.
**Answer**:
Sagar Mal Gopa (1900–1946 CE) was the vanguard of anti-autocratic resistance in Jaisalmer:
- **Intellectual Defiance**: Penned explosive treatises (*Jaisalmer Ka Gundaraj*) exposing royal atrocities; imprisoned treacherously in 1941.
- **Torture & Murder**: Endured five years of brutal interrogation; burned alive with kerosene in his cell by Jailor Guman Singh on 3–4 April 1946.
- **National Outrage**: Sparked pan-Indian condemnation, dismantled the moral legitimacy of Maharawal Jawahar Singh's administration, and energized the Jaisalmer Prajamandal.

#### Q10: Highlight the pivotal role played by women in Rajasthan's Prajamandal movements.
**Answer**:
Women transcended conservative feudal parda to become frontline satyagrahis:
- **Leadership & Arrests**: Anjana Devi Chaudhary was the first woman jailed in Rajasthan (1921); Narayani Devi Verma endured imprisonment with an infant, founding the Mahila Ashram (1944).
- **Mass Mobilization**: Kishori Devi mobilized 10,000 women at Katrathal (1934); Ratna Shastri nurtured female education at Banasthali Vidyapith.
- **Democratic Landmark**: Nagendra Bala carried the patriotic mantle, becoming India's first woman Zila Pramukh in 1960.

#### Q12: Trace the origin, clauses, and ideological rift caused by the Gentlemen's Agreement of 1942 in Jaipur state, and analyze how the crisis was resolved.
**Answer**:
The Gentlemen's Agreement of September 1942 highlights the complex tactical dilemmas faced by Prajamandals during the pan-Indian Quit India Movement:

1. **Origins & Context**:
   - Following Mahatma Gandhi's "Do or Die" call on 8 August 1942, British authorities arrested top Congress leaders.
   - In Jaipur, enlightened Prime Minister **Sir Mirza Ismail** sought to prevent British military occupation of the state, initiating secret negotiations with Prajamandal President **Hiralal Shastri**.

2. **Core Clauses of the Agreement**:
   - **Prajamandal Pledges**: Agreed **not to initiate civil disobedience or strikes** against Jaipur state, maintaining non-violent cooperation.
   - **State Pledges**: Agreed **not to supply war recruits or state funds to the British**, recognized the Prajamandal's right to peaceful anti-British meetings, and promised gradual responsible governance.

3. **The Nationalist Rift: Formation of Azad Morcha**:
   - Militant nationalist cadres accused Shastri of compromising national integrity to appease royal ministers.
   - Led by **Baba Harish Chandra**, **Ramkaran Joshi**, **Hans D. Roy**, and **Gulab Chand Kasliwal**, the dissenters broke away to form the **Azad Morcha (आजाद मोर्चा)** in September 1942.
   - Azad Morcha launched aggressive Quit India processions, courting mass arrests across Jaipur.

4. **Resolution & Legacy**:
   - In **1945**, following the end of World War II, **Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru** visited Jaipur and mediated an honorable reconciliation, merging Azad Morcha back into the main Jaipur Prajamandal.
   - While Shastri preserved institutional gains, Azad Morcha ensured that Jaipur’s patriotic contribution to the national Quit India struggle remained unblemished.
