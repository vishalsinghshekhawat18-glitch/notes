📰 [OCT-001] **RBI Brings FEMA (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 into Force: Realisation Windows Tightened**

- **Statutory Overhaul & Regulatory Consolidation**:
  - The Reserve Bank of India (**RBI**) notified that the *Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026* entered into statutory force on **October 1, 2026**.
  - Supersedes the erstwhile *Foreign Exchange Management (Export of Goods & Services) Regulations, 2015*, consolidating the export and import of physical goods, cross-border services, and software under a single, harmonized regulatory architecture.
- **Unified Export Declaration Form (EDF) Architecture**:
  - A single, integrated **Export Declaration Form (EDF)** now covers all export classifications (physical goods, software, and intangible services).
  - **Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) Ports**: For goods exported through EDI ports, the EDF is deemed legally submitted as part of the electronic shipping bill.
  - **Service Exporters Filing Window**: Exporters of cross-border services must file the declaration within **30 days** from the end of the month in which the service invoice is raised.
- **Realisation & Repatriation Timelines Slashed**:
  - **Standard Realisation Window**: The statutory period for realisation and repatriation of export proceeds has been reduced to **9 months** (down from the earlier 15-month allowance) from:
    - The date of shipment for physical goods;
    - The date of invoice for services;
    - The date of actual sale for goods dispatched to an overseas warehouse.
  - **INR-Invoiced / INR-Settled Exports**: Where exports are invoiced or settled in Indian Rupees under the Vostro/Special Rupee Vostro Account (SRVA) mechanism, the realisation period is **12 months** (tightened from 18 months).
  - **Project Exports**: Governed strictly by the payment schedule approved in the underlying contract.
  - Authorised Dealer (AD) Category-I banks are empowered to grant extensions on merit.
- **Simplified Closure Threshold & Caution Listing Rules**:
  - **₹10 Lakh Self-Declaration Threshold**: For shipping bills or invoices valued up to **₹10 lakh**, entries in the *Export Data Processing and Monitoring System (EDPMS)* and *Import Data Processing and Monitoring System (IDPMS)* can be formally closed on the basis of an exporter's self-declaration.
  - **Strict Trade Sanction on Overdue Proceeds**: Any exporter whose proceeds remain unrealised for **over 1 year beyond the statutory due date** is barred from exporting further, except against full advance payment or an irrevocable Letter of Credit (LC).
  - **Transitional Powers (Regulation 20)**: AD banks are authorized to deal with export, import, and merchanting trade transactions initiated prior to October 1, 2026, eliminating routine referral to the RBI.

🎯 Exam Angle →
- Effective Date: **October 1, 2026** (supersedes the 2015 Regulations).
- Realisation Timeline (Goods): Reduced from 15 months to **9 months**.
- Realisation Timeline (Rupee Settlement): Reduced from 18 months to **12 months**.
- Services EDF Filing Window: Within **30 days** from the end of the invoice month.
- EDPMS/IDPMS Self-Declaration Closure Limit: Invoices up to **₹10 Lakh**.
- 1-Year Rule Trap: Unrealised proceeds beyond 1 year force subsequent exports exclusively under **100% advance or irrevocable LC**.

📰 [OCT-002] **Ministry of Finance Freezes Small Savings Schemes (SSS) Interest Rates for Q3 FY2026-27**

- **Quarterly Determination & Benchmark Context**:
  - The Department of Economic Affairs (DEA), Ministry of Finance, maintained status quo on interest rates across all **Small Savings Schemes (SSS)** for the third quarter of FY2026-27 (**October 1 to December 31, 2026**), matching Q2 FY2026-27 levels.
  - SSS rates are notified quarterly based on the formula recommended by the **Shyamala Gopinath Committee (2010)**, tracking secondary market yields of comparable-maturity Government of India securities (G-Secs) with a sovereign spread of **25 to 100 basis points**.
- **Operative Rate Architecture (October–December 2026)**:
  - **Joint Highest Return Tier (8.20% p.a.)**:
    - **Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme (SCSS)**: **8.2%** (Quarterly payout).
    - **Sukanya Samriddhi Account Scheme (SSAS)**: **8.2%** (Compounded annually, tax-free under EEE regime).
  - **Medium-to-Long Term Savings**:
    - **National Savings Certificate (NSC VIII Issue)**: **7.7%** (5-year tenure).
    - **Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP)**: **7.5%** (Doubling period: **115 months** = 9 years and 7 months).
    - **Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS)**: **7.4%** (Monthly interest payout).
    - **Public Provident Fund (PPF)**: **7.1%** (15-year statutory tenure, annual compounding).
  - **Post Office Time Deposits (POTD)**:
    - 1-Year POTD: **6.9%** | 2-Year POTD: **7.0%** | 3-Year POTD: **7.1%** | 5-Year POTD: **7.5%**.
  - **Recurring Deposit & Basic Savings**:
    - 5-Year Post Office Recurring Deposit (RD): **6.7%**.
    - Post Office Savings Account: **4.0%**.
- **Crucial Scheme Ceilings & Structural Traps**:
  - **SCSS**: Open to citizens aged **60 years and above** (or 55–60 years for retirees under VRS/superannuation); minimum investment **₹1,000**, maximum ceiling **₹30 Lakh**; 5-year tenure extendable in blocks of **3 years**.
  - **Sukanya Samriddhi**: Open for girl children up to **10 years of age** (strictly 1 account per girl, max 2 girls per family); annual deposit limit **Min ₹250 to Max ₹1.5 Lakh**; maturity after **21 years** from date of opening.
  - **PPF**: Governed by the *PPF Scheme, 2019* (enacted under *PPF Act, 1968*); annual deposit limit **Min ₹500 to Max ₹1.5 Lakh**; matures in **15 full financial years**, extendable in blocks of **5 years**.
  - **POMIS Investment Caps**: Individual single account: **₹9 Lakh**; Joint account (up to 3 adults): **₹15 Lakh**.

🎯 Exam Angle →
- Rate Leaders: Both **SCSS and Sukanya Samriddhi** offer **8.2%**.
- KVP Maturity Trap: Matures/doubles money in **115 months** (9 years, 7 months) at 7.5%.

📰 [OCT-045] **UPSC Celebrates Centenary on October 1, 2026: Tracing Origins from Lee Commission to AI Facial Authentication**

- **Centenary Milestone & Historical Evolution**:
  - The Union Public Service Commission (**UPSC**) completed **100 years of establishment on October 1, 2026**.
  - Traces origins to October 1, 1926, following provisions in the **Government of India Act, 1919** and the recommendations of the **Lee Commission (Royal Commission on the Superior Civil Services in India, 1924)**.
  - Reconstituted as the Federal Public Service Commission under the *GoI Act, 1935*, and rechristened the **Union Public Service Commission on January 26, 1950**.
- **Constitutional Architecture**:
  - Governed by **Part XIV, Articles 315 to 323** of the Constitution of India.
  - **Article 315**: Mandates a Public Service Commission for the Union and State PSCs.
  - **Article 320**: Specifies functions (conducting examinations, advising on appointments, promotions, transfers, and disciplinary matters).
  - **Article 322**: Expenses charged directly onto the **Consolidated Fund of India**.
  - **Article 323**: Mandatory submission of an annual report to the **President of India**, who causes it to be laid before each House of Parliament.
- **Key Milestones in Examination Canon**:
  - 1969: Optional inclusion of Eighth Schedule languages for exams.
  - 1972: **Kiran Bedi** selected as the first woman Indian Police Service (IPS) officer.
  - 1979: Introduction of the 3-stage Civil Services Examination (Prelims, Mains, Interview).
  - 2026: Deployment of Universal Registration Numbers (URN) and AI-enabled facial authentication for 5.50 lakh prelims candidates.

🎯 Exam Angle →
- Centenary Date: **October 1, 2026** (Constituted October 1, 1926).
- Recommending Commission: **Lee Commission (1924)** under **Government of India Act, 1919**.
- Constitutional Range: **Articles 315–323, Part XIV**.
- Annual Report Recipient: Submitted to the **President of India** under **Article 323**.

    - Covers Over-the-Counter (OTC) derivatives, exchange-traded derivatives, Securities Financing Transactions (SFTs), and long-settlement transactions.
  - **Credit Valuation Adjustment (CVA) Framework**:
    - Notified under *CVA Framework Directions, 2026*, taking effect from **April 1, 2027**.
    - Introduces the **Basic Approach (BA-CVA)**, segregating systemic CVA risk from idiosyncratic counterparty risk, and refines supervisory risk-weights mapped to specific industry sectors and credit ratings.

🎯 Exam Angle →
- SA-CCR Threshold: **≥₹25,000 Crore derivative outstanding** or international footprint.
- Optional Alternative for smaller banks: **Current Exposure Method (CEM)**.
- CVA Framework Effective Date: **April 1, 2027**.
- Core CVA Method: **Basic Approach (BA-CVA)**.
