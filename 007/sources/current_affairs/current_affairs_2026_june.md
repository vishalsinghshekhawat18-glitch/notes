📰 [JUN-001] **RBI Relaxes Capital Requirements for Bank Loans under ECLGS 5.0 (Split Risk-Weight Structure)**

- **Regulatory Framework & Mandate**: Reserve Bank of India issued an amendment to the *Master Direction – RBI (Commercial Banks – Prudential Norms on Capital Adequacy) Directions, 2025*, significantly easing capital requirements for bank loans backed by the Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme (**ECLGS 5.0**).
- **Split Risk-Weight Mechanism**:
  - **75% of the Guaranteed Portion**: Attracts a **0% risk weight**, meaning banks are completely exempt from setting aside regulatory capital against this portion of the exposure.
  - **Remaining 25% Exposure**: Attracts a concessional **20% risk weight** (drastically reduced from standard unrated exposure weights).
- **Settlement Condition for 0% Weight**:
  - Banks can avail of the 0% risk weight benefit **only if** the guarantee claim is settled within **30 days** from the date of invocation.
  - If the 30-day settlement requirement is breached, the benefit of the 0% risk weight is revoked, and standard capital charges apply.
- **Strategic Intent**: Designed to release trapped bank capital, incentivize fresh liquidity flow to distressed and contact-intensive businesses, and align sovereign-backed portfolio risk with Basel III Pillar 1 norms.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Risk-Weight Split: **0% on 75%** of guaranteed portion vs **20% on remaining 25%** (a classic matching/statement MCQ).
- Time Boundary for Settlement: Must be settled within **30 days** of claim invocation to retain 0% risk weight.
- Policy Anchor: Governed under the *RBI (Commercial Banks – Prudential Norms on Capital Adequacy) Directions*.

📰 [JUN-002] **Launch of ARIFAC: National Industry Platform for AML/CFT Compliance with FIU-IND Oversight**

- **Genesis & Institutional Architecture**: India's financial sector formally launched the **Alliance of Reporting Entities in AML/CFT (ARIFAC)** as a premier national industry-led coordination platform to bolster Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Combating the Financing of Terrorism (CFT) frameworks.
- **Administrative Secretariat**: Jointly managed and operated by the **Payments Council of India (PCI)** and the **Fintech Convergence Council (FCC)**.
- **Official Regulatory Observer**: The **Financial Intelligence Unit – India (FIU-IND)** participates in ARIFAC in an official **observer role**, offering high-level guidance, regulatory alignment, and supervisory coordination.
- **Universal Membership Base**: Encompasses all reporting entities regulated under the *Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA)*:
  - Commercial Banks and Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs).
  - Payment System Operators (PSOs) and Payment Aggregators (PAs).
  - Securities market intermediaries, Mutual Funds, and Depository Participants.
  - Insurance companies and **Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) service providers**.
- **Operational Focus**: Standardizing suspicious transaction reporting (STR) formats, conducting joint capacity-building certifications, and creating early-warning networks to intercept financial mule rings.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Joint Secretariat: **Payments Council of India (PCI)** + **Fintech Convergence Council (FCC)** (NOT IBA alone).
- Observer Agency: **FIU-IND** (Financial Intelligence Unit – India).
- Legislative Anchor: *Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002*.

📰 [JUN-003] **Credit Guarantee Scheme for Microfinance Institutions 2.0 (CGSMFI-2.0): Expansion to ₹20,000 Crore Pool**

- **Framework Evolution**: Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services – DFS) and NCGTC operationalized **CGSMFI-2.0**, transitioning from emergency COVID-era relief (launched 28 June 2021) into a permanent, risk-based, tier-structured credit enhancement framework.
- **Pool Size & Validity**:
  - Total guarantee pool expanded to **₹20,000 crore**.
  - Extended validity up to **31 August 2026** (or until the ₹20,000 Cr guarantees are fully exhausted).
- **NCGTC Institutional Profile**:
  - Implemented by **National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company Limited (NCGTC)**, set up under DFS, Ministry of Finance (established in Union Budget 2013-14; incorporated 28 March 2014 under Companies Act 1956; paid-up capital **₹10 crore**; Headquartered in Mumbai).
- **Three-Tier MFI Classification & Loan Limits**:
  - **Small-size MFIs (AUM < ₹500 crore)**: Max loan capacity capped at **20% of AUM, subject to a maximum of ₹100 crore**; NCGTC guarantee cover of **80%**; mandatory bank allocation of minimum 5% of bank's scheme pool.
  - **Medium-size MFIs (AUM ₹500 crore to < ₹2,000 crore)**: Max loan capacity capped at **20% of AUM, subject to a maximum of ₹200 crore**; NCGTC guarantee cover of **75%**; mandatory bank allocation of minimum 10% of bank's scheme pool.
  - **Large-size MFIs (AUM ≥ ₹2,000 crore)**: Max loan capacity enhanced upward from ₹300 crore to **₹1,000 crore** (subject to 20% of AUM); NCGTC guarantee cover of **70%**; eligible for remaining available pool.
- **Interest Rate & On-Lending Structure**:
  - **Lending Rate to MFIs (MLIs to MFIs)**: Capped at **EBLR or MCLR + 2.0% per annum**.
  - **On-Lending Rate to Borrowers**: Capped at **1.0% below** the MFI's average lending rate of the preceding six months.
- **Guarantee Fee & Utilization**:
  - Guarantee fee fixed at **0.50% per annum** on the sanctioned amount in the 1st year, and on outstanding balance from Year 2 onwards.
  - Minimum **80% of funds** must be deployed for fresh microfinance loan assets within **4 months**.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Large MFI Max Loan Ceiling: Enhanced from ₹300 Cr to **₹1,000 crore** (capped at 20% of AUM).
- Tier-wise Guarantee Cover: Small = **80%**, Medium = **75%**, Large = **70%**.
- Interest Spread Cap: Lending to MFIs capped at **EBLR/MCLR + 2%**; on-lending capped at **1% below** average rate.
- NCGTC Foundation: Incorporated **28 March 2014**; initial paid-up capital **₹10 crore**.

📰 [JUN-004] **RBI Master Directions on Bank Lending to REITs & InvITs (49% Aggregate Exposure Cap)**

- **Policy Harmonization**: RBI released the *Master Directions on Bank Lending to Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) and Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs)*, permitting scheduled commercial banks to lend directly to business trusts under strict prudential boundaries.
- **Eligibility & Asset Composition Norms**:
  - Banks can lend **only to SEBI-registered and publicly listed** REITs and InvITs.
  - **Operational Asset Threshold**: At least **80% of the underlying assets** of the borrowing REIT/InvIT must comprise completed, revenue-generating properties/infrastructure projects that have yielded positive cash flows for **more than one year**.
- **Aggregate Lending & Exposure Limits**:
  - **Aggregate Bank Exposure Cap**: Total lending across all commercial banks to any single borrowing REIT or InvIT (including its SPVs and holding companies) must not exceed **49% of the value of the trust's total assets**.
  - **Funding Restriction**: Bank loans to REITs/InvITs **cannot** be utilized to fund underlying Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) that are already facing financial distress or have existing non-performing loans with regulated entities.
- **Risk Weights & Governance**:
  - Exposure to REITs attracts a **100% risk weight** (**125%** if categorized as capital market exposure).
  - Exposure to InvITs attracts corporate lending risk weights based on external credit ratings.
  - Board-approved policies mandatory; statutory migration to a comprehensive capital charge framework effective **1 April 2027**.
- **Statutory Effective Date**: Directions take effect from **1 October 2026** (banks permitted voluntary early adoption).

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Aggregate Exposure Cap: **≤49%** of the value of the trust's total assets (NOT 50% or 75%).
- Completed Asset Requirement: Minimum **80%** completed and revenue-generating with >1 year positive cash flow.
- Capital Charge Migration Date: **1 April 2027**; General compliance effective **1 October 2026**.

📰 [JUN-005] **RBI Master Directions on Authorisation of Payment System Operators (PSOs) — Perpetual Licences & Governance**

- **Statutory Authority**: Issued under **Section 4 of the Payment and Settlement Systems (PSS) Act, 2007**, governing entities seeking to operate payment systems in India.
- **Perpetual Validity Regime**:
  - All fresh Certificates of Authorisation (CoA) granted to PSOs will carry **perpetual validity** (eliminating the earlier 5-year periodic renewal requirement).
  - Existing authorized PSOs will be transitioned to perpetual validity at the time of their next renewal, subject to complete regulatory compliance.
  - **Non-Compliant Entities**: PSOs exhibiting governance or operational deficiencies will receive conditional renewals for a **limited period of 1 year only**, until deficiencies are fully rectified.
- **FDI & Foreign Investment from FATF Non-Compliant Jurisdictions**:
  - Investments from jurisdictions non-compliant with Financial Action Task Force (FATF) standards will not be treated at par with compliant countries.
  - Fresh investments from FATF non-compliant jurisdictions must account for **less than 20% of the voting power** of the PSO in aggregate.
- **Surrender of Certificate & Cooling-Off Period**:
  - Surrendering a CoA requires a written application to DPSS Central Office, accompanied by a **Statutory Auditor's certificate** confirming all customer, merchant, and bank liabilities are fully settled.
  - A mandatory **one-year cooling-off period** is imposed before an entity can re-apply if its CoA was revoked, renewal was rejected, or licence was surrendered.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Licence Validity: **Perpetual validity** for compliant PSOs; **1 year only** for non-compliant entities.
- FATF Non-Compliant Voting Cap: Must remain strictly **below 20%** of aggregate voting power.
- Cooling-Off Duration: **1 year** from revocation, surrender, or application rejection.

- **Framework Objective**: Comprehensive overhaul of the 2014 TReDS guidelines to accelerate MSME invoice monetization, expand participant categories, and eliminate duplicate factoring frauds.

  - Platform now formally accommodates: MSME Sellers, Corporate/PSU/Government Buyers, Financers (Banks, NBFC-Factors, DFIs), **Insurance Companies** (for credit risk cover), and notified **Credit Guarantee Fund Trusts**.

📰 [JUN-007] **RBI Revamped Kisan Credit Card (KCC) Scheme Directions 2026: Collateral-Free Limit Raised to ₹2 Lakh**

- **Comprehensive Overhaul**: RBI issued the *Reserve Bank of India (Kisan Credit Card – KCC) Directions, 2026*, applying to all Scheduled Commercial Banks, Small Finance Banks, RRBs, and Rural Co-operative Banks, taking effect from **1 January 2027** (existing loans continue till maturity/renewal).
- **Enhanced Collateral-Free Exemption Limit**:
  - Banks mandated to waive collateral security and margin requirements for agricultural loans up to **₹2 lakh per borrower** (raised from the earlier ₹1.60 lakh threshold).
  - Exemption equally covers allied agricultural activities (dairy, poultry, fisheries, animal husbandry).
  - Voluntary pledge of gold/silver ornaments for loans up to ₹2 lakh is permitted and will **not** be treated as a violation of collateral-free guidelines.
  - KCC loans up to **₹3 lakh** secured via hypothecation of crops or assets under formal tie-up recovery arrangements may also be exempted from collateral.
- **Standardized Crop Seasons**:
  - **Short-duration crops**: Standardized strictly to **12 months** (period from raising crop to harvesting and marketing).
  - **Long-duration crops**: Standardized strictly to **18 months**.
- **Flexi KCC for Marginal Farmers**:
  - Revolving credit facility between **₹10,000 to ₹50,000** designed for marginal farmers (landholding up to 1 hectare) based on land and cropping pattern, without requiring complex cost-of-cultivation documentation.
- **Term Loan Component Repayment Horizon**:
  - Investment credit/term loan portion under KCC is repayable within a maximum of **6 years** (if asset life/repayment requires >6 years, it must be treated as a separate loan facility outside KCC).
- **Maximum Permissible Limit (MPL)**:
  - Sanctioned limit based on scale of finance + 10% for post-harvest/household + 20% for maintenance/repairs.
  - For 2nd crop season onwards, MPL is notionally increased by **10% per annum** over the previous crop season's limit.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Collateral-Free Limit: **₹2 lakh per borrower** (enhanced from ₹1.60 lakh).
- Standardized Crop Seasons: Short-duration = **12 months**; Long-duration = **18 months**.
- Term Loan Under KCC: Maximum repayment tenor **6 years** (longer loans must sit outside KCC).
- Flexi KCC Limits: **₹10,000 to ₹50,000** for marginal farmers (holding ≤1 hectare).
- Effective Date: **1 January 2027**.

📰 [JUN-008] **RBI Revamps Lead Bank Scheme (LBS): 60% CD Ratio Benchmark & 3-Tier Governance**

- **Framework Background**: RBI issued revised comprehensive guidelines for the **Lead Bank Scheme (LBS)** (first introduced in 1969 following the Gadgil Study Group and Nariman Committee recommendations).
- **Key Administrative Designations**:
  - **Lead District Manager (LDM)**: Lead Bank must appoint an exclusive officer as LDM for each designated district (assignment of multiple districts to one LDM barred except in exceptional circumstances).
  - **District Development Manager (DDM)**: Appointed by **NABARD** in every district to liaise between district administration and financial institutions.
  - **Lead District Officer (LDO)**: Appointed by the **Reserve Bank of India** for each district to supervise credit flow.
  - **SLBC Convenor**: Convenor bank in every State/UT must designate an officer not below the rank of **General Manager (GM)** as SLBC Convenor.
- **Three-Tier Governance Structure**:
  1. *Tier 1 (Block Level)*: Block Level Bankers' Committees (**BLBCs**).
  2. *Tier 2 (District Level)*: District Consultative Committees (**DCCs**) and District Level Review Committees (DLRCs).
  3. *Tier 3 (State Level)*: State Level Bankers' Committees (**SLBCs** / UTLBCs).
- **Credit-Deposit (CD) Ratio Benchmarks & Corrective Action**:
  - Baseline Target: Minimum **60% CD Ratio** for rural and semi-urban branches on an all-India basis.
  - **CD Ratio between 40% and 60%**: Monitored by the District Consultative Committee (DCC) for step-up improvement.
  - **CD Ratio below 40%**: Mandatorily reviewed by a **Special Sub-Committee (SSC)** under DCC, chaired by the District Collector with LDM as Convenor.
  - **CD Ratio below 20%**: District placed under the **Special Category Districts** for rigorous monthly monitoring and structural intervention.
  - Similar Special Sub-Committees constituted if Annual Credit Plan (ACP) achievement falls **below 100%**.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- CD Ratio Thresholds: Ideal target = **60%**; Special Sub-Committee review triggered at **<40%**; Special Category status triggered at **<20%**.
- Rank of SLBC Convenor: Not below **General Manager (GM)**.
- Lead Bank Inception: Introduced in **1969** based on Nariman Committee / F.K.F. Nariman recommendations.
