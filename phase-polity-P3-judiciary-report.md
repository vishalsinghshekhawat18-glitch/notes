## 2. Canonical Concepts Created (10 Concepts across Topics 16, 17 & 18)

### Topic 16: Part V — The Union Judiciary (The Supreme Court of India) (`the-union-judiciary`, Order: 16)
1. **`CON-T16-01`**: *Supreme Court Architecture: Composition (34 Judges), Collegium & Removal*
   - Integrated single judicial hierarchy; Article 124 (1 CJI + 33 Judges under SC (Number of Judges) Amendment Act 2019, 65-year retirement age); Evolution of Collegium: First Judges Case (1981, executive primacy) $\rightarrow$ Second Judges Case (1993, CJI + 2 judges) $\rightarrow$ Third Judges Case (1998, CJI + 4 senior SC judges) $\rightarrow$ Fourth Judges Case (2015, 99th CAA / NJAC struck down for violating Basic Structure); Qualifications (5 yrs HC Judge / 10 yrs HC Advocate / Presidential **distinguished jurist**); Removal under Art 124(4) and Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968 (100 LS / 50 RS motion $\rightarrow$ 3-member inquiry committee $\rightarrow$ Special Majority in both Houses).
2. **`CON-T16-02`**: *Supreme Court Jurisdiction: Original (Art 131), Appellate & SLP (Art 136)*
   - Article 131 Exclusive Federal Original Jurisdiction (Centre vs States, State vs State; testing legal rights; **strict exclusions: pre-constitution treaties, inter-state river water disputes under Art 262, private citizen suits**); Appellate Jurisdiction (Constitutional Art 132, Civil Art 133, Criminal Art 134); Special Leave Petition under Article 136 (extraordinary, discretionary, plenary appellate power against any court or tribunal in India, with the sole exception of **Armed Forces Tribunals / Court Martials under Art 136(2)**).
3. **`CON-T16-03`**: *Advisory Jurisdiction (Art 143), Complete Justice (Art 142) & Law of Land (Art 141)*
   - Article 143 Presidential Reference: Clause 1 public importance (**SC may give or refuse advice**, e.g. refused in *Ayodhya 1994*) vs Clause 2 pre-constitution disputes (**SC bound to give advice**; both clauses non-binding on President); Article 142 inherent power to pass decrees for **"complete justice"** (*Bhopal Gas 1989*, *Ayodhya 2019*, *Shilpa Sailesh 2023* irretrievable breakdown of marriage); Article 141 law declared by SC is binding on all courts (*stare decisis*); Article 129 Court of Record and contempt powers.

### Topic 17: Part VI — State Judiciary (High Courts & Subordinate Courts) (`the-state-judiciary`, Order: 17)
4. **`CON-T17-01`**: *High Court Architecture: Appointments, Qualifications & Transfers (Arts 214–224A)*
   - 25 High Courts in India; Article 231 (7th CAA 1956 common High Courts); Appointed by President (consultation with CJI + Governor + HC Chief Justice; HC Collegium of CJI + 2 senior SC judges); Qualifications (Citizen, 10 yrs judicial office or 10 yrs HC advocate; **⚠️ NO distinguished jurist category for High Courts!**); Retirement age: **62 Years**; Article 222 transfer of judges (CJI + 4 senior SC judges collegium).
5. **`CON-T17-02`**: *High Court Jurisdiction: Writ Jurisdiction (Art 226 vs Art 32) & Superintendence (Art 227)*
   - Comprehensive comparison: Article 226 is **wider in subject-matter reach** (enforces Fundamental Rights + ordinary legal rights "for any other purpose") but **discretionary**; Article 32 is a Fundamental Right itself and mandatory, but restricted strictly to Part III; Article 227 administrative and judicial superintendence over all subordinate courts and statutory tribunals throughout the state territory (suo motu power; wider than Art 226); Article 215 Court of Record.
6. **`CON-T17-03`**: *Subordinate Judiciary Architecture (Arts 233–237) & High Court Control (Art 235)*
   - District Judges appointed by Governor in consultation with High Court (Art 233; min 7 years advocate practice for direct recruitment); Other judicial officers appointed via State PSC + HC consultation (Art 234); Article 235: Complete administrative, posting, promotion, and disciplinary control over subordinate courts is vested exclusively in the High Court to enforce Article 50 (Separation of Powers).
7. **`CON-T17-04`**: *Rajasthan High Court Architecture: Jodhpur Principal Seat & Jaipur Bench*
   - Inaugurated August 29, 1949 at Jodhpur by Rajpramukh Maharaja Sawai Man Singh under Rajasthan High Court Ordinance 1949; First Chief Justice: **Kamala Kant Verma**; Satyanarayan Rao Committee (1958) confirmed Jodhpur as sole Principal Seat; Permanent Jaipur Bench created under 1976 Order (operational January 31, 1977); Current sanctioned strength: **50 Judges** (38 permanent + 12 additional).

### Topic 18: Part XIV-A — Judicial Innovation, PIL & Tribunals Architecture (`judicial-innovation-and-tribunals`, Order: 18)
8. **`CON-T18-01`**: *Public Interest Litigation (PIL): Relaxation of Locus Standi & Epistolary Jurisdiction*
   - US origin; pioneered in India by Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer (*Mumbai Kamgar Sabha 1976*) and Justice P.N. Bhagwati (*S.P. Gupta 1981*); Democratic relaxation of traditional *locus standi*; Epistolary jurisdiction (letters/postcards treated as writ petitions); Landmark cases: *Hussainara Khatoon (1979)* (undertrials / speedy trial), *Bandhua Mukti Morcha (1984)* (bonded labour), *M.C. Mehta (1987)* (Oleum gas / absolute liability), *Vishaka (1997)* (workplace sexual harassment); *Balwant Singh Chaufal (2010)* safeguards against frivolous/private litigation.
9. **`CON-T18-02`**: *Judicial Review vs Judicial Activism vs Judicial Overreach*
   - Tripartite conceptual spectrum: Judicial Review (Constitutional duty to test law/order validity against Constitution; basic structure under *Kesavananda* & *L. Chandra Kumar*) vs Judicial Activism (Proactive assertion to vindicate rights and fill legislative vacuums) vs Judicial Overreach (Unconstitutional intrusion into executive policy and legislative drafting, violating separation of powers; e.g. 2016 highway liquor ban); Judicial Self-Restraint (*Aravali Golf Club 2008*).
10. **`CON-T18-03`**: *Tribunals Architecture: Part XIV-A (42nd CAA), Art 323A vs 323B & L. Chandra Kumar*
    - Part XIV-A inserted by 42nd CAA 1976 (Swaran Singh Committee); Article 323A (Administrative Tribunals for public service disputes, established **ONLY by Parliament**; CAT established Nov 1, 1985) vs Article 323B (Tribunals for taxation, land reforms, industry, rent; established by **Parliament OR State Legislatures**); *L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)* (7-judge bench held that judicial review under Arts 226/227 and 32 is part of the Basic Structure; exclusion of HC review is unconstitutional; all tribunal decisions **must be challenged before a Division Bench of the High Court** before approaching SC under Art 136).

## 5. Outdated Legacy Claims Corrected & Misconception Shields

1. **Distinguished Jurist Appointment Shield**: Highlighted the critical exam trap that a "distinguished jurist" can be appointed to the Supreme Court under Article 124(3)(c), but **cannot be appointed to High Courts** under Article 217(2).
2. **Article 136 Armed Forces Exception**: Corrected generalized claims by specifying that Article 136(2) strictly bars Special Leave Petitions against Court Martials and Armed Forces tribunals.
3. **Article 143 Advisory Discretion**: Disentangled Article 143(1) (public importance, SC may give or refuse advice) from Article 143(2) (pre-constitution disputes, SC bound to give advice), emphasizing that both are advisory and non-binding on the President.
4. **Article 323A vs 323B Legislative Competence**: Clarified that Administrative Tribunals (323A) can be established **only by Parliament**, whereas other tribunals (323B) can be created by either Parliament or State Legislatures.
5. **High Court Bypassing Trap**: Reinforced the *L. Chandra Kumar (1997)* holding that litigants cannot bypass the High Court Division Bench under Articles 226/227 to jump straight from a Tribunal to the Supreme Court.
