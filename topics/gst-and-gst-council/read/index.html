Goods and Services Tax (GST) & GST Council Architecture · Concept 1 of 2

The GST Economic Framework: Destination-Based Consumption Tax, Cascading Elimination & Input Tax Credit (ITC)

The core economic architecture of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) implemented on July 1, 2017. Analyzes the fundamental shift from an origin-based cascading tax regime to a comprehensive Destination-Based Consumption Tax applied at every stage of the supply chain. Examines the Value-Added Tax (VAT) mechanism where tax is paid strictly on value addition, the elimination of Tax-on-Tax (cascading), and the operational mechanics of the Input Tax Credit (ITC) formula: Net Tax Liability = Output Tax on Sales minus Input Tax Credit on Purchases.

Intuition & Context

Why Was GST Necessary? The Death of "Tax-on-Tax"

Before July 1, 2017, the Indian indirect tax landscape was a chaotic nightmare of 17 separate central and state taxes (Central Excise, Service Tax, State VAT, CST, Entry Tax, Octroi, Luxury Tax).

Suppose a factory manufactured a leather bag for ₹1,000:

  1. The Centre levied a $12.5%$ Central Excise Duty = ₹125 (Price = ₹1,125).
  2. When the bag was shipped to a retailer, the State levied a $14.5%$ State VAT on the entire ₹1,125! This meant the state was charging VAT on top of the Central Excise Duty (Tax on a Tax = Cascading).
  3. When the truck crossed the state border, it waited 3 days in a traffic jam to pay Entry Tax and Octroi.

GST destroyed this entire mess. It created "One Nation, One Tax, One Market", eliminated state border checkpoints, and allowed every business to deduct input taxes paid at earlier stages, so tax is paid ONLY on the fresh value added.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

Key Operational Features: E-Way Bill & Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)

1. The E-Way Bill (Electronic Way Bill)

  • Mandatory digital document generated on the GST portal for the movement of goods consignment valued at more than ₹50,000 (inter-state or intra-state).
  • Eliminated physical state border check-posts, drastically speeding up national logistics freight.

2. Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)

  • Normally, the supplier of goods/services collects tax from the buyer and deposits it with the government.
  • Under RCM (Section 9(3) & 9(4) of CGST Act), the legal liability to pay tax is reversed onto the recipient/buyer (e.g., when a registered corporate buys services from an unregistered supplier or goods transport agency).
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Origin vs Destination Principle: GST is a Destination-Based Tax. If a car is manufactured in Tamil Nadu and sold to a consumer in Rajasthan, the GST revenue belongs to Rajasthan (the consumption state), NOT Tamil Nadu.
  • Trap 2: Items Excluded from GST:
    • Alcoholic liquor for human consumption: Permanently excluded from GST by Article 366(12A) (taxed by States via State Excise & VAT).
    • 5 Petroleum Products (Petrol, Diesel, Crude, ATF, Gas): Temporarily deferred under Section 9(2) (can be brought under GST whenever GST Council decides).
    • Electricity & Real Estate Stamp Duty: Taxed exclusively by States outside GST.
  • Trap 3: E-Way Bill Threshold: The statutory threshold for generating an inter-state E-Way Bill is consignment value exceeding ₹50,000.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Destination principle, ITC utilization order, RCM rules, and cascading elimination.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Launch date (July 1, 2017), E-Way bill threshold (₹50k), and excluded goods (Alcohol & Petroleum).

Goods and Services Tax (GST) & GST Council Architecture · Concept 2 of 2

GST Constitutional Architecture: Dual GST (CGST/SGST/UTGST/IGST), GST Council (Art 279A) & GSTN

The constitutional governance structure and dual tax model established by the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016. Analyzes the Dual GST framework (simultaneous levy of CGST and SGST on intra-state supplies, and IGST on inter-state supplies under Article 269A), the constitutional architecture of the GST Council (Article 279A: Union Finance Minister as Chair, Union having 1/3rd voting weight, States together having 2/3rd voting weight, 3/4th majority required for decisions), the Goods and Services Tax Network (GSTN IT backbone), and the 5-year GST Compensation Cess mechanism (2017–2022).

Intuition & Context

Federal Harmony: How 28 States and the Centre Share One Steering Wheel

In most federal nations (like Canada or Brazil), indirect tax reforms caused bitter political fights where central governments bullied states or states rebelled.

India solved this with a constitutional masterpiece: The GST Council (Article 279A).

Neither the Centre nor the States can dictate terms alone:

  • The Centre has $33.33%$ of the voting power, meaning it has a veto over any state-led resolution (since 75%75%75% is needed to pass).
  • But all 28 States combined have $66.67%$ of the voting power, meaning the Centre cannot pass any tax hike unless at least 20 States agree with it!

This forces constant cooperative federalism: every tax rate, exemption, and rule must be negotiated through consensus.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

GST Council Voting Mathematics (Article 279A) & GSTN

1. Article 279A Voting Mathematics

  • Quorum: At least one-half (50%50%50%) of total members must be present.
  • Decision Threshold: At least three-fourths (75%75%75%) of the weighted votes of members present and voting.
  • Weighting Schema:

Total Weighted Vote=(Vote of Central Govt×13)+(∑Votes of State Govts×23×N)\text{Total Weighted Vote} = \left( \text{Vote of Central Govt} \times \frac{1}{3} \right) + \left( \sum \text{Votes of State Govts} \times \frac{2}{3 \times N} \right)Total Weighted Vote=(Vote of Central Govt×31​)+(∑Votes of State Govts×3×N2​)

  • Implication: Centre cannot pass a resolution alone (needs at least ≈62.5%\approx 62.5%≈62.5% of state votes); States cannot pass a resolution without the Centre.

2. Goods and Services Tax Network (GSTN)

  • The non-profit, state-of-the-art IT backbone managing invoice matching, return filing (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B), registration of 1.4+ Crore taxpayers, and automated ITC settlement.

3. GST Compensation Cess History (2017–2022)

  • Guaranteed States a 14%14%14% annual growth rate in tax revenues from 2015-16 base.
  • Funded by a cess on demerit goods (tobacco, pan masala, coal, luxury SUVs).
  • The 5-year guarantee expired in June 2022, but the cess collection was extended to March 2026 solely to repay the ₹2.69 Lakh Crore back-to-back market loans taken during COVID-19 to compensate states.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: GST Council Voting Weights: The Central Government holds 1/3rd (33.33%) of the weighted vote; all State Governments together hold 2/3rd (66.67%). A decision requires 3/4th (75%) majority.
  • Trap 2: IGST Levying Authority: Integrated GST (IGST) is levied and collected EXCLUSIVELY by the Central Government under Article 269A (NOT by state governments).
  • Trap 3: GST Compensation Period: The 5-year compensation guarantee ran from July 1, 2017 to June 30, 2022 (the extension of the cess to 2026 is strictly to repay borrowing principal/interest, NOT to pay new compensation to states).
  • Trap 4: UTGST vs SGST: UTGST applies only in the Union Territories without legislature (Ladakh, Chandigarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep). UTs with legislatures (Delhi, Puducherry, J&K) levy SGST.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & UPSC APFC: Article 279A voting formula, Article 269A IGST apportionment, and GST compensation loan architecture.
  • RPSC RAS & SBI/IBPS PO: GST Council chairperson (Union Finance Minister), voting threshold (75%), and Dual GST components.
