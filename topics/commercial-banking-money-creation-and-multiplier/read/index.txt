12:T577,To understand system-wide money creation, distinguish between two types of deposits:

1. **Primary Deposits (Passive)**:
   - Cash deposited by customers from their own currency holdings into a bank account.
   - Does not increase total money supply at the instant of deposit (it merely converts currency in hand into a bank deposit).

2. **Derivative Deposits (Active)**:
   - Deposits created by the bank when it sanctions a loan or purchases an asset.
   - Expands total money supply ($M_1/M_3$) because new purchasing power has been generated.

### The Mathematical Iteration of Credit Expansion
Suppose the Legal Reserve Ratio (LRR) = $10\%$ ($r = 0.10$), and Bank A receives an initial primary deposit of ₹1,000 in cash:
- **Round 1 (Bank A)**: Retains $10\%$ (₹100) as required reserve; lends excess reserve of ₹900. The borrower pays a supplier, who deposits ₹900 into Bank B.
- **Round 2 (Bank B)**: Retains $10\%$ of ₹900 (₹90) as reserve; lends ₹810. Deposited into Bank C.
- **Round 3 (Bank C)**: Retains $10\%$ of ₹810 (₹81); lends ₹729.

$$\text{Total Deposit Creation } (\Delta D) = 1000 + 900 + 810 + 729 + \dots = 1000 \times \left(\frac{1}{1 - 0.9}\right) = 1000 \times \frac{1}{0.10} = ₹10,000$$
$$\text{Total Credit Created } (\Delta L) = 900 + 810 + 729 + \dots = ₹9,000$$
$$\text{Total Required Reserves } (\Delta R) = 100 + 90 + 81 + \dots = ₹1,000$$13:T48e,The textbook simple deposit multiplier assumes that:
1. All money lent by banks is redeposited into the banking system ($0\%$ cash leakage).
2. Banks lend out $100\%$ of excess reserves ($0\%$ excess reserve holding).
3. Creditworthy borrowers with eligible collateral are infinitely available.

### Real-World Constraints in India
- **Cash Drain / Currency Leakage ($c = C/D$)**: When borrowers withdraw a portion of loans as physical currency to make payments in the informal economy, that cash exits the banking reserve pool, truncating the expansion cycle.
- **Excess Reserves / Liquidity Preference ($e = ER/D$)**: In periods of risk aversion or recession, banks park excess funds in the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) or Reverse Repo rather than lending (e.g., post-NPA crisis risk aversion).
- **Regulatory Capital Constraints (Basel III CRAR)**: Banks cannot expand balance sheets without sufficient Tier 1 and Tier 2 capital, regardless of reserve availability.
- **Statutory Reserve Pre-emptions (CRR + SLR)**: In India, RBI locks a portion of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) into CRR (cash at RBI) and SLR (G-Secs), reducing the loanable fraction.14:T4a9,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Single Bank vs Banking System**: A single commercial bank **cannot** create deposits beyond its own excess reserves (it can only lend ₹900 from a ₹1,000 deposit). Only the **entire banking system collectively** generates the ₹10,000 deposit expansion through multiple rounds of inter-bank clearing.
- **Trap 2: CRR vs SLR Impact on Money Creation**: Both CRR and SLR constrain credit creation. However, CRR removes reserves completely into zero-interest balances at RBI, whereas SLR channels loanable funds into sovereign debt (government securities), providing interest income to banks.
- **Trap 3: Deposit Multiplier vs Money Multiplier**: The deposit multiplier ($\frac{1}{r}$) relates primary deposits to total deposits. The money multiplier ($m = \frac{1+c}{c+r}$) relates high-powered reserve money ($M_0$) to broad money ($M_3$).

### Cross-Exam Focus
- **RBI Grade B, SEBI Grade A & IIBF DBF**: Numerical calculations on deposit expansion, balance sheet impacts, and constraints on credit creation.
- **SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS**: Distinctions between primary and derivative deposits, fractional reserve mechanics, and reserve ratio effects.15:T49a,Let:
- $M$ = Money Supply = Currency with Public ($C$) + Total Deposits ($D$)
- $M_0$ = Reserve Money = Currency with Public ($C$) + Total Reserves of Banks ($R$)

Divide numerator and denominator by Total Deposits ($D$):
$$m = \frac{M}{M_0} = \frac{C + D}{C + R} = \frac{\frac{C}{D} + 1}{\frac{C}{D} + \frac{R}{D}}$$

Defining:
- **Currency-Deposit Ratio ($c = \frac{C}{D}$)**: The proportion of money the public prefers to hold as cash relative to bank deposits.
- **Reserve-Deposit Ratio ($r = \frac{R}{D}$)**: The proportion of deposits banks hold as reserves ($r = \text{Required Reserves } r_r + \text{Excess Reserves } r_e$).

### The Master Multiplier Formula
$$m = \frac{1 + c}{c + r}$$

$$\text{Total Money Supply } M_3 = M_0 \times \left(\frac{1 + c}{c + r}\right)$$

### Comparative Statics (Direction of Changes)
- **$\frac{\partial m}{\partial r} < 0$**: Higher reserve requirement (CRR hike by RBI) $\implies$ lower money multiplier $\implies$ money supply contracts.
- **$\frac{\partial m}{\partial c} < 0$**: Higher cash holding by public (e.g., during election cycles or festive seasons) $\implies$ lower money multiplier $\implies$ banking liquidity tightens.16:T689,The money multiplier is not a mechanical constant; it is governed by behavioral decisions of the public, commercial banks, and the central bank:

```
                    DETERMINANTS OF MONEY MULTIPLIER (m)
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                          ▼                          ▼
  THE PUBLIC (c)            COMMERCIAL BANKS (r_e)      CENTRAL BANK (r_r)
• Income levels            • Risk perception           • CRR requirements
• Banking penetration      • Short-term interest rates • SLR stipulations
• Digital adoption (UPI)   • Liquidity buffers (SDF)   • Emergency windows (MSF)
• Informal economy share
```

### 1. Currency-Deposit Ratio ($c = C/D$)
- **Digital Payments (UPI/NEFT)**: Lowers $c$ because individuals need less physical cash for daily transactions. As cash moves into bank deposits, $m$ rises.
- **Interest Rates on Deposits**: Higher deposit interest rates incentivize public to deposit cash into banks, lowering $c$ and raising $m$.
- **Economic Uncertainty / Bank Runs**: Public rushes to withdraw deposits as cash, causing $c$ to spike and $m$ to collapse (the classic mechanism of monetary contraction in banking panics).

### 2. Reserve-Deposit Ratio ($r = R/D$)
- **Required Reserves ($r_r$)**: Directly set by RBI via CRR.
- **Excess Reserves ($r_e$)**: Kept by banks as insurance against liquidity shocks. When inter-bank call rates are low or economic risk is elevated, banks hold higher $r_e$, dampening $m$.17:T4d9,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Relationship between $c$ and $m$**: A frequent trap is assuming that because $c$ appears in both numerator and denominator, its effect is ambiguous. Since $r < 1$, the derivative $\frac{\partial m}{\partial c} = \frac{r - 1}{(c + r)^2} < 0$. Thus, **an increase in the currency-deposit ratio ALWAYS reduces the money multiplier**.
- **Trap 2: Demonetization Multiplier Shock**: During the November 2016 demonetization in India, the sudden mandatory deposit of high-denomination notes into banks caused $c$ to plummet temporarily, driving a sharp mechanical spike in the money multiplier, even as physical currency contracted.
- **Trap 3: Money Multiplier vs Velocity**: Money Multiplier ($m = M_3 / M_0$) is a **stock ratio** between two money aggregates. Velocity ($V = PY / M_3$) is a **flow-to-stock ratio** relating annual national output flow to money stock.

### Cross-Exam Focus
- **RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A**: Advanced numerical questions calculating $m$, change in $M_3$ from open market operations (OMO) changing $M_0$, and sensitivity analysis.
