Commercial Banking Money Creation & The Money Multiplier

Credit creation mechanism of commercial banks: fractional reserve banking, primary vs derivative deposits, step-by-step balance sheet iterations of the deposit expansion multiplier, central bank reserve requirements, credit creation constraints, and cash leakage frictions.

Commercial Banking Money Creation & The Money Multiplier · Concept 1 of 2

Commercial Bank Money Creation: Fractional Reserve Banking, Primary vs Derivative Deposits and the Credit Multiplier Cycle

The foundational macroeconomic process through which commercial banks generate new money in the form of demand deposits by lending out fractions of received primary deposits under fractional reserve banking. Covers primary vs derivative deposits ("loans create deposits"), the multi-bank deposit expansion chain, and real-world credit generation constraints.

Intuition & Context

Loans Create Deposits: Why Banks Are Not Mere Intermediaries

A popular misconception is that commercial banks act simply as passive middlemen—taking money from a saver and physically passing those exact banknotes to a borrower. In reality, modern commercial banks are money creators.

When a bank approves a ₹10,00,000 home loan, it does not open a vault and count out cash, nor does it wait for someone to deposit ₹10,00,000 in savings. It simply enters two numbers on its electronic ledger: an Asset (the loan agreement owed by the borrower) and a matching Liability (a newly created ₹10,00,000 deposit in the borrower's account). Thus, in the modern economy, loans create deposits.

Core Principle

Primary vs Derivative Deposits & The Deposit Multiplier Chain

To understand system-wide money creation, distinguish between two types of deposits:

  1. Primary Deposits (Passive):

    • Cash deposited by customers from their own currency holdings into a bank account.
    • Does not increase total money supply at the instant of deposit (it merely converts currency in hand into a bank deposit).

  2. Derivative Deposits (Active):

    • Deposits created by the bank when it sanctions a loan or purchases an asset.
    • Expands total money supply (M1/M3M_1/M_3M1​/M3​) because new purchasing power has been generated.

The Mathematical Iteration of Credit Expansion

Suppose the Legal Reserve Ratio (LRR) = 10%10%10% (r=0.10r = 0.10r=0.10), and Bank A receives an initial primary deposit of ₹1,000 in cash:

  • Round 1 (Bank A): Retains 10%10%10% (₹100) as required reserve; lends excess reserve of ₹900. The borrower pays a supplier, who deposits ₹900 into Bank B.
  • Round 2 (Bank B): Retains 10%10%10% of ₹900 (₹90) as reserve; lends ₹810. Deposited into Bank C.
  • Round 3 (Bank C): Retains 10%10%10% of ₹810 (₹81); lends ₹729.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

Frictions & Factual Constraints on Credit Creation

The textbook simple deposit multiplier assumes that:

  1. All money lent by banks is redeposited into the banking system (0%0%0% cash leakage).
  2. Banks lend out 100%100%100% of excess reserves (0%0%0% excess reserve holding).
  3. Creditworthy borrowers with eligible collateral are infinitely available.

Real-World Constraints in India

  • Cash Drain / Currency Leakage (c=C/Dc = C/Dc=C/D): When borrowers withdraw a portion of loans as physical currency to make payments in the informal economy, that cash exits the banking reserve pool, truncating the expansion cycle.
  • Excess Reserves / Liquidity Preference (e=ER/De = ER/De=ER/D): In periods of risk aversion or recession, banks park excess funds in the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) or Reverse Repo rather than lending (e.g., post-NPA crisis risk aversion).
  • Regulatory Capital Constraints (Basel III CRAR): Banks cannot expand balance sheets without sufficient Tier 1 and Tier 2 capital, regardless of reserve availability.
  • Statutory Reserve Pre-emptions (CRR + SLR): In India, RBI locks a portion of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) into CRR (cash at RBI) and SLR (G-Secs), reducing the loanable fraction.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Question Patterns

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Single Bank vs Banking System: A single commercial bank cannot create deposits beyond its own excess reserves (it can only lend ₹900 from a ₹1,000 deposit). Only the entire banking system collectively generates the ₹10,000 deposit expansion through multiple rounds of inter-bank clearing.
  • Trap 2: CRR vs SLR Impact on Money Creation: Both CRR and SLR constrain credit creation. However, CRR removes reserves completely into zero-interest balances at RBI, whereas SLR channels loanable funds into sovereign debt (government securities), providing interest income to banks.
  • Trap 3: Deposit Multiplier vs Money Multiplier: The deposit multiplier (1r\frac{1}{r}r1​) relates primary deposits to total deposits. The money multiplier (m=1+cc+rm = \frac{1+c}{c+r}m=c+r1+c​) relates high-powered reserve money (M0M_0M0​) to broad money (M3M_3M3​).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B, SEBI Grade A & IIBF DBF: Numerical calculations on deposit expansion, balance sheet impacts, and constraints on credit creation.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Distinctions between primary and derivative deposits, fractional reserve mechanics, and reserve ratio effects.

The Money Multiplier Framework: Reserve-Deposit Ratio, Currency-Deposit Ratio & High-Powered Money Transmission

The mathematical and macroeconomic relationship linking High-Powered Reserve Money ($M_0$) to total Money Supply ($M_3$). Examines the money multiplier ($m = \frac{1+c}{c+r}$), the behavioural determinants of the currency-deposit ratio ($c = C/D$) and reserve-deposit ratio ($r = R/D$), and the modern impact of digital payment systems (UPI/NEFT) on monetary velocity and central bank balance sheet transmission.

Intuition & Context

The High-Powered Lever: How Central Bank Base Money Expands

Why is Reserve Money (M0M_0M0​) called High-Powered Money? Because every single rupee of M0M_0M0​ issued by the central bank serves as the high-powered foundation upon which the commercial banking system constructs multiple rupees of broad money supply (M3M_3M3​).

The Money Multiplier (mmm) is the expansion factor that measures how much broad money is created per unit of central bank reserve money. If M0=₹50 Lakh CroreM_0 = \text{₹}50\text{ Lakh Crore}M0​=₹50 Lakh Crore and M3=₹250 Lakh CroreM_3 = \text{₹}250\text{ Lakh Crore}M3​=₹250 Lakh Crore, the money multiplier is m=5.0m = 5.0m=5.0. Understanding what makes mmm rise or fall is fundamental to central bank liquidity forecasting.

Core Principle

The Money Multiplier: Concept & Master Formula

Let:

  • MMM = Money Supply = Currency with Public (CCC) + Total Deposits (DDD)
  • M0M_0M0​ = Reserve Money = Currency with Public (CCC) + Total Reserves of Banks (RRR)

Divide numerator and denominator by Total Deposits (DDD):
m=MM0=C+DC+R=CD+1CD+RDm = \frac{M}{M_0} = \frac{C + D}{C + R} = \frac{\frac{C}{D} + 1}{\frac{C}{D} + \frac{R}{D}}m=M0​M​=C+RC+D​=DC​+DR​DC​+1​

Defining:

  • Currency-Deposit Ratio (c=CDc = \frac{C}{D}c=DC​): The proportion of money the public prefers to hold as cash relative to bank deposits.
  • Reserve-Deposit Ratio (r=RDr = \frac{R}{D}r=DR​): The proportion of deposits banks hold as reserves (r=Required Reserves rr+Excess Reserves rer = \text{Required Reserves } r_r + \text{Excess Reserves } r_er=Required Reserves rr​+Excess Reserves re​).

The Master Multiplier Formula

m=1+cc+rm = \frac{1 + c}{c + r}m=c+r1+c​

Total Money Supply M3=M0×(1+cc+r)\text{Total Money Supply } M_3 = M_0 \times \left(\frac{1 + c}{c + r}\right)Total Money Supply M3​=M0​×(c+r1+c​)

Comparative Statics (Direction of Changes)

  • Reserve Ratio Effect (r↑r \uparrowr↑): Higher reserve requirement (CRR hike by RBI)   ⟹  \implies⟹ lower money multiplier (m↓m \downarrowm↓)   ⟹  \implies⟹ money supply contracts.
  • Currency Holding Effect (c↑c \uparrowc↑): Higher cash holding by public (e.g., during election cycles or festive seasons)   ⟹  \implies⟹ lower money multiplier (m↓m \downarrowm↓)   ⟹  \implies⟹ banking liquidity tightens.
Mechanism & Operational Rule

Behavioral Drivers of $c$ and $r$ & Digital Disintermediation in India

The money multiplier is not a mechanical constant; it is governed by behavioral decisions of the public, commercial banks, and the central bank:

                    DETERMINANTS OF MONEY MULTIPLIER (m)
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                          ▼                          ▼
  THE PUBLIC (c)            COMMERCIAL BANKS (r_e)      CENTRAL BANK (r_r)
• Income levels            • Risk perception           • CRR requirements
• Banking penetration      • Short-term interest rates • SLR stipulations
• Digital adoption (UPI)   • Liquidity buffers (SDF)   • Emergency windows (MSF)
• Informal economy share

1. Currency-Deposit Ratio (c=C/Dc = C/Dc=C/D)

  • Digital Payments (UPI/NEFT): Lowers ccc because individuals need less physical cash for daily transactions. As cash moves into bank deposits, mmm rises.
  • Interest Rates on Deposits: Higher deposit interest rates incentivize public to deposit cash into banks, lowering ccc and raising mmm.
  • Economic Uncertainty / Bank Runs: Public rushes to withdraw deposits as cash, causing ccc to spike and mmm to collapse (the classic mechanism of monetary contraction in banking panics).

2. Reserve-Deposit Ratio (r=R/Dr = R/Dr=R/D)

  • Required Reserves (rrr_rrr​): Directly set by RBI via CRR.
  • Excess Reserves (rer_ere​): Kept by banks as insurance against liquidity shocks. When inter-bank call rates are low or economic risk is elevated, banks hold higher rer_ere​, dampening mmm.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps, Formulas & Multi-Exam Question Patterns

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Relationship between ccc and mmm: A frequent trap is assuming that because ccc appears in both numerator and denominator, its effect is ambiguous. Since bank reserves are only a fraction of deposits (r<1r < 1r<1), cash outside the banking system drains base money from multiple-deposit expansion. Thus, an increase in the currency-deposit ratio (c↑c \uparrowc↑) ALWAYS reduces the money multiplier (m↓m \downarrowm↓).
  • Trap 2: Demonetization Multiplier Shock: During the November 2016 demonetization in India, the sudden mandatory deposit of high-denomination notes into banks caused ccc to plummet temporarily, driving a sharp mechanical spike in the money multiplier, even as physical currency contracted.
  • Trap 3: Money Multiplier vs Velocity: Money Multiplier (m=M3/M0m = M_3 / M_0m=M3​/M0​) is a stock ratio between two money aggregates. Velocity (V=PY/M3V = PY / M_3V=PY/M3​) is a flow-to-stock ratio relating annual national output flow to money stock.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Advanced numerical questions calculating mmm, change in M3M_3M3​ from open market operations (OMO) changing M0M_0M0​, and sensitivity analysis.
  • UPSC APFC & RPSC RAS: Conceptual questions on the impact of digital payments, financial inclusion, and CRR hikes on the money multiplier.
