Economic Planning & NITI Aayog · Concept 1 of 2

Indian Economic Planning: Five-Year Plan Models (Harrod-Domar to Mahalanobis), Structural Shifts & Indicative Planning

The macroeconomic strategies, resource allocation models, and structural evolution of centralized economic planning in India (1951–2017). Analyzes: (1) Pre-Independence Planning Roots (Visvesvaraya Plan 1934, National Planning Committee 1938, Bombay Plan 1944, People's Plan, Gandhian Plan, Sarvodaya Plan 1950), (2) The 12 Five-Year Plans (First Plan: Harrod-Domar agricultural focus; Second Plan: Feldman-Mahalanobis heavy-industry capital goods model; Third Plan: "take-off" and Green Revolution prelude; Plan Holidays 1966-69; Fourth/Fifth Plans: Garibi Hatao and minimum needs; Rolling Plan 1978-80; Sixth/Seventh Plans: structural poverty and infrastructure; Eighth Plan: post-1991 Rao-Manmohan indicative planning; Eleventh/Twelfth Plans: "Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth"), and (3) The Economic Transition from centralized command allocation to market-based indicative planning and policy coordination.

Intuition & Context

Building a Steel Factory vs Buying Wheat: The Mahalanobis Dilemma

Imagine an independent, newly sovereign nation with empty pockets and a starving population:

  • Option A (Short-term focus): Spend all your limited tax revenue buying food grains and setting up textile mills. The people eat today and get cheap shirts tomorrow, but you must permanently import every single machine, tractor, and rail engine from foreign powers using scarce US Dollars.
  • Option B (The Mahalanobis Heavy-Industry Strategy): Direct every rupee into massive state-owned steel plants, machine-tool factories, and hydroelectric dams. In the short term, consumer goods remain scarce, food queues grow, and foreign exchange runs out. But 20 years later, you have the domestic industrial muscle to build your own trains, tractors, power plants, and modern industries without begging abroad.

This was the central intellectual debate of Indian economic planning.

From the Harrod-Domar agricultural boost (1st Plan) to the Mahalanobis heavy-industry push (2nd Plan) and the post-1991 Indicative Planning shift (8th Plan), Indian planning was not a boring bureaucratic routine—it was a high-stakes macroeconomic gamble to transform a colonial agrarian colony into a modern industrial superpower under severe capital constraints.

2. Imperative vs Indicative Planning Dynamics

  • Imperative / Command Planning (1st to 7th Plan): The state sets physical production quotas, allocates raw materials, fixes prices, and forbids private entry through industrial licensing (License-Permit-Quota Raj).
  • Indicative Planning (8th to 12th Plan): The state provides macroeconomic projections, builds foundational physical/digital public infrastructure, maintains price stability, and uses fiscal-monetary signals to guide private capital into national priority sectors.
Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Rolling Plan Period: The "Rolling Plan" was introduced by the Janata Party government under Prof. D.T. Lakdawala for 1978–1980 (rejecting the 5th Plan), NOT during the 1966–69 Plan Holidays.
  • Trap 2: Mahalanobis Plan Sequence: The Mahalanobis model was applied to the Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61), NOT the First Plan (which used Harrod-Domar).
  • Trap 3: Target vs Actual Performance: The plans that significantly exceeded their growth targets were the 1st Plan (2.1% target vs 3.6% actual), 5th Plan (4.4% vs 4.8%), 6th Plan (5.2% vs 5.7%), and 8th Plan (5.6% vs 6.7%). The 3rd Plan failed most severely (5.6% target vs 2.84% actual).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B (ESI): Mahalanobis capital goods equations, Harrod-Domar growth constraints, and indicative planning transition post-1991.
  • SEBI Grade A (Economics): Historical capital formation trends, public vs private sector investment shares across plan periods.
  • NABARD Grade A (ESI & ARD): Agriculture allocation shifts across plans, Plan Holidays & Green Revolution launch (1966–69), and IRDP/NABARD establishment in 6th Plan.
  • RPSC RAS (Mains Paper 1 & Prelims): Five-Year Plans objectives, Gadgil formula, poverty alleviation focus in 5th/6th Plans, and historical growth rates of India.
  • UPSC APFC: Labour policy evolution across plan periods, employment generation strategies (NREP/RLEGP), and the 12th Plan inclusive growth framework.

Economic Planning & NITI Aayog · Concept 2 of 2

NITI Aayog: Cooperative & Competitive Federalism, Policy Think-Tank Role & Flagship Transformation Frameworks

The institutional structure, economic rationale, and policy governance architecture of the National Institution for Transforming India (NITI Aayog). Analyzes: (1) The Institutional Transition from Planning Commission to NITI Aayog (established Jan 1, 2015 by Union Cabinet Resolution; shifting from top-down fund allocation and schematic approvals to a "Think Tank" providing strategic policy direction), (2) Federal Architecture (Cooperative Federalism via the Governing Council comprising the Prime Minister, all State Chief Ministers, and UT Lt. Governors; Competitive Federalism via evidence-based performance rankings), (3) Flagship Transformation Frameworks (SDG India Index, Multidimensional Poverty Index, Aspirational Districts Programme [ADP] covering 112 backward districts, Aspirational Blocks Programme [ABP] covering 500 blocks), and (4) Strategic Visioning (replacing 5-year plans with a 15-Year Vision Document, 7-Year Strategy, and 3-Year Action Agenda).

Intuition & Context

From School Principal with a Stick to a Strategic Management Consultant

Imagine how two different organizations manage 28 branch offices:

  • The Old Planning Commission Model (Command Allocation): The head office treats state Chief Ministers like schoolboys who must travel to New Delhi every year with begging bowls. The Planning Commission dictates: "You will spend ₹500 Crore on building canals, whether your desert state needs canals or solar power. If you don't obey our standard scheme guidelines, we will withhold your funds."
  • The NITI Aayog Model (Think-Tank & Federal Enabler): NITI Aayog holds no money bag. The Ministry of Finance and the Finance Commission disburse the funds directly to the states. Instead, NITI Aayog acts as a cutting-edge management consultancy and federal platform:
    1. It sits all 28 Chief Ministers around the same table (Cooperative Federalism).
    2. It publicly publishes rankings: "District X in Rajasthan improved child immunization by 24% this month, while District Y in another state fell behind" (Competitive Federalism).
    3. It identifies best-practice innovations from one district and replicates them nationwide.
Core Principle

Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog & Flagship Transformation Matrix

1. Institutional Architecture: Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog

Structural Dimension Legacy Planning Commission (1950–2014) NITI Aayog (2015–Present)
Legal Status Non-statutory, extra-constitutional body (Cabinet Resolution March 1950). Non-statutory, extra-constitutional body (Cabinet Resolution Jan 1, 2015).
Core Philosophy Top-Down ("One-Size-Fits-All") Command Planning. Bottom-Up ("Cooperative & Competitive Federalism").
Financial Disbursal Powers Allocated Plan Funds to Central Ministries and State Governments. ZERO financial allocation powers (transferred to Ministry of Finance).
State Participation States participated passively through the National Development Council (NDC). States are permanent co-architects via the Governing Council.
Policy Horizon Rigid 5-Year Plans + Annual Schematic Approvals. 15-Year Vision Document, 7-Year Strategy, and 3-Year Action Agenda.
Role in Policy Imposed centralized schemes and bureaucratic approvals. Serves as an apex Think Tank, knowledge catalyst, and performance evaluator.

2. NITI Aayog's 3 Pillar Transformation Engines

  1. Cooperative Federalism: The Governing Council enables Chief Ministers to set national agendas collaboratively.
  2. Competitive Federalism: Ranking states and districts via indices (SDG India Index, Export Preparedness Index, State Energy & Climate Index, Multidimensional Poverty Index).
  3. Targeted Micro-Development:
    • Aspirational Districts Programme (ADP, 2018): 112 backward districts monitored monthly on 49 KPIs (Health 30%, Education 30%, Agri/Water 20%, Financial Inclusion/Skills 10%, Infrastructure 10%).
    • Aspirational Blocks Programme (ABP, 2023): 500 underdeveloped blocks across 329 districts.

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Fund Allocation Authority: NITI Aayog does NOT allocate funds or grant schematic financial approvals to states. That power belongs exclusively to the Ministry of Finance and the Finance Commission.
  • Trap 2: Constitutional Status: NITI Aayog is NEITHER a Constitutional body NOR a Statutory body. It was created via an executive Cabinet Resolution on January 1, 2015.
  • Trap 3: Governing Council vs NDC: The National Development Council (NDC) was the legacy body under the Planning Commission. NITI Aayog operates through its Governing Council, which includes all Chief Ministers as voting members from Day 1.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • SEBI Grade A (Economics): Disinvestment advisory role of NITI Aayog and public asset monetization pipelines.
  • NABARD Grade A (ESI & ARD): Aspirational Districts & Blocks Programmes, rural agricultural performance indices, and water management index.

    • Chairperson: Prime Minister of India.
    • Governing Council: All CMs + UT Lt. Governors.
    • Full-time Leadership: Vice-Chairperson, Full-time Members, Ex-Officio Members (Union Ministers), and CEO.
