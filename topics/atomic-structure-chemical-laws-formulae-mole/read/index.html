Laws of Chemical Combination & Dalton's Atomic Theory

The foundational quantitative laws of chemistry: mass conservation (Lavoisier), constant proportions (Proust), multiple proportions (Dalton), and Dalton's atomic postulates explaining them.

Intuition & Context

Why 100 kg of Burning Wood Leaves 1 kg of Ash (Where Did 99 kg Go?)

If you burn a giant $100 , \text{kg}$ wooden log in a campfire, all that remains the next morning is a tiny pile of grey ash weighing barely $1 , \text{kg}. Did $99 , \text{kg} of matter magically vanish into non-existence? No! If you burned the log inside a sealed glass dome and weighed the entire container before and after, the scale would read EXACTLY $100 , \text{kg} + \text{mass of oxygen}! The missing $99 , \text{kg} simply escaped as invisible carbon dioxide gas (CO2CO_2CO2​) and water vapor (H2OH_2OH2​O) (Conservation of Mass).

How Dalton's Atomic Postulates Explain Chemical Laws

Causal Deductions of Dalton's Postulates:

  1. Deduction of Mass Conservation:
    • Postulate: "Atoms are indivisible units that cannot be created or destroyed in chemical reactions; a reaction is merely a rearrangement of existing atoms."
    • Because the exact same atoms exist before and after the reaction, total mass must remain strictly constant.
  2. Deduction of Definite Proportions:
    • Postulate: "All atoms of a given element have identical mass, and combine in fixed simple whole number ratios to form a specific compound."
    • Since 2 atoms of HHH (mass $2 \times 1 = 2$) always combine with 1 atom of OOO (mass $16$) to form H2OH_2OH2​O, the mass ratio 216=1:8\frac{2}{16} = \mathbf{1:8}162​=1:8 is mathematically immutable regardless of whether the water came from rain, ocean, or laboratory synthesis.

    • (a) Atoms are NOT indivisible: composed of subatomic particles (electrons, protons, neutrons).
    • (b) Atoms of the same element can have different masses (Isotopes, e.g. 12C^{12}C12C and 14C^{14}C14C).
    • (c) Atoms of different elements can have the same mass (Isobars, e.g. 40Ar^{40}Ar40Ar and 40Ca^{40}Ca40Ca).

Subatomic Particles & Atomic Models: Thomson, Rutherford & Bohr

The discovery of electrons (Thomson), protons (Goldstein), and neutrons (Chadwick), Rutherford's alpha-scattering nuclear discovery, and Bohr's discrete quantized orbits.

Intuition & Context

Shooting a Bullet at Tissue Paper and Having It Bounce Back at You

In 1911, Ernest Rutherford shot massive, heavy alpha particles (helium nuclei traveling at $15,000 , \text{km/s}$) at a gossamer-thin sheet of gold foil. Most bullets passed straight through like the foil was invisible air. But 1 out of every 12,000 bullets slammed into something so dense and rock-hard that it bounced straight back at the experimenter! Rutherford recalled: "It was as if you fired a 15-inch artillery shell at a piece of tissue paper and it bounced back and hit you." He had just discovered the atomic Nucleus.

    • The outermost shell cannot accommodate more than 8 electrons, even if it has the capacity to hold more (e.g. Potassium Z=19Z=19Z=19 has configuration $2, 8, 8, 1, NOT $2, 8, 9).
  3. Stepwise Filling: Electrons are not accommodated in a given shell unless the inner shells are completely filled (stepwise filling).

If protons weigh $1 , u$ and neutrons weigh $1 , u, why does Chlorine listed on the Periodic Table weigh $35.5 , u? You cannot cut a neutron in half! Electronic Valency, Ions (Cations vs Anions) & Criss-Cross Formula Construction

The combining capacity of atoms governed by valence electrons, formation of electropositive cations and electronegative anions, and the criss-cross algorithm for writing balanced chemical formulae.

Intuition & Context

Why Table Salt is NaCl but Calcium Chloride is CaCl₂ (The Handshake Rule)

Think of valency as the number of "hands" an atom has for holding other atoms: Sodium (NaNaNa) has 1 hand (+1+1+1), and Chlorine (ClClCl) has 1 hand (−1-1−1). When they shake hands, one NaNaNa pairs perfectly with one ClClCl, making NaClNaClNaCl. But Calcium (CaCaCa) has 2 hands (+2+2+2). A single 1-handed Chlorine cannot satisfy Calcium—Calcium must shake hands with TWO separate Chlorine atoms simultaneously, making CaCl2CaCl_2CaCl2​!

Molecular Mass, The Mole Concept & Avogadro's Number (6.022 × 10²³)

The counting bridge between sub-microscopic atoms and macroscopic laboratory masses: unified atomic mass units, molecular/formula mass, Avogadro's number, and mole conversions.

Intuition & Context

The Chemist's "Dozen": Why We Count Atoms by Weighing Grams

If you go to a bakery, you don't buy 1,200 individual grains of rice—you buy a kilogram bag. If you buy eggs, you buy them by the "Dozen" (12 eggs). Because atoms are too unimaginably tiny to count one-by-one under a microscope, chemists invented their own super-sized dozen: The MOLE ($6.022 \times 10^{23}$ particles). The beauty of the mole is that if you weigh out the atomic mass of any element in grams on a kitchen scale (e.g. $12 , \text{g}$ of Carbon or $18 , \text{g}$ of Water), you are holding EXACTLY 1 MOLE ($6.022 \times 10^{23}$ molecules) in your hand!
