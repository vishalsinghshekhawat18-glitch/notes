16:T5d4,In 1848, Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels predicted in The Communist Manifesto that proletarian revolution would erupt in the most industrially advanced capitalist nations—such as Great Britain or Germany—where factory wage laborers constituted the overwhelming majority.

Yet in 1917, history defied theoretical prediction: the world's first successful socialist revolution erupted in Tsarist Russia—a vast, economically backward empire where over 85% of the population were peasants cultivating land through the traditional village commune (Mir), and urban factory workers comprised less than 5% of the populace!

How did this improbable transformation occur?
1. The Rotting Autocracy: Tsar Nicholas II ruled without parliamentary accountability, refusing constitutional power-sharing while dragging millions of ill-equipped peasant-conscripts to slaughter on the Eastern Front of World War I.
2. The Dual Power Stalemate (Dvoyevlastiye): When women workers and soldiers overthrew the Tsar on International Women's Day in February 1917, the newly installed bourgeois Provisional Government insisted on staying in WWI, while the Petrograd Soviet held actual military control over troops and rail lines.
3. Lenin's Strategic Clarity: Returning from Swiss exile in April 1917, Vladimir Lenin formulated the April Theses, crystallizing popular demands into three words: "Peace, Land, and Bread". ### 2. Early Socialists & Marxist Theoretical Foundations
- **Robert Owen (1771–1858):** Leading English manufacturer who sought to build a cooperative community called **New Harmony** in Indiana (USA) based on voluntary individual initiative.
- **Louis Blanc (1813–1882):** French socialist who argued that individual enterprise could not produce cooperatives on a large scale; demanded that governments actively encourage cooperatives to replace capitalist enterprises.
- **Karl Marx (1818–1883) & Friedrich Engels (1820–1895):** Formulated scientific socialism. Industrial society was capitalist; profit produced by workers was appropriated by private capitalists. Workers could free themselves only by overthrowing capitalism, abolishing private property, and establishing a radically communist society.
- **Second International:** Formed in the 1870s to coordinate socialist workers' associations across Europe; in Britain, socialists and trade unionists formed the **Labour Party (1905)**, and in France, the **Socialist Party**.

### 3. Russian Agrarian Uniqueness: The Mir (Commune)
- Unlike European peasants, Russian peasants had a historic custom: they pooled their land together periodically, and their village commune—the ***Mir***—divided it according to the needs of individual families.
- Because of this communal habit, Russian socialists (the Socialist Revolutionaries) argued that Russian peasants were "natural socialists", and that Russia would bypass capitalism straight into socialism!
- *Lenin's Counter-Argument:* Lenin and the Social Democrats disagreed. Lenin observed that the peasantry was not a homogeneous group: some were poor laborers, others rich kulaks employing workers. The factory proletariat, disciplined by modern industry, had to lead the revolution.

### 4. Detailed Revolutionary Trajectory (1905–1924)
1. **1905 Revolution ("The Dress Rehearsal"):**
   - *Bloody Sunday (January 22, 1905):* Procession of peaceful workers led by **Father Gapon** carrying petitions to the Winter Palace; fired upon by police, killing over 100 workers.
   - Widespread strikes, mutinies (Battleship Potemkin), and creation of the first **St. Petersburg Soviet**.
   - Tsar conceded the **October Manifesto** creating an elected parliament (**Duma**), but repeatedly dissolved the 1st and 2nd Dumas to preserve autocratic veto.
2. **The February Revolution of 1917 (Julian Calendar Feb / Gregorian March):**
   - Began on **February 23 (International Women's Day)** with bread strikes by women textile workers in Petrograd.
   - When troops refused to fire on protestors and mutinied, Tsar Nicholas II abdicated on **March 2 (March 15 Gregorian)**, ending the 300-year Romanov autocracy.
   - Formation of **Dual Power (*Dvoyevlastiye*)**: Bourgeois Provisional Government under Prince Lvov / Kerensky vs Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies.
3. **Lenin's April Theses (April 1917):**
   - Vladimir Lenin returned from Switzerland via a German sealed train.
   - Issued three clear demands: (a) Immediate end to World War I; (b) Land transfer to peasants; (c) Nationalization of all banks. Coined the slogan: *"All Power to the Soviets!"*
4. **The October Revolution of 1917 (October 24–25 / November 6–7):**
   - Coordinated by **Leon Trotsky**, head of the Military Revolutionary Committee.
   - Bolshevik Red Guards seized telegraph offices, bridges, and railways; cruiser *Aurora* shelled the Winter Palace; ministers of Provisional Government arrested.
   - All-Russian Congress of Soviets approved the Bolshevik seizure of power.
5. **Post-October Decrees & Civil War (1918–1920):**
   - Land declared social property; banks nationalized (Nov 1917); nobility titles banned; non-Russian nationalities given self-determination.
   - Bolsheviks signed the humiliating **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918)** with Germany to exit WWI.
   - Civil War between the **Reds** (Bolsheviks), **Whites** (Tsarists/liberals supported by Britain, France, USA, Japan), and **Greens** (Socialist Revolutionaries). Red Army under Trotsky emerged victorious.18:T779,### 1. War Communism (1918–1921) vs. New Economic Policy (1921–1928)
- **War Communism:** Emergency civil war policy:
  * Total nationalization of all industries and factories.
  * Total ban on private trade and private ownership of grain.
  * Forced grain requisitioning (*Prodrazvyorstka*) by armed worker squads.
  * *Outcome:* Destruction of agricultural incentives, peasant revolts, catastrophic 1921 famine, and the **Kronstadt Naval Mutiny (March 1921)** by previously loyal sailors.
- **Lenin's New Economic Policy (NEP, 1921):** Pragmatic tactical retreat:
  * Replaced forced confiscation with a fixed agricultural tax in kind (*Prodnalog*).
  * Permitted peasants to sell surplus grain in open markets.
  * Allowed small-scale private enterprise, trade, and retail shops (*NEPmen*).
  * State retained control over the "commanding heights" (heavy industry, railways, banking, foreign trade).

### 2. Stalinist Collectivization & Industrialization (1928–1939)
- **The Grain Crisis of 1927–28:** State procurement prices were low; peasants refused to sell grain, causing severe urban shortages. Stalin blamed rich peasants—**Kulaks**—for hoarding.
- **Collectivization Program (*Kolkhoz*, 1929):**
  * State forced all peasants to cultivate collective farms (*Kolkhoz*).
  * Bulk of land and implements transferred to collective ownership; profits shared according to work done.
  * Enraged peasants resisted by slaughtering livestock; between 1929 and 1931, the number of cattle in Russia fell by one-third!
- **Liquidation of Kulaks as a Class:** Millions of prosperous peasants were classified as Kulaks, forcibly dispossessed, deported to Siberian labour camps (Gulags), or executed.

- **The Dual Revolution Trap:**
  * *February Revolution (March 1917):* Overthrew **Tsar Nicholas II** and ended Romanov autocracy.
  * *October Revolution (November 1917):* Overthrew the **Provisional Government** (Kerensky) and established Soviet Bolshevik power.

- **Liberals vs. Radicals on Voting Rights:** Liberals opposed universal suffrage and excluded women; Radicals supported universal suffrage and women's voting rights.
- **Kulaks & Kolkhoz Terminology:**
  * *Kulak:* Prosperous, well-to-do peasant owning land/hiring labor.
  * *Kolkhoz:* Collective farm where peasants worked jointly.
  * *Mir:* Traditional village commune that redivided land periodically before the revolution.

### 2. Global Impact & Indian Resonance (NCERT Explicit Highlight)
- **The Comintern (Communist International):** Formed in 1919 by Bolsheviks as an international union of pro-Bolshevik socialist parties; attracted delegates across Asia and Latin America.
- **M.N. Roy:** Indian revolutionary who played a founding role in the Mexican Communist Party and the Communist Party of India (Tashkent, 1920), and served on the executive presidium of the Comintern.
- **Rabindranath Tagore:** Visited Soviet Russia in 1930; wrote *Letters from Russia*, marveling at mass literacy campaigns while cautioning against intellectual totalitarianism and lack of individual freedom.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru:** Deeply impressed by Soviet Five-Year Plans during his 1927 visit, inspiring the post-1947 Indian Planning Commission model.
