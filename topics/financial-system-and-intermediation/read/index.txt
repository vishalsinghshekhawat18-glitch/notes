12:T537,Information asymmetry is the condition where one party to a financial transaction possesses superior information compared to the other. In credit markets, this generates two distinct friction types:

### 1. Adverse Selection (Ex-Ante / Before the Loan)
- **The Problem**: Borrowers with high-risk, speculative projects are the most eager to seek bank loans at high interest rates because they capture massive upside if they succeed, while the bank bears the default loss if they fail.
- **Market Outcome**: If lenders cannot distinguish creditworthy borrowers from reckless borrowers, raising interest rates drives honest borrowers out of the market, leaving only lemons/high-risk borrowers (credit rationing).
- **Intermediary Solution**: Banks conduct rigorous credit screening, financial statement audits, and collateral pledging.

### 2. Moral Hazard (Ex-Post / After the Loan)
- **The Problem**: Once the loan is disbursed into the borrower's bank account, the borrower has an incentive to undertake high-risk gambles or divert funds into speculative ventures rather than the agreed productive purpose.
- **Intermediary Solution**: Banks perform **Delegated Monitoring** (Douglas Diamond, 1984)—tracking borrower bank statements, placing restrictive covenants in loan contracts, and staging loan disbursements across milestones.13:T57d,Commercial banks and financial intermediaries create macroeconomic liquidity through three structural transformations:

```
                      FINANCIAL TRANSFORMATION PIPELINE
                                     │
     ┌───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                               ▼                               ▼
MATURITY TRANSFORMATION          LIQUIDITY TRANSFORMATION          RISK TRANSFORMATION
• Deposits: Short-Term (1-3 yrs) • Deposits: Liquid (Instant UPI)  • Single loan: High default risk
• Loans: Long-Term (10-20 yrs)   • Loans: Illiquid (Factory/Home)  • Portfolio: Diversified risk pooling
• Result: Long-term capex funded • Result: Elastic spending power  • Result: Reduced systemic variance
```

### Structural Vulnerability of Intermediation (The Diamond-Dybvig Model)
Because banks perform maturity and liquidity transformation (holding illiquid long-term assets against liquid demand liabilities), they are inherently susceptible to **coordination failure and self-fulfilling Bank Runs**. If depositors collectively fear that other depositors are withdrawing funds, rushing to withdraw cash becomes rational, triggering bank insolvency even for a fundamentally solvent institution.14:T4f7,Macroeconomies differ in their structural reliance on banks vs capital markets:

| Dimension | Bank-Based System (e.g., Germany, Japan, India historically) | Market-Based System (e.g., United States, United Kingdom) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Capital Conduit** | Commercial banks and institutional credit | Stock exchanges and corporate bond markets |
| **Risk Allocation** | Concentrated on bank balance sheets | Dispersed across broad investor base |
| **Corporate Governance** | Close relationship banking (monitoring by lead banks) | Market for corporate control (takeovers, shareholder activism) |
| **Information Flow** | Private/proprietary information gathered by bank | Public disclosure and price discovery on exchanges |
| **SME Access** | Superior for small, unrated MSMEs | Favors large, rated corporate issuers |
| **Crisis Vulnerability** | Systemic banking crisis halts total credit flow | Capital market liquidity freezes (mitigated by shadow banking) |

*The Indian Transition*: India historically possessed an overwhelmingly bank-dependent financial architecture. Over the last two decades, SEBI regulatory deepening, mutual fund penetration (SIPs), and corporate bond market reforms have created a balanced **hybrid financial architecture**.15:T5dd,The financial system is bifurcated along maturity and issuance dimensions:

```

```

### Primary vs Secondary Market Interdependence
- **Primary Market (Capital Formation)**: Funds flow directly from investors to the issuing company to create physical capital (factories, infrastructure).
- **Secondary Market (Liquidity Enabler)**: No new capital is raised by the company when shares trade on NSE/BSE. However, without a vibrant secondary market, investors would refuse to purchase shares in the primary market because their capital would remain permanently locked
