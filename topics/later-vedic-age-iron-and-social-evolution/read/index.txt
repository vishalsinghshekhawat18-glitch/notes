14:T9dd,The Later Vedic period (c. 1000–600 BCE) is reconstructed from the Sama, Yajur, and Atharva Vedas, the Brahmanas, and the archaeology of **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)**:

### 1. The Eastward Shift & Composite Kingdoms:
| Realm / Region | Composite Tribes | Capital Cities | Textual & Archaeological Landmark |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kuru Realm** | Merger of **Bharata** + **Puru** tribes | **Asandivat**, **Hastinapur**, **Kaushambi** | Formed the premier Vedic kingdom under King **Parikshit** and King **Janamejaya**; celebrated in the Atharvaveda and Mahabharata traditions. |
| **Panchala Realm** | Merger of five smaller Vedic clans (Krivi, Turvasha, Keshin, etc.) | **Ahichchhatra** (Bareilly, UP) & **Kampilya** (Farrukhabad, UP) | Renowned for royal patron-philosophers like King **Pravahana Jaivali**; center of Upanishadic debates. |
| **Kosala Realm** | Eastward expansion along the Sarayu River | **Ayodhya** / **Shravasti** | Emerged as a major agricultural monarchy. |
| **Videha Realm** | Expansion across the **Sadanira (Gandak) River** | **Mithila** | King **Janaka**’s court hosted the greatest philosophical debates (featuring Sage Yajnavalkya, Gargi, and Maitreyi). |

### 2. Painted Grey Ware (PGW) Ceramic Culture:
- **Material Markers:** Wheel-turned, fine-grained, high-temperature reduction-fired grey fabric with smooth surfaces painted in black with simple geometric designs (swastikas, concentric circles, sigmas, floral dots).
- **Primary Excavated Sites:** **Hastinapur** (Meerut), **Atranjikhera** (Etah), **Ahichchhatra** (Bareilly), **Noh** (Bharatpur, Rajasthan), **Jakhera** (UP), **Bhagwanpura** (Kurukshetra, Haryana — where PGW overlaps with Late Harappan).
- **Life in PGW Settlements:** Mud-brick and wattle-and-daub houses, circular hearths, domestic horse breeding, terracotta discs, and iron toolkits.

### 3. Iron Technology (*Syama Ayas*):
- **Terminology:** 
  * Early Vedic: *Ayas* = Copper / Bronze.
  * Later Vedic: *Lohita Ayas* = Red metal (Copper); *Syama Ayas* / *Krishna Ayas* = Black metal (**Iron**).
- **Atranjikhera Evidence (c. 1000 BCE):** Excavations by R.C. Gaur yielded the earliest clear stratigraphic evidence of iron weapons (arrowheads, spearheads, daggers) and craft tools (axes, chisels, tongs, smelting slag), followed later by agricultural implements.
- **Jakhera Discovery:** Yielded an early iron socketed ploughshare and iron sickle dating to the late PGW phase (~800–700 BCE), confirming iron's transition into agricultural production.15:T470,The transformation across the Later Vedic horizon followed a multi-step causal chain:

```
[Discovery of Iron Ore Deposits in Aravalli & Vindhyan Outcrops (c. 1000 BCE)]
  --> [Smelting Iron Tools: Heavy Axes & Socketed Hoes/Ploughshares (*Phala*)]
  --> [Massive Deforestation of Gangetic Monsoon Jungle (*Videgha Mathava Legend*)]
  --> [Deep Tillage of Heavy Gangetic Alluvium -> Transition from Barley to Wet Rice (*Vrihi*)]
  --> [Massive Agrarian Surplus -> Permanent Villages -> Emergence of *Janapadas*]
```

### The Legend of Videgha Mathava (Satapatha Brahmana 1.4.1.14):
The text describes prince **Videgha Mathava** accompanied by his family priest **Gautama Rahugana**. They followed **Agni Vaishvanara** (the sacrificial fire deity), who rolled eastwards from the sacred Sarasvati, burning all forests until he reached the **Sadanira (modern Gandak River in Bihar)**. Agni refused to cross the river until Videgha Mathava established a new kingdom on its eastern bank (**Videha**). Historians interpret this myth as an accurate literary memory of the eastward iron-and-fire agricultural colonization of the Gangetic plain.16:T4af,### Critical Examiner Traps:
- **Trap 1 (Ayas vs Syama Ayas):**
  * *Ayas* (Rigveda) = Copper / Bronze.
  * *Syama Ayas* / *Krishna Ayas* (Later Vedic) = **Iron**.
  * *Lohita Ayas* (Later Vedic) = Copper.
- **Trap 2 (Ceramic Association):**
  * Early Vedic = Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) / Black and Red Ware (BRW) overlap.
  * Later Vedic = **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)**.
  * Second Urbanisation / Maurya = **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)**.
  Do NOT mix PGW (Later Vedic) with NBPW (Mauryan/Second Urbanisation)!
- **Trap 3 (The Sadanira River):** The ancient river **Sadanira** mentioned in the *Satapatha Brahmana* is identified by historians as the **Gandak River** (marking the boundary between Kosala and Videha), NOT the Sarayu or Son.
- **Trap 4 (Hastinapur Flood):** Excavations by B.B. Lal at **Hastinapur** revealed that a massive Ganga flood destroyed the PGW capital during the reign of King **Nichakshu**, forcing the Kurus to shift their capital to **Kaushambi** (matching Puranic records).
- **Trap 5 (Rice Terminology):** In Later Vedic texts, **Vrihi** refers to **Rice** (the staple of the Gangetic plain), **Godhuma** refers to **Wheat**, and **Yava** refers to **Barley**.17:T445,Imagine the dramatic shift from a humble tribal cattle chief (*Gopati*) who sat around campfires with his kinsmen in the Early Vedic age, to an imperial monarch (**Samrat**) of the Later Vedic era.

As agriculture generated massive food surpluses, kings were no longer satisfied with voluntary gifts. They established **hereditary dynasties**, claimed descent from the gods, collected **compulsory taxes**, and surrounded themselves with a formal bureaucratic court of ministers and royal officers called the **12 Ratnins (Jewel-bearers)**.

To legitimize their supreme power over newly conquered lands, kings performed monumental, weeks-long sacrificial spectacles:
- Unleashing a consecrated stallion to conquer the subcontinent (**Ashvamedha**).
- Undergoing divine water consecration (**Rajasuya**).
- Rigging a chariot race so the king would always win and display revitalized royal vigor (**Vajapeya**).

Meanwhile, the old democratic folk assemblies (**Vidatha**, **Samiti**) were silenced or converted into elite aristocratic councils, and women were stripped of their political voice.18:Td43,The state apparatus of the Later Vedic period was formalized through territorial nomenclature, royal officials, and sacrificial rituals:

### 1. Classification of Kingship & Titles (Aitareya Brahmana 8.14):
| Cardinal Direction | Territorial Zone | Royal Title | Performing Sacrificial Rite | Example Kingdom |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **East (Prachi)** | Eastern Gangetic Plain | **Samrat** (*Samrajya*) | Consecrated via *Vajapeya* | Magadha, Anga |
| **West (Pratichi)** | Western Arid / Gujarat | **Svarat** (*Svarajya*) | Consecrated via *Ashvamedha* | Saurashtra, Anarta |
| **North (Udichi)** | Himalayan / Trans-Himalayan | **Virat** (*Vairajya*) | Consecrated via *Aindra Mahabhisheka* | Uttarakuru, Uttaramadra |
| **South (Dakshina)** | Peninsular / Deccan | **Bhoja** (*Bhojya*) | Consecrated via *Sarvamedha* | Satvat clans / Vidarbha |
| **Center (Madhya Desha)** | Kuru-Panchala Realm | **Raja** (*Rajya*) | Consecrated via *Rajasuya* | Kuru, Panchala, Matsya |
| **Universal Sovereign** | Entire Subcontinent | **Ekarat / Sarvabhauma** | Ruler of all known lands (*Chakravartin*) | Mahapadma Nanda (later) |

### 2. The 12 Ratnins (Jewel-Bearers / State Council):
During the *Ratnahavimshi* ceremony of the Rajasuya, the king made personal ritual offerings at the houses of these twelve foundational officers:
1. **Purohita:** Royal chaplain and chief advisor.
2. **Senani:** Commander-in-chief of the military forces.
3. **Mahishi:** Chief queen (represented fertility and royal continuity).
4. **Suta:** Royal charioteer, herald, and court chronicler/bard.
5. **Gramani:** Head of the village; key liaison to rural peasantry and local militia.
6. **Kshatta:** Chamberlain / Master of the royal household.
7. **Sangrahitri:** Treasurer / Superintendent of the royal treasury.
8. **Bhagadugha:** **Collector of taxes** (literally *"one who milks the share"*).
9. **Akshavapa:** Superintendent of gambling / dice games and royal accounts.
10. **Govikartana:** Master of the royal hunts and keeper of cattle/forests.
11. **Palagala:** Royal messenger / close companion of the king.
12. **Vavata:** The favored wife of the king.

### 3. The Three Great State Rituals:
- **1. Rajasuya Yajna (Royal Consecration Ceremony):**
  * Conferred supreme imperial status and divine power on the king.
  * Core rites: **Abhisheka** (ritual sprinkling of the king with waters collected from 17 sacred rivers), the *Ratnahavimshi* visits, and a symbolic ritual game of dice where the king was made victorious.
- **2. Ashvamedha Yajna (Horse Sacrifice for Imperial Sovereignty):**
  * A specially consecrated sacrificial stallion accompanied by 400 armed warriors was set free to wander across neighboring territories for **one full year**.
  * Any king through whose land the horse roamed had to either submit to the emperor or fight. After one year, the stallion was brought back and ritually sacrificed, establishing the king as an undisputed sovereign over the entire traversed realm.
- **3. Vajapeya Yajna (Chariot Race of Rejuvenation):**
  * Literally means *"the drink of strength"*.
  * Featured a ritual chariot race in which **17 chariots** participated, with the king’s chariot engineered to cross the finish line first. Revitalized the king’s physical and mystical prowess, elevating him to the status of a *Samrat*. Involved drinking *Sura* (a fermented beverage).19:T454,The transformation of political power operated through an institutional feedback loop:

```
[Agrarian Surplus & Settled Territorial Janapadas]
  --> [Monopolization of Sacrificial Legitimacy by Brahmanas & Kshatriyas]
  --> [Conversion of Voluntary *Bali* into Compulsory Regular Taxation (*Bhaga*)]
  --> [Creation of Royal Bureaucracy (12 Ratnins) to Extract & Store Surplus]
  --> [Marginalization of Popular Assemblies (Vidatha disappears, Sabha aristocratic)]
```

### The Transition from Voluntary Bali to Compulsory Bhaga:
In the Rigvedic era, *Bali* was a voluntary offering. In the Later Vedic period, taxation became compulsory and regularized:
- **Bhaga:** The king’s legitimate share of agricultural produce, fixed typically at **1/6th (Shadbhaga)** of the harvest, collected by the *Bhagadugha*.
- **Shulka:** Customs duties and tolls on trade and crafts.
- **Visamatta:** The king was described in texts as *Visamatta* ("the devourer of the peasantry/Vaishyas"), reflecting the heavy fiscal burden placed exclusively on the agrarian Vaishya varna to sustain the Brahmanas and Kshatriya state.1a:T442,### Critical Examiner Traps:
- **Trap 1 (Bhagadugha vs Sangrahitri):**
  * **Bhagadugha:** The **Tax Collector** (*"collector of the king’s share"*).
  * **Sangrahitri:** The **Treasurer** (*"keeper of the collected treasury"*).
  Examiners frequently swap their roles.
- **Trap 2 (Vidatha Fate):** The **Vidatha** was NOT reformed; it **completely vanished and ceased to exist** in the Later Vedic period.
- **Trap 3 (Women in Sabha):** Women were **strictly excluded from attending the Sabha** in the Later Vedic age (whereas in the Early Vedic age, women attended as *Sabhavati*).
- **Trap 4 (Vajapeya vs Rajasuya):**
  * **Rajasuya** = Royal consecration ceremony (sprinkling with 17 sacred waters).
  * **Vajapeya** = Chariot race ritual of physical/mystical rejuvenation (involving 17 chariots and drinking *Sura*).
  * **Ashvamedha** = Horse sacrifice roaming for 1 year to claim territory.
- **Trap 5 (Ratnins Count):** The standard canonical count of Ratnins in the *Satapatha Brahmana* is **12**, including non-royal commoners (Gramani, Suta) and royal queens (Mahishi, Vavata).1b:T402,### Architecture of Later Vedic Polity & Administration
- **Territorial Statehood (*Janapadas*):**
  * Directional Sovereignty (Aitareya Brahmana): East (Samrat), West (Svarat), North (Virat), South (Bhoja), Center (Raja), Universal (Ekarat).
- **The 12 Ratnins (State Council):**
  * Purohita (priest), Senani (commander), Mahishi (chief queen), Suta (charioteer), Gramani (village head), Kshatta (chamberlain), Sangrahitri (treasurer), Bhagadugha (tax collector), Akshavapa (dice/accounts), Govikartana, Palagala, Vavata.
- **Grand State Sacrifices:**
  * **Rajasuya:** Consecration, Abhisheka with 17 river waters, Ratnahavimshi.
  * **Ashvamedha:** Consecrated stallion roams for 1 year to assert sovereignty.
  * **Vajapeya:** 17-chariot race engineered for royal victory, drinking of Sura.
- **Decline of Assemblies & Rise of Fiscal Extraction:**
  * Vidatha disappeared; Sabha excluded women and became aristocratic.
  * Bali transformed into compulsory Bhaga (1/6th share). King termed *Visamatta* (eater of peasants).1c:T440,As the Later Vedic period matured (c. 800–600 BCE), society grew rigid, stratified, and suffocated by ritualism.

The fluid occupational mobility of the Early Vedic era hardened into **four strictly hereditary Varnas** based on birth. The priestly Brahmanas asserted total spiritual monopoly, requiring kings and wealthy merchants to sponsor immense, bloody animal sacrifices (*Yajnas*) costing hundreds of cattle and gold coins.

In response to this ritual exploitation and animal slaughter, a profound intellectual revolution erupted across the forest hermitages of northern India: the **Upanishads (Vedanta — the culmination of the Vedas)**.

Sages and philosopher-kings (like King Janaka, Pravahana Jaivali, and Sage Yajnavalkya) declared that outer animal sacrifices were completely useless for spiritual liberation. They taught that true freedom (**Moksha**) comes not from chanting ritual mantras or killing cattle, but from inner experiential realization: realizing that the individual soul (**Atman**) is identical to the supreme, infinite cosmic consciousness (**Brahman**).1d:T133f,The social and philosophical institutions of the Later Vedic era were codified as follows:

### 1. The Four Varnas & Social Stratification:
| Varna | Mythological Origin (Purusha Sukta 10.90) | Prescribed Societal Role (*Dharma*) | Initiation Status (*Dvija*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Brahmana** | Mouth of cosmic Purusha | Performing sacrifices (*Yajana*), conducting rites (*Yaajana*), teaching and studying Vedas, receiving gifts (*Dana*). | **Dvija** (Twice-born); initiated at 8 years with cotton thread. |
| **Kshatriya / Rajanya** | Arms of cosmic Purusha | Warfare, governance, protecting the realm and subjects, dispensing justice, granting gifts. | **Dvija**; initiated at 11 years with hemp thread. |
| **Vaishya** | Thighs of cosmic Purusha | Agriculture, cattle-rearing, artisan trades, and commerce. **The sole tax-paying varna** supporting the state. | **Dvija**; initiated at 12 years with woollen thread. |
| **Shudra** | Feet of cosmic Purusha | Serving the upper three varnas; manual labor and service. | **Non-Dvija**; strictly excluded from *Upanayana* and Vedic recitation; subject to severe social disabilities. |

### 2. The Institution of Gotra & Exogamy:
- **Gotra (Cow-pen):** Originally referred to a place where cattle belonging to the same clan were kept; evolved into an institutional patrilineal descent group tracing lineage to one of the eight primordial sages (*Saptarshi + Agastya*: Vishvamitra, Jamadagni, Bharadvaja, Gautama, Atri, Vashistha, Kashyapa, and Agastya).
- **Gotra Exogamy:** Marriage within the same gotra (*Sagotra*) was strictly prohibited to prevent inbreeding.

### 3. The Four Ashramas (Stages of Human Life):
| Ashrama | Life Stage & Target Age | Core Purpose & Prescribed Duties | Primary Purushartha Pursued |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Brahmacharya** | Student phase (Initiation to ~25 years) | Celibacy, residence at Guru's hermitage (*Gurukula*), disciplined study of Vedas and sciences. | **Dharma** (Moral duty & learning) |
| **2. Grihastha** | Householder phase (~25 to 50 years) | Marriage, raising progeny, earning wealth, supporting the other three ashramas through daily charity and *Pancha Mahayajnas*. | **Artha** (Wealth) & **Kama** (Desire/Family) |
| **3. Vanaprastha** | Hermit / Forest retreat (~50 to 75 years) | Gradual withdrawal from household duties, retirement to the forest, meditation, detachment. | **Dharma** & Preparation for Moksha |
| **4. Sannyasa** | Total Renunciation (~75+ years) | Complete ascetic renunciation of all worldly attachments, wandering monkhood, seeking union with Brahman. | **Moksha** (Spiritual liberation) |
*(Textual Milestone: The **Jabala Upanishad** is the first text to mention all 4 Ashramas together; the earlier Chandogya Upanishad mentioned only 3).*

### 4. Religious Transition to the Trinity & Upanishadic Maxims:
- **Decline of Early Gods:** Indra, Agni, and Varuna lost their pre-eminence.
- **Rise of the Later Vedic Trinity:**
  * **Prajapati (Brahma):** The supreme Creator deity.
  * **Rudra:** The fierce god of beasts and storms (proto-Shiva).
  * **Vishnu:** The Preserver and protector of cosmic righteousness.
  * **Pushan:** Formerly the protector of cattle, relegated to the deity of Shudras.
- **The Upanishadic Philosophical Revolution (Vedanta):**
  * Number of Principal Upanishads: 108 total, with **11 Principal (Mukhya) Upanishads** commented on by Adi Shankara.
  * **Core Teaching:** Rejection of ritual animal slaughter in favor of *Jnana* (knowledge), *Samsara* (transmigration of souls), *Karma* (action and retribution), and *Moksha* (liberation).

| Upanishad | Celebrated Philosophical Maxim (*Mahavakya*) / Story | Core Philosophical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chandogya Upanishad** | *"Tat Tvam Asi"* (*"That Thou Art"*) | Proclaimed by Sage Uddalaka Aruni to his son Shvetaketu: the individual soul is identical to the universal divine reality. |
| **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** | *"Aham Brahmasmi"* (*"I am Brahman"*) & Dialogue of Yajnavalkya-Gargi-Maitreyi | The ultimate reality resides within; philosophical debates at King Janaka’s court. |
| **Mundaka Upanishad** | *"Satyameva Jayate"* (*"Truth Alone Triumphs"*) & Condemnation of sacrifices as "leaky boats" | National motto of India; sharp critique of ritual animal sacrifices. |
