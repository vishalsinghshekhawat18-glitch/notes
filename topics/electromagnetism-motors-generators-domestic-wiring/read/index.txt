15:T494,### Core Rules of Magnetic Field Lines ($B$):
1. **Closed Continuous Loops**:
   - **Outside Magnet**: Directed from **North ($N$) $\to$ South ($S$)**.
   - **Inside Magnet**: Directed from **South ($S$) $\to$ North ($N$)**.
2. **Tangent Gives Field Direction**: The tangent drawn at any point on a field line indicates the direction of magnetic field vector $\vec{B}$.
3. **The Non-Intersection Rule**: **Two magnetic field lines NEVER intersect**. If they intersected, a compass placed at that junction would point in two different directions at once, which is physically impossible!
4. **Relative Density**: Crowded field lines indicate a **strong magnetic field** (dense at poles); sparse lines indicate a weak field.

### Solenoid: The Artificial Bar Magnet:
- A solenoid is a tightly wound helical coil of insulated copper wire.
- It produces a magnetic field with a distinct North pole at one end and South pole at the other.
- **Inside the Solenoid**: Magnetic field lines are **straight, parallel, and equidistant**, proving the **magnetic field INSIDE a solenoid is strictly UNIFORM and constant**:
  $$\mathbf{B = \mu_0 n I = \mu_0 \left(\frac{N}{L}\right) I}$$16:T48e,### Comparison of Magnet Types

$$\mathbf{F = B \cdot I \cdot L \cdot \sin\theta}$$

- $F = \text{Mechanical Force (Newtons, N)}$
- $B = \text{Magnetic Field Strength (Tesla, T)}$
- $I = \text{Electric Current (Amperes, A)}$
- $L = \text{Length of conductor inside magnetic field (metres, m)}$
- $\theta = \text{Angle between Current direction and Magnetic Field lines}$

### Critical Angle Cases:
- **Perpendicular ($\theta = 90^\circ$)**: $\sin 90^\circ = 1 \implies \mathbf{F_{\text{max}} = B I L}$ (**Maximum Force**).
- **Parallel ($\theta = 0^\circ \text{ or } 180^\circ$)**: $\sin 0^\circ = 0 \implies \mathbf{F = 0}$ (**Zero Force**).
