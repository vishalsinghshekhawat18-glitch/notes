16:T459,Let us examine the central bank's balance sheet across both operations:

### 1. Unsterilized Forex Purchase (Liquidity Expands)
1. **Forex Market Action**: RBI buys US \USD 1 Billionillion ($+₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ Net Foreign Assets [NFA]).
2. **Domestic Payment**: RBI credits commercial bank balances with ₹8,400 Crore ($+₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ Bank Reserves).
3. **Balance Sheet Impact**: Reserve Money ($M_0 = NFA + NDA$) expands by ₹8,400 Crore $\\implies$ Broad Money ($M_3$) surges via money multiplier $\\implies$ **Inflationary Risk**.

### 2. Sterilized Forex Purchase (Liquidity Neutralized)
1. **Forex Market Action**: RBI buys US \USD 1 Billionillion ($+₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ NFA).
2. **Sterilization Action**: RBI simultaneously sells ₹8,400 Crore of Government Securities (G-Secs) to banks under Open Market Operations (OMOs) ($-₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ Net Domestic Assets [NDA]).

$$\\Delta M_0 = \\Delta NFA (+8,400) + \\Delta NDA (-8,400) = 0$$
*Result*: Net Reserve Money ($M_0$) remains perfectly constant. Exchange rate appreciation is stopped, but domestic inflation is prevented!17:T566,```

### The Quasi-Fiscal Cost of Sterilization
Sterilization is not free. When the RBI holds US \USD 10 Billionillion of foreign exchange, it invests them in US Treasury Bills earning $\\sim 4\%$. But to sterilize the domestic liquidity, the government/RBI must issue domestic bonds paying $\\sim 7\%$. The **$3\%$ negative interest spread** ($7\% - 4\%$) represents the **Quasi-Fiscal Carrying Cost** borne by the public treasury to maintain exchange rate stability.18:T437,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Can MSS Funds be Spent by Government?**: **NO.** Under the MoU between Ministry of Finance and RBI (2004), cash balances collected through MSS bond issuances are **strictly frozen/impounded in a separate account with the RBI**. The government CANNOT spend MSS money on budget projects.
- **Trap 2: Sterilization Formula**: Sterilization means $\\Delta NFA + \\Delta NDA = 0$. Buying foreign assets ($+NFA$) is sterilized by selling domestic assets ($-NDA$).
- **Trap 3: Defending Currency vs Building Reserves**: Central banks have **infinite capacity to buy dollars** (they can print unlimited domestic currency), but **finite capacity to defend depreciation** (they can only sell dollars until their foreign reserves hit zero).

1. **Wish 1: Perfect Currency Stability** (Keep ₹80 / USD rock-solid forever so businesses have zero exchange risk).
2. **Wish 2: Free Capital Flows** (Let global investors bring billions in and out of Mumbai freely).
3. **Wish 3: Independent Interest Rates** (Let the RBI set interest rates at 6.5% to manage Indian inflation, ignoring the US Fed).

Can India accept all three wishes? **Mathematically impossible.**

Why? Suppose the US Fed cuts rates to 1%, while the RBI keeps rates at 6.5%.
- Global hedge funds borrow in USA at 1% and flood into Mumbai to earn 6.5% (**Free Capital Mobility**).
- This dollar flood puts massive appreciation pressure on the Rupee.
- To keep the exchange rate fixed at ₹80 (**Fixed Exchange Rate**), the RBI must print trillions of Rupees to buy all the dollars.
- This floods the domestic market with cash, collapsing domestic interest rates from 6.5% down to 1%! **Monetary Independence is destroyed!**

You MUST give up one of the three goals. That is the **Impossible Trinity**.1a:T778,The Trilemma is visualized as a triangle where an economy can choose any single side (two vertices), but never all three vertices simultaneously:

### The Three Corner Solutions
1. **Side A (Fixed Rate + Free Capital Mobility = Zero Monetary Autonomy)**:
   - *Examples*: Hong Kong Currency Board, Eurozone member states (Italy/Greece).
   - Domestic interest rates must 100% mirror foreign rates. Central bank cannot cut rates to fight domestic recessions.
2. **Side B (Fixed Rate + Independent Monetary Policy = Closed Capital Account)**:
   - *Examples*: Bretton Woods System (1944–1971), China pre-2005, India pre-1991.
   - Strict capital controls, travel quotas, import bans. Capital cannot freely exit.
3. **Side C (Free Capital Mobility + Independent Monetary Policy = Floating Exchange Rate)**:
   - *Examples*: USA, UK, Eurozone as a whole, Australia.
   - Central bank sets domestic rates freely; exchange rate fluctuates based on market demand/supply.1b:T60c,Emerging market economists (including former RBI Governors Y.V. ### Why India Cannot Move to 100% Free Capital Mobility Overnight
If India opened its capital account completely to short-term foreign debt, sudden shifts in US Federal Reserve interest rates would trigger massive capital flight, overwhelming RBI foreign reserves and destroying domestic monetary autonomy.1c:T450,### Critical Examiner Traps

Thus, the Trilemma morphs into a **Dilemma**: Independent Monetary Policy vs Capital Mobility.
- **Trap 2: Eurozone Member States**: Germany, France, and Italy surrendered their domestic monetary independence when adopting the Euro. The European Central Bank (ECB) sets interest rates for all members.
