RBI Foreign Exchange Intervention: Unsterilized vs Sterilized Operations & Liquidity Management

The balance-sheet and monetary mechanisms of central bank foreign exchange intervention. Dissects how the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) operates in the interbank market: buying foreign currency to prevent Rupee appreciation vs selling foreign currency to defend against depreciation. Distinguishes Unsterilized Intervention (which expands or contracts the domestic monetary base, $M_0$, altering money supply and inflation) from Sterilized Intervention (neutralizing the domestic liquidity impact through Open Market Operations [OMOs], the Market Stabilization Scheme [MSS], or the Standing Deposit Facility [SDF]). Analyzes the quasi-fiscal carrying costs of sterilization.

Intuition & Context

The Liquidity Flood: Why Buying Dollars Injects Rupees into Banks

Imagine global tech investors bring US \USD 10 Billionillion into Mumbai to invest in Indian startups. If left alone, this flood of dollars would cause the Rupee to appreciate violently from ₹84 to ₹75/USD, destroying Indian textile and software exporters.

To protect exporters, the RBI steps in and buys all US \USD 10 Billionillion at ₹84/USD.

Where does the RBI get the Rupees to pay for the dollars? The RBI creates ₹84,000 Crore of fresh Indian Rupees and credits them to commercial bank accounts.

Now, a new problem emerges: Indian banks are suddenly swimming in ₹84,000 Crore of surplus cash! If banks lend this money out, it will fuel an inflationary credit bubble.

To prevent inflation, the RBI must "Sterilize" the cash: it sells ₹84,000 Crore worth of Government Bonds to the banks, sucking the ₹84,000 Crore right back out. The Rupee was protected, the reserves grew by US \USD 10 Billion, and domestic inflation was avoided!

Core Principle

The Mechanics of Unsterilized vs Sterilized Intervention

Let us examine the central bank's balance sheet across both operations:

1. Unsterilized Forex Purchase (Liquidity Expands)

  1. Forex Market Action: RBI buys US \USD 1 Billionillion (+₹8,400 Crore+\text{₹}8,400 \text{ Crore}+₹8,400 Crore Net Foreign Assets [NFA]).
  2. Domestic Payment: RBI credits commercial bank balances with ₹8,400 Crore (+₹8,400 Crore+\text{₹}8,400 \text{ Crore}+₹8,400 Crore Bank Reserves).
  3. Balance Sheet Impact: Reserve Money (M0=NFA+NDAM_0 = NFA + NDAM0​=NFA+NDA) expands by ₹8,400 Crore   ⟹  \implies⟹ Broad Money (M3M_3M3​) surges via money multiplier   ⟹  \implies⟹ Inflationary Risk.

2. Sterilized Forex Purchase (Liquidity Neutralized)

  1. Forex Market Action: RBI buys US \USD 1 Billionillion (+₹8,400 Crore+\text{₹}8,400 \text{ Crore}+₹8,400 Crore NFA).
  2. Sterilization Action: RBI simultaneously sells ₹8,400 Crore of Government Securities (G-Secs) to banks under Open Market Operations (OMOs) (−₹8,400 Crore-\text{₹}8,400 \text{ Crore}−₹8,400 Crore Net Domestic Assets [NDA]).

DeltaM0=DeltaNFA(+8,400)+DeltaNDA(−8,400)=0\Delta M_0 = \Delta NFA (+8,400) + \Delta NDA (-8,400) = 0DeltaM0​=DeltaNFA(+8,400)+DeltaNDA(−8,400)=0
Result: Net Reserve Money (M0M_0M0​) remains perfectly constant. Exchange rate appreciation is stopped, but domestic inflation is prevented!

The Quasi-Fiscal Cost of Sterilization

Sterilization is not free. When the RBI holds US \USD 10 Billionillion of foreign exchange, it invests them in US Treasury Bills earning sim4%\sim 4%sim4%. But to sterilize the domestic liquidity, the government/RBI must issue domestic bonds paying sim7%\sim 7%sim7%. The 3%3%3% negative interest spread (7%−4%7% - 4%7%−4%) represents the Quasi-Fiscal Carrying Cost borne by the public treasury to maintain exchange rate stability.

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Can MSS Funds be Spent by Government?: NO. Under the MoU between Ministry of Finance and RBI (2004), cash balances collected through MSS bond issuances are strictly frozen/impounded in a separate account with the RBI. The government CANNOT spend MSS money on budget projects.
  • Trap 2: Sterilization Formula: Sterilization means DeltaNFA+DeltaNDA=0\Delta NFA + \Delta NDA = 0DeltaNFA+DeltaNDA=0. Buying foreign assets (+NFA+NFA+NFA) is sterilized by selling domestic assets (−NDA-NDA−NDA).
  • Trap 3: Defending Currency vs Building Reserves: Central banks have infinite capacity to buy dollars (they can print unlimited domestic currency), but finite capacity to defend depreciation (they can only sell dollars until their foreign reserves hit zero).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • UPSC APFC, SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Definitions of sterilized intervention, MSS purpose, and impact of RBI dollar buying on money supply.

The Impossible Trinity (Policy Trilemma): Theoretical Geometry & India's Intermediate Open-Economy Strategy

The foundational constraint of open-economy macroeconomics established by the Mundell-Fleming framework. Proves the Impossible Trinity (Policy Trilemma): a nation cannot simultaneously achieve (1) a Fixed Exchange Rate, (2) Free Capital Mobility (Full Capital Account Convertibility), and (3) an Independent Domestic Monetary Policy. Analyzes the three classic corner solutions (Gold Standard/Currency Boards, Floating Inflation Targeting, and Closed Bretton Woods capital controls) and dissects India's calibrated "Middle Ground" / intermediate solution (Managed Float + Calibrated Capital Controls + Flexible Inflation Targeting).

Intuition & Context

The Trilemma Paradox: You Cannot Have All Three Gifts

Imagine a magical macroeconomic genie offers India's Finance Minister three wishes:

  1. Wish 1: Perfect Currency Stability (Keep ₹80 / USD rock-solid forever so businesses have zero exchange risk).
  2. Wish 2: Free Capital Flows (Let global investors bring billions in and out of Mumbai freely).
  3. Wish 3: Independent Interest Rates (Let the RBI set interest rates at 6.5% to manage Indian inflation, ignoring the US Fed).

Can India accept all three wishes? Mathematically impossible.

Why? Suppose the US Fed cuts rates to 1%, while the RBI keeps rates at 6.5%.

  • Global hedge funds borrow in USA at 1% and flood into Mumbai to earn 6.5% (Free Capital Mobility).
  • This dollar flood puts massive appreciation pressure on the Rupee.
  • To keep the exchange rate fixed at ₹80 (Fixed Exchange Rate), the RBI must print trillions of Rupees to buy all the dollars.
  • This floods the domestic market with cash, collapsing domestic interest rates from 6.5% down to 1%! Monetary Independence is destroyed!

You MUST give up one of the three goals. That is the Impossible Trinity.

Core Principle

The Three Corner Solutions

  1. Side A (Fixed Rate + Free Capital Mobility = Zero Monetary Autonomy):
    • Examples: Hong Kong Currency Board, Eurozone member states (Italy/Greece).
    • Domestic interest rates must 100% mirror foreign rates. Central bank cannot cut rates to fight domestic recessions.
  2. Side B (Fixed Rate + Independent Monetary Policy = Closed Capital Account):
    • Examples: Bretton Woods System (1944–1971), China pre-2005, India pre-1991.
    • Strict capital controls, travel quotas, import bans. Capital cannot freely exit.
  3. Side C (Free Capital Mobility + Independent Monetary Policy = Floating Exchange Rate):
    • Examples: USA, UK, Eurozone as a whole, Australia.
    • Central bank sets domestic rates freely; exchange rate fluctuates based on market demand/supply.
Mechanism & Operational Rule

Why India Cannot Move to 100% Free Capital Mobility Overnight

If India opened its capital account completely to short-term foreign debt, sudden shifts in US Federal Reserve interest rates would trigger massive capital flight, overwhelming RBI foreign reserves and destroying domestic monetary autonomy.

Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Hélène Rey's "Dilemma, Not Trilemma" Hypothesis: In modern international finance, economist Hélène Rey argued that due to the Global Financial Cycle driven by US Fed monetary policy, independent monetary policy is impossible whenever capital is fully mobile, even under a floating exchange rate. Thus, the Trilemma morphs into a Dilemma: Independent Monetary Policy vs Capital Mobility.
  • Trap 2: Eurozone Member States: Germany, France, and Italy surrendered their domestic monetary independence when adopting the Euro. The European Central Bank (ECB) sets interest rates for all members.
  • Trap 3: Hong Kong Currency Board: The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) must match every interest rate hike by the US Fed on the exact same day, regardless of whether Hong Kong is in a recession!
