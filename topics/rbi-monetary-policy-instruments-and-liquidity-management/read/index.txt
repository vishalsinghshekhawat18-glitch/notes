11:T40b,The direct reserve instruments differ fundamentally across statutory authority, asset type, and yield:

| Dimension | Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) | Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Statute** | **Section 42(1), RBI Act, 1934** | **Section 24, Banking Regulation Act, 1949** |
| **Applicability** | Scheduled Commercial & Cooperative Banks | All Commercial & Cooperative Banks |
| **Asset Form** | **Cash balances only** | **Cash, Gold, and Unencumbered G-Secs/T-Bills** |
| **Custody Location** | Maintained **with the Reserve Bank of India** | Maintained **by the bank itself** in its own vault/treasury |
| **Interest / Yield** | **Zero Interest** (No interest paid by RBI) | **Earns Coupon / Interest** on G-Secs and T-Bills |
| **Statutory Limits** | **No Floor, No Ceiling** (Abolished in 2006) | **No Floor** (Abolished in 2007), **Max Ceiling 40%** |
| **Primary Objective** | Liquidity control and monetary contraction/expansion | Solvency, bank liquidity, and sovereign borrowing absorption |12:T4db,Both CRR and SLR are calculated as percentages of a bank's **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)**:

$$\text{NDTL} = \text{Demand Liabilities} + \text{Time Liabilities} + \text{Other Demand & Time Liabilities} + \text{Net Inter-Bank Liabilities}$$
Where:
- **Demand Liabilities**: Current deposits, demand portion of savings deposits, unpaid demand drafts, overdue FDs.
- **Time Liabilities**: Fixed deposits (FDs), recurring deposits (RDs), time liabilities portion of savings deposits.
- **Net Inter-Bank Liabilities**: Assets with other banks subtracted from liabilities to other banks (if positive, added to NDTL; if negative, treated as zero).

### Maintenance & Compliance Cycles
- **Reporting Fortnight**: CRR is maintained on an average daily basis over a **14-day reporting fortnight** (starting Saturday and ending second Friday).
- **Daily Minimum Requirement**: Banks must maintain a minimum daily CRR balance (currently **$90\%$ of required CRR**) on every single day of the fortnight, while maintaining $100\%$ on average across the full fortnight.
- **Penalties for Default**: If a bank falls short, RBI levies penal interest: **Bank Rate $+ 3\%$** for the first day of default, and **Bank Rate $+ 5\%$** for subsequent days.13:T752,Following the landmark monetary policy reform on **April 8, 2022**, the RBI restructured the LAF corridor to its modern, symmetric format:

```
                      THE LAF INTEREST RATE CORRIDOR
                                    │
  ▲  MSF Rate = Bank Rate (Repo Rate + 25 bps) ─── CEILING (Emergency Overnight Borrowing)
  │                                                 (Collateral: G-Secs dipped from SLR)
  │
  │  POLICY REPO RATE (Benchmark Policy Anchor) ── CENTER (Target for WACR)
  │                                                 (Collateral: Unencumbered G-Secs)
  │
  ▼  SDF Rate (Repo Rate - 25 bps) ────────────── FLOOR (Uncollateralized Absorption)
                                                    (No G-Sec collateral required)
```

### The Core Instruments
1. **Policy Repo Rate (Center)**:
   - The rate at which RBI injects liquidity against unencumbered government securities (Repo = Repurchase Agreement).
2. **Standing Deposit Facility / SDF (Floor = Repo - 25 bps)**:
   - Operationalized on **April 8, 2022** under **Section 17(1A) of the RBI Act**.
   - Absorbs surplus liquidity from commercial banks **without RBI providing any G-Sec collateral**.
   - Eliminated the collateral constraint that previously limited RBI's liquidity absorption capacity.
3. **Marginal Standing Facility / MSF (Ceiling = Repo + 25 bps)**:
   - Introduced in 2011.
   - Emergency overnight borrowing window for scheduled commercial banks against **dipping into their SLR portfolio** (up to $2\%$ of NDTL) at a penalty rate.
4. **Bank Rate (Section 49, RBI Act 1934)**:
   - The rate at which RBI buys or rediscounts bills of exchange or other commercial papers.
   - Today, it is strictly aligned with the **MSF rate** and serves as the reference rate for determining penal interest on CRR/SLR shortfalls.14:T52b,A comparative analysis of the operational mechanics of each instrument:

| Feature | Policy Repo Rate | Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) | Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) | Fixed Rate Reverse Repo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Function** | Liquidity Injection | Liquidity Absorption | Emergency Injection | Legacy Absorption |
| **Transacting Parties**| RBI $\to$ Scheduled Banks | Banks $\to$ RBI | Scheduled Banks $\to$ RBI | Banks $\to$ RBI |
| **Collateral Requirement**| **Collateralized** (Unencumbered G-Secs) | **Uncollateralized** (Zero collateral) | **Collateralized** (Can dip into SLR quota) | **Collateralized** (RBI gives G-Secs) |
| **Corridor Position** | **Center** (Benchmark) | **Floor** (Repo - 25 bps) | **Ceiling** (Repo + 25 bps) | Sub-floor (discretionary) |
| **Tenure** | Overnight / 14-day | Overnight (discretionary) | Overnight | Overnight |
| **Rate Setting** | Set by **MPC** | Fixed spread to Repo | Fixed spread to Repo | Fixed spread to Repo |

### Impact on Market Rates
- When liquidity is **deficit**, banks borrow at Repo/MSF $\implies$ WACR moves toward the ceiling (MSF).
- When liquidity is **surplus**, banks park funds at SDF $\implies$ WACR moves toward the floor (SDF).
- The ultimate goal of RBI liquidity management is to align **WACR with the Policy Repo Rate**.15:T4fb,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Collateral in SDF vs Reverse Repo**: Reverse Repo requires RBI to transfer government securities as collateral to the depositing bank. **SDF is uncollateralized** (RBI gives NO securities). This was the primary reason SDF was introduced under Section 17(1A) of the RBI Act.
- **Trap 2: Dipping into SLR**: Under standard Repo, banks **cannot** use SLR-locked securities as collateral (must use unencumbered excess G-Secs). Under **MSF**, banks are legally permitted to **dip into their SLR quota** (up to a specified percentage, typically $2\%$ of NDTL).
- **Trap 3: Bank Rate vs Repo Rate**: The Bank Rate requires no collateral (rediscounting), but is currently aligned to the MSF rate and used for statutory penalty computations. The Repo Rate is collateralized and set by the MPC.
- **Trap 4: Corridor Width**: The standard symmetric LAF corridor width is **50 basis points** ($\text{SDF} = \text{Repo} - 25\text{ bps}$; $\text{MSF} = \text{Repo} + 25\text{ bps}$).

```
                     THE FOREX STERILIZATION CYCLE
                                   │
   1. Massive Foreign Capital Inflow (Foreign Capital FDI/FPI enters India)
                                   │
   2. RBI purchases USD to prevent Rupee over-appreciation
                                   │
   3. RBI issues fresh Indian Rupees into commercial banks
      (Side-effect: Massive Domestic Liquidity Overhang & Inflation Risk)
                                   │
   4. STERILIZATION INTERVENTION:
      RBI sells Government Securities / MSS Bonds to absorb the excess Rupees
                                   │
                                   ▼
          DOMESTIC MONEY SUPPLY & EXCHANGE RATE STABILIZED
```

### The Market Stabilization Scheme (MSS - 2004)
- **The Problem**: During 2003–2004, massive foreign capital inflows forced RBI to absorb so many dollars that it began running out of government securities in its own portfolio to sell for sterilization!
- **The Solution**: In April 2004, the Government of India and RBI signed a landmark MoU creating the **Market Stabilization Scheme (MSS)**.
- **How it Works**: The Government of India issues special dated securities/Treasury bills (MSS Bonds) specifically to absorb excess liquidity. The money raised is kept in a separate, sterile **MSS Cash Account at the RBI** and **cannot be used by the government to finance its fiscal deficit**.17:T42b,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Can Government spend MSS funds?**: **NO**. Funds collected under MSS are sterilized in a separate cash account at RBI and **cannot be spent by the government for expenditure or deficit financing** (otherwise the absorbed liquidity would immediately re-enter the economy).
- **Trap 2: OMO vs Repo**: OMO is an **outright sale/purchase** (permanent change in ownership of the security and durable liquidity impact). Repo is a **temporary collateralized loan with a repurchase agreement** (frictional/transitory liquidity impact).
- **Trap 3: Sterilization Purpose**: The purpose of sterilization is to **neutralize the domestic money supply impact of foreign exchange interventions**, not to manipulate tax revenues or commercial bank profits.
