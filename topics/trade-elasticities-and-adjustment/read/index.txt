12:T435,Let the Trade Balance ($TB$) expressed in domestic currency be:
$$TB = P \cdot X - S \cdot P^* \cdot M$$
- $X(S)$: Export volume (depends positively on exchange rate $S$);
- $M(S)$: Import volume (depends negatively on exchange rate $S$);
- $S$: Nominal exchange rate (Direct quote: ₹/USD).

Differentiating $TB$ with respect to $S$ and expressing in terms of price elasticities of demand ($e_x = \frac{dX/X}{dS/S}$ and $e_m = -\frac{dM/M}{dS/S}$):

$$\frac{dTB}{dS} = P^* \cdot M \cdot \left[ |e_x + e_m| - 1 \right]$$

### The Three Elasticity Regimes
1. **$|e_x + e_m| > 1$ (Elastic Demand)**: $\frac{dTB}{dS} > 0 \implies$ **Currency Depreciation Improves Trade Balance** (Marshall-Lerner Holds).
2. **$|e_x + e_m| = 1$ (Unit Elastic)**: $\frac{dTB}{dS} = 0 \implies$ Currency depreciation leaves the trade balance completely unchanged.
3. **$|e_x + e_m| < 1$ (Inelastic Demand / "Elasticity Pessimism")**: $\frac{dTB}{dS} < 0 \implies$ **Currency Depreciation Worsens Trade Balance** (Because price increases on inelastic imports outweigh small volume gains on exports).13:T674,```

### Policy Taxonomy: Expenditure-Switching vs Expenditure-Reducing (Harry Johnson)
| Policy Classification | Policy Instruments | Mechanism | Economic Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Expenditure-Switching** | • Currency Depreciation / Devaluation\n• Import Tariffs\n• Export Subsidies | Changes relative prices ($P_{\\text{import}} / P_{\\text{domestic}}$), inducing buyers to switch demand from foreign goods to domestic goods. | Redirects existing spending toward domestic output without necessarily cutting total national demand. |
| **Expenditure-Reducing** | • Monetary Tightening (Hike Repo Rate)\n• Fiscal Consolidation (Cut Budget Deficit)\n• Increase Direct Taxes | Compresses total domestic aggregate absorption ($A = C + I + G$). | Shrinks total domestic purchasing power, directly suppressing national import demand. |14:T428,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Devaluation Instantly Fixes Trade Deficits**: False! Because of the **J-Curve Effect**, a currency depreciation initially *widens* the trade deficit in the short run (3–6 months) before improving it.
- **Trap 2: When Does Marshall-Lerner Fail?**: If a country exports raw primary commodities with low price elasticity and imports essential crude oil/medicines ($|e_x + e_m| < 1$), devaluation will permanently worsen the trade balance and fuel imported inflation (**Elasticity Pessimism**).
- **Trap 3: Switching vs Reducing**: Raising interest rates is an **Expenditure-Reducing** policy (cuts aggregate absorption); devaluing the currency is an **Expenditure-Switching** policy (switches spending to domestic goods).
