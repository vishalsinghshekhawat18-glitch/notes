Mendelian Genetics: Pea Experiments, Monohybrid (3:1) & Dihybrid (9:3:3:1) Laws

The foundational laws of classical inheritance discovered by Gregor Mendel: 7 garden pea traits, monohybrid phenotypic (3:1) and genotypic (1:2:1) ratios, dihybrid assortment (9:3:3:1), Law of Dominance, Segregation, and Independent Assortment.

Intuition & Context

How an Austrian Monk Breeding Peas Cracked the Code of All Heredity

In 1856, in the quiet garden of an Austrian monastery, a monk named Gregor Mendel crossed pure-breeding giant tall pea plants with tiny dwarf plants. He expected the children to be medium-sized. Instead, every single baby in the F1F_1F1​ generation was 100% giant Tall! Where did the dwarf trait go? When he crossed those tall babies together, the dwarf trait suddenly re-appeared in the grandchildren in a perfect mathematical ratio: 3 Tall to 1 Dwarf (3:13:13:1)! Mendel proved that genes are discrete particles that never blend or dilute.

Core Principle

Mendel's 7 Contrasting Pea Characters & Ratios Matrix

The 7 Contrasting Characters Studied by Mendel in Pisum sativum

Botanical Character Dominant Trait (Expressed in F1F_1F1​) Recessive Trait (Suppressed in F1F_1F1​)
1. Plant Height Tall (TTT) Dwarf / Short (ttt)
2. Seed Shape Round (RRR) Wrinkled (rrr)

5. Pod Shape Inflated / Full (III) Constricted (iii)

7. Flower Position Axial (along stem) Terminal (at tip of stem)

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps: Phenotypic vs Genotypic 3:1 vs 1:2:1 & Pod vs Seed Color

Top 4 Mendelian Genetics Traps:

  1. Phenotypic vs Genotypic Ratio Distinction:
    • Monohybrid PHENOTYPIC Ratio =3:1= \mathbf{3 : 1}=3:1 (Physical appearance).
    • Monohybrid GENOTYPIC Ratio =1:2:1= \mathbf{1 : 2 : 1}=1:2:1 (Genetic allele makeup).
    • Dihybrid PHENOTYPIC Ratio =9:3:3:1= \mathbf{9 : 3 : 3 : 1}=9:3:3:1.
  2. Seed Color vs Pod Color Inversion:
    • Seed Color: Yellow is Dominant, Green is Recessive.
    • Pod Color: Green is Dominant, Yellow is Recessive.
    • (A tricky trap: candidates often confuse seed color dominance with pod color dominance!)
  3. Law of Segregation has NO Exceptions: The Law of Segregation is universally true in all diploid sexually reproducing organisms because homologous chromosomes must segregate in Meiosis I.
  4. Father of Genetics: Gregor Johann Mendel (Austrian Augustinian monk).

Post-Mendelian Non-Mendelian Genetics Architecture: 1) Incomplete Dominance: Heterozygote exhibits intermediate blended phenotype (Snapdragon / Antirrhinum majus: Red RR × White rr → 100% Pink Rr, F₂ ratio 1:2:1 for BOTH phenotype and genotype); 2) Codominance: Both alleles express simultaneously without blending (ABO Blood Group: I^A I^B genotype produces Type AB blood); 3) Pleiotropy & Polygenic inheritance.

Molecular Genetics: DNA Double Helix & Chromosomal Sex Determination

The molecular substrate of heredity: Watson and Crick's DNA double helix (A=T, G≡C), the Central Dogma, and chromosomal (XX female vs XY male) versus environmental sex determination mechanisms.

Intuition & Context

Why Science Completely Clears Mothers of Blame for Having a Girl

In patriarchal societies for thousands of years, unscientific traditions unfairly blamed mothers if they gave birth to daughters instead of sons. Modern genetics exposes how absurd this is: Every single human mother can ONLY give an X-chromosome in her egg (100% X). It is the Father's sperm that carries either an X or a Y. If a father's X-sperm wins the race, the baby is a girl (XXXXXX); if his Y-sperm wins, the baby is a boy (XYXYXY). The father's sperm alone determines the baby's sex with an exact 50:50 mathematical flip of a coin!

Environmental vs Chromosomal Sex Determination Spectrum

Comparative Spectrum of Sex Determination:

  1. Human Beings (XX−XYXX-XYXX−XY):
    • Female =XX= XX=XX (Homogametic), Male =XY= XY=XY (Heterogametic)   ⟹  \implies⟹ Father determines sex.
  2. Birds (ZZ−ZWZZ-ZWZZ−ZW):
    • Male =ZZ= ZZ=ZZ (Homogametic), Female =ZW= ZW=ZW (Heterogametic)   ⟹  \implies⟹ Mother determines sex!
  3. Reptiles (Temperature-Dependent):
    • Green Sea Turtles: Hot incubation nests (>31∘C>31^\circ\text{C}>31∘C) hatch 100%100%100% Females; Cool nests (<28∘C<28^\circ\text{C}<28∘C) hatch Males.
    • Crocodiles & Alligators: Intermediate warm temperatures (32−33∘C32-33^\circ\text{C}32−33∘C) produce Males; extreme hot/cold produces Females.

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps: Father Determines Sex & Bird ZW System

Top 4 Sex Determination Traps:

  1. Father's Genetic Responsibility:
    • Question: "Who genetically determines whether a human newborn will be a boy or a girl?"
    • Answer: THE FATHER (Sperm carries either X or Y chromosome).
  2. Bird Sex Determination Inversion:
    • In humans, the male is XYXYXY (heterogametic).
    • In BIRDS, the FEMALE is ZWZWZW (heterogametic) and the MALE is ZZZZZZ (homogametic)!
  3. Hydrogen Bonds in DNA:
    • Adenine and Thymine share 2 Hydrogen bonds (A=TA=TA=T).
    • Guanine and Cytosine share 3 Hydrogen bonds (G≡CG \equiv CG≡C) (DNA with high G−CG-CG−C content has a higher melting temperature).
  4. Chargaff's Rule Formula: In double-stranded DNA, A+GT+C=1\frac{A + G}{T + C} = 1T+CA+G​=1 (Total Purines === Total Pyrimidines).

Evolution & Evidence: Darwin's Natural Selection, Homologous vs Analogous Organs

The scientific mechanisms of evolutionary change: Darwinian natural selection (1859), speciation drivers (genetic drift, isolation), comparative anatomy (Homologous divergent vs Analogous convergent organs), and paleontological fossils (Carbon-14 dating, Archaeopteryx).

Intuition & Context

Why a Human Hand, a Whale Flipper, and a Bat Wing Share the Same 5 Bones

If you strip the skin and flesh off a human arm, a cheetah's front leg, a 20-ton blue whale's swimming flipper, and a bat's flying wing, you discover an astounding geological secret: every single one of them contains the exact same bone blueprint: 1 humerus, 2 forearm bones (radius & ulna), 8 wrist carpals, and 5 fingers! Why would a swimming whale and a flying bat have the exact same bones as your writing hand? Because 350 million years ago350 \text{ million years ago}350 million years ago, we all inherited them from a single common four-legged ancestor (Homologous Organs & Divergent Evolution)!

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps: Homologous vs Analogous Examples & Acquired Traits

Top 4 Evolutionary Traps:

  1. Homologous vs Analogous Plant Examples:
    • Thorns of Bougainvillea and Tendrils of Cucurbita / Passionflower: Both are HOMOLOGOUS (both originate as modified axillary buds).
    • Sweet Potato (modified Root) and Potato (modified Stem): Both store starch   ⟹  \implies⟹ ANALOGOUS!
  2. Bird Wing vs Bat Wing:
    • As forelimbs: They are Homologous (same vertebrate arm bones).
    • As flying wings: They are Analogous (bat wing is skin stretched between fingers; bird wing is feathers on whole arm).
  3. Acquired Traits Cannot Drive Evolution: Piercing ears, building muscular biceps, or amputating a limb do NOT change DNA in sperm/eggs, and are never inherited.
  4. Fossil Age Determination: Calculated using Carbon-14 (14C^{14}C14C) Radioactive Dating (for organic remains <50,000<50,000<50,000 years) or Uranium-Lead dating for ancient rocks.

🏛️ 5-Minute Architecture & Key Revision

Population Genetics & Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium Architecture: 1) Hardy-Weinberg Principle: Allele frequencies remain constant across generations (p² + 2pq + q² = 1) in absence of natural selection, mutation, genetic drift, gene flow, and non-random mating; 2) Founder Effect & Genetic Bottleneck: Extreme reduction in population size alters allele frequencies drastically by chance.
