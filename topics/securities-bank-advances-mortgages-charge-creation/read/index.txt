11:T76d,### Taxonomy of Mortgages under Section 58 of the Transfer of Property Act 1882

| Mortgage Type | Statutory Section | Possession Delivered? | Personal Covenant to Pay? | Legal Remedy upon Default | Key Legal Distinction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Simple Mortgage** | Section 58(b) | **NO** (Retained by mortgagor) | **YES** (Personal covenant express/implied) | **Court Sale of Property** (Decree for sale under Sec 67); no foreclosure | Mortgagor retains possession; must be registered if value $\ge$ ₹100. |
| **2. Mortgage by Conditional Sale** | Section 58(c) | **NO** (Usually ostensible sale) | **NO** personal liability | **Foreclosure** (Sec 67); no right of sale | Ostensible sale with condition to become absolute on default (condition must be in the same deed - Proviso 1929). |
| **3. Usufructuary Mortgage** | Section 58(d) | **YES** (Delivered to mortgagee) | **NO** personal liability | **Retain possession & appropriate rents/profits**; NO sale, NO foreclosure | Mortgagee pays self from rents/profits; mortgagor cannot be sued personally. |
| **4. English Mortgage** | Section 58(e) | **YES** (Constructive/Actual absolute transfer) | **YES** (Personal covenant to repay on a certain date) | **Sale of Property**; right of private sale under Sec 69 in certain cases | Absolute transfer with proviso for re-transfer upon repayment on fixed date. |
| **5. Mortgage by Deposit of Title Deeds (Equitable)** | Section 58(f) | **NO** (Retained by mortgagor) | **YES** (Implied personal liability) | **Sale of Property** (similar to Simple Mortgage) | Created by mere deposit of original title deeds in notified towns. |
| **6. Anomalous Mortgage** | Section 58(g) | As per contract terms | As per contract terms | As per contract terms (combination of two or more forms) | Any mortgage which is not one of the five standard types (e.g. Usufructuary + Simple). |12:T525,```mermaid

- **Section 59 (Execution & Registration)**: Where principal money secured is ₹100 or upwards, a mortgage (other than equitable mortgage) can be effected only by a **registered instrument signed by the mortgagor and attested by at least two witnesses**.
- **Section 60 (Right of Redemption)**: The mortgagor has an absolute statutory right to redeem property (*'Once a mortgage, always a mortgage'*—any clog on redemption is void).
- **Section 67 (Right to Foreclosure or Sale)**: Mortgagee's remedy upon debt becoming due.13:T485,### High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams
1. **'Clog on Redemption' Doctrine (Section 60)**: Any condition in a mortgage deed that permanently prevents or penalizes the borrower from redeeming the property upon paying the debt is a **Clog on Equity of Redemption** and is **strictly void** in law (*Stanley v. Wilde*).
2. **Mortgage by Conditional Sale Proviso (1929 Amendment)**: Under the proviso to Section 58(c), no transaction shall be deemed to be a mortgage by conditional sale unless the condition of re-transfer / ostensible sale is **embodied in the very document which effects or purports to effect the sale** (two separate documents cannot create Sec 58(c) mortgage).
3. **Foreclosure vs Sale Distinction**:
   - **Foreclosure** (barring mortgagor forever from redeeming): Available **ONLY in Mortgage by Conditional Sale and Anomalous Mortgages**.
   - **Sale**: Available in Simple, English, and Equitable Mortgages.
   - **Usufructuary Mortgage**: Has **NEITHER right of sale NOR foreclosure**.
4. **Attestation Requirement (Section 59)**: A registered mortgage deed must be attested by a minimum of **two witnesses** who saw the mortgagor sign.14:T42b,### Architectural Matrix: Section 58 Transfer of Property Act 1882
- **Statutory Definitions & Rights**:
  - **Section 58(a)**: Mortgage, Mortgagor, Mortgagee, Mortgage-money, Mortgage-deed.
  - **Section 58(b)**: Simple Mortgage (Personal covenant + Court sale; No possession).
  - **Section 58(c)**: Mortgage by Conditional Sale (Condition in same deed; Foreclosure remedy).
  - **Section 58(d)**: Usufructuary Mortgage (Possession + Rents/Profits; No personal liability; No sale/foreclosure).
  - **Section 58(e)**: English Mortgage (Absolute transfer + Fixed repayment date + Re-conveyance proviso).
  - **Section 58(f)**: Mortgage by Deposit of Title Deeds (Equitable).
  - **Section 58(g)**: Anomalous Mortgage (Combination / custom).
- **Essential Statutory Rules**:
  - **Section 59**: Registration mandatory (if $\ge$ ₹100) + Minimum 2 Attesting Witnesses.
  - **Section 60**: Right of Redemption ('Once a mortgage, always a mortgage'; clogs strictly void).
  - **Section 67**: Mortgagee's right to Foreclosure (Sec 58c/58g only) or Sale (Sec 58b/58e/58f).15:T898,### The Three Indispensable Ingredients of Section 58(f)
1. **Existence of a Debt**: Present advance, past debt, or future facility.
2. **Physical Deposit of Title Deeds**: Must be **original** documents establishing clear title (e.g. registered Sale Deed, Lease Deed, Patta, Share Certificate of Co-op Society). Certified copies or photocopies are invalid unless originals are proven destroyed.
3. **Intention to Create Security**: Intention (*animus*) is the soul of an equitable mortgage. Handing over deeds for safe custody or legal scrutiny does NOT create a mortgage.

### Comparative Analysis: Memorandum of Entry vs Registered Mortgage Deed

| Feature | Equitable Mortgage (Section 58(f)) | Simple Registered Mortgage (Section 58(b)) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mode of Creation** | Physical deposit of original title deeds in a notified town | Execution of a written mortgage deed |
| **Registration Requirement** | **Not mandatory under Section 17 Registration Act** if recorded as an internal bank memorandum of past deposit (subject to state stamp laws) | **Strictly Mandatory** under Section 59 TP Act / Sec 17 Registration Act |
| **Attestation** | No attestation by witnesses needed | **Minimum 2 Attesting Witnesses mandatory** |
| **Geographical Scope** | Deposit must be in **Notified Town**; property can be **anywhere in India** | Property and registration at local Sub-Registrar jurisdiction |
| **Stamp Duty Impact** | Low / Nominal stamp duty on Memorandum in most states (some states now mandate notice filing) | Full ad valorem mortgage stamp duty (1% to 3%+) |
| **CERSAI Filing** | **Mandatory within 30 days** on CERSAI portal | **Mandatory within 30 days** on CERSAI portal |

### The CERSAI Regulatory Architecture (2026 Baseline)
- **Statutory Mandate**: Established under Chapter IV-A of the **SARFAESI Act 2002** (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interests of India).
- **30-Day Rule**: Banks must register creation/modification/satisfaction of equitable mortgages within **30 days**.
- **Priority Rule (Section 26C/26D SARFAESI)**: Registered charge on CERSAI has statutory priority over un-registered charges and state tax dues.16:T4a9,### High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams
1. **Location of Property vs Location of Deposit Trap**: The deposit of title deeds MUST occur in a **Notified Town** under Section 58(f). However, the **immovable property itself can be located ANYWHERE in India** (even in a remote village with 50 residents).
2. **Photocopy / Certified Copy Trap**: A valid equitable mortgage **CANNOT be created by depositing photocopies** or certified copies of title deeds. If originals are lost, an equitable mortgage can only be created if complete secondary evidence (FIR, public newspaper notice, certified copy with affidavit) establishes the destruction of originals (*Syndicate Bank v. Modern Tiles*).
3. **Notice of Intimation (State Stamp Amendments)**: States like Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Tamil Nadu have amended state laws requiring mandatory electronic filing of a **'Notice of Intimation'** of equitable mortgage within 30 days at the SRO, failing which penal fines apply.
4. **Oral Deposit Validity**: An equitable mortgage is completely valid even if created **purely orally** without a single piece of paper, as long as debt, original deeds, and intention exist (*K.J. Nathan v. S.V. Maruthi Rao*).17:T8b5,### 1. Rule of Priority & The Section 78 Gross Neglect Exception
- **General Rule (Section 48)**: *Qui prior est tempore, potior est jure* (He who is earlier in time has the superior right). The first registered/created mortgage takes precedence over subsequent mortgages.
- **The Exception (Section 78 - Postponement of Prior Mortgage)**: A prior mortgagee loses priority to a subsequent mortgagee if the prior mortgagee was guilty of:
  1. **Fraud**
  2. **Misrepresentation**
  3. **Gross Neglect** (e.g. returning the original title deeds to the borrower, allowing the borrower to create a fraudulent second mortgage by showing clean possession of deeds).

### 2. Doctrine of Marshalling Securities (Section 81)
- **Rule**: If Common Debtor mortgages Property 1 + Property 2 to Creditor A, and subsequently mortgages only Property 1 to Creditor B:
  - Creditor B can insist that Creditor A satisfy its debt first out of **Property 2**.
- **Essential Safeguard**: Marshalling cannot prejudice the rights of the prior mortgagee (Creditor A must not suffer loss) or any subsequent purchaser for value.

### 3. Doctrine of Contribution (Section 82)
- **Rule**: Where several properties are mortgaged together to secure a single common debt, and the ownership of the properties subsequently vests in different persons, each property must contribute to the debt **rateably according to its relative market value** on the date of the mortgage.
- **Formula**: $\text{Contribution of Property A} = \text{Total Debt} \times \frac{\text{Value of Property A}}{\text{Value of Property A} + \text{Value of Property B}}$.

### 4. Doctrine of Subrogation (Section 92)
- **Meaning**: 'Stepping into the shoes of the redeemed creditor'.
- **Legal Subrogation (Sec 92 Para 1)**: Arises by operation of law when a person with an interest (e.g. second mortgagee, co-mortgagor, surety) pays off a prior mortgage.
- **Conventional Subrogation (Sec 92 Para 3)**: Arises by written registered contract when a third-party lender advances money to the mortgagor specifically to pay off a prior mortgage.
- **Full Subrogation Rule**: Subrogation is permitted ONLY when the prior mortgage debt is paid off **in FULL** (partial subrogation is prohibited).18:T402,### High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams
1. **Section 78 Gross Neglect Trap**: If Bank A grants a loan and carelessly leaves the original title deeds with the borrower (or returns them for 'safe custody'), and the borrower uses those deeds to take an equitable mortgage from Bank B, **Bank A loses its priority to Bank B under Section 78** due to gross neglect.
2. **The Mortgagor Cannot Claim Subrogation**: Section 92 explicitly states that the **Mortgagor cannot claim subrogation** by paying their own debt (paying one's own debt merely extinguishes the mortgage; it does not subrogate).
3. **Full Payment Requirement in Subrogation**: A person paying only 80% or 90% of a prior mortgage cannot claim partial subrogation. Subrogation operates **strictly upon full and final discharge** of the prior mortgage.
4. **Marshalling vs Contribution Conflict**: Marshalling (Sec 81) regulates the rights of *competing mortgagees*, while Contribution (Sec 82) regulates the liabilities of *different properties / co-owners* rateably.19:T415,### Architectural Framework: Mortgage Equity Doctrines (TP Act 1882)
- **Rule of Priority (Sections 48 & 78)**:
  - General Rule: Chronological priority (*qui prior est tempore potior est jure*).
  - Section 78 Exception: Postponement of prior mortgagee for Fraud, Misrepresentation, or Gross Neglect.
- **Doctrine of Marshalling (Section 81)**:
  - Beneficiary: Subsequent mortgagee.
  - Mandate: Prior lender must exhaust unencumbered collateral first.
  - Limitation: Must not prejudice prior lender or third-party bona fide purchasers.
- **Doctrine of Contribution (Section 82)**:
  - Beneficiary: Co-owners / Distinct property mortgagors.
  - Mandate: Debt shared rateably in proportion to property values at mortgage date.
- **Doctrine of Subrogation (Section 92)**:
  - Meaning: Stepping into the priority shoes of the paid-off creditor.
  - Types: Legal Subrogation (Sec 91 interested party) & Conventional Subrogation (registered contract).
