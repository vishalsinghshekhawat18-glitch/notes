Nomination, Deceased Claims & Inoperative Accounts

Nomination, Deceased Claims & Inoperative Accounts · Concept 1 of 3

Nomination Facility in Banks: Sections 45ZA to 45ZF Banking Regulation Act

The statutory nomination architecture introduced by the Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 1983 under Sections 45ZA to 45ZF of the Banking Regulation Act 1949 and Banking Companies (Nomination) Rules 1985 (Form DA-1 for making nomination, Form DA-2 for cancellation, Form DA-3 for modification), conferring upon the nominee the statutory right to receive payment and giving the bank a full statutory discharge, while the nominee holds the funds merely as a trustee for the legal heirs as established by the Supreme Court in Sarbati Devi and Ram Chander Talwar.

Intuition & Context

Nominee vs Legal Heir: The Classic Confusion Settled by Law

Suppose a father deposits ₹10 Lakh in a bank account and nominates his eldest son. When the father dies, does the eldest son get to pocket all ₹10 Lakh and leave his mother and sister with nothing?

No! Under the historic Supreme Court rulings in Sarbati Devi and Ram Chander Talwar, a nominee is merely an authorized receiver (a trustee for the estate). The nomination gives the bank a safe, lawful way to release the funds without going through prolonged court succession battles. However, once the nominee receives the money, the funds belong to all legal heirs according to personal succession laws.

Core Principle

Key Statutory Rules:

  1. Single Nominee Rule: In deposit accounts, nomination can be made in favor of only ONE individual person (multiple nominees are prohibited for a single deposit account).
  2. Minor as Nominee: A minor can be appointed as a nominee, provided the depositor appoints an adult guardian to receive funds on the minor's behalf in Form DA-1.
  3. Accounts Ineligible for Nomination: Accounts opened in the name of sole proprietorships (can have nomination as individual), partnerships, trusts, companies, and HUFs cannot have nomination facilities (nomination is strictly for individual human depositors).

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps & Nomination Rules

Critical Examiner Traps:

  1. Multiple Nominee Trap in Deposit Accounts: In a deposit account, only ONE nominee is legally permitted, regardless of whether the account is held solely or jointly. (Multiple nominees are allowed only in Joint Lockers via Form SL-1A).
  2. Will vs Nomination Trap: If a depositor leaves a Will naming 'Person X', but registered 'Person Y' as the nominee with the bank, the bank must pay Person Y (the nominee). Person X must seek remedy from Person Y in civil court. The bank is not bound by an unprobated Will presented after nomination.
  3. Witness Requirement Trap: Form DA-1 requires a witness signature ONLY if the depositor is illiterate (thumb impression). If the depositor is literate and signs normally, no witness is legally required.

  2. Form Architecture:
    • Deposits: Form DA-1 (Creation), Form DA-2 (Cancellation), Form DA-3 (Modification).
    • Safe Custody: Form SC-1, SC-2, SC-3.
    • Lockers: Form SL-1 (Sole), Form SL-1A (Joint), SL-2, SL-3.
  3. Single Nominee Rule: Deposit accounts allow strictly 1 nominee (joint lockers allow up to 2).
  4. Minor Nominee: Appointee adult required to receive funds if depositor dies during nominee's minority.
  5. Sarbati Devi & Ram Chander Talwar Doctrine: Nominee is an authorized receiver for bank discharge; beneficial ownership vests in legal heirs.

Settlement of Deceased Depositor Claims: With/Without Nomination & Succession Documents

The regulatory and statutory procedure for settling credit balances in accounts of deceased depositors, encompassing settlement with Nomination / Survivorship mandates (settled within 15 days of claim without insisting on succession certificates under RBI Master Directions), settlement in accounts without nomination (simplified procedure up to bank board-approved threshold via Indemnity Bond and Letter of Disclaimer vs requiring Succession Certificate, Probate of Will, or Letters of Administration under the Indian Succession Act 1925).

Intuition & Context

Why RBI Mandates Simplified Deceased Settlement

When a family loses their breadwinner, the surviving spouse often faces severe immediate financial hardship. If banks forced every grieving family to hire lawyers and spend 2 years in court getting a Succession Certificate just to withdraw ₹50,000 for groceries, the banking system would be inflicting cruel injustice.

To prevent this, the RBI created strict deceased claim guidelines: If a valid nomination or survivor clause exists, the bank MUST pay within 15 days without demanding court papers. For small accounts without nomination, banks use simplified indemnity bonds.

Core Principle

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps & Mandatory Presidencies

Critical Examiner Traps:

  1. 15-Day Timeline Trap: Under RBI guidelines, the mandatory timeline for settling claims with nomination or survivorship is 15 days from the date of receipt of the complete claim.
  2. Presidency Towns Probate Trap: Under Section 213(2) of the Indian Succession Act 1925, Probate of a Will is mandatory only for Wills made by Hindus/Buddhists/Sikhs/Jains within the territorial limits of the Presidency Towns of Kolkata, Chennai, and Mumbai (or involving immovable property situated therein).
  3. Indemnity Bond on Nomination Trap: A bank CANNOT demand an Indemnity Bond or Surety from a registered Nominee as a condition for payment; demanding an indemnity bond where a valid nomination exists violates RBI Customer Service Directions.

Inoperative Accounts, DEA Fund (Section 26A BR Act) & UDGAM Portal

The regulatory regime for unoperated bank accounts and unclaimed balances in India, covering the classification of Inoperative/Dormant accounts (no customer-induced transactions for over 2 years), the mandatory transfer of balances unclaimed for 10 or more years to the Depositor Education and Awareness Fund (DEA Fund) under Section 26A of the Banking Regulation Act 1949, interest calculation norms, prohibition of penalty charges on dormant accounts, and search/retrieval via the RBI UDGAM portal (Unclaimed Deposits - Gateway to Access inforMation).

Intuition & Context

Why Accounts Go Dormant and What Happens to Forgotten Money

People open bank accounts when changing jobs or moving cities and often forget about a ₹25,000 balance left behind. After 2 years of zero activity, the account is classified as Inoperative / Dormant to prevent fraudsters or rogue bank staff from siphoning money through forged signatures.

If 10 years pass and the owner never shows up, Section 26A of the BR Act mandates that the bank transfer the money to the DEA Fund managed by the RBI. But the depositor never loses their money! At any time—even 30 years later—the depositor or their legal heirs can find the money on the UDGAM portal and claim a full refund with interest.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

Customer-Induced vs Bank-Induced Transactions & UDGAM Architecture

Customer-Induced Transactions (Resets the 2-Year Clock):

  1. Cash deposit or withdrawal at branch/ATM.
  2. Cheque deposit or clearing.
  3. Digital transfer via UPI, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, Net Banking.
  4. Standing instruction executed on behalf of the customer (e.g., SIP, loan EMI, insurance premium).
  5. Dividend warrant credited directly via ECS/NACH.

Bank-Induced Transactions (DO NOT Reset Clock):

  1. Periodic interest credited by bank.
  2. Service charges or SMS alert fees debited by bank.
  3. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) debits.

UDGAM Portal (Unclaimed Deposits - Gateway to Access inforMation):

Launched by the RBI in August 2023, UDGAM allows citizens to search across all public, private, foreign, and cooperative banks for unclaimed deposits using PAN, Mobile Number, Name, and Date of Birth.

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps & 2024 Regulatory Freshness

Critical Examiner Traps:

  1. Penalty Prohibition Trap (2024 Directions): Under the revised 2024 guidelines, banks CANNOT levy any penalty charges for non-maintenance of minimum balance on inoperative accounts. Any rule charging dormancy fees is illegal.
  2. Free Reactivation Trap: Reactivation of an inoperative account must be done completely free of charge upon basic KYC verification (including V-CIP or biometric update).
  3. 10-Year DEA Fund Threshold Trap: The DEA Fund transfer occurs after 10 years of continuous inoperation (or 10 years from maturity for fixed deposits), NOT after 2 years.
  4. Interest Continuation Trap: Fixed deposits transferred to the DEA Fund earn interest at the specific DEA Fund interest rate notified by the RBI from time to time.

Core scoring chapter: Section 26A BR Act, 2-year and 10-year rules, 2024 guidelines.

🏛️ 5-Minute Architecture & Key Revision
  1. Inoperative Classification: 2 years of zero customer-induced activity (bank interest/charges excluded).
  2. 2024 Revised Guidelines: Total ban on penal charges for minimum balance non-maintenance in dormant accounts; free reactivation.
  3. DEA Fund (Section 26A BR Act): Established under Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2012; 10-year unclaimed deposits transferred monthly to RBI.
  4. Claim Refund: Depositor can claim refund at any future date; bank pays with DEA interest and claims reimbursement from RBI.
  5. UDGAM Portal (2023): Centralized multi-bank search gateway launched by RBI.
