Fiscal Federalism Principles: Vertical & Horizontal Imbalances, Divisible Pool & Centrally Sponsored Schemes

The foundational principles, constitutional architecture, and inter-governmental transfer mechanisms of Indian Fiscal Federalism. Analyzes: (1) Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI: the structural gap arising because the Union Government controls the most buoyant tax bases while State Governments bear the primary constitutional responsibilities for social and economic services like health, education, and agriculture), (2) Horizontal Fiscal Imbalance (HFI: fiscal capacity disparities across rich vs poor States), (3) The Divisible Pool under Article 270 (gross central taxes minus cesses, surcharges, and collection costs), and (4) The scheme architecture of Central Sector Schemes (100% Union funded) vs Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS: cost-shared between Centre and States, categorized as Core of Core, Core, and Optional).

Intuition & Context

The Great Indian Family Budget: Who Earns vs Who Spends?

Imagine a joint family:

  • The Father (Central Government) has all the high-paying corporate jobs and investments (earning 60%60%60% of total family income via Income Tax, Corporate Tax, and Customs).
  • The Children (State Governments) have the duty of actually running daily life—buying groceries, paying school tuition, cooking food, taking sick members to the doctor, and policing the house (60%60%60% of total family expenses on Health, Education, Roads, and Police!).

If the Father doesn't share his salary every month, the children will go bankrupt in days! This mismatch is the Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI).

Furthermore, some brothers are rich and prosperous while other brothers are sick and struggling (Horizontal Fiscal Imbalance). The Constitution creates formal mechanisms to transfer money fairly to keep the entire union strong.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS) vs Central Sector Schemes (CS)

How central funds flow to the States:

1. Central Sector Schemes (100% Central Funding)

  • Directly funded and executed by the Union Government on Union List subjects.
  • Examples: PM-KISAN (₹6,000/yr direct income support), Central Sector Crop Insurance, Space research, Urea fertilizer subsidies.

2. Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS - Cost-Shared)

  • Implemented by State Governments on State and Concurrent List subjects, with joint funding:
    • General States: 60% Centre : 40% State
    • NE & Himalayan States (Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, NE): 90% Centre : 10% State
    • UTs without legislature: 100% Centre
  • The 3-Tier CSS Classification (NITI Aayog 2015):
    1. Core of the Core Schemes (Mandatory, first charge on funds: MGNREGA, National Social Assistance Programme / NSAP).
    2. Core Schemes (Major national missions: Jal Jeevan Mission, PM Awas Yojana, Samagra Shiksha, Ayushman Bharat - PMJAY).
    3. Optional Schemes (States can choose to implement or substitute with own schemes).
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Cesses and Surcharges Exclusion: Under Article 271, Cesses (e.g., Health & Education Cess, Road & Infrastructure Cess) and Surcharges are NOT shared with States; they are retained 100% by the Centre. (A major source of Centre-State friction!).
  • Trap 2: CSS Funding Ratios: Normal CSS funding ratio is 60:40 for general states and 90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States.
  • Trap 3: Central Sector vs Centrally Sponsored: Central Sector Schemes are 100% funded by Centre (e.g. PM-KISAN). Centrally Sponsored Schemes are cost-shared with states (e.g. Samagra Shiksha, PMAY).
  • Trap 4: Article 270 vs Article 271: Article 270 creates the Divisible Pool; Article 271 allows the Centre to levy surcharges outside the divisible pool.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & UPSC APFC: Article 270 divisible pool arithmetic, cess/surcharge growth impact on effective devolution, and CSS rationalization.
  • RPSC RAS & NABARD Grade A: 2-mark definitions of VFI vs HFI, CSS sharing formulas (60:40 / 90:10), and Core of the Core schemes (MGNREGA/NSAP).

The Finance Commission (Article 280): Mandate, 15th FC Devolution Formula & Article 275 Grants

The constitutional mandate, institutional design, and horizontal devolution mechanics of the Finance Commission of India established under Article 280 of the Constitution. Analyzes the four constitutional mandates of the Finance Commission (vertical tax sharing, horizontal distribution criteria, principles governing Article 275 Grants-in-Aid, and augmenting Consolidated Funds of States for Panchayats and Municipalities under Articles 243-I and 243-Y). Rigorously examines the 15th Finance Commission (headed by N.K. Singh for 2021-26) recommendations: the 41% vertical devolution share (adjusted from 42% post-bifurcation of J&K) and the 6-criterion horizontal devolution formula (Income Distance 45%, Population 2011 15%, Area 15%, Forest & Ecology 10%, Demographic Performance 12.5%, Tax Effort 2.5%).

Intuition & Context

The Great Federal Balance: How to Share ₹100 Fairly Among 28 States?

Imagine the Central Government collects ₹100 in sharable taxes.

  • Step 1 (Vertical Devolution): The 15th Finance Commission says: "Keep ₹59 for the Union Government, and give ₹41 to the 28 State Governments."
  • Step 2 (Horizontal Devolution): Now, how do you divide that ₹41 among 28 different states?
    • Should you give more money to poorer states (Bihar, UP) because they desperately need schools and hospitals? (Income Distance = 45%).
    • Should you reward rich southern states (Kerala, Tamil Nadu) for successfully controlling their population? (Demographic Performance = 12.5%).
    • Should you compensate mountainous green states (Madhya Pradesh, Arunachal) for preserving dense forests that absorb national carbon? (Forest & Ecology = 10%).

The Finance Commission horizontal formula is the mathematical compromise that balances equity and efficiency!

Core Principle

The 15th Finance Commission Horizontal Devolution Formula (2021–26)

The exact criteria and weights assigned by the 15th Finance Commission (N.K. Singh Commission):

Criterion Weight (%) Rationale / Measurement
Income Distance 45.0% Measures the distance of a State's per capita GSDP from the top state (Goa/Haryana); ensures progressive fiscal equity
Population (2011 Census) 15.0% Reflects the absolute scale of public service delivery needs (1971 census fully dropped)
Area 15.0% Compensates for administrative cost disabilities in geographically large states (e.g., Rajasthan, MP)
Forest and Ecology 10.0% Share of dense and moderately dense forest cover; rewards ecological carbon sequestration
Demographic Performance 12.5% Rewards States that achieved lower Total Fertility Rate (TFR); incentivizes population control
Tax & Fiscal Effort 2.5% Rewards States with higher tax collection efficiency relative to GSDP capacity
TOTAL 100.0% Master allocation formula determining each State's percentage share

Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: 42% vs 41% Devolution: The 14th Finance Commission recommended 42%. The 15th Finance Commission reduced it to 41% (a 1% adjustment to provide funds for the newly formed Union Territories of J&K and Ladakh from the Centre's share).
  • Trap 2: Census Year Used: The 15th Finance Commission used ONLY the 2011 Census data for population, completely eliminating the 1971 Census data (which was used in 14th FC with 17.5% weight).
  • Trap 3: Article 280 vs Article 275: Article 280 establishes the Finance Commission; Article 275 authorizes statutory Grants-in-Aid on the recommendation of the Finance Commission.
  • Trap 4: Nature of FC Recommendations: Finance Commission recommendations are advisory in nature, but by long-standing constitutional convention, the Union Government accepts the tax devolution and grants recommendations in full.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & UPSC APFC: 15th FC 6-criterion weights table, 41% vs 42% justification, PDRD grants, and Article 280(3) mandates.
  • RPSC RAS & SBI/IBPS PO: 15th FC Chairman (N.K. Singh), 41% devolution percentage, 2021-26 period, and 2-mark definitions of Finance Commission.
