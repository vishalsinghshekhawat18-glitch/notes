```

P_w ──────────────e───f───────g───h────────── World Price (P_w)
  │              /                 \
  │             /                   \  Domestic Demand (D)

### Dissection of the Welfare Areas
1. **Consumer Surplus Loss = $-(a + b + c + d)$**: Total loss borne by domestic buyers.
2. **Producer Surplus Gain = $+a$**: Transferred from consumers to domestic producers (Protective Effect).
3. **Government Tariff Revenue = $+c$**: $\text{Imports } (Q_3 - Q_2) \times \text{Tariff } t$. Transferred from consumers to the state treasury.
4. **Production Distortion Loss (Deadweight Loss $b$)**: $\frac{1}{2} \times (Q_2 - Q_1) \times t$. Represents real resources wasted producing goods domestically at a cost higher than the world price.
5. **Consumption Distortion Loss (Deadweight Loss $d$)**: $\frac{1}{2} \times (Q_4 - Q_3) \times t$. Represents the utility lost by consumers forced to curtail their consumption due to artificially inflated prices.

$$\text{Net National Welfare Change} = -(b + d) < 0$$13:T4b1,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Tariffs in Large vs Small Countries**: In a small country, a tariff *always* reduces national welfare (deadweight loss $b + d$). In a **large country** (capable of affecting world prices), a tariff can theoretically improve national welfare if the **Terms of Trade gain (area $e$)** exceeds the deadweight loss (the Optimum Tariff argument).
- **Trap 2: Quota Rents Allocation**: A quota does NOT yield government revenue unless the government auctions licenses. If licenses are given free to importers (First-Come-First-Served or historical quota), the revenue is captured as private **Quota Rent**.
- **Trap 3: Export Subsidies Make Goods Cheaper Domestically**: False! An export subsidy makes the good **more expensive domestically** ($P_d = P_w + s$) because domestic producers will refuse to sell locally unless local buyers match the subsidized export price.

- **UPSC APFC, IIBF DBF & RPSC RAS**: Definitions of specific vs ad valorem tariffs, tariff-quota equivalents, and anti-dumping duties.14:T49d,The Effective Rate of Protection is defined as the percentage increase in value added per unit of output made possible by the tariff structure compared to free trade:

$$ERP = \frac{V' - V}{V}$$
- $V$ = Value added under free trade per unit of output.
- $V'$ = Value added under the tariff structure per unit of output.

### The Corden-Balassa Formula
$$ERP = \frac{t_f - a \cdot t_i}{1 - a}$$
- $t_f$ = Nominal tariff rate on the final good.
- $t_i$ = Nominal tariff rate on imported intermediate inputs.
- $a$ = Ratio of the cost of imported inputs to the price of the final good under free trade ($a = \frac{\text{Input Cost}}{\text{Final Price}}$).

### Three Critical Tariff Structure Scenarios
1. **Cascading Tariffs ($t_i < t_f$) $\implies ERP > t_f$**: Effective protection on domestic value added exceeds the nominal tariff on the finished good.
2. **Uniform Tariffs ($t_i = t_f$) $\implies ERP = t_f$**: Effective protection equals the nominal tariff.
3. **Negative Effective Protection ($t_i > \frac{t_f}{a}$) $\implies ERP < 0$**: High duties on raw materials with low duties on finished products actually tax and penalize domestic manufacturers relative to free trade!15:T61d,```

### Why Infant-Industry Protection Often Fails
In practice, infant industries rarely grow up. Political rent-seeking and regulatory capture cause temporary tariffs to become permanent subsidies, breeding uncompetitive domestic monopolies (e.g., India's Pre-1991 License-Permit Raj). Direct production subsidies are economically superior to tariffs because they do not distort consumer consumption choices.16:T42d,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Nominal vs Effective Protection**: A 10% tariff on finished electronic goods does NOT mean the domestic assembly plant is protected by 10%. If parts are imported duty-free, the ERP can easily exceed 50% to 100%.
- **Trap 2: Negative ERP**: If a country slaps a 30% duty on raw steel but only a 10% duty on finished machinery, domestic machine-makers face a **Negative ERP**—they are penalized compared to foreign competitors.
- **Trap 3: Bastable vs Mill Test**: Mill's test requires the protected industry to eventually stand on its own feet. Bastable's test is stricter: it requires the net present discounted value of future gains to exceed the past deadweight welfare costs incurred during protection.
