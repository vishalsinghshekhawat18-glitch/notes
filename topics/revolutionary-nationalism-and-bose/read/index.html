Revolutionary Nationalism & Subhas Chandra Bose's INA · Concept 1 of 2

Revolutionary Nationalism: First and Second Phases (Anushilan Samiti, Ghadar Party, HRA/HSRA & Chittagong Armoury Raid)

The armed revolutionary stream of the freedom struggle: Phase 1 (1907–1917): Anushilan Samiti, Jugantar, Alipore Bomb Case (1908), Curzon Wyllie assassination by Madan Lal Dhingra, Ghadar Movement (1913, Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, San Francisco, Komagata Maru incident 1914); Phase 2 (1920s–1930s): Hindustan Republican Association (HRA 1924, Ramprasad Bismil, Ashfaqullah Khan, Kakori Train Action 1925), transformed into Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA 1928, Feroz Shah Kotla, Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad), Saunders assassination (1928), Central Legislative Assembly bombing (1929, Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt), Lahore Conspiracy Case, martyrdom of Jatin Das (63-day hunger strike), and Surya Sen's Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930) with Pritilata Waddedar and Kalpana Datta.

Intuition & Context

Beyond Moral Mendicancy: The Genesis and Evolution of the Armed Anti-Colonial Underground

Why did thousands of educated, brilliant young Indians abandon constitutional agitation and pick up the gun and the bomb between 1907 and 1934?

Revolutionary terrorism in modern Indian history was not born out of blind violence; it emerged as a direct structural response to political impasses:

  1. The First Phase (1907–1917): Born from the disillusionment following the collapse of the Swadeshi Movement (1905–1908) and the brutal British crackdown on peaceful protesters. Emulating Russian nihilists, Irish Fenians, and Italian Carbonari, young Bengali and Maharashtrian nationalists sought to assassinate oppressive colonial magistrates to shatter the myth of British invincibility and rouse the slumbering masses.
  2. The Second Phase (1920s–1930s): Born from the crushing betrayal felt by Indian youth when Mahatma Gandhi abruptly withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922 following the Chauri Chaura incident. Refusing to succumb to apathy, revolutionaries like Ramprasad Bismil, Chandrashekhar Azad, and Bhagat Singh regrouped. Crucially, their ideology matured from individual heroic assassinations into scientific socialism, aiming not merely to replace white rulers with brown rulers, but to completely dismantle the colonial-capitalist order and end the "exploitation of man by man".

2. Deep-Dive: Phase 1 Transnational Networks & Trials

  • Alipore Bomb Case (1908):
    • Following the Muzaffarpur bomb thrown by Khudiram Bose (executed at age 18) and Prafulla Chaki (shot himself), the British raided the Manicktolla garden house in Calcutta.
    • Aurobindo Ghosh and his brother Barindra Kumar Ghosh were arrested. Aurobindo was brilliantly defended by Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das) and acquitted, subsequently retiring to Pondicherry for spiritual pursuits.
    • Informer Narendra Goswami was shot dead inside Alipore jail by revolutionaries Satyendranath Bose and Kanailal Dutt.
  • The Ghadar Movement (1913–1915):
    • Founded in 1913 as the Pacific Coast Hindustan Association at San Francisco; published the weekly newspaper The Ghadar (tagline: "Angrezi Raj ka Dushman" / Enemy of British Rule).
    • Leadership: Lala Har Dayal (intellectual backbone), Sohan Singh Bhakna (first president), Kartar Singh Sarabha (martyred at 19), Bhai Parmanand, and Barkatullah.
    • Komagata Maru Incident (1914): Japanese steamship chartered by Baba Gurdit Singh carrying 376 Punjabis was turned back from Vancouver, Canada under discriminatory "continuous journey" laws. On returning to Budge Budge (Calcutta) in Sept 1914, British firing killed 20 passengers, igniting nationwide revolutionary fury.
    • Ghadarites attempted a pan-Indian military uprising scheduled for February 21, 1915, led by Rash Behari Bose and Sachin Sanyal, but were betrayed by traitor Kirpal Singh, leading to the Defence of India Act 1915 and severe executions.

3. Deep-Dive: Phase 2 HRA, HSRA & Chittagong

  • From HRA to HSRA (Kanpur 1924 →\to→ Feroz Shah Kotla 1928):
    • HRA (October 1924, Kanpur): Formed by Sachindranath Sanyal (author of the revolutionary bible Bandi Jiwan), Ramprasad Bismil, and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee. Published the manifesto The Revolutionary (1925), declaring the goal of establishing a "Federal Republic of the United States of India".
    • Kakori Train Action (August 9, 1925): 10 revolutionaries looted the railway treasury cash of 8-Down train near Lucknow. Trials resulted in the hanging of 4 leaders: Ramprasad Bismil (Gorakhpur), Ashfaqullah Khan (Faizabad), Thakur Roshan Singh (Naini/Allahabad), and Rajendra Lahiri (Gonda). Chandrashekhar Azad evaded capture.
    • HSRA (September 9–10, 1928, Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi): Under Bhagat Singh's ideological leadership and Chandrashekhar Azad's military command, the organization added "Socialist" to its title, adopting Marxist class struggle as its doctrine.
  • The Lahore Trials & The Assembly Bomb:
    • Saunders Assassination (Dec 17, 1928): Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Chandrashekhar Azad shot British police officer J.P. Saunders in Lahore to avenge the death of Lala Lajpat Rai, who died following brutal lathicharge blows directed by James A. Scott during the anti-Simon Commission protests.
    • Central Legislative Assembly Bombing (April 8, 1929): Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt dropped two harmless smoke bombs and leaflets ("To Make the Deaf Hear") from the gallery into an empty space in the Delhi Assembly to protest the draconian Public Safety Bill and Trade Disputes Bill. They intentionally surrendered to use the courtroom as a national propaganda platform.
    • Martyrdom of Jatin Das (Sept 13, 1929): Died after a heroic 63-day hunger strike in Lahore Central Jail demanding that revolutionary undertrials be treated as "political prisoners", not common criminals.
    • Execution (March 23, 1931): Bhagat Singh, Shivaram Rajguru, and Sukhdev Thapar were hanged in Lahore Central Jail for the Saunders murder (Lahore Conspiracy Case). Azad was martyred in Alfred Park, Allahabad on February 27, 1931.
  • Surya Sen & The Chittagong Armoury Raid (April 18, 1930):
    • Masterda Surya Sen, a national school teacher, formed the Chittagong branch of the Indian Republican Army.
    • On Good Friday, April 18, 1930, 65 revolutionaries raided the police armoury and auxiliary force armoury, severed telegraph communications, and proclaimed a Provisional Revolutionary Government.
    • Fought the pitched Battle of Jalalabad Hill (April 22, 1930) against British troops.
    • Heroic Women Revolutionaries:
      • Pritilata Waddedar: Led the armed raid on the Pahartali European Club (1932) bearing the sign "Dogs and Indians not allowed"; swallowed potassium cyanide to avoid capture.
      • Kalpana Datta: Fought alongside Surya Sen; captured and sentenced to transportation for life.
      • Bina Das: Shot at Bengal Governor Stanley Jackson at point-blank range during the Calcutta University convocation (Feb 1932).
      • Santi Ghosh & Suniti Chaudhuri: Schoolgirls who shot District Magistrate C.G.B. Stevens dead in Comilla (Dec 1931).
Mechanism & Operational Rule

The Ideological Transformation: From Terrorism to Scientific Socialism

The Intellectual Evolution of Bhagat Singh & The HSRA

The critical historical divide between Phase 1 and Phase 2 lies in their ideological mechanics:

  1. Rejection of Individual Terrorism:

    • In his seminal tract "Why I am an Atheist" (written in Lahore Central Jail in 1930) and his letters to young comrades, Bhagat Singh unequivocally renounced individual assassinations ("terrorism is a phase, a necessary inevitable phase, but not the end").
    • He emphasized that revolution must be conducted by the masses—the peasants in fields and laborers in factories—not by elite individual pistol-wielders.

  2. The Definition of Revolution:

    • In the court statement drafted by Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt during the Assembly Bomb trial:

    Revolution≠Cult of the Bomb  ⟹  Revolution=Total overthrow of Capitalism & Imperialism\text{Revolution} \neq \text{Cult of the Bomb} \quad \implies \quad \text{Revolution} = \text{Total overthrow of Capitalism &amp; Imperialism}Revolution=Cult of the Bomb⟹Revolution=Total overthrow of Capitalism & Imperialism

    • Revolution meant replacing the predatory capitalist structure with a socialist republic governed by the working class.

  3. The Philosophy of the Bomb (1930):

    • Written by Bhagwati Charan Vohra (in consultation with Azad and Bhagat Singh) as a direct ideological rebuttal to Mahatma Gandhi's critical article "The Cult of the Bomb" (written after the HSRA attempted to blow up Viceroy Lord Irwin's special train in Dec 1929).
    • Vohra argued that while Gandhi preached non-violence from moral dogma, the state itself ruled through unmitigated physical terror, making armed resistance a legitimate act of defensive self-liberation.

Exam Application & Traps

High-Frequency Traps, Chronological Sequences & Examiner Pitfalls

⚠️ Top 5 High-Frequency Traps for UPSC & State PCS

  1. The Central Assembly Bombing Trap:

    • Trap: Assuming Sukhdev or Rajguru was with Bhagat Singh during the Central Assembly bombing.
    • Fact: Only Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw the bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly on April 8, 1929.
    • Crucial Legal Fact: Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev were executed on March 23, 1931 for the Lahore Conspiracy Case (the assassination of Saunders), NOT for the Assembly bombing (for which they were sentenced to transportation for life).

  2. HRA vs HSRA Confusion:

    • HRA (1924, Kanpur): Founded by Sachindranath Sanyal, Ramprasad Bismil, Jogesh Chatterjee.
    • HSRA (1928, Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi): Reorganized under Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad; added the word "Socialist".

  3. Kakori Train Action Execution Locations (MPSC/UPPSC/BPSC Favourite!):

    • Ramprasad Bismil: Hanged in Gorakhpur Jail.
    • Ashfaqullah Khan: Hanged in Faizabad Jail (first Muslim revolutionary to be hanged in a conspiracy trial).
    • Thakur Roshan Singh: Hanged in Naini (Allahabad) Jail.
    • Rajendra Lahiri: Hanged in Gonda Jail (hanged 2 days ahead of schedule on Dec 17, 1927).

  4. Jatin Das Hunger Strike Geography:

    • Trap: Claiming Jatin Das died in the Cellular Jail (Kalapani) in the Andamans.
    • Fact: Jatin Das died on September 13, 1929 after a 63-day hunger strike in Lahore Central Jail. (Mahavir Singh and Mohan Kishore Namadas died in the Andamans during a hunger strike in 1933 due to forced feeding).

Revolutionary Nationalism & Subhas Chandra Bose's INA · Concept 2 of 2

Subhas Chandra Bose & The Indian National Army: Forward Bloc, The Great Escape, Azad Hind Govt & Red Fort Trials

Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose and the external armed struggle for Indian independence: Resignation from ICS, Haripura (1938) and Tripuri (1939) Congress presidencies, conflict with Gandhi and formation of the Forward Bloc (1939); The Great Escape from house arrest in Calcutta (1941) via Kabul to Berlin (Free India Centre / Legion); arrival in Southeast Asia via German-Japanese submarine rendezvous (1943); reorganization of the Indian National Army (INA / Azad Hind Fauj, originally founded by Mohan Singh with Japanese Major Fujiwara); Provisional Government of Free India (*Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind*) formed in Singapore (Oct 21, 1943); Rani of Jhansi Regiment (Captain Lakshmi Sahgal); military campaign in Burma, Imphal and Kohima; and the post-war INA Red Fort Trials (1945, defence by Bhulabhai Desai, Nehru, Sapru) sparking national mass mobilization and the Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny (Feb 1946).

Intuition & Context

The Realpolitik of National Liberation: External Sword and Internal Upheaval

Could non-violent moral persuasion alone compel a ruthlessly entrenched global imperial power to relinquish the "Jewel in the Crown"?

Subhas Chandra Bose believed it could not. Netaji represented the supreme synthesis of uncompromising anti-imperialism and international realpolitik:

  • While Mahatma Gandhi viewed the struggle through the prism of absolute ethical means and moral purification, Bose operated on the classical axiom of foreign policy: "The enemy of my enemy is my friend" and "Britain's difficulty is India's opportunity".
  • Bose recognized that World War II created a unique, non-repeatable geopolitical window. By escaping colonial custody, traveling across continents in submarines, building a provisional government recognized by world powers, and mobilizing an army of Indian prisoners of war and diaspora civilians, Netaji demonstrated that Indians were capable of organizing a modern military force to liberate their own homeland.
  • Even in military defeat, the INA achieved its ultimate strategic objective: by putting the loyalty of the British Indian Armed Forces into question, the INA made the continued British occupation of India logistically and militarily impossible.
Core Principle

The Four Chapters of Netaji's Struggle: From Tripuri to the Red Fort

1. The Congress Years & The Ideological Rift (1938–1939)

  • Haripura Session (1938): Bose elected Congress President unopposed. Pioneered modern economic planning by establishing the National Planning Committee with Jawaharlal Nehru as its Chairman.
  • Tripuri Crisis (1939):
    • Bose contested the presidency for a second term against the wishes of Gandhi, who nominated Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya.
    • Bose defeated Sitaramayya by 1,580 to 1,377 votes. Gandhi publicly declared: "Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat".
    • The Old Guard moved the Govind Ballabh Pant Resolution, mandating the President to appoint the Congress Working Committee (CWC) strictly according to Gandhi's wishes. Gandhi refused to nominate members or cooperate.
    • To avoid crippling factional deadlock, Bose resigned as Congress President in April 1939. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected in his place.
  • Formation of the Forward Bloc (May 3, 1939): Formed by Bose at Makur, Unnao (UP) as a radical leftist faction within the Congress to unite all anti-imperialist progressive forces. Later in August 1939, Bose was disqualified from holding Congress executive offices for 3 years due to his protest against CWC resolutions.

2. The Great Escape & The European Odyssey (1941–1943)

  • The Escape (January 16–17, 1941): Under house arrest at Elgin Road, Calcutta, Bose disguised himself as an insurance agent named "Maulvi Ziauddin" (later as "Orlando Mazzotta").
  • Aided by Kirti Kisan Party operative Bhagat Ram Talwar (code-named "Silver"), Bose trekked through Peshawar to Kabul, and secured an Italian passport to reach Moscow, flying to Berlin in April 1941.
  • Free India Centre & Indian Legion (Germany):
    • Established the Zentrale Freies Indien (Free India Centre) in Berlin and set up the Indian Legion (Indische Legion) of 3,000 Indian POWs captured in North Africa.
    • Founded the Azad Hind Radio broadcasting in seven languages.
    • Here, Indian soldiers first hailed him with the honorific "Netaji" and adopted the universal greeting "Jai Hind" (coined by Zain-ul-Abedin Hasan).
  • The Submarine Rendezvous (Feb–May 1943):
    • Realizing Nazi Germany was bogged down in Russia and incapable of aiding India militarily, Bose undertook an epic 90-day submarine voyage.
    • Travelled on German submarine U-180 around the Cape of Good Hope, rendezvoused in rough seas off Madagascar, and transferred to Japanese submarine I-29, reaching Tokyo in May 1943 to meet Prime Minister Hideki Tojo.

3. The Reorganization of the INA & The Azad Hind Government (1943–1945)

4. The Post-War INA Red Fort Trials & The RIN Mutiny (1945–1946)

  • The Joint Red Fort Court Martial (November 1945):
    • The British colonial government placed three INA officers on joint trial at the Red Fort, Delhi on charges of treason and murder:
      1. Colonel Prem Kumar Sahgal (Hindu)
      2. Colonel Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (Sikh)
      3. Major General Shah Nawaz Khan (Muslim)
    • This joint trial unwittingly created unprecedented communal solidarity across the country under the slogan: "Lal Qile se aayi aawaz: Sahgal, Dhillon, Shahnawaz!".
  • The INA Defence Committee:
    • Formed by the Congress, led by brilliant legal counsel Bhulabhai Desai, supported by Jawaharlal Nehru (who donned his barrister's gown after 25 years), Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru, Kailash Nath Katju, and Asaf Ali.
    • Desai argued that the INA was the regular armed force of a sovereign, recognized provisional government waging a legitimate war of national liberation, and could not be tried under domestic municipal treason laws.
  • The Spark of National Mutiny:
    • Although the court martial found them guilty, Commander-in-Chief Claude Auchinleck was forced by public fury to remit their sentences of transportation for life.
    • The INA agitation directly catalyzed the Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny (Feb 18–23, 1946), when 20,000 ratings on HMIS Talwar and 78 other ships struck against racial discrimination and trials of INA heroes. This proved to British Prime Minister Clement Attlee that the Indian armed forces could no longer be relied upon to suppress an Indian revolt.
Mechanism & Operational Rule

The Diplomatic & Strategic Mechanism of Netaji's Statecraft

The Legitimacy Blueprint of the Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind

Bose understood that armed liberation without sovereign institutional legitimacy would be branded by international law as mere "banditry" or "mercenary rebellion". Therefore, he constructed a complete de jure state structure:

  1. Constitutional Sovereignty:

    • Proclaimed on October 21, 1943 in Singapore, Netaji assumed the roles of Head of State, Prime Minister, Minister of War, and Minister of Foreign Affairs.
    • Established a national bank (Azad Hind Bank in Rangoon, 1944) issuing currency and promissory notes, a civil legal code, postage stamps, and diplomatic missions.

  2. The "Father of the Nation" Address:

    • On July 6, 1944, broadcasting from Azad Hind Radio in Rangoon, Bose addressed Mahatma Gandhi:

    "Father of our Nation! In this holy war for India's liberation, we ask for your blessings and good wishes."

    • This was the very first time in history that Gandhi was addressed as the "Father of the Nation", proving that Netaji's political divergence with Gandhi never degraded into personal bitterness.

  3. Demolishing British Military Hegemony:

    • By convincing 40,000 Indian sepoys to break their oath of loyalty to the British King-Emperor and fight under the Indian national flag, the INA detonated the foundational pillar of the British Raj. When British Intelligence confirmed that native sepoys would refuse to shoot fellow Indians in a post-war mass movement, the British decision to quit India became irreversible.

Spatial & Strategic Dynamics:

  1. The Continental & Oceanic Escape: Netaji's multi-modal journey (train through UP, car to Peshawar, trek across Hindu Kush, train to Moscow/Berlin, and 90-day submarine voyage) linked anti-British resistance to Axis geopolitical rivalry.
  2. The Southeast Asian Base: Mobilizing 2 million Indian diaspora civilians across Malaya, Singapore, and Burma provided the INA with financial independence (Total Mobilization) and recruits (Rani of Jhansi Regiment).
  3. The Imphal Bottleneck: The failure to capture Imphal before the onset of torrential monsoon rains cut off mountain supply lines, forcing a disastrous logistical retreat through the Burmese jungle.
Exam Application & Traps

High-Frequency Traps, Chronological Sequences & Examiner Pitfalls

⚠️ Top 5 High-Frequency Traps for UPSC & State PCS

  1. Founding of the INA Trap:

    • Trap: Claiming Subhas Chandra Bose founded the Indian National Army (INA).
    • Fact: The INA was originally conceived and formed in 1942 by Captain Mohan Singh along with Major Fujiwara and Pritam Singh. Subhas Chandra Bose arrived in Singapore in July 1943 and reorganized the Second INA.

  2. Shaheed Dweep vs Swaraj Dweep Inversion Trap:

    • Trap: Examiners flip the names of Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
    • Fact: Netaji renamed Andaman →\to→ Shaheed Dweep and Nicobar →\to→ Swaraj Dweep. (Mnemonic: A-S / N-S — Andaman = Shaheed, Nicobar = Swaraj).

  3. Tripuri Congress Presidential Succession Trap:

    • Trap: Believing Pattabhi Sitaramayya became President after Bose resigned at Tripuri in 1939.
    • Fact: When Bose resigned in April 1939, Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected Congress President (NOT Sitaramayya!).

  4. The Red Fort Trial Defendants:

    • The famous joint trial of November 1945 involved three officers: Prem Kumar Sahgal, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, and Shah Nawaz Khan. (Captain Rashid Ali was tried separately, sparking the "Rashid Ali Day" protests in Calcutta in Feb 1946).
    • Lead defence counsel was Bhulabhai Desai (NOT Nehru, though Nehru appeared as an assisting counsel).

🏛️ 5-Minute Architecture & Key Revision

Strategic Architecture of Bose's Military Campaign: 1) Institutional Legitimacy: Established the Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind with sovereign attributes (bank, currency, civil administration, diplomatic recognition from 9 Axis states). 2) Psychological Demolition of the Sepoy Myth: Proved Indian soldiers could take up arms against the Crown, undermining the coercive foundation of British imperial control. 3) The Catalyst of 1945–46: Even though militarily repulsed at Imphal/Kohima, the public court-martial of three officers from three major religious communities at the Red Fort transformed the INA from a military defeat into a decisive political victory that broke the British hold on the armed forces.
