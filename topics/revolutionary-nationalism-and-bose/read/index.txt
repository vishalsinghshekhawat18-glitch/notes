12:T52d,Why did thousands of educated, brilliant young Indians abandon constitutional agitation and pick up the gun and the bomb between 1907 and 1934?

Revolutionary terrorism in modern Indian history was not born out of blind violence; it emerged as a direct structural response to political impasses:
1. **The First Phase (1907–1917):** Born from the disillusionment following the collapse of the Swadeshi Movement (1905–1908) and the brutal British crackdown on peaceful protesters. Emulating Russian nihilists, Irish Fenians, and Italian Carbonari, young Bengali and Maharashtrian nationalists sought to assassinate oppressive colonial magistrates to shatter the myth of British invincibility and rouse the slumbering masses.
2. **The Second Phase (1920s–1930s):** Born from the crushing betrayal felt by Indian youth when Mahatma Gandhi abruptly withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922 following the Chauri Chaura incident. Refusing to succumb to apathy, revolutionaries like Ramprasad Bismil, Chandrashekhar Azad, and Bhagat Singh regrouped. Crucially, their ideology matured from individual heroic assassinations into **scientific socialism**, aiming not merely to replace white rulers with brown rulers, but to completely dismantle the colonial-capitalist order and end the "exploitation of man by man".13:T1d82,### 1. ### 2. Deep-Dive: Phase 1 Transnational Networks & Trials
* **Alipore Bomb Case (1908):**
  * Following the Muzaffarpur bomb thrown by **Khudiram Bose** (executed at age 18) and **Prafulla Chaki** (shot himself), the British raided the Manicktolla garden house in Calcutta.
  * **Aurobindo Ghosh** and his brother Barindra Kumar Ghosh were arrested. Aurobindo was brilliantly defended by **Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das)** and acquitted, subsequently retiring to Pondicherry for spiritual pursuits.
  * Informer **Narendra Goswami** was shot dead inside Alipore jail by revolutionaries **Satyendranath Bose** and **Kanailal Dutt**.
* **The Ghadar Movement (1913–1915):**
  * Founded in 1913 as the *Pacific Coast Hindustan Association* at San Francisco; published the weekly newspaper **The Ghadar** (tagline: *"Angrezi Raj ka Dushman"* / Enemy of British Rule).
  * Leadership: **Lala Har Dayal** (intellectual backbone), **Sohan Singh Bhakna** (first president), **Kartar Singh Sarabha** (martyred at 19), Bhai Parmanand, and Barkatullah.
  * **Komagata Maru Incident (1914):** Japanese steamship chartered by **Baba Gurdit Singh** carrying 376 Punjabis was turned back from Vancouver, Canada under discriminatory "continuous journey" laws. On returning to Budge Budge (Calcutta) in Sept 1914, British firing killed 20 passengers, igniting nationwide revolutionary fury.
  * Ghadarites attempted a pan-Indian military uprising scheduled for **February 21, 1915**, led by **Rash Behari Bose** and Sachin Sanyal, but were betrayed by traitor Kirpal Singh, leading to the Defence of India Act 1915 and severe executions.

### 3. Deep-Dive: Phase 2 HRA, HSRA & Chittagong
* **From HRA to HSRA (Kanpur 1924 $\to$ Feroz Shah Kotla 1928):**
  * **HRA (October 1924, Kanpur):** Formed by Sachindranath Sanyal (author of the revolutionary bible *Bandi Jiwan*), Ramprasad Bismil, and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee. Published the manifesto *The Revolutionary* (1925), declaring the goal of establishing a "Federal Republic of the United States of India".
  * **Kakori Train Action (August 9, 1925):** 10 revolutionaries looted the railway treasury cash of 8-Down train near Lucknow. Trials resulted in the hanging of 4 leaders: **Ramprasad Bismil** (Gorakhpur), **Ashfaqullah Khan** (Faizabad), **Thakur Roshan Singh** (Naini/Allahabad), and **Rajendra Lahiri** (Gonda). Chandrashekhar Azad evaded capture.
  * **HSRA (September 9–10, 1928, Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi):** Under Bhagat Singh's ideological leadership and Chandrashekhar Azad's military command, the organization added **"Socialist"** to its title, adopting Marxist class struggle as its doctrine.
* **The Lahore Trials & The Assembly Bomb:**
  * **Saunders Assassination (Dec 17, 1928):** Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Chandrashekhar Azad shot British police officer J.P. Saunders in Lahore to avenge the death of **Lala Lajpat Rai**, who died following brutal lathicharge blows directed by James A. Scott during the anti-Simon Commission protests.
  * **Central Legislative Assembly Bombing (April 8, 1929):** Bhagat Singh and **Batukeshwar Dutt** dropped two harmless smoke bombs and leaflets ("To Make the Deaf Hear") from the gallery into an empty space in the Delhi Assembly to protest the draconian **Public Safety Bill** and **Trade Disputes Bill**. They intentionally surrendered to use the courtroom as a national propaganda platform.
  * **Martyrdom of Jatin Das (Sept 13, 1929):** Died after a heroic **63-day hunger strike** in Lahore Central Jail demanding that revolutionary undertrials be treated as "political prisoners", not common criminals.
  * **Execution (March 23, 1931):** Bhagat Singh, Shivaram Rajguru, and Sukhdev Thapar were hanged in Lahore Central Jail for the Saunders murder (Lahore Conspiracy Case). Azad was martyred in Alfred Park, Allahabad on February 27, 1931.
* **Surya Sen & The Chittagong Armoury Raid (April 18, 1930):**
  * Masterda **Surya Sen**, a national school teacher, formed the Chittagong branch of the **Indian Republican Army**.
  * On Good Friday, April 18, 1930, 65 revolutionaries raided the police armoury and auxiliary force armoury, severed telegraph communications, and proclaimed a Provisional Revolutionary Government.
  * Fought the pitched **Battle of Jalalabad Hill** (April 22, 1930) against British troops.
  * **Heroic Women Revolutionaries:**
    * **Pritilata Waddedar:** Led the armed raid on the Pahartali European Club (1932) bearing the sign *"Dogs and Indians not allowed"*; swallowed potassium cyanide to avoid capture.
    * **Kalpana Datta:** Fought alongside Surya Sen; captured and sentenced to transportation for life.
    * **Bina Das:** Shot at Bengal Governor Stanley Jackson at point-blank range during the Calcutta University convocation (Feb 1932).

The critical historical divide between Phase 1 and Phase 2 lies in their ideological mechanics:

1. **Rejection of Individual Terrorism:**
   * In his seminal tract *"Why I am an Atheist"* (written in Lahore Central Jail in 1930) and his letters to young comrades, Bhagat Singh unequivocally renounced individual assassinations (*"terrorism is a phase, a necessary inevitable phase, but not the end"*).

The Congress Years & The Ideological Rift (1938–1939)
* **Haripura Session (1938):** Bose elected Congress President unopposed. Pioneered modern economic planning by establishing the **National Planning Committee** with **Jawaharlal Nehru** as its Chairman.
* **Tripuri Crisis (1939):**
  * Bose contested the presidency for a second term against the wishes of Gandhi, who nominated **Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya**.
  * Bose defeated Sitaramayya by 1,580 to 1,377 votes. Gandhi publicly declared: *"Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat"*.
  * The Old Guard moved the **Govind Ballabh Pant Resolution**, mandating the President to appoint the Congress Working Committee (CWC) strictly according to Gandhi's wishes. Gandhi refused to nominate members or cooperate.
  * To avoid crippling factional deadlock, Bose **resigned** as Congress President in April 1939. **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected in his place.
* **Formation of the Forward Bloc (May 3, 1939):** Formed by Bose at Makur, Unnao (UP) as a radical leftist faction within the Congress to unite all anti-imperialist progressive forces. Later in August 1939, Bose was disqualified from holding Congress executive offices for 3 years due to his protest against CWC resolutions.

### 2. The Great Escape & The European Odyssey (1941–1943)
* **The Escape (January 16–17, 1941):** Under house arrest at Elgin Road, Calcutta, Bose disguised himself as an insurance agent named **"Maulvi Ziauddin"** (later as "Orlando Mazzotta").
* Aided by Kirti Kisan Party operative **Bhagat Ram Talwar** (code-named "Silver"), Bose trekked through Peshawar to Kabul, and secured an Italian passport to reach Moscow, flying to Berlin in April 1941.
* **Free India Centre & Indian Legion (Germany):**
  * Established the *Zentrale Freies Indien* (Free India Centre) in Berlin and set up the **Indian Legion** (*Indische Legion*) of 3,000 Indian POWs captured in North Africa.
  * Founded the **Azad Hind Radio** broadcasting in seven languages.
  * Here, Indian soldiers first hailed him with the honorific **"Netaji"** and adopted the universal greeting **"Jai Hind"** (coined by Zain-ul-Abedin Hasan).
* **The Submarine Rendezvous (Feb–May 1943):**
  * Realizing Nazi Germany was bogged down in Russia and incapable of aiding India militarily, Bose undertook an epic 90-day submarine voyage.
  * Travelled on German submarine **U-180** around the Cape of Good Hope, rendezvoused in rough seas off Madagascar, and transferred to Japanese submarine **I-29**, reaching Tokyo in May 1943 to meet Prime Minister Hideki Tojo.

### 4. The Post-War INA Red Fort Trials & The RIN Mutiny (1945–1946)
* **The Joint Red Fort Court Martial (November 1945):**
  * The British colonial government placed three INA officers on joint trial at the Red Fort, Delhi on charges of treason and murder:
    1. **Colonel Prem Kumar Sahgal** (Hindu)
    2. **Colonel Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon** (Sikh)
    3. **Major General Shah Nawaz Khan** (Muslim)

* **The INA Defence Committee:**
