14:T436,The institutional framework, market failures, and digital unification reforms of agricultural marketing in India. Dissects the traditional state Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) architecture: established under State APMC Acts to protect farmers from moneylender exploitation, which gradually degenerated into monopsonistic cartels characterized by high entry barriers for private buyers, compounding market fees (mandi fees, commission charges), lack of transparency in open auctions, and farm-to-retail price spreads exceeding 60%. Analyzes structural reform pathways: (1) The Model APLM Act, 2017 (proposing unified single state trading licenses, single-point levy of market fee, and establishment of private wholesale markets), (2) The electronic National Agriculture Market (e-NAM, launched in April 2016 by SFAC): a pan-India electronic trading portal integrating physical APMC mandis via transparent online bidding, scientific assaying/grading, and electronic payments, and (3) Development of Gramin Agricultural Markets (GrAMs) as primary aggregation centers.15:T4ad,A farmer in Nashik, Maharashtra, harvests red onions at a production cost of ₹8 per kg. He takes them to the local APMC mandi:\n1. In the mandi, a closed cartel of 6 licensed traders bids just **₹10 per kg**. The farmer has no choice because state law prohibits him from selling to anyone outside this physical mandi yard.\n2. The trader sells to a district wholesaler for ₹22/kg (+ mandi tax + commission fees).\n3. The wholesaler sells to a Delhi interstate distributor for ₹45/kg (+ transport + loading charges).\n4. The distributor sells to a local retail vegetable vendor for ₹65/kg.\n5. The consumer in Delhi buys the onions for **₹80 per kg**!\n\n**Where did the ₹70 difference go?**\nIt vanished into multiple layers of unstandardized commissions, compounding mandi fees, loading/unloading tolls, and middlemen markups, while the farmer made a meager ₹2 profit!\n\n**e-NAM (Electronic National Agriculture Market)** cuts this knot: The Nashik farmer uploads an assaying report of his onion lot onto the e-NAM portal. A retailer in Delhi bids ₹35/kg directly online. The farmer gets ₹35 (instead of ₹10), and the Delhi consumer pays ₹50 (instead of ₹80)! Both win.16:T695,```\n                      AGRICULTURAL MARKETING REFORM EPOCHS\n                                       │\n     ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐\n     ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼\n1.
