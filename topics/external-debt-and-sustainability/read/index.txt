### Critical Structural Parameters of India's External Debt
1. **Currency Composition**: US Dollar-denominated debt is the largest ($\\sim 54\\% - 56\\%$), followed by Indian Rupee-denominated debt ($\\sim 29\\% - 31\\%$), SDRs ($\\sim 5\\% - 6\\%$), Yen, and Euro.
   - *Advantage of Rupee Debt*: Foreign holding of Indian G-Secs/Bonds is Rupee-denominated; the foreign investor bears the exchange rate risk, shielding India from currency mismatch!
2. **Maturity Profile**: Long-term debt (original maturity $> 1$ year) comprises $\\sim 80\\% - 82\\%$; Short-term debt by original maturity is $\\sim 18\\% - 20\\%$.
3. **Debt-to-GDP Ratio**: India's external debt-to-GDP ratio is $\\sim 18.5\\% - 19.5\\%$, among the lowest across G20 and emerging market peers.15:T46f,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Public Debt vs External Debt**: Examiners often conflate the two. **Public Debt** is total debt owed by the government (domestic + external, $\\sim 82\\%$ of GDP, mostly domestic). **External Debt** is debt owed to non-residents by *everyone* (government + private corporates, $\\sim 19\\%$ of GDP). Over $95\\%$ of India's government debt is domestic, NOT external!
- **Trap 2: Is Rupee Debt Owed to Foreigners External Debt?**: **YES.** Under IMF definition, external debt is determined by the **Residency of the creditor**, not currency. If a foreign fund in London buys Rupee G-Secs, it is part of India's External Debt.
- **Trap 3: Largest Component of External Debt**: Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) form the single largest component ($\\sim 32\\% - 34\\%$), followed by NRI Deposits ($\\sim 23\\% - 24\\%$).
