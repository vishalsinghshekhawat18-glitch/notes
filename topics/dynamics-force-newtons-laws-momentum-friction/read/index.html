Galileo's Principle of Inertia & Newton's First Law of Motion

The foundational law of dynamics stating that a body remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless compelled to change by an unbalanced external force.

Intuition & Context

Why You Lurch Forward When a Bus Brakes

For two thousand years before Galileo, Aristotle taught that "things move only while you push them, and stop when you let go." Galileo discovered that stopping is NOT a natural desire of objects—it is caused by the hidden invisible force of friction. In a frictionless vacuum, a rolled ball rolls forever. When a speeding bus hits the brakes, the bus stops, but your body's upper half has inertia: it keeps moving forward at $60 , \text{km/h}$ until your seatbelt pulls on you.

Core Principle

The Three Types of Inertia & Everyday Manifestations

Classification of Inertia

Type of Inertia Physical Phenomenon Everyday Life Examples
Inertia of Rest Tendency of a stationary body to remain at rest. • Passengers fall backward when a stationary bus starts suddenly.
• Dust particles fall out when a carpet is beaten with a stick.
• Fruits detach and fall when a tree branch is vigorously shaken.
Inertia of Motion Tendency of a moving body to maintain its speed. • Passengers lurch forward when a moving bus brakes suddenly.
• An athlete takes a long running start before a long jump.
• A ball thrown upward in a moving train returns to the thrower's hands.
Inertia of Direction Tendency of a body to maintain its straight-line path. • Passengers lean outward when a car takes a sharp turn.
• Mud flies off tangentially from rotating bicycle tyres (hence mudguards).
• Sparks fly tangentially from a rotating grinding wheel.
Mechanism & Operational Rule

Mass as the Quantitative Measure of Inertia

1. The Mass-Inertia Relationship

Inertia is not a separate physical unit or dimension; it is an inherent property of matter directly proportional to Mass (mmm):
Inertia∝Mass (m)\text{Inertia} \propto \text{Mass } (m)Inertia∝Mass (m)

  • Kicking a football (m=0.45 kgm = 0.45 , \text{kg}m=0.45kg) sends it flying.
  • Kicking a stone of the exact same size (m=10 kgm = 10 , \text{kg}m=10kg) breaks your toe because its inertia is over 20 times greater.

2. Qualitative Definition of Force

Newton's First Law provides the Qualitative Definition of Force:

  • Force is that external agency (push or pull) which changes or tends to change the state of rest or uniform motion of a body.

Linear Momentum, Newton's Second Law (F=ma) & Impulse Dynamics

The quantitative law of dynamics relating applied force to the time rate of change of momentum, and impulse as force integrated over time.

A tiny table tennis ball hit at $50 , \text{km/h}$ bounces off your palm harmlessly, but a heavy leather cricket ball at that same speed can fracture your fingers. Why? Newton's Third Law of Motion & Conservation of Linear Momentum

The law of mutual interaction stating that forces always occur in matched action-reaction pairs acting on different bodies, and the conservation of total momentum in isolated systems.

Intuition & Context

Why You Cannot Pull Yourself Up by Your Own Hair

Forces never exist as single, isolated entities in the universe; they are mutual interactions between two separate objects. You cannot lift yourself into the air by pulling up on your shoelaces because internal forces cancel out. To walk forward, your foot must push the Earth backward (Action), and the Earth pushes your foot forward (Reaction). Every push requires two bodies.

Core Principle

Newton's Third Law & The 'Do Forces Cancel?' Paradox

The Fundamental Rule of Action-Reaction Pairs:

F⃗AB=−F⃗BA\vec{F}{AB} = -\vec{F}{BA}FAB​=−FBA​
(Force exerted by body AAA on body BBB is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force exerted by body BBB on body AAA.)

Why Action and Reaction NEVER Cancel Each Other Out:

  • Two forces cancel out only if they act on the SAME body (like two people pulling a single rope in opposite directions).
  • Action and Reaction act on TWO DIFFERENT bodies:
    1. Foot pushes Ground (Force on Ground).
    2. Ground pushes Foot (Force on Person).
  • Therefore, each body experiences an unbalanced net force and accelerates (a=F/ma = F/ma=F/m).

Friction Mechanics: Microscopic Causes, Static, Limiting, Kinetic & Rolling Friction

The tangential contact force opposing relative motion between surfaces, its microscopic roughness origins, and the hierarchy of friction types.

Intuition & Context

Why It Is Harder to Start a Box Moving Than to Keep It Moving

Even the smoothest polished metal table looks like a jagged mountain range under a microscope. When a heavy box sits stationary on a floor, the microscopic jagged peaks of the box sink deep into the microscopic valleys of the floor, forming microscopic "cold welds." To start moving the box, you must forcefully snap these interlocking welds (Limiting Friction). Once sliding, the peaks merely skim past each other before they have time to lock, which is why pushing a moving box feels noticeably easier.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

Friction as a Necessary Evil & Engineering Controls

1. Laws of Friction

  1. Friction is directly proportional to the Normal Reaction Force (N=mgN = mgN=mg): f∝Nf \propto Nf∝N.
  2. Friction is independent of the apparent area of contact (as long as normal force is constant).
  3. Friction depends entirely on the nature and roughness of the materials in contact (quantified by coefficient μ\muμ).

2. Friction as a "Necessary Evil"

  • Why it is NECESSARY:
    • Walking (without friction, feet slip backward like on wet ice).
    • Writing on paper with a pen/chalk on a board.
    • Vehicle brakes stopping tires.
    • Holding nails in walls and knots in ropes.
  • Why it is an EVIL:
    • Wears out machine parts, shoe soles, and car tires.
    • Dissipates ~20% of engine power as wasted heat energy.

3. Methods of Modifying Friction

  • To Reduce Friction:
    • Lubrication (oils, grease, graphite create thin liquid layer separating peaks).
    • Ball Bearings (converts high sliding friction into low rolling friction).
    • Polishing surfaces / Streamlining shapes.
  • To Increase Friction:
    • Treading vehicle tyres (grooves allow water drainage and grip).
    • Spikes on athletic shoes.
    • Gymnasts applying chalk powder to hands for better grip.
Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps & Common Distractors

Top Friction Traps:

  1. Static Friction is SELF-ADJUSTING: If limiting friction is $50 , \text{N}$ and you push with $10 , \text{N}, the static friction is $10 , \text{N}, NOT $50 , \text{N}. It only reaches $50 , \text{N} when applied force reaches $50 , \text{N}$.
  2. Area of Contact Fallacy: Tilting a brick from its broad face to its narrow side does NOT change the frictional force, because the normal force (mgmgmg) remains unchanged.
  3. Ball Bearings Application: Ball bearings in bicycle hubs or ceiling fans replace sliding friction with rolling friction.

Fluid Friction, Viscous Drag, Terminal Velocity & Aerodynamic Streamlining

The frictional resistance exerted by fluids (liquids and gases) on moving objects, Stokes' law, terminal velocity, and streamlining.

Intuition & Context

Why Raindrops Do Not Kill You Like Bullets

A raindrop falls from clouds $2,000 , \text{metres}$ high. Under pure vacuum gravity (v=2ghv = \sqrt{2gh}v=2gh​), it would hit your head at over $700 , \text{km/h}—fast enough to puncture a skull. But in the real world, raindrops hit your face at a gentle $20 , \text{km/h}. Why? Because as the drop accelerates, air resistance (Drag) shoots up until upward air drag balances downward gravity. At that instant, acceleration stops, and the raindrop coasts down at a safe, constant Terminal Velocity.

Core Principle

Factors Affecting Fluid Drag & Streamlining Architecture

Factors Determining Fluid Drag:

  1. Speed of the Object (vvv): Drag increases dramatically with speed (Fdrag∝vF_{\text{drag}} \propto vFdrag​∝v at low speeds; Fdrag∝v2F_{\text{drag}} \propto v^2Fdrag​∝v2 at high speeds).
  2. Shape of the Object: Blunt, flat surfaces create large turbulent wakes (high drag); streamlined shapes maintain laminar flow (low drag).
  3. Viscosity of the Fluid (η\etaη): Resistance is much higher in honey or oil than in water or air.

The Biomimetic Principle of Streamlining

  • Nature's Blueprint: Birds flying through air and fish swimming through water evolved smooth, teardrop-like tapered profiles.
  • Human Engineering: Airplanes, high-speed bullet trains (Vande Bharat / Shinkansen), sports cars, and submarines are deliberately streamlined to minimize fuel consumption.
