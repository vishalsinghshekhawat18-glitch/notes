12:T457,In 1979, the Indian government decided that being "not poor" meant earning enough money to buy **2,400 calories a day in villages** and **2,100 calories in cities** (the Alagh Committee).

For 30 years, officials simply updated this food basket using inflation indices. But by 2005, a bizarre paradox emerged:
- Millions of Indians whose incomes had clearly risen were actually buying *fewer* coarse grain calories and spending their money on private schooling, mobile recharges, medicines, and packaged milk.
- Under the old calorie formula, these families were officially labeled "falling into poverty" because their calorie intake dropped, even though their real standard of living was improving!

Professor Suresh Tendulkar stepped in and said: *"People do not live on raw calories alone. If a family must spend ₹500 a month on private clinic visits and private school fees because public services failed, that spending is not a luxury—it is an absolute necessity of modern life."*

Tendulkar rebuilt the poverty line from scratch, incorporating health, education, and modern urban consumption patterns.13:T594,| Dimension | Y.K. Alagh (1979) | D.T. Lakdawala (1993) | Suresh Tendulkar (2009) | C. Rangarajan (2014) |

- **Uniform Reference Period (URP)**: Asks households to recall consumption of **all items** over a uniform **30-day** period. *Flaw*: Serious under-reporting of low-frequency purchases (coats, refrigerators) and over-reporting of daily food.
- **Mixed Reference Period (MRP)**: Uses **365-day recall** for 5 non-food items (clothing, footwear, durable goods, education, and institutional medical expenses) and **30-day recall** for all other goods. *Impact*: Captures lumpy annual health and schooling costs.
- **Modified Mixed Reference Period (MMRP)**:
  - **7-Day Recall**: Highly perishable food items (edible oils, eggs, fish, meat, vegetables, fruits, spices).
  - **365-Day Recall**: Clothing, footwear, education, institutional medical care, durables.
  - **30-Day Recall**: All remaining items (grains, fuel, rent, non-institutional medical).

### 2. The Current Institutional Reality (NITI Aayog Approach)
- Since 2014, the Government of India has **not officially notified any new consumption-expenditure poverty line** to replace the Tendulkar 2011-12 baseline.
- In 2021, NITI Aayog formally shifted to the **National Multidimensional Poverty Index (National MPI)** as the official government metric for tracking poverty alleviation, monitoring 12 deprivation indicators from the National Family Health Surveys (NFHS-4 and NFHS-5).15:T5ac,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Tendulkar vs Rangarajan Poverty Lines**: Tendulkar set the 2011-12 poverty line at **₹816 rural / ₹1000 urban** (poverty headcount **21.9%**). Rangarajan set it higher at **₹972 rural / ₹1407 urban** (poverty headcount **29.5%**).
- **Trap 2: Current Official Consumption Poverty Line**: If an exam asks for India's "latest officially accepted consumption poverty line", it is still the **Tendulkar Committee baseline (21.9% for 2011-12)**; the Rangarajan report was never officially notified.
- **Trap 3: Recall Period in Tendulkar**: Tendulkar used **MRP (Mixed Reference Period)**, NOT MMRP (which was introduced by Rangarajan and adopted in recent NSO surveys).

Where did the other 85 Paise go?
- **Ghost Beneficiaries**: Corrupt officials created fake names in paper registers and pocketed their ration grains and cash pensions.
- **Middlemen & Cut Money**: Rural citizens had to pay bribes to village heads and bank clerks just to withdraw their own government payments.
- **Administrative Friction**: Paper checks took 6 months to clear, and cash got lost in transit.

Between 2014 and 2024, India replaced this leaky administrative pipeline with a **Digital Tap (The JAM Trinity)**:
1. **J (Jan Dhan)**: Every poor family got a zero-balance bank account (50+ Crore accounts opened).
2. **A (Aadhaar)**: Biometric identity proved that one physical human equals one unique account (killing ghost registers).
3. **M (Mobile)**: Immediate SMS confirmation when the Treasury in New Delhi deposits cash directly into a village account via National Automated Clearing House (NACH).

Today, when ₹2,000 is sent under PM-KISAN, the full ₹2,000 hits the farmer's account within seconds—with zero middlemen.18:T6e8,| Policy Dimension | Targeted Welfare (Current India Paradigm) | Universal Basic Income (UBI Paradigm) |

### The 3 Pillars of Modern Indian Anti-Poverty Strategy
1. **Direct Income/Cash Transfers**: PM-KISAN (₹6,000/year), Old-Age & Widow Pensions (NSAP), DBT-PAHAL (LPG subsidy).
2. **Basic Asset Creation**: PM Awas Yojana (Pucca houses), PM Ujjwala Yojana (Clean cooking gas), Jal Jeevan Mission (Tap water), Swachh Bharat (Sanitation).
3. **Livelihood & Wage Guarantees**: MGNREGA (100 days statutory wage work), DAY-NRLM (SHG microfinance livelihoods).19:T4e3,### 1. The Mathematics of Targeting Errors
Let $N$ be total population, $P$ be truly poor, and $NP$ be non-poor.
$$\text{Exclusion Error (Type I)} = \frac{\text{Truly Poor Individuals Excluded from Benefit}}{\text{Total Truly Poor Population } (P)} \times 100$$
$$\text{Inclusion Error (Type II)} = \frac{\text{Non-Poor Ineligible Individuals Included in Benefit}}{\text{Total Program Beneficiaries}} \times 100$$
- *The Policy Dilemma*: Tightening eligibility rules (demanding land records, biometric scans, income certificates) drives Inclusion Errors down toward zero, but inevitably causes **Exclusion Errors to spike**, punishing the most illiterate and vulnerable individuals.

### 2. The JAM Trinity Technical Architecture
- **Public Financial Management System (PFMS)**: Centralized cloud database of the Ministry of Finance tracking fund releases.
- **Aadhaar Payment Bridge (APB) / NPCI**: Routes funds using the 12-digit Aadhaar number as a financial address, automatically locating the beneficiary's latest linked bank account.
- **AePS (Aadhaar Enabled Payment System)**: Micro-ATMs operated by Village Business Correspondents (Bank Mitras) allowing rural citizens to withdraw cash using biometric fingerprint scans right at the doorstep.1a:T526,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: PM-KISAN Landholding Ceiling**: Initially, PM-KISAN was restricted to Small and Marginal Farmers (landholding $< 2$ hectares). - **Trap 2: Exclusion vs Inclusion Error**: Type I Error is **Exclusion of the needy** (false negative). Type II Error is **Inclusion of the ineligible** (false positive).
- **Trap 3: Cash vs In-Kind Welfare**: DBT cash transfers do NOT completely replace in-kind food distribution under NFSA; rather, India operates a **hybrid model** (free foodgrains under PMGKAY + direct cash under PM-KISAN/DBT).
