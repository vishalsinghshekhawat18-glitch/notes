12:T520,Imagine an Indian company in Mumbai importing crude oil from an oil firm in Dubai:
- **The Traditional US Dollar Routing**:
  1. The Indian company converts Indian Rupees (₹) into **US Dollars (USD)**, paying a currency conversion fee to a Wall Street bank.
  2. The wire transfer travels to New York through the **Fedwire / SWIFT clearing system**.
  3. If the US government imposes sanctions on the transaction, the funds can be frozen in New York.
  4. The Dubai oil firm receives US Dollars and converts them into **UAE Dirhams (AED)**, paying a second conversion fee.
  5. Both parties lose money on exchange spreads and face pure foreign exchange volatility.

- **The Local Currency Settlement (LCS) Revolution**:
  1. In July 2023, the RBI and the Central Bank of the UAE signed a landmark agreement.
  2. The Indian buyer pays **directly in Indian Rupees into an authorized bank account**.
  3. The Dubai seller receives **UAE Dirhams instantly**.
  4. The transaction bypasses the US Dollar, bypasses New York clearing banks, eliminates double currency conversions, and cuts transaction costs by 3%–5%.

### 2. The RBI Special Rupee Vostro Account (SRVA) Mechanism
- **Nostro vs Vostro Distinction**: 
  - *Nostro Account* ("Our account with you"): An Indian bank's foreign currency account held in an overseas bank (e.g. SBI's USD account in Citibank New York).
  - *Vostro Account* ("Your account with us"): A foreign partner bank's Indian Rupee account held inside an Indian domestic bank (e.g. Sberbank of Russia's INR account in IndusInd Bank Mumbai).
- **Special Rupee Vostro Account (SRVA)**: Foreign banks deposit export receipts in INR and draw payments for Indian goods, completely bypassing third-party clearing currencies (USD/Euro).14:T447,### 1. The 3 Functions of an Internationalized Currency
According to the RBI Inter-Departmental Group on Rupee Internationalization (2023):
$$\text{International Currency} = \text{Unit of Account (Trade Invoicing)} + \text{Medium of Exchange (Settlement)} + \text{Store of Value (Central Bank Reserves)}$$
- **Phase 1 (Achieved)**: Bilateral invoicing and settlement in INR via SRVA with 22+ countries (Russia, UAE, Sri Lanka, Mauritius).
- **Phase 2 (In Progress)**: Inclusion of Indian Government Securities in global bond indices (J.P. Morgan GBI-EM Index inclusion in June 2024), drawing tens of billions of non-resident debt capital.
- **Phase 3 (Long-Term Horizon)**: Full Capital Account Convertibility (CAC) and foreign central banks holding INR as official foreign exchange reserves.

India's Model BIT (2015) vs Legacy 1st-Generation BITs

| Treaty Dimension | Legacy Indian BITs (Pre-2015) | India's Model BIT (2015 Edition) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dispute Settlement (ISDS)** | Foreign investors could directly drag Indian government to international arbitration (UNCITRAL/ICSID) at any time. | **Exhaustion of Local Remedies**: Foreign investors must pursue litigation in Indian domestic courts/tribunals for **at least 5 years** before approaching ISDS. |

| **Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN)** | Broad MFN clauses allowed investors to "treaty-shop" favorable provisions from other bilateral treaties. | **MFN Clause Completely Abolished** to prevent backdoor legal arbitrage. |

### 2. Modern Geo-Economic Terminology Guide
- **Off-Shoring**: Moving production to low-cost foreign countries regardless of geopolitical alignment (traditional 1990s globalisation).
- **Near-Shoring**: Relocating production geographically closer to home markets (e.g. US firms moving to Mexico; European firms moving to Eastern Europe).
- **Friend-Shoring (Janet Yellen)**: Restricting sourcing and manufacturing to nations with shared democratic values and low geopolitical risk (e.g. US/European tech expanding in India).
- **Geo-Economic Fragmentation**: Splitting the global economy into rival technological, monetary, and trade blocs (e.g. US/Allies vs China/Russia).18:T6c3,### Critical Examiner Traps
