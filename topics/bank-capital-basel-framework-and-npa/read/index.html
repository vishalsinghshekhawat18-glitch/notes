Bank Capital, Basel Framework, NPAs & Asset Quality

Bank capital regulation and asset quality management: Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA), Basel I/II/III architecture, Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR), Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1), Additional Tier 1 (AT1), Tier 2 capital, Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB), Leverage Ratio, NPA classification stages (Standard, Sub-Standard, Doubtful, Loss, SMA-0/1/2), Provisioning Coverage Ratio (PCR), and resolution mechanisms (SARFAESI, IBC 2016, June 7 Circular, NARCL).

Bank Capital, Basel Framework, NPAs & Asset Quality · Concept 1 of 3

Bank Capital Adequacy & The Basel III Framework: Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA), Capital Tiers, CRAR & Buffers

The core risk-sensitive capital architecture of commercial banking under the Basel III framework (codified in RBI Master Circular on Capital Regulations). Covers Risk-Weighted Assets (Credit, Market, Operational Risk), the Capital Tiers hierarchy (Common Equity Tier 1 / CET1, Additional Tier 1 / AT1, Tier 2 / Gone-Concern Capital), minimum CRAR requirements in India (9% minimum CRAR, 11.5% with 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer / CCB; 12% for PSBs), the non-risk-based Leverage Ratio (minimum 3.5% / 4% for D-SIBs), and Countercyclical Capital Buffers (CCyB).

Intuition & Context

The Shock Absorber: Why Bank Capital Protects Depositors from Ruin

Why cannot a bank operate with 100% deposit liabilities and zero equity capital?

Suppose a bank has ₹100 Crore in deposits and ₹100 Crore in corporate loans, with ₹0 equity capital. If a single factory defaults on a ₹2 Crore loan, the bank's assets fall to ₹98 Crore. The bank is now insolvent and cannot repay its depositors in full!

Bank Capital (CRAR) is the bank's unencumbered equity shock-absorber. If the bank has ₹12 Crore in equity capital and ₹100 Crore in deposits (Total Assets = ₹112 Crore), a ₹5 Crore loan default is absorbed entirely by the equity shareholders. Depositors still get 100% of their money back. Capital adequacy regulations ensure banks hold enough equity to survive severe economic recessions without failing.

Core Principle

Mechanism & Operational Rule

Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) & Capital Conservation Buffers

1. Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) Computation

Instead of treating all assets equally, each asset is multiplied by a regulatory Risk Weight (RWRWRW):

  • Cash & Sovereign G-Secs: RW=0%RW = 0%RW=0% (Risk-free).
  • Claims on Banks (AAA Rated): RW=20%RW = 20%RW=20%.
  • Prime Residential Home Loans (LTV ≤80%\le 80%≤80%): RW=35%−50%RW = 35% - 50%RW=35%−50%.
  • Standard Rated Corporate Loans: RW=20%RW = 20%RW=20% (AAA) to 150%150%150% (Unrated/D-rated).
  • Unsecured Consumer Loans / Credit Cards: RW=125%−150%RW = 125% - 150%RW=125%−150% (High risk penalty).

Total Credit RWA=∑(Asset Valuei×RWi)+∑(Off-Balance Sheet Credit Conversion Factor×RW)\text{Total Credit RWA} = \sum (\text{Asset Value}_i \times RW_i) + \sum (\text{Off-Balance Sheet Credit Conversion Factor} \times RW)Total Credit RWA=∑(Asset Valuei​×RWi​)+∑(Off-Balance Sheet Credit Conversion Factor×RW)

2. The Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB = 2.5%)

  • Mandated to be held entirely in pure Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1).
  • Built up outside periods of stress. When a bank's capital falls into the CCB band (5.5%−8.0%5.5% - 8.0%5.5%−8.0% CET1), the bank faces automatic legal restrictions on paying dividends, share buybacks, and executive bonuses until the buffer is rebuilt.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Indian Basel III Standards are Stricter than Global Basel: Global Basel III mandates minimum total CRAR of 8.0% (10.5% with CCB). The RBI mandates 9.0% (11.5% with CCB for SCBs, and 12% for PSBs).
  • Trap 2: AT1 Bond Loss Absorption (PONV): Additional Tier 1 (AT1) bonds are not risk-free fixed deposits; under RBI Basel III norms, AT1 bonds can be permanently written down or converted to equity if the bank hits the Point of Non-Viability (PONV), as occurred during the YES Bank reconstruction in 2020.
  • Trap 3: Leverage Ratio is Non-Risk-Based: Unlike CRAR (which divides by Risk-Weighted Assets), the Leverage Ratio divides by Total Nominal Exposure without applying any risk weights.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B, SEBI Grade A & IIBF DBF: CRAR math (9% + 2.5% CCB = 11.5%), CET1 (5.5% + 2.5% = 8%), AT1 write-down rules, and leverage ratio.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Basel acronym (Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, Switzerland), 3 Pillars of Basel II/III, and CRAR definition.

Non-Performing Assets (NPAs): Asset Classification Stages, Special Mention Accounts (SMA), Provisioning Norms & PCR

The statutory asset classification and loan-loss provisioning framework of the Reserve Bank of India (Income Recognition and Asset Classification / IRAC Norms). Covers the 90-day overdue rule for Non-Performing Assets (NPAs), early warning Special Mention Account tiers (SMA-0: 1–30 days, SMA-1: 31–60 days, SMA-2: 61–90 days), the 4-tier asset classification (Standard, Sub-Standard, Doubtful [D1, D2, D3], Loss Assets), statutory provisioning percentages (0.25% to 100%), the Gross NPA vs Net NPA calculation, and the Provisioning Coverage Ratio (PCR).

Intuition & Context

The Slippage Pipeline: From Performing Loan to Irrecoverable Loss

When a bank lends ₹10 Crore to an automobile components firm, the loan does not suddenly collapse overnight. Financial distress follows a predictable deterioration path:

  1. First 30 Days (Cash Flow Hiccup): The firm delays its monthly EMI by 15 days due to late customer receipts (SMA-0).
  2. 60 Days (Operational Stress): The firm fails to pay two consecutive EMIs (SMA-1).
  3. 90 Days (Critical Distress): Three EMIs missed; insolvency risk is severe (SMA-2).
  4. Day 91 (The Cliff Edge): The account legally crosses into Non-Performing Asset (NPA) territory. The bank must immediately stop booking accrued interest income and start locking away real capital as Provisions.

Early identification via Special Mention Accounts (SMA) ensures banks intervene to restructure viable firms before irreversible default occurs.

Agricultural Loan NPA Rules

  • Short Duration Crops (Paddy, Wheat): NPA if installment is unpaid for two crop seasons.
  • Long Duration Crops (Sugarcane, Tea): NPA if installment is unpaid for one crop season.
Mechanism & Operational Rule

Gross NPA vs Net NPA & The Provisioning Coverage Ratio (PCR)

To evaluate a bank's true balance sheet health, analysts look at Gross NPA, Net NPA, and PCR:

1. Gross NPA vs Net NPA

  • Gross NPA (GNPA): The total face value of all loans classified as NPAs on the bank's balance sheet.

GNPA Ratio=(Gross NPAsGross Total Advances)×100\text{GNPA Ratio} = \left(\frac{\text{Gross NPAs}}{\text{Gross Total Advances}}\right) \times 100GNPA Ratio=(Gross Total AdvancesGross NPAs​)×100

  • Net NPA (NNPA): The real residual default risk remaining on the bank's books after deducting loan loss provisions, claims received from ECGC/DICGC, and partial write-offs:

Net NPA=Gross NPA−(Cumulative Provisions+ECGC/DICGC Claims+Interest in Suspense)\text{Net NPA} = \text{Gross NPA} - (\text{Cumulative Provisions} + \text{ECGC/DICGC Claims} + \text{Interest in Suspense})Net NPA=Gross NPA−(Cumulative Provisions+ECGC/DICGC Claims+Interest in Suspense)
NNPA Ratio=(Net NPAsNet Total Advances)×100\text{NNPA Ratio} = \left(\frac{\text{Net NPAs}}{\text{Net Total Advances}}\right) \times 100NNPA Ratio=(Net Total AdvancesNet NPAs​)×100

2. Provisioning Coverage Ratio (PCR)

PCR=(Total Loan Loss Provisions HeldGross NPAs)×100\text{PCR} = \left(\frac{\text{Total Loan Loss Provisions Held}}{\text{Gross NPAs}}\right) \times 100PCR=(Gross NPAsTotal Loan Loss Provisions Held​)×100

  • Why PCR is Vital: A high PCR (e.g., 75%−85%75% - 85%75%−85%) means the bank has already set aside cash from past profits to absorb 80%80%80% of its bad loans. Even if borrowers default completely, future bank profits and capital will not be damaged.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: SMA-0 vs SMA-1 vs SMA-2 Days:
    • SMA-0 = 1 to 30 days overdue.
    • SMA-1 = 31 to 60 days overdue.
    • SMA-2 = 61 to 90 days overdue.
    • NPA = Over 90 days overdue.
  • Trap 2: Sub-Standard Duration: An asset remains Sub-Standard for up to 12 months from the date it became an NPA. On Day 366 as an NPA, it automatically downgrades to Doubtful (D1).
  • Trap 3: Agricultural Crop Seasons: Short duration crops require 2 crop seasons; Long duration crops require 1 crop season to become an NPA.
  • Trap 4: Doubtful Asset Provisioning on Unsecured Portion: In Doubtful assets (D1, D2, D3), the unsecured portion is ALWAYS provisioned at 100%, regardless of whether it is D1, D2, or D3!

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B, SEBI Grade A & IIBF DBF: Mastery of IRAC provisioning percentages (15%, 25%, 40%, 100%), SMA tiers, PCR formula, and GNPA/NNPA math.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & NABARD: 90-day NPA rule, agricultural crop season rules, and SMA-0/1/2 definitions.

Stressed Asset Resolution Architecture: SARFAESI Act, 2002, The Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code (IBC, 2016) & NARCL (Bad Bank)

The legal, institutional, and structural toolkit for resolving non-performing loans and stressed corporate assets in India. Covers the SARFAESI Act, 2002 (enforcement of security interest without court intervention under Section 13), the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC - Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process / CIRP timeline, Committee of Creditors / CoC, Section 14 moratorium, Section 29A disqualification, Section 53 liquidation waterfall), the RBI Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets (June 7, 2019 Circular / Inter-Creditor Agreement), and the National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited (NARCL / "Bad Bank" with sovereign Security Receipt guarantees).

Intuition & Context

Ending the Debtor's Paradise: The Shift from Recovery to Time-Bound Resolution

Prior to 2016, India was known as a "Debtor's Paradise". When a promoter defaulted on a ₹5,000 Crore bank loan, the bank spent 10 to 15 years filing cases in civil courts and Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs). While the court cases dragged on, the promoter continued living lavishly and controlling the company, while the factory assets rusted away and banks bled red ink.

The enactment of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC, 2016) revolutionized Indian credit culture. It introduced a Creditor-in-Control model: the moment a corporate borrower defaults on ₹1 Crore, the promoter loses management control, and a professional Resolution Professional takes over the company to sell it to the highest bidder within 180 days.

The IBC Liquidation Waterfall (Section 53)

If resolution fails and the company is liquidated, proceeds are distributed in a strict statutory hierarchy:

  1. Rank 1: Insolvency resolution process costs and liquidation costs (Paid in full first).
  2. Rank 2: Workmen's dues (past 24 months) + Debts owed to Secured Creditors (Pari-Passu).
  3. Rank 3: Wages and unpaid dues owed to employees (other than workmen) for past 12 months.
  4. Rank 4: Unsecured financial creditors.
  5. Rank 5: Central Government and State Government dues (taxes) + Unsecured debts.
  6. Rank 6: Preference shareholders.
  7. Rank 7: Equity shareholders (Residual owners at the very bottom).
Mechanism & Operational Rule

RBI June 7, 2019 Prudential Framework & The NARCL Dual Structure

1. RBI Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets (June 7, 2019)

  • Enacted after the Supreme Court struck down the February 12, 2018 circular (Dharani Sugars case).
  • 30-Day Review Period: The moment a borrower defaults (Day 1), lenders get a 30-day review period to design a resolution strategy.
  • Inter-Creditor Agreement (ICA): Lenders sign an ICA where decisions approved by 75%75%75% of lenders by value and 60%60%60% of lenders by number are legally binding on all lenders.
  • 180-Day Resolution Window: If a Resolution Plan (RP) is not implemented within 180 days, banks must make additional penal provisions (20%20%20% after 180 days, and an additional 15%15%15% after 365 days, totaling 35%35%35% extra provision).

2. The NARCL-IDRCL "Bad Bank" Architecture

  • NARCL (Public Sector Asset Reconstruction Company): Majority owned (51%51%51%) by Public Sector Banks. Acquires stressed loans from banks.
  • IDRCL (India Debt Resolution Company Limited): Majority owned (51%51%51%) by Private Sector Lenders. Acts as the specialized asset management agency to turn around and liquidate the operational assets.
  • Government Guarantee: The Union Government provides a sovereign backstop guarantee on the 85% Security Receipts for up to 5 years, covering any shortfall between realized value and face value.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Exemptions under SARFAESI: SARFAESI Act does NOT apply to Agricultural Land (Section 31(i)), nor does it apply to loans where the outstanding balance is less than ₹1 Lakh or less than 20%20%20% of the principal and interest.
  • Trap 2: CoC Voting Threshold under IBC: Passing a Resolution Plan or replacing a Resolution Professional requires 66% of voting share of Financial Creditors. Routine administrative decisions require 51%.
  • Trap 3: Section 29A Disqualification: Promoters who are willful defaulters or whose accounts have been NPAs for >= 1 year are strictly barred from bidding for their own company under Section 29A of IBC.
  • Trap 4: Government Dues in IBC Waterfall: Under Section 53 of IBC, Government taxes rank BELOW secured financial creditors and unsecured financial creditors (overriding the historic Crown Debt doctrine).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: IBC Section 7/9/14/29A/53, Section 13 SARFAESI notice, June 7 Circular ICA thresholds (75% value / 60% number), and NARCL 15:85 structure.
  • IIBF DBF, SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: 60-day SARFAESI notice, 180-day CIRP timeline, and Bad Bank concept.
