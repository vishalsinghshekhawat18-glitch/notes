### The Mathematical Formulas
$$\text{CRAR} = \left(\frac{\text{Eligible Tier 1 Capital} + \text{Eligible Tier 2 Capital}}{\text{Total Risk-Weighted Assets (Credit + Market + Operational RWA)}}\right) \times 100$$

$$\text{Leverage Ratio} = \left(\frac{\text{Tier 1 Capital}}{\text{Total On- and Off-Balance Sheet Exposure}}\right) \times 100 \ge 3.5\%$$12:T42d,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Indian Basel III Standards are Stricter than Global Basel**: Global Basel III mandates minimum total CRAR of **8.0%** (10.5% with CCB). The **RBI mandates 9.0%** (11.5% with CCB for SCBs, and 12% for PSBs).
- **Trap 2: AT1 Bond Loss Absorption (PONV)**: Additional Tier 1 (AT1) bonds are **not risk-free fixed deposits**; under RBI Basel III norms, AT1 bonds can be permanently written down or converted to equity if the bank hits the Point of Non-Viability (PONV), as occurred during the YES Bank reconstruction in 2020.
- **Trap 3: Leverage Ratio is Non-Risk-Based**: Unlike CRAR (which divides by Risk-Weighted Assets), the **Leverage Ratio divides by Total Nominal Exposure** without applying any risk weights.

### Agricultural Loan NPA Rules
- **Short Duration Crops** (Paddy, Wheat): NPA if installment is unpaid for **two crop seasons**.
- **Long Duration Crops** (Sugarcane, Tea): NPA if installment is unpaid for **one crop season**.14:T465,To evaluate a bank's true balance sheet health, analysts look at Gross NPA, Net NPA, and PCR:

### 1. Gross NPA vs Net NPA
- **Gross NPA (GNPA)**: The total face value of all loans classified as NPAs on the bank's balance sheet.
$$\text{GNPA Ratio} = \left(\frac{\text{Gross NPAs}}{\text{Gross Total Advances}}\right) \times 100$$
- **Net NPA (NNPA)**: The real residual default risk remaining on the bank's books after deducting loan loss provisions, claims received from ECGC/DICGC, and partial write-offs:
$$\text{Net NPA} = \text{Gross NPA} - (\text{Cumulative Provisions} + \text{ECGC/DICGC Claims} + \text{Interest in Suspense})$$
$$\text{NNPA Ratio} = \left(\frac{\text{Net NPAs}}{\text{Net Total Advances}}\right) \times 100$$

### 2. Provisioning Coverage Ratio (PCR)
$$\text{PCR} = \left(\frac{\text{Total Loan Loss Provisions Held}}{\text{Gross NPAs}}\right) \times 100$$
- **Why PCR is Vital**: A high PCR (e.g., $75\% - 85\%$) means the bank has already set aside cash from past profits to absorb $80\%$ of its bad loans. Even if borrowers default completely, future bank profits and capital will not be damaged.15:T417,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: SMA-0 vs SMA-1 vs SMA-2 Days**:
  - SMA-0 = **1 to 30 days** overdue.
  - SMA-1 = **31 to 60 days** overdue.
  - SMA-2 = **61 to 90 days** overdue.
  - NPA = **Over 90 days** overdue.
- **Trap 2: Sub-Standard Duration**: An asset remains Sub-Standard for **up to 12 months** from the date it became an NPA. On Day 366 as an NPA, it automatically downgrades to **Doubtful (D1)**.
- **Trap 3: Agricultural Crop Seasons**: Short duration crops require **2 crop seasons**; Long duration crops require **1 crop season** to become an NPA.
- **Trap 4: Doubtful Asset Provisioning on Unsecured Portion**: In Doubtful assets (D1, D2, D3), the **unsecured portion is ALWAYS provisioned at 100%**, regardless of whether it is D1, D2, or D3!

### The IBC Liquidation Waterfall (Section 53)
If resolution fails and the company is liquidated, proceeds are distributed in a strict statutory hierarchy:
1. **Rank 1**: Insolvency resolution process costs and liquidation costs (Paid in full first).
2. **Rank 2**: Workmen's dues (past 24 months) + Debts owed to Secured Creditors (Pari-Passu).
3. **Rank 3**: Wages and unpaid dues owed to employees (other than workmen) for past 12 months.
4. **Rank 4**: Unsecured financial creditors.
5. **Rank 5**: Central Government and State Government dues (taxes) + Unsecured debts.
6. **Rank 6**: Preference shareholders.
7. **Rank 7**: Equity shareholders (Residual owners at the very bottom).17:T539,### 1. RBI Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets (June 7, 2019)
- Enacted after the Supreme Court struck down the February 12, 2018 circular (Dharani Sugars case).
- **30-Day Review Period**: The moment a borrower defaults (Day 1), lenders get a 30-day review period to design a resolution strategy.
- **Inter-Creditor Agreement (ICA)**: Lenders sign an ICA where decisions approved by **$75\%$ of lenders by value and $60\%$ of lenders by number** are legally binding on all lenders.
- **180-Day Resolution Window**: If a Resolution Plan (RP) is not implemented within 180 days, banks must make **additional penal provisions** ($20\%$ after 180 days, and an additional $15\%$ after 365 days, totaling $35\%$ extra provision).

### 2. The NARCL-IDRCL "Bad Bank" Architecture
- **NARCL (Public Sector Asset Reconstruction Company)**: Majority owned ($51\%$) by Public Sector Banks. Acquires stressed loans from banks.
- **IDRCL (India Debt Resolution Company Limited)**: Majority owned ($51\%$) by Private Sector Lenders. Acts as the specialized asset management agency to turn around and liquidate the operational assets.
- **Government Guarantee**: The Union Government provides a sovereign backstop guarantee on the 85% Security Receipts for up to 5 years, covering any shortfall between realized value and face value.18:T4ca,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Exemptions under SARFAESI**: SARFAESI Act **does NOT apply to Agricultural Land** (Section 31(i)), nor does it apply to loans where the outstanding balance is **less than ₹1 Lakh** or less than $20\%$ of the principal and interest.
- **Trap 2: CoC Voting Threshold under IBC**: Passing a Resolution Plan or replacing a Resolution Professional requires **66% of voting share** of Financial Creditors. Routine administrative decisions require **51%**.
- **Trap 3: Section 29A Disqualification**: Promoters who are **willful defaulters or whose accounts have been NPAs for >= 1 year** are strictly **barred from bidding** for their own company under Section 29A of IBC.
- **Trap 4: Government Dues in IBC Waterfall**: Under Section 53 of IBC, **Government taxes rank BELOW secured financial creditors and unsecured financial creditors** (overriding the historic Crown Debt doctrine).
