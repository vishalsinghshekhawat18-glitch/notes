Principles of Sound Lending, Credit Appraisal & Working Capital · Concept 1 of 3

Principles of Sound Lending & 5 Cs Credit Assessment Framework: Safety, Liquidity, Purpose, Profitability & Risk-Adjusted Return

Foundational canons of commercial bank lending (Safety, Liquidity, Purpose, Profitability, Security, Spread/Diversification) combined with the institutional '5 Cs of Credit' framework (Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, Conditions) governing credit appraisal, risk pricing, and underwriting.

Intuition & Context

Why Banks Cannot Lend Like Pawn Shops: The Fallacy of Security

Novice lenders often believe that as long as a borrower pledges a ₹10 Crore property against a ₹5 Crore loan, the loan is 100% safe. This is the Pawn Shop Fallacy.

A commercial bank is not a real estate liquidator. If a borrower has no business cash flows and defaults, evicting the occupant, auctioning property through SARFAESI, fighting court stays in DRT, and realizing cash can take 5 to 10 years. Meanwhile, depositors demand their cash back every morning. Therefore, sound banking dictates that loans must be repaid from operational business cash flows (Capacity & Safety), with collateral serving solely as a secondary airbag in the event of an unexpected crash.

Core Principle

The Six Canons of Sound Lending & The 5 Cs Underwriting Matrix

The 6 Canons of Sound Bank Lending

  1. Safety: Foremost canon. The bank must ensure that the borrower has both the willingness and financial capability to repay.
  2. Liquidity: The loan maturity must align with the bank's Asset-Liability Management (ALM) profile to meet depositor demands.
  3. Purpose: Loan must be utilized strictly for productive, economic purposes (not for speculative trading, gambling, or siphoning).
  4. Profitability: Loan pricing must provide an adequate Net Interest Margin (NIM) and Return on Capital after covering operating costs and credit risk premium.
  5. Security (Collateral): Acts as an insurance/cushion against unexpected business failure; never the primary source of repayment.
  6. Spread (Diversification): Credit portfolio must be diversified across sectors, industries, and geographies to prevent single-borrower/group concentration risk.

The 5 Cs of Credit Appraisal Matrix

'C' of Credit Core Definition Key Financial & Non-Financial Parameters Evaluated
1. Character Willingness to repay; promoter integrity Credit Information Report (CIBIL/Equifax/Experian/CRIF), MCA defaults, willful defaulter list, vendor market feedback.
2. Capacity Ability to service debt from operations Operating Cash Flows, Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR), Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR), Working Capital cycle length.
3. Capital Promoter's stake and net worth Debt-to-Equity (Leverage Ratio), Tangible Net Worth (TNW), Quasi-equity / Unsecured promoter loans subordinated to bank debt.
4. Collateral Secondary source of repayment Primary Security (hypothecation of stock/book debts) & Collateral Security (mortgage of immovable property, FDR, LIC policies).
5. Conditions External business environment Industry lifecycle stage, raw material price volatility, interest rate cycle, Government policy/tariff shifts, ESG compliance.

Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps, Credit Bureau Rules & Loan Restrictions

High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams

  1. Safety vs Security Hierarchy Trap: If an exam asks 'Which is the most essential principle of sound lending?', the answer is Safety (NOT Security). Security is secondary.
  2. Statutory Restrictions on Loans (Section 20 BR Act): Banks are strictly prohibited from granting loans or advances on the security of their own shares, or granting loans to their own Directors (except minor staff loans or within RBI threshold limits).
  3. Penal Interest vs Penal Charges (2024 Rule): Under RBI's Fair Lending Guidelines (effective 2024), banks are strictly banned from compounding penal charges or adding penal interest to the loan interest rate. Penal charges must be levied as separate, non-compounded fees.
  4. CRILC Reporting Threshold: Banks must report credit information of all borrowers having aggregate exposure of ₹5 Crore and above to the Central Repository of Information on Large Credits (CRILC) on a weekly/monthly basis.

Working Capital Assessment: Tandon Committee MPBF (Methods I & II), Nayak Committee Turnover Method & Cash Budget Method

Standardized regulatory methodologies for assessing working capital finance in Indian commercial banking: Tandon Committee Maximum Permissible Bank Finance (MPBF Method I & Method II with minimum Current Ratio 1.33:1), Nayak Committee Turnover Method for Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs), and the Cash Budget Method for seasonal/service sectors.

Intuition & Context

How Much Working Capital Should a Bank Actually Fund?

A manufacturing company needs raw materials, pays labor wages, and waits 60 days for customers to pay invoices. All these assets tied up in operations constitute Current Assets (CA). Some suppliers provide credit, and statutory dues are accrued (Other Current Liabilities - OCL). The difference between CA and OCL is the Working Capital Gap (WCG).

If a bank funds 100% of this gap, the business owner has zero skin in the game, and the company becomes dangerously over-leveraged. The Tandon Committee and Nayak Committee introduced mathematical guardrails to ensure that the business promoter funds a mandatory portion of current assets from long-term capital (equity/retained earnings), guaranteeing business solvency and liquidity.

Core Principle

Mathematical Formulas & Comparison of Working Capital Assessment Methods

1. Tandon Committee MPBF Methods (Working Capital Gap = CA - OCL)

(Note: OCL = Total Current Liabilities excluding Bank Borrowings)

  • Method I (25% Margin on Working Capital Gap):
    MPBF1=0.75×(Current Assets−OCL)=0.75×WCG\text{MPBF}_1 = 0.75 \times (\text{Current Assets} - \text{OCL}) = 0.75 \times \text{WCG}MPBF1​=0.75×(Current Assets−OCL)=0.75×WCG
    • Borrower Margin = $0.25 \times \text{WCG}$
    • Minimum Resultant Current Ratio = 1.17 : 1
  • Method II (25% Margin on Total Current Assets - Standard Banking Benchmark):
    MPBF2=(0.75×Current Assets)−OCL\text{MPBF}_2 = (0.75 \times \text{Current Assets}) - \text{OCL}MPBF2​=(0.75×Current Assets)−OCL
    • Borrower Margin = $0.25 \times \text{Current Assets}$
    • Minimum Resultant Current Ratio = 1.33 : 1

2. Nayak Committee Turnover Method (MSEs / Limits up to ₹5 Crore)

  • Total Working Capital Requirement = 25% of Projected Annual Turnover
  • Bank Finance (MPBF) = Minimum 20% of Projected Annual Turnover
  • Borrower Margin (Equity) = Minimum 5% of Projected Annual Turnover

3. Cash Budget Method (Seasonal / Real Estate / Service Sectors)

  • Used for industries with volatile/seasonal cash flows (Sugar mills, tea plantations, EPC contractors, software firms).
  • Tracks monthly projected cash inflows vs cash outflows; bank finance covers the peak cumulative monthly cash deficit.

Examiner Traps, Numerical Case Studies & Benchmark Ratios

High-Yield Traps & Numerical Applications

  1. Current Ratio 1.33:1 Origin Trap: Questions frequently ask: 'Which working capital assessment method enforces a minimum Current Ratio of 1.33:1?' The answer is Tandon Committee Method II.
  2. Numerical MPBF Calculation Trap:
    • Given: Current Assets (CA) = ₹1,000 Lakh; Other Current Liabilities (OCL) = ₹200 Lakh.
    • Working Capital Gap (WCG) = $1000 - 200 = ₹800\text{ Lakh}$.
    • Method I MPBF = $0.75 \times 800 = ₹600\text{ Lakh}$ (Margin = ₹200 Lakh).
    • Method II MPBF = (0.75×1000)−200=750−200=₹550 Lakh(0.75 \times 1000) - 200 = 750 - 200 = \text{₹}550\text{ Lakh}(0.75×1000)−200=750−200=₹550 Lakh (Margin = ₹250 Lakh).
  3. Nayak Method Turnover Trap: If an MSE projects annual turnover of ₹400 Lakh, total working capital requirement is ₹100 Lakh (25%), bank finance is ₹80 Lakh (20%), and promoter margin is ₹20 Lakh (5%). If promoter margin available is only ₹15 Lakh, bank finance cannot exceed ₹80 Lakh.

Term Loan Appraisal & Financial Feasibility: DSCR, IRR, NPV, Break-Even Analysis & Sensitivity Testing

Comprehensive financial, technical, and commercial appraisal framework for long-term project and term loan financing: evaluation of Debt Service Coverage Ratio (Gross DSCR and Average DSCR benchmarks ≥ 1.50–2.00), Discounted Cash Flow metrics (NPV, IRR vs Hurdle Rate / WACC), Break-Even Point (BEP) capacity utilization, and Sensitivity Analysis under adverse shock parameters.

Intuition & Context

Why Working Capital Looks at Today, but Term Loans Look at the Decade

When assessing a working capital loan, a banker examines the current inventory and receivables that will liquidate into cash in 60 days. But when financing a ₹100 Crore cement plant through a 10-year Term Loan, the banker is betting on future cash flows that do not yet exist.

The banker must act as a financial futurist: Will the plant be built on time (Technical Appraisal)? Will cement prices collapse (Sensitivity Analysis)? At what factory production level does the company stop making losses (Break-Even Analysis)? And most critically: after paying taxes, how many times over do annual cash profits cover the required principal and interest EMIs (Debt Service Coverage Ratio - DSCR)?

Core Principle

Core Financial Metrics & Formulas in Term Loan Appraisal

1. Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

DSCR=PAT+Depreciation+Interest on Term LoanInterest on Term Loan+Principal Repayment Installment\text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Interest on Term Loan}}{\text{Interest on Term Loan} + \text{Principal Repayment Installment}}DSCR=Interest on Term Loan+Principal Repayment InstallmentPAT+Depreciation+Interest on Term Loan​

  • Interpretation:
    • DSCR<1.0\text{DSCR} < 1.0DSCR<1.0: Project cannot service debt from operations (cash deficit / default risk).
    • DSCR=1.0\text{DSCR} = 1.0DSCR=1.0: Bare break-even (zero margin of safety).
    • DSCR=1.50 to 2.00\text{DSCR} = 1.50 \text{ to } 2.00DSCR=1.50 to 2.00: Ideal Banking Standard (adequate cushion for business downturns).
    • DSCR>2.50\text{DSCR} > 2.50DSCR>2.50: Highly comfortable, but may indicate loan tenure is unnecessarily stretched.

  • Net Present Value (NPV): Sum of discounted future net cash inflows minus initial capital outlay. Project accepted if NPV>0\text{NPV} > 0NPV>0.

4. Sensitivity Analysis Parameters

Banks stress-test the project model by modeling:

  • 10% increase in Raw Material / Operating Costs.
  • 10% reduction in Selling Price / Demand.
  • 1-year delay in Commercial Operations Date (COD) with cost overruns.

Institutional Project Appraisal Lifecycle

graph TD
    A[DPR Detailed Project Report Submitted] --> B[Technical Feasibility: Location, Technology, Environmental Clearances]
    B --> C[Commercial & Market Appraisal: Demand-Supply Gap, Competitor Pricing]
    C --> D[Financial Feasibility: Cost of Project & Means of Finance Debt-Equity 2:1]

  • Means of Finance: Promoter contribution (Equity/Quasi-equity) typically 25% to 33% (Debt-Equity ratio between 1.5:1 and 2:1).
  • Moratorium / Grace Period: Principal repayment deferred during construction plus 6-12 months stabilization period; interest is serviced during construction or capitalized (IDC - Interest During Construction).
  • Trust and Retention Account (TRA): In major infrastructure/project finance, cash flows are routed through an escrow TRA to ensure priority debt servicing.
Exam Application & Traps

Examiner Traps, Numerical DSCR Calculations & Common Errors

High-Yield Traps & Numerical Applications

  1. Depreciation in DSCR Trap: Candidates often forget to add back Depreciation to PAT. Depreciation is a non-cash expense; it does not represent an actual cash outflow and is therefore fully available for debt servicing.
  2. Working Capital Interest vs Term Loan Interest Trap: In the DSCR numerator and denominator, ONLY Term Loan Interest is included. Short-term Working Capital / Cash Credit interest is an operating cost already deducted before arriving at Operating Profit / PAT and must NOT be added back or included in the DSCR denominator.
  3. Moratorium Period Definition: A moratorium period (holiday period) is granted for repayment of Principal, during which the borrower is usually expected to service interest monthly (unless IDC is capitalized).
  4. IRR vs NPV Conflict: When evaluating mutually exclusive projects, if NPV and IRR give conflicting rankings, NPV takes precedence because it measures absolute wealth creation.
