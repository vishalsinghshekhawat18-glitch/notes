12:T407,Imagine a neighborhood credit union formed by 190 countries:
1. **The Subscription (Quota)**: Every country contributes money to the pool based on the size of its economy. The USA puts in the most, India puts in a substantial amount, and smaller nations contribute less.
2. **The Voting Rules**: Unlike the United Nations General Assembly (where every country gets 1 equal vote), the IMF is a **shareholder financial institution**—voting power is directly tied to the size of your Quota subscription. The US holds $\\sim 16.5\\%$ of the votes, while major decisions require an **$85\%$ supermajority**, giving Washington an absolute single-handed veto!
3. **The SDR (Special Drawing Right)**: To ensure countries don't run out of international liquidity, the IMF created its own artificial accounting currency called the **SDR** (a synthetic cocktail of Dollars, Euros, Yuan, Yen, and Pounds).

The IMF does not build schools or dams—it acts as the global macroeconomic fire department to extinguish Balance of Payments crises.13:T590,```

### The Five Currencies of the SDR Valuation Basket
The SDR (XDR) is reviewed every 5 years by the IMF Executive Board. Its current composition reflects global trade and financial depth:
1. **US Dollar (USD)**: $\\sim 43.38\\%$ weight (Dominant reserve currency)
2. **Euro (EUR)**: $\\sim 29.31\\%$ weight
3. **Chinese Renminbi (CNY / Yuan)**: $\\sim 12.28\\%$ weight (*Historic inclusion in October 2016*)
4. **Japanese Yen (JPY)**: $\\sim 7.59\\%$ weight
5. **British Pound Sterling (GBP)**: $\\sim 7.44\\%$ weight

*Note on India's Quota*: India holds a **Quota of $\\sim 2.75\\%$** and a **Voting Share of $\\sim 2.63\\%$**, placing India in the top 10 shareholder nations (represented on the 24-member IMF Executive Board).14:T531,How does an SDR allocation expand global reserves without creating debt?

### The August 2021 Historic Allocation
Following the COVID-19 pandemic, the IMF executed its largest-ever general allocation of **SDR 456 Billion (equivalent to US \USD 650 Billionillion)**. India received SDR 12.57 Billion ($\\sim \\text{US \USD 17.86 Billionillion}$), which immediately boosted India's foreign exchange reserves.15:T407,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Is SDR a Currency?**: **NO.** The SDR is **neither a currency nor a claim on the IMF**. It is an unconditional potential claim on the freely usable currencies of IMF member states. You cannot buy a coffee in London with SDRs.
- **Trap 2: SDR Basket Composition**: Examiners frequently test the 5 currencies. It contains **USD, EUR, CNY, JPY, GBP**. Swiss Franc (CHF) and Indian Rupee (INR) are NOT in the SDR basket.
- **Trap 3: US Veto in IMF**: Important decisions (Quota changes, Articles amendments) require an **85% supermajority**. Because the United States holds over **16.5% of the total voting power**, the US is the only country with a unilateral veto over major IMF structural reforms.

### Reserve Tranche vs Upper Credit Tranches
- **Reserve Tranche (Automatic Drawing)**: The 25% of a member's quota paid in foreign currency. A member can draw this down **instantly on demand with zero interest and zero policy conditions**.
- **Upper Credit Tranches & EFF**: Borrowings exceeding the reserve tranche. Subject to strict, phased **macroeconomic conditionality** and quarterly performance reviews.17:T639,Beyond crisis lending, the IMF performs continuous global surveillance:

### India's Historic Interaction with the IMF
India was a frequent borrower from the IMF between 1957 and 1991 (including Stand-By loans in 1981 and the famous 1991 EFF program). Following the 1991 reforms and the build-up of foreign exchange reserves, **India fully repaid all IMF loans ahead of schedule by May 2000**. Today, India is no longer an IMF borrower; India is a **net creditor to the IMF**, contributing to IMF liquidity support arrangements (New Arrangements to Borrow
