16:T49a,### The Scientific Work Formula:
$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = F \cdot s \cdot \cos\theta$$

### Variable Definitions:
- $W = \text{Work done (Joules, J)}$
- $F = \text{Magnitude of applied force (Newtons, N)}$
- $s = \text{Magnitude of displacement (metres, m)}$
- $\theta = \text{Angle between the force vector } \vec{F} \text{ and displacement vector } \vec{s}$

### Classification by Angle $\theta$:

| Work Type | Angle Range | Sign of $\cos\theta$ | Real-World Physical Example |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Positive Work** | $0^\circ \le \theta < 90^\circ$ | Positive ($>0$) | • A horse pulling a cart forward along a road.<br>• Gravity doing work on a falling apple ($\theta = 0^\circ$). |
| **Negative Work** | $90^\circ < \theta \le 180^\circ$ | Negative ($<0$) | • Frictional force opposing sliding motion ($\theta = 180^\circ, \cos 180^\circ = -1$).<br>• Gravity doing work when throwing a ball upward. |
| **Zero Work** | $\theta = 90^\circ$ OR $s = 0$ | Zero ($=0$) | • Pushing a stationary wall ($s = 0$).<br>• Porter walking horizontally with luggage ($\theta = 90^\circ$).<br>• Centripetal force in circular orbits ($\theta = 90^\circ$). |17:T41d,### 1. The Railway Porter Paradox (Why $W = 0$)
- Porter lifts suitcase upward onto his head: **Positive Work** ($W = +mgh$).
- Porter holds suitcase stationary on head: **Zero Work** ($s = 0$).
- Porter walks horizontally with velocity $v$:
  - Downward gravitational force: $\vec{F}_g = mg \hat{j}$ (vertically downward).
  - Horizontal displacement: $\vec{s} = s \hat{i}$ (horizontal).
  - Angle between force and displacement: $\theta = 90^\circ$.
  $$W_{\text{gravity}} = F \cdot s \cdot \cos 90^\circ = F \cdot s \cdot 0 = \mathbf{0 \, \text{Joules}}$$

### 2. Work Done by Gravitational Force on Orbiting Satellites
At every point along a circular orbit:
- Gravitational force $\vec{F}_g$ acts radially **inward towards Earth's centre**.
- Instantaneous displacement $d\vec{s}$ is **tangential** to the circular orbit.
- Angle $\theta = 90^\circ$ at all times $\implies W = \int F_g \cdot ds \cdot \cos 90^\circ = \mathbf{0}$.
- *Consequence*: Earth's gravity does zero work on the Moon; the Moon circles forever without losing mechanical energy!18:T436,### 1. Derivation of Kinetic Energy from Work-Energy
A body of mass $m$ at rest ($u = 0$) accelerates to velocity $v$ under constant force $F$ over distance $s$:
- Work done $W = F \cdot s = (ma) \cdot s = m(as)$.
- From 3rd equation of motion ($v^2 - u^2 = 2as \implies v^2 - 0 = 2as \implies as = \frac{1}{2}v^2$):
$$W = m \left(\frac{1}{2}v^2\right) = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$
Since work done equals the kinetic energy acquired:
$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

### 2. The High-Yield Momentum-Kinetic Energy Relationship
Linear momentum $p = mv \implies v = p/m$. Substituting into kinetic energy:
$$E_k = \frac{1}{2} m \left(\frac{p}{m}\right)^2 = \mathbf{\frac{p^2}{2m}} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \mathbf{p = \sqrt{2m E_k}}$$
- If a heavy body ($M$) and a light body ($m$) have **EQUAL Linear Momentum ($p$)**:
  $$E_k \propto \frac{1}{m} \implies \mathbf{\text{The LIGHTER body has GREATER Kinetic Energy!}}$$
- If a heavy body and a light body have **EQUAL Kinetic Energy ($E_k$)**:
  $$p \propto \sqrt{m} \implies \mathbf{\text{The HEAVIER body has GREATER Linear Momentum!}}$$19:T476,### 1. Conservative vs Non-Conservative Forces

| Feature | Conservative Forces | Non-Conservative Forces |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Work Path Dependence** | **Path-Independent** (Depends only on start & end). | **Path-Dependent** (Longer path = more work dissipated). |
| **Work in Closed Loop** | **$\oint \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = 0$** (Zero work). | **$\oint \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} \ne 0$** (Negative dissipated work). |
| **Mechanical Energy** | Total Mechanical Energy is **strictly conserved**. | Mechanical energy is **converted into heat, sound, light**. |
| **Master Examples** | **Gravitational force, Electrostatic force, Elastic spring force**. | **Friction, Viscous drag, Air resistance**. |

### 2. Simple Pendulum Energy Transformations
- At **Extreme Positions** (maximum swing height $h$): Velocity $v = 0 \implies E_k = 0, E_p = mgh$ (Maximum).
- At **Mean Position** (lowest equilibrium point $h = 0$): Height $h = 0 \implies E_p = 0, E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ (Maximum).
- A pendulum in a vacuum swings forever without stopping.
