12:T524,How did an introspective, London-trained barrister transform into the indisputable mass leader of the largest anti-colonial struggle in human history?

Mahatma Gandhi did not arrive in India in 1915 as an untested political philosopher. He arrived having completed a twenty-year laboratory experiment in South Africa (1893–1914):
* In South Africa, confronting severe racial discrimination against Indian indentured labourers and merchants (poll tax, Asiatic Registration Act, invalidation of non-Christian marriages), Gandhi forged the radical technique of **Satyagraha** (*Satya* = Truth, *Agraha* = Firm adherence / Soul-force).
* Influenced by Leo Tolstoy (*The Kingdom of God is Within You*), John Ruskin (*Unto This Last*), and Henry David Thoreau (*Civil Disobedience*), Gandhi rejected both the "polite mendicancy" of the Indian Moderates and the "individual terror" of the Revolutionaries.
* On the advice of his political guru, **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**, Gandhi spent his first two years in India (1915–1916) travelling in third-class train compartments across the subcontinent with his "ears open and mouth shut", absorbing the lived agony of ordinary peasants. ### 2. The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (April 13, 1919)
* **Immediate Context:** Arrest of popular nationalist leaders **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal** in Amritsar under the Rowlatt Act on April 10, 1919, leading to local riots.
* **The Atrocity (April 13, 1919 - Baisakhi Day):**
  * A peaceful gathering of 20,000 men, women, and children gathered in the walled enclosure of Jallianwala Bagh to protest the arrests and celebrate Baisakhi.
  * Acting Brigadier-General **Reginald Dyer** marched in with Gurkha and Baluchi riflemen, sealed the only narrow exit, and without giving any warning, ordered his troops to fire 1,650 rounds into the trapped crowd until ammunition was exhausted.
  * Officially 379 dead; independent estimates by the Congress inquiry committee established over 1,000 dead.
* **National Repercussions:**
  * **Rabindranath Tagore** renounced his British **Knighthood** (*"The time has come when badges of honour make our shame glaring in the incongruous context of humiliation..."*).
  * Gandhi renounced his **Kaiser-i-Hind** gold medal and called off the Rowlatt Satyagraha on April 18, 1919, describing launching mass civil disobedience with an untrained populace as a **"Himalayan Blunder"**.
  * **Hunter Commission (Disorders Inquiry Committee):** British official inquiry; Dyer was censured and removed from command but exonerated of murder and hailed as the "Savior of the Punjab" by the British House of Lords and British public (who presented him a purse of £26,000 and a jewelled sword).
  * **Michael O'Dwyer:** The Lieutenant Governor of Punjab who endorsed Dyer's firing was later assassinated on March 13, 1940 at Caxton Hall, London by revolutionary **Udham Singh** (Shaheed Ram Mohammad Singh Azad).14:T4a8,### The Mechanics of Mass Conversion via Suffering

Gandhian Satyagraha operated on a psychological mechanism that paralyzed the British colonial apparatus:

1. **The Tactical Disarmament of Imperial Coercion:**
   $$\text{Colonial Hegemony} = \text{Physical Brutality} \times \text{Moral Justification ("Civilizing Mission")}$$
   * When an empire uses batons and bullets against armed rebels, it easily justifies itself as the protector of public order.
   * But when British police beat unarmed, non-violent, praying men and women who refuse to retaliate or flee, the moral justification for empire shatters in the eyes of world opinion and the British electorate itself.

2. **The Technique of the 25% Refund at Champaran:**
   * Why did Gandhi agree to a mere **25% refund** of the illegally extorted money from the Champaran planters, when the ryots demanded 100%?
   * Gandhi explained: The monetary amount was secondary; what mattered was that the European planters—who had behaved as hereditary feudal lords above the law—were forced to surrender their prestige, status, and legal invulnerability. The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922) was made possible by the confluence of three distinct rivers of anti-colonial fury:
1. **The Punjab Wrongs:** Nationwide outrage over the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and the whitewashing Hunter Commission report.
2. **The Constitutional Betrayal:** Deep dissatisfaction with the hollow Government of India Act 1919 and post-war economic misery (hyper-inflation, plague, crop failure).
3. **The Khilafat Question:** The humiliation of the Ottoman Sultan (who was revered as the *Khalifa* or spiritual head of Sunni Muslims worldwide) following the dismemberment of Turkey by the Allies through the **Treaty of Sèvres (1920)**.

Gandhi recognized this as a once-in-a-century historical opportunity to fuse Hindu and Muslim aspirations into an indomitable anti-imperialist front: *"An opportunity of uniting Hindus and Muslims as would not arise in a hundred years"*. By bringing the entire organizational weight of the Congress behind the Khilafat Committee, Gandhi forged the first truly pan-Indian mass upsurge across religion, region, and class.19:T1980,### 1. The Twin Congress Sessions of 1920
* **Special Calcutta Session (September 1920):**
  * Presided over by **Lala Lajpat Rai**.
  * Gandhi moved the historic Non-Cooperation resolution. Despite initial opposition from stalwarts like C.R. Das, Bipin Chandra Pal, and Annie Besant (who feared council boycotts and radicalization), the resolution was passed with an overwhelming majority.
* **Regular Nagpur Session (December 1920) — The Watershed Moment:**
  * Presided over by **C. Vijayaraghavachariar**.
  * **C.R. Das** dramatically dropped his opposition and himself moved the main Non-Cooperation resolution!
  * **Radical Structural Transformation of Congress:**
    1. **Goal Redefined:** The objective of the Congress was altered from self-government within the British Empire through constitutional means to **"the attainment of Swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means"**.
    2. **Working Committee Established:** Created a 15-member **Congress Working Committee (CWC)** to act as a permanent, year-round executive steering body.
    3. **Linguistic Provincial Committees:** Reorganized Provincial Congress Committees (PCCs) on a **linguistic basis** (demolishing colonial administrative boundaries and taking politics to the regional vernacular).
    4. **Mass Membership:** Reduced annual membership fee to **4 annas (25 paise)**, opening Congress doors to peasants, artisans, and industrial workers.
    5. **Tilak Swaraj Fund:** Announced following the demise of Bal Gangadhar Tilak (died August 1, 1920, the day NCM formally began); aimed to collect **₹1 Crore** to finance the movement, which was oversubscribed within months!

### 3. Regional Outbursts & Subaltern Mobilization
The Non-Cooperation Movement was not merely an urban middle-class protest; it triggered volcanic subaltern peasant uprisings across the country:
* **Awadh Peasant Movement (UP):** Led by **Baba Ramchandra** (a wandering sanyasi who carried the *Ramcharitmanas*); organized peasants against illegal cesses (*Nazrana*) and evictions (*Bedakhali*). **Awadh Kisan Sabha** formed in Oct 1920 with Jawaharlal Nehru.
* **Eka Movement (1921, UP):** Led by **Madari Pasi** in Hardoi, Bahraich, and Sitapur; peasants pledged to pay only recorded rents and refuse forced labour (*Begar*).
* **Mappila (Moplah) Rebellion (1921, Malabar, Kerala):** Muslim tenant cultivators rose in armed rebellion against oppressive upper-caste Hindu landlords (*Janmis*) and British police. Initially aligned with Khilafat, it unfortunately acquired tragic communal overtones.
* **Assam & Bengal Strikes:** Massive tea-garden coolie walkouts in Assam and the legendary strike on the Assam-Bengal Railway and river steamer services organized under the leadership of **J.M. Sengupta**.
* **Akali Movement (Punjab):** Peaceful agitation to liberate historical Sikh Gurudwaras from corrupt, pro-British hereditary priests (*Mahants*), leading to the Sikh Gurdwaras Act 1925.

### 4. Chauri Chaura & The Abrupt Retreat (February 1922)
* **The Incident (February 4/5, 1922):**
  * At **Chauri Chaura**, a village in Gorakhpur district, United Provinces (UP), police fired upon a peaceful procession of Congress-Khilafat volunteers protesting liquor sales and food prices.
  * An enraged crowd of peasants retaliated, chased the police into the police station, locked the doors, and set it ablaze. **22 policemen were burnt alive**.
* **The Bardoli Resolution (February 12, 1922):**
  * Horrified by the eruption of violence, Gandhi immediately summoned the Congress Working Committee at **Bardoli** and unilaterally called off the entire Non-Cooperation Movement.
  * Gandhi maintained that Indians were not yet spiritually trained in non-violence, and that allowing the movement to become violent would give the British armed state the moral and military justification to crush it with artillery.
* **National Shock and Division:**
  * Nationalist leaders were devastated. **Subhas Chandra Bose** called the withdrawal a *"national calamity"*. **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Motilal Nehru** expressed deep dismay from prison.
  * Taking advantage of the retreat, the British government arrested Gandhi on **March 10, 1922**. In a historic trial, **Judge C.N. Broomfield** sentenced him to 6 years imprisonment.
  * The collapse of NCM fractured the Hindu-Muslim alliance, led to the disintegration of the Khilafat movement (especially after **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk** abolished the Ottoman Caliphate entirely in 1924), and spawned the formation of the **Swaraj Party** in 1923 by C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru.1a:T466,### How Non-Cooperation Pierced the "Colonial Consent"

Colonial rule never depended solely on British bayonets; it rested upon the psychological cooperation and perceived indispensability of the Raj in the minds of the colonized:

$$\text{Colonial Power} = \frac{\text{Military Coercion}}{\text{Voluntary Native Compliance}}$$

   * In *Hind Swaraj* (1909), Gandhi wrote: *"The English have not taken India; we have given it to them. They are not in India because of their strength, but because we keep them. If we cease to cooperate, British rule will collapse in a year."*
   * By refusing to attend courts, schools, council chambers, and revenue offices, Non-Cooperation demonstrated in practice that the British Raj was an illusion maintained by Indian collaboration.

2. **The Eradication of Fear (*Nirbhayata*):**
   * As Jawaharlal Nehru remarked in *The Discovery of India*, the supreme gift of Gandhi in 1920 was **fearlessness**. The intellectual and constitutional debate between Mahatma Gandhi and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar at Poona was one of the most consequential turning points in modern Indian jurisprudence:

1. **The Mechanism of Separate Electorates (The MacDonald Formula):**
   * Under separate electorates, a legislative constituency is divided purely along communal/caste lines.
   * Only voters belonging to the Depressed Classes could vote to elect a Depressed Class candidate.
   * Gandhi argued that this would legally segregate untouchables from mainstream Hindu society forever, perpetuating social untouchability by giving it a permanent constitutional boundary.

2. **The Mechanism of Joint Electorates with Reservation (The Poona Pact Formula):**
   * Under joint electorates, the entire electorate (caste Hindus, Depressed Classes, and others) votes together.
   * However, the seat is **reserved exclusively** for a candidate from the Depressed Classes.
   * **The Primary Election Sub-system:** To ensure genuine representation, the Poona Pact devised a two-tier electoral filter:
     $$\text{Depressed Class Voters Only} \xrightarrow{\text{Primary Election}} \text{Select a panel of 4 candidates} \xrightarrow{\text{General Joint Electorate}} \text{Final Winner}$$
   * The candidate securing the highest votes from the entire joint community in the final election was elected.
   * **Quantitative Outcome:**
     * Provincial Assembly seats for Depressed Classes doubled: from **71** under the Communal Award to **147 seats** under the Poona Pact (e.g., Bengal 30, Madras 30, UP 20, Central Provinces 20, Bombay 15, Bihar/Orissa 18, Punjab 8, Assam 7).
