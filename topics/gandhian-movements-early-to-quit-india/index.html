01INTERMEDIATE5 Blocks • 2 MCQs

The Advent of Mahatma Gandhi: South African Laboratory, Champaran (1917), Ahmedabad (1918), Kheda (1918) & Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)

The entry and crystallization of Gandhian mass satyagraha technique in India: Return from South Africa (Jan 9, 1915 - Pravasi Bharatiya Divas), Gopal Krishna Gokhale as political guru, Sabarmati Ashram; Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar, first Civil Disobedience, Rajkumar Shukla, Tinkathia 3/20th system of indigo cultivation); Ahmedabad Mill Strike (March 1918, first Hunger Strike, 35% wage bonus demand for plague allowance); Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat, first Non-Cooperation, crop failure remission, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel); Rowlatt Act (Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act 1919, "No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal"); Rowlatt Satyagraha; and the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (April 13, 1919, General Dyer, Hunter Commission, Rabindranath Tagore renouncing Knighthood).

02ADVANCED5 Blocks • 2 MCQs

The First Pan-Indian Mass Upsurge: Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922), The Khilafat Alliance & Chauri Chaura

The unprecedented forging of Hindu-Muslim unity and mass mobilization against colonial rule: The Khilafat Question (dismemberment of Ottoman Caliphate by Treaty of Sevres, Ali Brothers - Shaukat Ali and Mohammad Ali, Maulana Azad); Special Calcutta Session (Sept 1920) and Nagpur Session (Dec 1920, C. Vijayaraghavachariar president: Congress goal transformed to Swaraj through peaceful means, linguistic provincial congress committees, ₹1 crore Tilak Swaraj Fund); Programme of Non-Cooperation (surrender of titles, boycott of government schools, law courts, foreign cloth, picketing of liquor shops); massive participation of students, women, and peasants (Eka and Awadh Kisan Sabhas); Baba Ramchandra; and the abrupt withdrawal of the movement following the Chauri Chaura incident (Feb 5, 1922, 22 policemen burnt alive in Gorakhpur), leading to the Bardoli Resolution and Gandhi's imprisonment.

03ADVANCED5 Blocks • 2 MCQs

Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934): Salt Satyagraha, Round Table Conferences, Communal Award & The Poona Pact

The second nationwide civil disobedience campaign: Lahore Congress (Dec 1929, Jawaharlal Nehru president, Purna Swaraj declaration, Jan 26, 1930 as Independence Day); Gandhi's 11-point ultimatum to Lord Irwin; Dandi March (March 12 to April 6, 1930, 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi with 78 volunteers, breaking the Salt Law); Dharasana Satyagraha (Sarojini Naidu, Webb Miller report); First Round Table Conference (1930, boycotted by INC); Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact, March 5, 1931: release of political prisoners, suspension of CDM); Karachi Congress (March 1931, Sardar Patel, Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme); Second Round Table Conference (1931, Gandhi sole INC representative, failure on communal question); Ramsay MacDonald's Communal Award (Aug 1932, separate electorates for Depressed Classes); Gandhi's fast-unto-death in Yerwada Jail; and the landmark **Poona Pact (Sept 24, 1932)** between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and caste Hindu leaders (Madan Mohan Malaviya signed for caste Hindus), abandoning separate electorates in exchange for increased reserved seats (from 71 to 147 in provincial legislatures).

04ADVANCED5 Blocks • 2 MCQs

The August Kranti: The Quit India Movement (1942), Parallel Governments & Total Mass Mobilization

The climactic final mass rebellion of the Gandhian era: Failure of the Cripps Mission (March 1942, "post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"); Japanese advance to the borders of Assam; All-India Congress Committee (AICC) meeting at Gowalia Tank Ground, Bombay; adoption of the Quit India Resolution (August 8, 1942); Gandhi's immortal call to the nation: **"Do or Die" (*Karo ya Maro*)**; Operation Zero Hour (midnight Aug 8–9 arrest of entire top Congress leadership including Gandhi, Nehru, Patel); spontaneous leaderless mass eruption across India; underground resistance network (Aruna Asaf Ali hoisting the flag at Gowalia Tank, Ram Manohar Lohia, Jayaprakash Narayan escaping Hazaribagh jail, Usha Mehta running Congress Secret Underground Radio); establishment of **Parallel Governments (*Prati Sarkar*)**: Ballia (Chittu Pandey, first parallel govt), Tamluk (Jatiya Sarkar in Midnapore, Bengal, Matangini Hazra martyrdom), and Satara (Nana Patil, longest running Prati Sarkar); and violent British state repression involving machine-gun strafing from aircraft.
