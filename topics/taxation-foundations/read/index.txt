12:T421,The distinction between Direct and Indirect taxes is tested in every competitive examination:

| Feature | Direct Tax | Indirect Tax |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Imposed directly on the income, wealth, or profit of an individual or firm | Imposed on the manufacture, sale, or consumption of goods and services |
| **Impact vs Incidence** | **Impact and Incidence fall on the SAME entity** (Cannot be shifted) | **Impact and Incidence fall on DIFFERENT entities** (Shifted to consumer) |
| **Tax Shifting** | **STRICTLY IMPOSSIBLE** (You cannot shift your personal income tax to your neighbour) | **POSSIBLE & COMMON** (Seller embeds tax into final retail selling price) |
| **Equity Nature** | Inherently **Progressive** (Higher tax rates for higher income slabs) | Inherently **Regressive** (A poor person and a billionaire pay the exact same ₹18 GST on a bar of soap) |
| **Examples in India** | Personal Income Tax, Corporation Tax, Capital Gains Tax, STT | Goods and Services Tax (GST), Customs Duties, Union Excise Duties (Petroleum) |13:T4fd,The exact formula governing tax burden sharing is:

$$\frac{\Delta P_{\text{buyer}}}{\Delta P_{\text{seller}}} = \frac{E_s}{|E_d|}$$

- **Case 1: Perfectly Inelastic Demand ($E_d = 0$, e.g., Life-saving insulin)**:
  - Consumers have no choice but to buy. **Buyers bear $100\%$ of the tax burden**.
- **Case 2: Perfectly Inelastic Supply ($E_s = 0$, e.g., Fixed land supply)**:
  - Sellers cannot reduce supply or leave the market. **Sellers bear $100\%$ of the tax burden**.

```
                    DEADWEIGHT LOSS (HARBERGER'S TRIANGLE)
      Price (₹)
          ▲
          │             / Supply + Tax
      P_b │────────────/─┐
          │           /  │\  ◄── HARBERGER'S DEADWEIGHT LOSS TRIANGLE
      P_0 │- - - - - / - ┼ - - - (Loss of CS & PS not collected by Govt)
          │         /    │/
      P_s │────────/─────┘
          │       / Demand
          └──────┴───────┴────────► Quantity (Q)
                 Q_tax   Q_0
```

*Key Takeaway*: Because $DWL \propto t^2$, doubling the tax rate **quadruples** the deadweight economic loss! This explains why economists recommend a **broad tax base with low, moderate tax rates**.14:T45f,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Tax Buoyancy vs Elasticity**: **Tax Buoyancy includes discretionary changes** (rate hikes, new cesses). **Tax Elasticity excludes discretionary changes** (pure automatic response).
- **Trap 2: Regressive Tax Definition**: A regressive tax does NOT mean rich people pay less nominal money; it means the tax represents a **lower percentage of income** as income increases.
- **Trap 3: Adam Smith's Canons**: The four original canons are **Equality, Certainty, Convenience, Economy**. (Canons like Productivity and Elasticity were added later by Charles Bastable).
- **Trap 4: Benefit Principle vs Ability to Pay**: The Benefit Principle (paying based on benefits received, like highway tolls) cannot be used to fund redistributive welfare, because the poor who need welfare have zero ability to pay for it.

### Cross-Exam Focus
- **RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A**: Tax buoyancy vs elasticity formulas, Harberger efficiency, and progressive rate mathematics.
