15:T4b3,### 1. Shape of Earth (Poles vs Equator)
Since Earth is flattened at poles: $R_{\text{equator}} > R_{\text{pole}}$ (by $\approx 21 \, \text{km}$).
$$g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \implies \mathbf{g_{\text{pole}} > g_{\text{equator}}}$$
*(A gold bar bought at the equator by weight weighs slightly more at the poles).*

### 2. Variation with Altitude (Height $h$)
At height $h$ above Earth surface ($h \ll R$):
$$g_h = g \left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right) \implies g \text{ decreases as you go higher}.$$

### 3. Variation with Depth (Depth $d$)
Inside a mine at depth $d$ below surface:
$$g_d = g \left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right) \implies g \text{ decreases as you go deeper}.$$
- **At Earth's Centre ($d = R$)**: $g_{\text{centre}} = g(1 - 1) = \mathbf{0}$.

### 4. Apparent Weight in an Elevator / Lift (Mass $m$)
- **Lift at rest or moving at uniform speed ($a = 0$)**: $W_{\text{app}} = mg$ (Normal weight).
- **Lift accelerating UPWARD with acceleration $a$**: $W_{\text{app}} = m(g + a)$ (Feels heavier).
- **Lift accelerating DOWNWARD with acceleration $a$**: $W_{\text{app}} = m(g - a)$ (Feels lighter).
- **Free Fall (Cable snaps, $a = g$)**: $W_{\text{app}} = m(g - g) = \mathbf{0}$ (State of **Weightlessness**).16:T671,### Kepler's Three Laws of Planetary Motion

| Kepler's Law | Formal Statement | Physical / Mathematical Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Law: Law of Orbits** | Every planet moves in an **elliptical orbit** with the Sun located at one of the two foci. | Ellipse eccentricity ($e$). Sun is at one focus; the other focus is empty space. |
| **Second Law: Law of Areas** | The radius vector joining the planet to the Sun sweeps out **equal areas in equal intervals of time** ($dA/dt = \text{constant}$). | Direct consequence of **Conservation of Angular Momentum** ($L = m v r = \text{const}$). Planet moves **fastest at Perihelion** (closest to Sun) and **slowest at Aphelion** (farthest from Sun). |
| **Third Law: Law of Periods** | The square of the orbital period ($T$) is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis ($r$): $\mathbf{T^2 \propto r^3}$. | $T^2 = \left(\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}\right) r^3$. Outer planets take vastly longer to complete one orbit (Mercury $= 88 \text{ days}$, Neptune $= 165 \text{ years}$). |

### Solar System Architecture & Key Astronomical Facts:
- **Inner Rocky / Terrestrial Planets**: Mercury (smallest, no atmosphere), Venus ("Morning/Evening Star", hottest planet due to $96\% \, CO_2$ runaway greenhouse, rotates retrograde East-to-West), Earth, Mars ("Red Planet" due to iron oxide).
- **Asteroid Belt**: Millions of rocky remnants orbiting between **Mars and Jupiter**.
- **Outer Gas Giants**: Jupiter (largest planet, Great Red Spot storm, moons Ganymede/Europa), Saturn (spectacular ring system, Titan moon).
- **Ice Giants**: Uranus (rotates on its side), Neptune (coldest, farthest).17:T699,### 1. Orbital Velocity ($v_o$)
A satellite of mass $m$ orbiting Earth at distance $r = R + h$:
$$\text{Centripetal Force} = \text{Gravitational Force} \implies \frac{m v_o^2}{r} = \frac{G M m}{r^2}$$
$$v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R + h}}$$
- Near Earth's surface ($h \approx 0$):
  $$v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}} = \sqrt{g R} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 6.4 \times 10^6} \approx \mathbf{7.92 \, \text{km/s}} \approx 8 \, \text{km/s}$$

### 2. Escape Velocity ($v_e$)
Minimum velocity to escape Earth's gravitational field entirely (total energy $= 0$):
$$\frac{1}{2} m v_e^2 - \frac{G M m}{R} = 0 \implies v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2 g R}$$
$$\mathbf{v_e = \sqrt{2} \cdot v_o \approx 1.414 \times 7.92 \approx 11.2 \, \text{km/s}}$$
- **Escape velocity on Moon**: $v_{e,\text{moon}} \approx 2.38 \, \text{km/s}$. Because gas molecules at lunar temperatures have thermal speeds exceeding $2.4 \, \text{km/s}$, gases escaped $\implies$ **The Moon has NO atmosphere**.

### 3. Geostationary vs Polar Satellite Orbits
- **Geostationary Orbit (GEO)**:
  - Altitude: $\mathbf{\approx 35,786 \, \text{km}}$ (often rounded to $36,000 \, \text{km}$).
  - Time Period: **24 Hours** (equal to Earth's rotation period).
  - Direction: **West to East** (same as Earth rotation).
  - Position: Parked directly over the **Equator**.
  - Application: Satellite TV (INSAT), telecommunications, continuous weather monitoring.
- **Polar / Low Earth Orbit (LEO)**:
  - Altitude: $500 - 800 \, \text{km}$; Time period: $\approx 90 - 100 \, \text{minutes}$.
  - Orbits North-South over poles while Earth spins beneath.
  - Application: Earth Remote Sensing (IRS), military reconnaissance, mapping.18:T447,### Top Space & Gravitation Traps:

2. **Escape Velocity vs Orbital Velocity Ratio**: $\frac{v_e}{v_o} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$. If a satellite's speed in circular orbit is increased by **$41.4\%$** (multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$), it escapes into deep space along a parabolic path.

In an enclosed hydraulic system filled with oil:
$$P_1 = P_2 \implies \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2} \implies F_2 = F_1 \left(\frac{A_2}{A_1}\right)$$
- If the output piston area $A_2$ is $100$ times larger than input piston $A_1$, applying a modest $100 \, \text{N}$ force on $A_1$ generates a massive **$10,000 \, \text{N}$ force** on $A_2$, lifting a heavy automobile with one foot!

### 2. Hydrostatic Depth Pressure
$$P = \rho g h$$
- Dams are built **much thicker at the bottom than at the top** because water pressure increases linearly with depth ($h$).
- Submarines must have thick titanium hulls to withstand extreme deep-sea pressure.

### 3. Atmospheric Pressure & Torricelli's Barometer (1643)
- Atmospheric pressure arises from the weight of the air column above Earth.
$$P_{\text{atm}} = \rho_{\text{Hg}} \cdot g \cdot h = (13,600 \, \text{kg/m}^3)(9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2)(0.76 \, \text{m}) = \mathbf{1.013 \times 10^5 \, \text{Pa}}$$
- **Why Mercury is Used in Barometers**: Mercury has extreme high density ($13.6 \, \text{g/cm}^3$), requiring only a compact $76 \, \text{cm}$ column. If water were used, atmospheric pressure would support a giant water column of **$10.33 \, \text{metres}$** high!1a:T4dc,### Archimedes' Principle Formula:
$$\mathbf{F_B = W_{\text{fluid displaced}} = V_{\text{submerged}} \cdot \rho_{\text{fluid}} \cdot g}$$

### The Three States of Immersion:

| Condition | Relationship between Forces | Densities | Physical Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Case 1: Sinking** | Weight of body $>$ Buoyant force ($W > F_B$). | $\rho_{\text{object}} > \rho_{\text{fluid}}$ | Object accelerates downward and **sinks to the bottom** (e.g. iron nail in water). |
| **Case 2: Neutral Buoyancy** | Weight of body $=$ Buoyant force ($W = F_B$). | $\rho_{\text{object}} = \rho_{\text{fluid}}$ | Object **floats fully submerged** anywhere inside the liquid (e.g. fish, submarine). |
| **Case 3: Floating with Partial Immersion** | Weight of body $=$ Buoyant force ($W = F_B$). | $\rho_{\text{object}} < \rho_{\text{fluid}}$ | Object **floats partially above surface** (e.g. wooden block, ice cube). |

### Fraction of Floating Volume Submerged:
$$\frac{V_{\text{submerged}}}{V_{\text{total}}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{object}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}$$
- Ice density $\rho_{\text{ice}} \approx 0.9 \, \text{g/cm}^3$, Water density $\rho_w = 1.0 \, \text{g/cm}^3 \implies \mathbf{90\%}$ of an iceberg lies hidden beneath the water line!1b:T583,### 1. Anomalous Expansion of Water at $4^\circ\text{C}$
- Unlike almost all substances that contract continuously upon cooling, water has **Maximum Density ($1.000 \, \text{g/cm}^3 = 1000 \, \text{kg/m}^3$) at exactly $4^\circ\text{C}$**.
- Below $4^\circ\text{C}$, water expands as it freezes into ice ($ho_{\text{ice}} = 0.917 \, \text{g/cm}^3$).
- **Aquatic Life Survival in Frozen Lakes**: Surface water cools to $0^\circ\text{C}$ and freezes into floating insulating ice, while denser $4^\circ\text{C}$ liquid water remains at the lake bottom, preserving fish and aquatic ecosystems.

### 2. Swimming in Fresh Water vs Ocean vs Dead Sea
- Salt water has higher density than fresh water ($\rho_{\text{sea}} \approx 1.025 \, \text{g/cm}^3$).
- **It is much easier to swim in the sea than in a fresh water swimming pool** because dense sea water provides greater upward buoyant force ($F_B = V \rho g$).
- In the **Dead Sea** (salinity ~34%, $\rho \approx 1.24 \, \text{g/cm}^3$), the buoyant force exceeds human body density ($~1.06 \, \text{g/cm}^3$), making it impossible for a human to sink.
