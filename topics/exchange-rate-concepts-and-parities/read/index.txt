11:T55f,To eliminate bilateral distortions, central banks construct trade-weighted multilateral indices:

### 1. Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER)
$$NEER = \\prod_{i=1}^{n} \\left( \\frac{e_i}{e_{i,0}} \\right)^{w_i} \\times 100$$
- $e_i$: Bilateral exchange rate of the Rupee against currency $i$.
- $w_i$: Trade weight of country $i$ in India's total trade (exports + imports), where $\\sum w_i = 1$.
- $n$: Number of partner countries (40-currency basket for broad index; 6-currency basket for major advanced peers).

### 2. Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER)
$$REER = \\prod_{i=1}^{n} \\left[ \\left( \\frac{e_i}{e_{i,0}} \\right) \\times \\left( \\frac{P}{P_i} \\right) \\right]^{w_i} \\times 100$$
- $P$: Domestic Consumer Price Index (CPI) of India.
- $P_i$: Consumer Price Index (CPI) of partner country $i$.

### 3. Interpreting REER Benchmark Values (Base = 100)
- **REER = 100**: Rupee is trading at fair real macroeconomic parity against trading partners.
- **REER > 100 (e.g., REER = 105)**: The Rupee is **Overvalued** by 5% in real terms. Indian goods are relatively more expensive abroad $\\implies$ **Loss of export price competitiveness**; imports become artificially cheaper.
- **REER < 100 (e.g., REER = 95)**: The Rupee is **Undervalued** by 5% in real terms. Indian goods are cheaper in global markets $\\implies$ **Gain in export price competitiveness**.12:T4fc,```

### Why RBI Uses Consumer Price Index (CPI) for REER
Prior to 2014, the RBI calculated REER using the Wholesale Price Index (WPI). Under Governor Raghuram Rajan and the Urjit Patel Committee, the RBI shifted to **CPI-based REER** because CPI captures the true retail purchasing power and cost of living that drives domestic labor wages.13:T511,International macroeconomics rests on three foundational parity conditions:

### 1. Absolute Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
$$S = \frac{P}{P^*}$$
- $S$: Spot exchange rate (Domestic currency per foreign currency unit, e.g. ₹/USD).
- $P$: Domestic price level; $P^*$: Foreign price level.
- *Core Claim*: 1 unit of currency converted at the spot rate buys the identical basket of goods in both countries (The Big Mac Index).

### 2. Relative Purchasing Power Parity (RPPP)
$$\frac{S_1 - S_0}{S_0} \approx \pi - \pi^*$$
- $\pi$: Domestic inflation rate; $\pi^*$: Foreign inflation rate.
- *Core Claim*: The percentage change in the exchange rate equals the inflation rate differential. If India's inflation is $6\%$ and US inflation is $2\%$, the Rupee should depreciate by approximately $4\%$ per year.

### 3. Covered Interest Rate Parity (CIP)
$$\frac{F - S}{S} = \frac{i - i^*}{1 + i^*} \approx i - i^*$$
- $F$: Forward exchange rate; $S$: Spot exchange rate.
- $i$: Domestic interest rate (e.g. 7% in India); $i^*$: Foreign interest rate (e.g. 5% in US).
- *Core Claim*: The forward currency premium/discount eliminates riskless foreign exchange arbitrage. If India's interest rates are 2% higher than US rates, the US Dollar must trade at a **2% Forward Premium** against the Rupee ($F > S$).14:T5d3,```

### Why PPP Fails in the Short Run
In reality, PPP does not hold in the short run due to: (1) Transport costs and import tariffs, (2) Non-tradable goods (haircuts, real estate), (3) Sticky nominal prices, and (4) Massive speculative cross-border capital flows ($> 90\%$ of daily forex turnover is financial investment, not goods trade).15:T42d,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Absolute vs Relative PPP**: Absolute PPP requires price levels to be identical ($S = P/P^*$). Relative PPP requires only that the *change* in exchange rate matches the inflation differential (%ΔS = π - π*). Relative PPP can hold even if Absolute PPP fails due to transport costs.
- **Trap 2: Forward Premium Rule**: If domestic interest rate ($i$) is HIGHER than foreign interest rate ($i^*$), the foreign currency must trade at a **Forward Premium** ($F > S$), and the domestic currency trades at a forward discount.
- **Trap 3: Market GDP vs PPP GDP Ranking**: India is the **5th largest economy** by Market Exchange Rate GDP (surpassing UK), but the **3rd largest economy** by PPP GDP (surpassing Japan and Germany) due to the Balassa-Samuelson effect on cheap non-tradables.
