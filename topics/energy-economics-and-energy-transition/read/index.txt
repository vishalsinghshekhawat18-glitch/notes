12:T40b,The macroeconomic dynamics, external vulnerability channels, and institutional architecture of India's primary energy economy. Analyzes: (1) Primary Energy Matrix & Import Exposure (Crude oil import dependency at ~87–88%, Natural gas import dependency at ~50% via LNG, and Coal providing ~70–75% of grid electricity generation), (2) The Macroeconomic Oil Price Transmission Channel (a USD 10/barrel rise in crude oil prices widens Current Account Deficit [CAD] by ~0.4–0.5% of GDP, elevates CPI inflation by ~30–40 basis points, weakens the Rupee, and inflates fiscal fuel/fertilizer subsidies), (3) Strategic Petroleum Reserve Architecture (Indian Strategic Petroleum Reserves Ltd [ISPRL]: 5.33 Million Metric Tonnes [MMT] underground rock cavern storage across Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, and Padur, providing ~9.5 days of net crude import cover), and (4) Energy Pricing & Subsidy Reforms (transition from administered pricing mechanisms to daily market-linked pricing and targeted Direct Benefit Transfer for LPG under PAHAL).13:T50e,Imagine global oil prices spike by **USD 20 a barrel** because of geopolitical tensions in the Persian Gulf:
- What happens to a vegetable vendor in Delhi who has never seen an oil refinery in his life?

Here is the rapid **oil-price transmission chain**:
1. **External Sector (BoP & CAD)**: India imports ~88% of its crude oil. Paying for USD 90 oil drains India's foreign exchange reserves and inflates the trade deficit. The Rupee depreciates from ₹83 to ₹85 per US Dollar.
2. **Inflation (CPI & Transport)**: Diesel prices jump by ₹6 per liter. Truck freight operators raise transport charges by 15%. The price of potatoes, tomatoes, and milk rises across city markets (**Cost-Push Inflation**).
3. **Fiscal Balance (Government Deficit)**: The government subsidizes cooking gas (LPG) and fertilizers. As feedstock petroleum prices rise, the central fertilizer and fuel subsidy bill explodes by thousands of crores.
4. **Monetary Policy (Interest Rates)**: The RBI Monetary Policy Committee sees inflation breaching the 4% target and **hikes the Repo Rate**, raising home loan and business EMI costs nationwide.

Energy is not just another economic commodity; **it is the master macroeconomic input that dictates India's inflation, currency value, fiscal deficit, and interest rates**.14:T637,### 1. Primary Energy Matrix & Import Exposure Profile

| Energy Commodity | Share in India's Energy / Electricity Mix | Import Dependency Level | Key Supplying Nations & Pricing Structure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Crude Oil** | ~30% of Primary Energy; 99% of Transport fuel. | **87.7% Import Dependent** (~232 MMT imported). | Russia (top supplier post-2022 ~35–40%), Iraq, Saudi Arabia, UAE. Priced in USD via Indian Basket (Brent/Dubai/Oman). |
| **2. Coal** | **~70–75% of Grid Electricity Generation**. | **~20–25% Import Dependent** (High-grade coking coal for steel). | Domestic production by Coal India Ltd (~80%); imported coking coal from Australia, South Africa, Indonesia. |
| **3. Natural Gas** | ~6.5% of Primary Energy (Goal: 15% by 2030). | **~50% Import Dependent** (Imported as Liquefied Natural Gas - LNG). | Qatar, USA, UAE. Used for city gas distribution (CNG/PNG) and fertilizer plants. |
| **4. Renewable Power** | **~44–45% of Total Installed Capacity** (~190+ GW non-fossil). | Domestic generation (Solar equipment partially imported). | Solar, Wind, Large Hydro, Nuclear, Bio-power. |\n
### 2. Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPR) Infrastructure (Phase-1 & Phase-2)
- **Phase-1 Established Capacity (5.33 MMT ~9.5 days crude cover)**:
  - **Visakhapatnam (Andhra Pradesh)**: 1.33 MMT capacity.
  - **Mangaluru (Karnataka)**: 1.50 MMT capacity.
  - **Padur (Karnataka)**: 2.50 MMT capacity.
- **Phase-2 Approved Commercial Expansion (6.5 MMT under PPP model)**:
  - **Chandikhol (Odisha)**: 4.0 MMT.
  - **Padur (Karnataka - Expansion)**: 2.5 MMT.15:T591,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: SPR Locations in Phase-1**: The three operational Phase-1 underground rock cavern locations are **Visakhapatnam (AP), Mangaluru (Karnataka), and Padur (Karnataka)**. Chandikhol (Odisha) is in **Phase-2**, NOT Phase-1.
- **Trap 2: Crude vs Coal Import Dependency**: India is **~88% import dependent on Crude Oil**, but only **~20–25% import dependent on Coal** (primarily metallurgical coking coal for steel, as thermal coal is domestically mined by Coal India).
- **Trap 3: Natural Gas 2030 Target**: India's stated policy target is to raise the share of Natural Gas in the primary energy mix from ~6.5% to **15% by 2030**.

- **UPSC APFC**: Energy security policy, strategic petroleum reserve legal framework under OIDB, and subsidy rationalization under PAHAL.16:T478,Imagine the national electricity grid on a hot April afternoon:
- At **1:00 PM**, Rajasthan and Gujarat solar panels flood the national grid with **70,000 MW of cheap clean electricity**. The wholesale price of electricity drops to nearly zero.
- At **7:00 PM**, the sun sets, but 1.4 billion Indians return home, switch on air conditioners, TVs, and induction stoves (**Peak Electricity Demand**).

This creates the famous **"Duck Curve"** problem:
- Solar energy vanishes exactly when the nation needs electricity the most.
- If you don't have massive **Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)** or **Pumped Hydro Dams**, you are forced to burn thousands of tons of dirty coal to prevent blackouts.

Furthermore, what happens to **Dhanbad in Jharkhand**, where 80% of local families depend on Coal India paychecks?

If India closes coal mines without creating new green manufacturing jobs, entire mining regions will collapse into catastrophic poverty.

This is why India's green growth strategy balances **₹19,744 Crore Green Hydrogen incentives** with a **"Just Transition"** that protects coal-mining workers and invests in energy storage.17:T61c,### 1. India's "Panchamrit" Five Climate Commitments (Glasgow COP26 & Updated NDCs)

### 2. National Green Hydrogen Mission (SIGHT) Architecture
- **Total Budget Outlay**: **₹19,744 Crore** (Approved January 2023).
- **Target by 2030**: Minimum **5 Million Metric Tonnes (MMT)** of Green Hydrogen per annum.
- **SIGHT Sub-Schemes**: 
  - Component I: ₹4,440 Crore for domestic Electrolyser Manufacturing.
  - Component II: ₹13,050 Crore for Green Hydrogen Production.
- **Decarbonization Impact**: Replaces grey hydrogen (made from imported natural gas) in **oil refineries, urea fertilizer plants, and steel mills**.18:T4fe,### 1. The Intermittency "Duck Curve" and Storage Economics
```
  Grid Load (GW)
     │              Evening Peak Demand Surge (7 PM - 10 PM)
     │                       /\  <── Dispatched from BESS & Pumped Hydro
     │                      /  \
     │   Solar Midday Drop /    \     Normal Baseload
     │  (The Belly of Duck)      \──────────────────────────
     └────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Time (24 Hrs)
        6 AM       12 Noon       6 PM        12 Midnight
```
- **Levelized Cost of Storage (LCOS)**: The economic barrier to 100% renewables. Falling lithium-ion battery and pumped-storage capital costs are critical to making Round-the-Clock (RTC) clean power competitive with coal baseload.

### 2. Statutory Renewable Purchase Obligations (RPO)
Under the Electricity Act 2003 and Ministry of Power mandates, all electricity distribution companies (DISCOMs) and open-access consumers are legally required to purchase a minimum percentage of electricity from renewable sources (Wind RPO, Hydro RPO, and Other RPO), progressing from ~27% in 2023 to $>43\%$ by 2030.19:T738,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Net-Zero Target Year**: India's target for Net-Zero greenhouse gas emissions is strictly **2070** (NOT 2050 as committed by Western nations or 2060 as committed by China).
- **Trap 2: Emissions Intensity Baseline Year**: The 45% reduction in GDP emissions intensity by 2030 is measured against the **2005 baseline year**, NOT 2015 or 1990.
- **Trap 3: Green Hydrogen Production Target**: The National Green Hydrogen Mission targets **5 MMT per annum by 2030**, with a budget outlay of **₹19,744 Crore**.
- **Trap 4: Green vs Grey vs Blue Hydrogen**:
  - *Grey Hydrogen*: Produced from fossil fuels (natural gas) via Steam Methane Reforming $\implies$ emits $CO_2$.
  - *Blue Hydrogen*: Produced from fossil fuels with Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS).
  - *Green Hydrogen*: Produced strictly by splitting water via electrolysis using **100% renewable electricity** $\implies$ ZERO emissions.
