Rajasthan State Budget Architecture: State Own Tax Revenue (SOTR), Mining Royalties & Central Fiscal Flows

The statutory revenue structure and expenditure profile of the State Government of Rajasthan under the Constitution of India and State financial rules. Analyzes the four revenue pillars: (1) State's Own Tax Revenue (SOTR: State GST / SGST, State Excise on potable alcohol, Stamp Duty and Registration fees, Sales Tax/VAT on petroleum products, and Motor Vehicle Tax), (2) State's Own Non-Tax Revenue (SONTR: predominantly royalties and dead rent from Major and Minor Minerals, oil and gas exploration in Barmer basin), (3) Share in Central Taxes (Devolution from 15th FC), and (4) Grants-in-Aid from Centre (CSS, PDRD, and disaster funds). Examines Rajasthan's expenditure rigidities across power discom subsidies, salaries, and interest burdens.

Intuition & Context

Where Does the Rajasthan Government Get and Spend Its Money?

Imagine the State Treasury in Jaipur:

  • Where does the money come from?

    1. Taxes paid by Rajasthan citizens (SOTR): When you buy a shirt in Jaipur (SGST), buy petrol at a pump (Rajasthan VAT), purchase land (Stamp Duty), or buy liquor (State Excise).
    2. Gifts from Mother Earth (Non-Tax Mining Royalties): Rajasthan is the mineral powerhouse of India (Zinc, Marble, Lead, and Barmer Crude Oil royalties!).
    3. Transfers from New Delhi: 15th Finance Commission tax share and Centrally Sponsored Scheme funds.

  • Where does the money go?

    • Salaries of teachers, doctors, and police; pensions of retired government servants; massive interest payments on past debt; and electricity subsidies to farmers to keep state discoms running!

Mechanism & Operational Rule

Expenditure Profile & Power Sector Subsidy Burden

Why does Rajasthan face acute budgetary rigidity?

  1. The "Committed Expenditure" Squeeze:
    • Salaries & Allowances: Over 25−30%25-30%25−30% of revenue receipts.
    • Pensions: Over 12−15%12-15%12−15% of revenue receipts.
    • Interest Payments: Over 18−20%18-20%18−20% of revenue receipts.
    • Total Committed Outlays alone consume over 55−60%55-60%55−60% of all revenue!
  2. The Discom Electricity Subsidy Burden:
    • To provide subsidized/free electricity to farmers (agricultural power) and domestic consumers, the State Government disburses over ₹15,000 to ₹20,000 Crore annually in tariff subsidies to Jaipur, Jodhpur, and Ajmer Vidyut Vitran Nigams (Discoms).
  3. CapEx Squeeze: After paying committed expenses and power subsidies, capital expenditure (CapEx on roads, canal networks, and industrial parks) is constrained to approximately 12−15%12-15%12−15% of total expenditure.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Mining Royalties Classification: Mining Royalties are State's Own NON-TAX Revenue (SONTR), NOT tax revenue!
  • Trap 2: Major Mineral Royalty Power: Under the MMDR Act, 1957, the Central Government fixes royalty rates for Major Minerals, but the State Government collects and retains 100% of the royalty revenue.
  • Trap 3: Rajasthan's 15th FC Share: Rajasthan receives ~6.026% of the 41% Divisible Pool under the 15th Finance Commission formula (benefiting from its large geographic area of 10.4% of India).
  • Trap 4: Alcohol Taxation: Alcohol for human consumption is taxed exclusively by the State via State Excise Duty (Entry 51 List II) and State VAT; it is strictly excluded from GST (Article 366(12A)).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RPSC RAS (Core Priority): In-depth questions in RAS Mains Paper 1 (Unit 1: Economy of Rajasthan): 2-mark definitions of SOTR / Mining Royalties; 5-mark question on Revenue Receipts Composition; 10-mark essay on Fiscal challenges of Rajasthan.
  • RBI Grade B & NABARD Grade A: Sub-national public finances, state discom liabilities, and state tax buoyancy.

Rajasthan Fiscal Management: Rajasthan FRBM Act 2005, Debt-GSDP Dynamics & The State Finance Commission

The statutory fiscal discipline and local body devolution architecture in Rajasthan. Analyzes: (1) The Rajasthan Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2005 (statutory fiscal deficit ceiling of 3% of GSDP, revenue deficit elimination targets, and borrowing ceilings regulated under Article 293(3) by the Ministry of Finance), (2) State Public Debt and Debt-to-GSDP dynamics (SDLs / State Development Loans, UDAY scheme legacy debt, and discom liabilities), and (3) The Rajasthan State Finance Commission constituted under Articles 243-I and 243-Y of the Constitution (examining the 6th State Finance Commission headed by Pradyuman Singh: 6.75% devolution of net state tax revenues to Panchayati Raj Institutions and Urban Local Bodies).

Intuition & Context

Who Controls the State's Credit Card?

Can the Chief Minister of Rajasthan borrow unlimited money to build colleges or announce free welfare schemes?

No!

The State Government is bound by two powerful locks:

  1. The State's Own Statutory Law (Rajasthan FRBM Act, 2005): Legally caps the State's annual fiscal deficit at 3%3%3% of GSDP.
  2. The Central Government's Constitutional Gatekeeper (Article 293(3)): Because Rajasthan has outstanding loans from the Central Government, it cannot borrow one single rupee on the market (issuing State Development Loans / SDLs) without prior permission from the Union Ministry of Finance!

Furthermore, the State cannot keep all its tax money in Jaipur: under the Constitution, it must share a fixed percentage ($6.75%$ under the 6th SFC) with village Panchayats and city Municipalities!

Mechanism & Operational Rule

The 6th Rajasthan State Finance Commission (2020–2025)

The constitutional mechanism for local body empowerment under Articles 243-I and 243-Y:

1. Constitutional Mandate

  • Constituted by the Governor of Rajasthan every 5 years.
  • Recommends the principles governing the devolution of state tax revenues to Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs).

2. Key Recommendations of the 6th SFC (Chaired by Pradyuman Singh)

  • Overall Devolution Quantum: $6.75%$ of the State's Net Own Tax Revenue (SOTR).
  • Rural vs Urban Distribution (Based on 2011 Census population proportions $75.1% : 24.9%$):
    • Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs): $75.1%$ of the devolved fund.
      • PRI 3-Tier Split: Zila Parishad (5%5%5%), Panchayat Samiti (20%20%20%), Gram Panchayat (75%75%75% - largest share directly at grass-roots!).
    • Urban Local Bodies (ULBs): $24.9%$ of the devolved fund.
      • ULB Tier Split: Municipal Corporations / Nagar Nigam (40%40%40%), Municipal Councils / Nagar Parishad (20%20%20%), Municipalities / Nagar Palika (40%40%40%).
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: State Finance Commission Articles: PRI Finance Commission is under Article 243-I; Municipality Finance Commission is under Article 243-Y (both tasks are performed by the same single State Finance Commission).
  • Trap 2: 6th SFC Devolution Percentage: The 6th SFC recommended 6.75% of Net Own Tax Revenue (NOT gross total revenue!).
  • Trap 3: PRI Distribution Ratios in 6th SFC: Gram Panchayats receive 75% of the rural share, Panchayat Samitis receive 20%, and Zila Parishads receive 5%.
  • Trap 4: Article 293(3) Consent Rule: A State Government CANNOT raise market loans (SDLs) without the consent of the Centre if any part of a central loan is still outstanding.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RPSC RAS (Top Priority): High yield for RAS Prelims and Mains (Paper 1 & Paper 3): 2-mark definitions of 6th SFC / SDLs; 5-mark question on 6th SFC Devolution Formula (6.75%, 75.1:24.9 split); 10-mark question on Rajasthan FRBM Act 2005 & Debt Management.
  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: State Development Loans (SDL) auction mechanisms, spreads over central G-Secs, and UDAY power discom debt legacy.
