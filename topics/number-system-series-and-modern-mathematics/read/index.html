Number System: Divisibility Rules, Prime Factorization, HCF-LCM & Remainders

Structural arithmetic properties: universal divisibility tests (2 to 19), prime factorization, factor counting formulas, HCF-LCM product and circular tolling models, and remainder theorem frameworks.

Core Principle

The Number Theoretic Foundation of Banking Quant

Number properties provide the underlying engine for arithmetic problem-solving, option verification, and remainder mechanics across Banking Prelims and Mains.

Key components include:

  1. Universal Divisibility Tests: Recognizing whether multi-digit integers are divisible by prime and composite factors ($7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 72, 88, 99$) using modular arithmetic and alternating sum rules.
  2. HCF & LCM Core Models: Understanding HCF as the greatest common measure and LCM as the minimal periodic synchronization cycle.
  3. Factor & Unit Digit Cyclicity: Calculating total divisors, odd/even divisors, and tracking modular powers of $2, 3, 7, 8$ using cyclicity of 4.
SYSTEMATIC CATALOGUE

Divisibility Invariants, HCF-LCM Theorems & Factorization

1. Master Divisibility Rules

  • Divisibility by $3$ and $9: Sum of digits must be divisible by $3 or $9$.
  • Divisibility by $4$ and $8: Last $2 digits must be divisible by $4; last $3 digits divisible by $8$.
  • Divisibility by $7: Double the last digit and subtract from the remaining number; result must be divisible by $7.
  • Divisibility by $11: Difference between the \sum of digits at odd places and \sum of digits at even places must be $0 or a multiple of $11$.
  • Divisibility by $13: Multiply the last digit by $4 and add to the remaining number; result must be divisible by $13$.
  • Composite Divisibility (Coprime Rule): To test divisibility by N=p×qN = p \times qN=p×q where gcd(p,q)=1gcd(p, q) = 1gcd(p,q)=1, the number must independently satisfy divisibility for both ppp and qqq (e.g. $72 = 8 imes 9; 88 = 8 imes 11; 99 = 9 imes 11$).

2. Prime Factorization & Divisor Formulas

If N=pa⋅qb⋅rcN = p^a \cdot q^b \cdot r^cN=pa⋅qb⋅rc where p,q,rp, q, rp,q,r are prime numbers:

  • Total Number of Factors: F(N)=(a+1)(b+1)(c+1)F(N) = (a + 1)(b + 1)(c + 1)F(N)=(a+1)(b+1)(c+1)
  • Sum of Factors: S(N)=(pa+1−1p−1)(qb+1−1q−1)(rc+1−1r−1)S(N) = \left(\frac{p^{a+1}-1}{p-1}\right) \left(\frac{q^{b+1}-1}{q-1}\right) \left(\frac{r^{c+1}-1}{r-1}\right)S(N)=(p−1pa+1−1​)(q−1qb+1−1​)(r−1rc+1−1​)
  • Number of Ways to Express as Product of Two Factors: $ rac{1}{2} F(N)$ (if NNN is not a square); $ rac{F(N)+1}{2}$ (if NNN is a perfect square).

3-Tier Worked Problem: HCF-LCM Remainder Modeling

3-Tier Worked Problem

Problem: Find the least number which when divided by $12, 16, 24,$ and $36$ leaves remainders $8, 12, 20,$ and $32$ respectively.

  • Tier 1 (First Principles):
    1. Calculate differences between divisors and remainders:
      • $12 - 8 = 4$
      • $16 - 12 = 4$
      • $24 - 20 = 4$
      • $36 - 32 = 4$
    2. Notice the common constant difference d=4d = 4d=4.
    3. Compute $ ext{LCM}(12, 16, 24, 36)$:
      • $12 = 2^2 imes 3$
      • $16 = 2^4$
      • $24 = 2^3 imes 3$
      • $36 = 2^2 imes 3^2$
      • $ ext{LCM} = 2^4 imes 3^2 = 16 imes 9 = 144$.
    4. The required number is N=LCM−d=144−4=140N = \text{LCM} - d = 144 - 4 = \mathbf{140}N=LCM−d=144−4=140.
  • Tier 2 (Banking Standard): Identify Constant Difference Model   ⟹  N=LCM(12,16,24,36)−4=144−4=140\implies N = \text{LCM}(12,16,24,36) - 4 = 144 - 4 = 140⟹N=LCM(12,16,24,36)−4=144−4=140.
  • Tier 3 (10-Second Option Elimination): Check options for divisibility: $140 + 4 = 144, which is a multiple of $36 and $16$. Matches instantly.

Number Series: Difference Trees, Geometric Multipliers & Wrong Number Diagnostics

Algorithmic pattern recognition in number series: level-1, level-2, and level-3 difference trees, geometric and alternating operations, decimal multiplier chains, and the 2-Delta1 / 3-Delta2 wrong number diagnostic protocol.

Core Principle

Algorithmic Number Series Decomposition

Number Series (Missing and Wrong Number Series) constitutes a mandatory 5-mark section in virtually every Banking Prelims examination.

Rather than guessing patterns randomly, series must be classified algorithmically based on their Growth Rate Morphology:

  1. Linear / Gentle Expansion: Governed by Difference Trees (Delta1,Delta2,Delta3Delta_1, Delta_2, Delta_3Delta1​,Delta2​,Delta3​).
  2. Steep / Exponential Expansion: Governed by Multiplication Chains (Tn=Tn−1×k±mT_n = T_{n-1} \times k \pm mTn​=Tn−1​×k±m) or powers (n2±c,n3±cn^2 \pm c, n^3 \pm cn2±c,n3±c).
  3. U-Turn / Decay-Growth Patterns: Governed by Decimal Multipliers ($ imes 0.5, imes 1.0, imes 1.5, imes 2.0$).
SYSTEMATIC CATALOGUE

The Difference Tree Algorithm & Wrong Number Diagnostics

1. The Step-Difference Hierarchy

  • Level 1 (Delta1Delta_1Delta1​): Compute consecutive differences: dn=Tn−Tn−1d_n = T_{n} - T_{n-1}dn​=Tn​−Tn−1​.
    • If Delta1Delta_1Delta1​ is constant   ⟹  \implies⟹ Arithmetic Progression (APAPAP).
    • If Delta1Delta_1Delta1​ consists of squares ($1, 4, 9, 16, 25 dots) or cubes ($1, 8, 27, 64 \dots)   ⟹  \implies⟹ Quadratic/Cubic gap series.
    • If Delta1Delta_1Delta1​ consists of prime numbers ($2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 dots$)   ⟹  \implies⟹ Prime difference series.
  • Level 2 (Delta2Delta_2Delta2​): If Delta1Delta_1Delta1​ is not constant, compute differences of differences: Delta2=dn−dn−1Delta_2 = d_{n} - d_{n-1}Delta2​=dn​−dn−1​.
    • Constant Delta2  ⟹  Delta_2 \impliesDelta2​⟹ Underlying quadratic polynomial (an2+bn+can^2 + bn + can2+bn+c).
  • Level 3 (Delta3Delta_3Delta3​): Constant Delta3  ⟹  Delta_3 \impliesDelta3​⟹ Underlying cubic polynomial.

2. Multiplicative & Alternating Series Recognition

  • Ratio Test: Look at the ratio between the last two large terms: $ rac{T_n}{T_{n-1}}$.
    • If the ratio is roughly $2.9 implies$ test $ imes 3 pm k$.
    • If the ratio grows as $2, 3, 4, 5 implies$ test $ imes 1 + 1, imes 2 + 2, imes 3 + 3, imes 4 + 4$.
  • Decimal Multiplier Signature: If a series drops on term 2 and then increases steadily:
    exte.g.12,7,8,13.5,29,75  ⟹  ×0.5+1,×1+1,×1.5+1.5,×2+2,×2.5+2.5ext{e.g. } 12, 7, 8, 13.5, 29, 75 \implies \times 0.5 + 1, \times 1 + 1, \times 1.5 + 1.5, \times 2 + 2, \times 2.5 + 2.5exte.g.12,7,8,13.5,29,75⟹×0.5+1,×1+1,×1.5+1.5,×2+2,×2.5+2.5

3. The Wrong Number Series Diagnostic Model

In a single-error sequence where term TkT_kTk​ is incorrect:

  • In Level 1 Difference (Delta1Delta_1Delta1​), exactly TWO consecutive differences (dk−1d_{k-1}dk−1​ and dkd_kdk​) will be anomalous.
  • In Level 2 Difference (Delta2Delta_2Delta2​), exactly THREE consecutive values will be anomalous.
  • Localization Invariant: The incorrect number in the original series is ALWAYS the term at the exact intersection of the two faulty Delta1Delta_1Delta1​ differences!

Modern Mathematics: Permutations, Combinations, Circular Arrangements & Probability

Combinatorial and probability frameworks: nPr vs nCr, repetition permutations, circular vs necklace arrangements, grouping method for togetherness, probability addition/multiplication axioms, and the At-Least-One rule.

Core Principle

Combinatorics & Probability Framework for Banking Exams

Modern Mathematics (Permutations, Combinations, and Probability) accounts for 2–4 high-yield questions in Banking Prelims and forms the backbone of advanced Caselet and Data Sufficiency problems in Mains.

The fundamental distinction lies in Order Dependency:

  • Permutation (PPP): Order MATTERS (Arrangements, Words, Digit Codes, Rank orders).
  • Combination (CCC): Order DOES NOT MATTER (Selections, Committees, Handshakes, Drawing balls from an urn).
SYSTEMATIC CATALOGUE

Master Combinatorial Formulas & Probability Invariants

1. Fundamental Permutation & Combination Formulas

  • Permutation (nPrn\text{P}_rnPr​): nPr=n!(n−r)!n\text{P}_r = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}nPr​=(n−r)!n!​
  • Combination (nCrn\text{C}_rnCr​): nCr=n!r!(n−r)!n\text{C}_r = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!}nCr​=r!(n−r)!n!​
  • Symmetry & Boundary Properties:
    • nCr=nCn−rn\text{C}r = n\text{C}{n-r}nCr​=nCn−r​

    • nCr+nCr−1=(n+1)Crn\text{C}r + n\text{C}{r-1} = (n+1)\text{C}_rnCr​+nCr−1​=(n+1)Cr​ (Pascal's Rule)

3-Tier Worked Problem: Independent Target Probability

3-Tier Worked Problem

Problem: A can hit a target with probability $0.6, and B can hit the same target with probability $0.5. If both shoot independently at the target, what is the probability that the target is hit at least once?

  • Tier 1 (First Principles / Addition Theorem):
    1. P(A)=0.6,P(B)=0.5P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.5P(A)=0.6,P(B)=0.5.
    2. Since shots are independent, P(AcapB)=P(A)×P(B)=0.6×0.5=0.30P(A cap B) = P(A) \times P(B) = 0.6 \times 0.5 = 0.30P(AcapB)=P(A)×P(B)=0.6×0.5=0.30.
    3. P(AcupB)=P(A)+P(B)−P(AcapB)=0.6+0.5−0.30=0.80P(A cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A cap B) = 0.6 + 0.5 - 0.30 = \mathbf{0.80}P(AcupB)=P(A)+P(B)−P(AcapB)=0.6+0.5−0.30=0.80.
  • Tier 2 (Banking Standard / The "At Least One" Rule):
    1. P(Target Missed by A)=1−0.6=0.4P(\text{Target Missed by A}) = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4P(Target Missed by A)=1−0.6=0.4.
    2. P(Target Missed by B)=1−0.5=0.5P(\text{Target Missed by B}) = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5P(Target Missed by B)=1−0.5=0.5.
    3. P(Target Missed by Both)=0.4×0.5=0.20P(\text{Target Missed by Both}) = 0.4 \times 0.5 = 0.20P(Target Missed by Both)=0.4×0.5=0.20.
    4. P(Target Hit at least once)=1−P(Both miss)=1−0.20=0.80P(\text{Target Hit at least once}) = 1 - P(\text{Both miss}) = 1 - 0.20 = \mathbf{0.80}P(Target Hit at least once)=1−P(Both miss)=1−0.20=0.80.
  • Tier 3 (10-Second Mental Elimination): $1 - (0.4 imes 0.5) = 1 - 0.2 = 0.8$ directly.
