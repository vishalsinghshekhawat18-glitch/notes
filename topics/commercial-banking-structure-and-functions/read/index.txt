12:T5d7,Commercial bank deposit liabilities are bifurcated into Demand Deposits and Time/Term Deposits:

### 1. Deposit Product Hierarchy
- **Demand Deposits (Withdrawable at any time without prior notice)**:
  - **Current Account**: Maintained by businesses and corporates for high-frequency transactions. Earns **$0\%$ interest** (RBI prohibits interest on current accounts, with narrow exemptions).
  - **Savings Account**: Maintained by households for savings and daily retail payments. Earns low nominal interest (e.g., $2.5\% - 3.5\% p.a.$).
- **Term / Time Deposits (Withdrawable after a contracted maturity period)**:
  - **Fixed Deposit (FD)**: Lump-sum deposit locked for a fixed duration (7 days to 10 years). Earns higher interest (e.g., $6.5\% - 7.5\% p.a.$). Premature withdrawal incurs penal charges.
  - **Recurring Deposit (RD)**: Fixed regular monthly installments deposited over a specified tenure.

### 2. The CASA Ratio Metric
$$\text{CASA Ratio} = \left(\frac{\text{Current Account Deposits} + \text{Savings Account Deposits}}{\text{Total Deposits}}\right) \times 100$$
- **Why CASA Matters**: CASA deposits are low-cost/zero-cost liabilities. A bank with a $45\%$ CASA ratio has a far lower **Cost of Funds (CoF)** than a bank with a $20\%$ CASA ratio relying on expensive wholesale term deposits. This structural advantage drives higher **Net Interest Margins (NIM)**:
$$\text{NIM} = \frac{\text{Interest Income} - \text{Interest Expended}}{\text{Total Average Earning Assets}}$$13:T6b8,Bank lending is structured into two fundamental categories:

```
                      COMMERCIAL BANK CREDIT FACILITIES
                                      │
       ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
       ▼                                                             ▼
FUNDED FACILITIES (Cash Outflow)                             NON-FUNDED FACILITIES (Contingent)
```

### Funded vs Non-Funded Mechanics
1. **Cash Credit (CC) & Overdraft (OD)**: Running credit limits where the borrower is charged interest **only on the actual amount drawn on a daily basis**, not on the entire sanctioned limit.
2. **Term Loans**: The full sanctioned amount is disbursed upfront or in scheduled tranches. Interest is charged on the entire outstanding loan balance.
3. **Letters of Credit (LC)**: A documentary commitment issued by a bank on behalf of a buyer, assuring the seller that payment will be made provided shipping documents conform to terms. If the buyer pays, the bank disburses zero cash and pockets a fee. If the buyer defaults, the LC "devolves" into a funded liability of the bank.14:T424,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Statutory Definition of Banking**: Section 5(b) of the Banking Regulation Act requires **BOTH** accepting deposits AND lending/investing. An institution that only lends (like an NBFC) or only accepts deposits without lending (like a pure custodian) is **not a bank** under Section 5(b).
- **Trap 2: Interest on Current Accounts**: Under RBI regulations, commercial banks are **strictly prohibited from paying interest on Current Accounts** (subject to specific exemptions like RRB balances or deceased depositor claims).
- **Trap 3: Funded vs Non-Funded**: LCs and Bank Guarantees are **non-funded** facilities that appear as **Contingent Liabilities (Off-Balance Sheet items)**, generating fee income rather than interest income.

### The Fundamental Balance Sheet Equation
$$\text{Total Assets} = \text{Total Liabilities} + \text{Bank Capital (Equity)}$$
$$\text{Bank Capital (Net Worth)} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}$$
*Bank Capital functions as the shock-absorbing equity cushion protecting depositors from loan default losses.*16:T4c3,Because bank assets (long-term loans) and liabilities (short-term deposits) have different maturities and repricing dates, banks face two primary balance sheet risks:

### 1. Structural Liquidity Gap Analysis
Banks slot all cash inflows (maturing assets) and cash outflows (maturing liabilities) into standardized **time buckets** (e.g., Next Day, 2–7 Days, 8–14 Days, 15–30 Days, 1–3 Months, 3–6 Months, 6–12 Months, 1–3 Years, 3–5 Years, Over 5 Years):
$$\text{Maturity Gap}_t = \text{Maturing Assets}_t - \text{Maturing Liabilities}_t$$
- **Negative Gap (Deficit)**: Maturing liabilities exceed maturing assets. Bank must scramble to borrow fresh short-term funds or refinance.
- **Regulatory Prudential Limits**: RBI mandates strict tolerance limits on negative mismatches (e.g., maximum $20\%$ cumulative mismatch in the 1–14 day bucket).

### 2. Interest Rate Risk: Net Interest Income (NII) Sensitivity
- **Asset-Sensitive (Positive Gap)**: Rate-sensitive assets ($RSA$) exceed rate-sensitive liabilities ($RSL$). A policy rate hike **increases NIM**.
- **Liability-Sensitive (Negative Gap)**: $RSL > RSA$. A policy rate hike **squeezes NIM** because deposit costs rise faster than loan
