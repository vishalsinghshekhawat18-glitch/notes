12:T767,### The 4 Mandatory Pillars of 'Payment in Due Course' (Section 10)
To claim statutory protection under Sections 85, 85A, 89, or 128, the paying banker must satisfy ALL four conditions:
1. **In Accordance with Apparent Tenor**: Payment must strictly match the instrument's face (e.g. not paying a post-dated cheque before its date, not paying a stale cheque after 3 months, not paying cash for a crossed cheque).
2. **In Good Faith**: Payment made honestly without collusion or fraud.
3. **Without Negligence**: Exercising standard banking prudence and reasonable care (e.g. checking signature of drawer, matching words and figures, checking apparent alterations).
4. **To the Person in Possession under Reasonable Circumstances**: No suspicious grounds to suspect the presenter's title.

1. **'Once a Bearer, Always a Bearer' (Section 85(2))**: If Drawer writes a bearer cheque, and Payee writes *'Pay to Suresh'* (special endorsement) on the back, the paying bank can STILL pay cash to any bearer who walks into the bank. The paying bank is completely protected under Section 85(2). The special endorsement does not affect the paying banker.
2. **Paying Post-Dated Cheque Trap**: Paying a post-dated cheque before its stated date is **NEVER a payment in due course**. If a bank pays a PDC on the 10th when dated the 25th, and as a result another legitimate cheque drawn on the 12th bounces, the bank is liable for damages for wrongful dishonour under Section 31.
3. **Forged Drawer Signature Trap**: Section 85 protects ONLY against forged *endorsements*, NEVER against a forged *drawer's signature*. Payment of a cheque with a forged drawer signature is a complete nullity and the bank must refund the money to the customer.
4. **Counter Cash Payment of Crossed Cheque (Section 129)**: If a paying teller pays cash across the counter for a crossed cheque, statutory protection under Section 128 is completely lost, and the bank is directly liable to the true owner.15:T42d,### Architectural Matrix: Paying Banker Protections & Duties (NI Act)
- **Statutory Pillars of Protection**:
  - **Section 10**: Payment in Due Course (Apparent tenor, good faith, without negligence).
  - **Section 31**: Statutory duty to honour customer cheques (liable in damages for wrongful dishonour).
  - **Section 85(1)**: Order Cheques — immunity against forged endorsements.
  - **Section 85(2)**: Bearer Cheques — absolute immunity upon payment to bearer ('Once a bearer always a bearer').
  - **Section 85A**: Demand Drafts — immunity against forged payee endorsements.
  - **Section 89**: Material Alteration — immunity where alteration is non-apparent.
  - **Section 128**: Crossed Cheques — immunity when paid to a banker in due course.
- **Absolute Banker Liabilities (No Protection)**:
  - Forged signature of Drawer (*Bihta Cooperative Union*).
  - Payment of post-dated cheque before stated date.
  - Payment across counter of a crossed cheque (Section 129).
  - Payment after receiving Stop Payment notice, Death notice, or Garnishee Order.16:T79e,### Grounds for Termination / Revocation of Banker's Mandate
1. **Countermand of Payment (Stop Payment)**: Customer instructs bank (with cheque number, date, amount, payee) not to pay. The bank must note it in CBS immediately.
2. **Notice of Customer's Death**: Cheques signed by customer before death cannot be paid once authentic notice (death certificate/letter) is received. Reason: 'Drawer Deceased'.
3. **Notice of Insolvency / Bankruptcy**: Individual declared insolvent or corporate undergoing CIRP liquidation under IBC 2016. Reason: 'Drawer Insolvent'.
4. **Notice of Lunacy / Insanity**: When bank receives notice of judicial inquisition/medical certification of customer's insanity.
5. **Notice of Assignment**: Customer assigns credit balance to a third party under Section 130 Transfer of Property Act.

1. **Garnishee Order on Joint Account (A & B)**:
   - If the Garnishee order is against **A alone**, the bank **CANNOT attach a joint account of A and B** (because the debt is not due to A individually).
   - If the Garnishee order is against **A and B jointly**, the bank **CAN attach both individual accounts of A and B as well as their joint account**.
2. **Income Tax Attachment on Joint Account**: Unlike a Garnishee Order, under Section 226(3)(iii) of the IT Act, the TRO **CAN attach a joint account** for individual tax dues of A (presuming equal 50% shares unless proven otherwise).
3. **Unutilized Overdraft Limit Trap**: A Garnishee Order attaches only *'debts due or accruing'*. An unutilized Cash Credit / Overdraft limit is a facility extended by the bank, NOT a debt owed by the bank to the customer. Therefore, **unutilized OD limits CANNOT be attached**.
4. **Stop Payment for Lost Cheque vs Sec 138 Trap**: If a drawer issues a stop payment instruction to escape criminal liability when funds are insufficient, the Supreme Court ruled in *Goa Plast (P) Ltd v. Chico Ursula D'Souza* that **Section 138 still applies** even if the cheque was returned with memo 'Payment stopped by drawer'.19:T6e6,### Five Indispensable Conditions to Claim Section 131 Protection
1. **The Cheque Must Be Crossed**: Section 131 applies **ONLY to crossed cheques** (crossed generally or specially). If a bank collects an **open (uncrossed) cheque** with a defective title, it receives ZERO statutory protection and is 100% liable for conversion!
2. **Collection for a 'Customer'**: The person for whom the cheque is collected must be an existing account holder (having an account with the bank). Collecting for a casual walk-in stranger forfeits protection.
3. **Acted as an Agent**: The bank must receive payment strictly as an agent for collection (not as a holder for value / purchaser of the cheque).
4. **Good Faith**: Acted honestly and without collusion.
5. **Absence of Negligence**: Followed all standard banking precautions, KYC rules, and verification standards.

### What Constitutes Negligence by a Collecting Banker? (Forfeits Sec 131 Protection)
- **Failure to follow RBI KYC Norms**: Opening an account without verifying Official Valid Documents (OVDs), PAN/Form 60, or address verification.
- **Crediting 'Account Payee' Cheque to Third Party**: Crediting a cheque drawn to 'M/s ABC Corp' into the personal account of 'Director X' without board resolution.
- **Ignoring Endorsement Irregularities**: Collecting an order cheque where the endorsement chain is visibly broken or suspicious.
- **Sudden High-Value Clearing in Dormant/Student Account**: Depositing a ₹50,00,000 cheque into a newly opened zero-balance student account without source verification.
