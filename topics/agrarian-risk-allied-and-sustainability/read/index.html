Crop Insurance Economics: Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), Restructured WBCIS & Risk Mitigation

The microeconomic principles, actuarial pricing, and operational architecture of agricultural risk mitigation in India. Dissects the market failures inherent to agricultural insurance: Covariant Risk (systemic crop failure across entire regions), Information Asymmetry, Moral Hazard, and Adverse Selection. Analyzes **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)**, launched in Kharif 2016 (replacing NAIS and MNAIS): (1) Uniform low premium rates payable by farmers: **2.0% of sum insured for Kharif crops**, **1.5% for Rabi foodgrains and oilseeds**, and **5.0% for annual Commercial / Horticultural crops**, with the remaining actuarial premium split equally (50:50) between Central and State Governments (90:10 for North-Eastern States), (2) Comprehensive risk coverage from pre-sowing to post-harvest losses, (3) Use of Crop Cutting Experiments (CCEs) and remote sensing technology, (4) Voluntary enrollment for all farmers (revamped in 2020), and (5) Contrasting PMFBY area-yield approach with the **Restructured Weather-Based Crop Insurance Scheme (RWBCIS)**.

Intuition & Context

Why Private Insurance Fails Farmers: The Mystery of Covariant Risk

If you sell car insurance in Delhi, you are safe because only 1 out of 100 cars crashes on any given day. You collect premiums from 99 safe drivers to pay for the 1 crashed car (Independent Risk).\n\nWhat happens if you sell private drought insurance to farmers in Rajasthan?\n- In a drought year, 100% of farmers in the entire district suffer crop failure simultaneously!\n- This is Covariant Risk (systemic catastrophe). A private insurance company would go bankrupt overnight if it had to pay every single policyholder at the same time!\n\nThis is why commercial market insurance collapses in agriculture without massive government subsidies.\n\nUnder PMFBY:\n- The farmer pays only a token 1.5% to 2.0% premium.\n- If the real actuarial market cost of insuring the crop is 16%, the Central and State Governments pay the remaining 14% subsidy!\n- This protects the farmer from bankruptcy after hailstorms, droughts, or pest attacks.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

PMFBY (Area-Yield) vs RWBCIS (Weather-Index) Comparison

| Dimension | PMFBY (Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana) | Restructured WBCIS (Weather Based Crop Insurance) |\n| :--- | :--- | :--- |\n| Trigger Mechanism | Area-Yield Approach: Claim triggered if the actual average crop yield in a notified Defined Area falls below the Threshold Yield (calculated from past 5 to 7 years historical average). | Weather-Index Approach: Claim triggered if recorded weather triggers (rainfall, temperature, frost, humidity, wind) breach pre-defined strike levels at local automatic weather stations. |\n| Assessment Method | Physical Crop Cutting Experiments (CCEs) conducted in sample plots, increasingly verified with satellite imagery (YES-TECH) and drones. | Automated data feeds from Automatic Weather Stations (AWS) and Rain Gauges. |\n| Settlement Speed | Requires harvesting and CCE compilation; historical claim settlement delays of 3–6 months. | Faster automated settlement; zero physical crop harvesting inspection required. |\n| Basis Risk Vulnerability | Low basis risk (measures actual crop output loss in the insurance unit). | High Basis Risk: A farmer's field may suffer localized damage, but the weather station 10 km away records normal rainfall, denying compensation. |

Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps\n- Trap 1: Premium Rates: Kharif is 2.0%, Rabi is 1.5%, and Commercial/Horticultural crops is 5.0%. A question stating Kharif is 1.5% and Rabi is 2.0% is a deliberate inversion trap!\n- Trap 2: Is PMFBY Mandatory for KCC Loanees?: NO. Since the February 2020 revamp, PMFBY is 100% voluntary for all farmers, including loanee farmers holding Kisan Credit Cards.\n- Trap 3: Post-Harvest Loss Duration: Post-harvest loss coverage is strictly valid for up to 14 days from harvesting while the crop is kept in "cut and spread" condition in the field.\n\n### Cross-Exam Focus\n- NABARD Grade A (ARD & ESI): PMFBY premium rates (2%, 1.5%, 5%), 50:50 sharing (90:10 NE), voluntary revamp 2020, 14-day post-harvest limit, RWBCIS comparison, CCE technology.\n- RBI Grade B & UPSC APFC: Covariant risk in agriculture, fiscal burden of crop insurance subsidies, delayed claim settlements by private insurers.\n- RPSC RAS & SBI/IBPS PO: Premium rate MCQs, PMFBY coverage for Bajra and Mustard in Rajasthan, localized calamity claim reporting window (72 hours via Crop Insurance App).

Allied Agricultural Sectors & Sustainable Agriculture: Animal Husbandry, Blue Economy (PMMSY), Climate-Smart & Organic Farming

The diversification economics, nutritional security roles, and sustainable transitions of India's allied agricultural and agro-ecological systems. Dissects: (1) **Livestock Economics & Dairying**: India as the world's largest milk producer (~24% of global output), the cooperative dairy model (Operation Flood, National Dairy Development Board [NDDB], Verghese Kurien), and the Rashtriya Gokul Mission, (2) **Blue Economy & Fisheries**: India as the 2nd largest fish producing country, Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY: ₹20,050 Cr investment for marine, inland fisheries, and Matsya Setu app), and Kisan Credit Card expansion for fisheries/animal husbandry, (3) **Sustainable & Climate-Smart Agriculture**: Soil Health Card scheme (evaluating 12 critical soil parameters), Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY: promoting organic farming clusters via Participatory Guarantee System [PGS-India]), and Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF: Jeevamrit, Beejamrit, Mulching, Waaphasa).

Intuition & Context

Beyond the Crop: The Two Buffers of Rural Survival

If a farmer depends 100% on a single crop of rainfed cotton and a severe drought strikes in July, his income crashes to ₹0. His family faces starvation.\n\nNow, consider his neighbor who owns 2 acres of cotton PLUS 2 crossbred dairy cows and a small backyard poultry coop:\n- Even if the drought ruins the cotton, his cows produce 20 liters of milk every day.\n- He sells the milk to the local village dairy cooperative every morning, earning ₹800 in fresh cash daily!\n- This daily cash flow pays for groceries, school fees, and cattle fodder, preventing debt default.\n\nThis is why Allied Sectors (Livestock, Dairying, Fisheries) are the true economic backbone of rural India. They generate steady, daily, non-monsoon-dependent income for 86% of smallholder families.
