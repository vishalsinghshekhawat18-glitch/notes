11:T402,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: External Debt Proportion**: External sovereign debt is **LESS THAN 5%** of total central government liabilities. Many candidates confuse total external debt (including private corporate ECBs) with Central Government sovereign external debt.
- **Trap 2: Constitutional Borrowing Powers**:
  - Central Government borrows under **Article 292** (can borrow domestically and externally).
  - State Governments borrow under **Article 293** (States are strictly **PROHIBITED from borrowing abroad** directly; all foreign loans must be channeled through the Centre!).
- **Trap 3: Weighted Average Maturity**: Weighted average maturity of central debt is **elongated (> 11 years)**, not short-term.

- **Trap 1: Domar Condition Inequality**: Debt ratio stabilizes when **$g > r$ (Growth rate is GREATER than interest rate)**. If $r > g$, the debt ratio explodes unless there is a primary surplus!
- **Trap 2: Primary Balance vs Primary Deficit**: In the debt equation, a **Primary Deficit is entered as a negative primary balance ($-pb_t$)**, which increases debt ($\Delta d > 0$). A **Primary Surplus ($pb_t > 0$)** reduces debt.
- **Trap 3: Real vs Nominal in $(r - g)$**: You can use Nominal $r$ and Nominal $g$, OR Real $r$ and Real $g$. The differential $(r - g)$ is mathematically identical because inflation cancels out in both terms!
- **Trap 4: Economic Survey 2020-21 Focus**: The Economic Survey dedicated a flagship chapter arguing that for India, **growth leads to debt sustainability**, making public investment in CapEx self-financing.
