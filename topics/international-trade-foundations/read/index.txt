14:T6e0,Let us examine a 2-country (India and Germany), 2-commodity (Textiles and Machinery) model with labor as the only input:\n\n### Output Produced per 100 Hours of Labor\n| Country | Textiles (Bales) | Machinery (Units) | Domestic Opportunity Cost of 1 Unit of Machinery | Domestic Opportunity Cost of 1 Bale of Textiles |\n| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **India** | 100 bales | 20 units | $\\frac{100}{20} = 5$ bales of textiles | $\\frac{20}{100} = 0.2$ units of machinery |\n| **Germany** | 120 bales | 60 units | $\\frac{120}{60} = 2$ bales of textiles | $\\frac{60}{120} = 0.5$ units of machinery |\n\n### Analytical Takeaways\n1. **Absolute Advantage**: Germany has an absolute advantage in **both** textiles ($120 > 100$) and machinery ($60 > 20$).\n2. **Comparative Advantage**:\n   - In Germany, producing 1 machine costs **2 bales of textiles**.\n   - In India, producing 1 machine costs **5 bales of textiles**.\n   - Therefore, **Germany has a comparative advantage in Machinery** (lower opportunity cost: $2 < 5$).\n   - In India, producing 1 bale of textiles costs **0.2 machines**, whereas in Germany it costs **0.5 machines**.\n   - Therefore, **India has a comparative advantage in Textiles** (lower opportunity cost: $0.2 < 0.5$).\n3. **Mutually Beneficial Terms of Trade (TOT)**:\n   Trade will occur at any exchange ratio between the two domestic opportunity cost ratios:\n   $$2 \\text{ bales of textiles} < 1 \\text{ unit of machinery} < 5 \\text{ bales of textiles}$$\n   If the international price settles at $1 \\text{ machine} = 3.5 \\text{ bales of textiles}$, Germany gains 1.5 bales per machine exported, and India saves 1.5 bales per machine imported. Theoretical Progression:\n   - Mercantilism (16th-18th c.): Trade is zero-sum; maximize gold reserves via export surplus.\n   - Absolute Advantage (Adam Smith 1776): Positive-sum trade based on absolute labor productivity differences.\n   - Comparative Advantage (David Ricardo 1817): Positive-sum trade determined by relative domestic opportunity costs.\n   - Opportunity Cost Theory (Gottfried Haberler 1936): Replaced labor theory with PPF Marginal Rate of Transformation ($MRT$).\n2. Income Terms of Trade ($I$ / Capacity to Import)\n$$I = N \\times Q_x = \\left(\\frac{P_x \\times Q_x}{P_m}\\right)$$\n- $Q_x$ = Export Volume Index. Represents the total purchasing power of export earnings over imports. If export prices fall by $10\\%$ but export volume expands by $50\\%$, $N$ deteriorates but $I$ significantly improves!\n\n### 3. Single Factoral Terms of Trade ($S$)\n$$S = N \\times Z_x$$\n- $Z_x$ = Productivity Index in the domestic export sector. Measures the volume of imports a nation can purchase per unit of domestic factor (labor/capital) input.\n\n### 4. Double Factoral Terms of Trade ($D$)\n$$D = N \\times \\left(\\frac{Z_x}{Z_m}\\right)$$\n- $Z_m$ = Productivity Index in foreign export sectors. Compares the productivity efficiency of domestic factors against foreign factors.\n\n### 5. Gross Barter Terms of Trade ($G$)\n$$G = \\left(\\frac{Q_m}{Q_x}\\right) \\times 100$$\n- Measures physical quantities exchanged.
