12:T4a9,Article 169 provides an extraordinary constitutional mechanism that allows a State to create or abolish a Legislative Council without undergoing the rigid Article 368 constitutional amendment procedure:

### Stage 1: State Assembly Special Majority Resolution:
• The Legislative Assembly of the State must pass a resolution requesting Parliament to create or abolish the Council.
• **Majority Required:** **Special Majority under Article 169(1)**:
  1. A majority of the **total membership** of the Assembly, **AND**
  2. A majority of **not less than two-thirds of the members of the Assembly present and voting**.

### Stage 2: Parliamentary Ordinary Law:
• Upon receiving the State resolution, **Parliament enacts an Act** abolishing or creating the Council.
• **Majority Required in Parliament:** **Simple Majority** (like an ordinary bill).
• **Article 169(3):** The law passed by Parliament is **NOT deemed to be an amendment of the Constitution for the purposes of Article 368**.

### The Two-Stage Delaying Timeline:
• **First Instance (3 Months):** The Council can hold or reject the bill for a maximum of **3 months**.
• If the Council rejects the bill, makes unacceptable amendments, or takes no action for 3 months, the **Assembly may pass the bill a SECOND TIME** (with or without amendments) and transmit it again to the Council.
• **Second Instance (1 Month):** In the second instance, the Council can hold or reject the bill for a maximum of **1 month**.
• If the Council rejects the bill again or takes no action for 1 month, **the Bill is deemed to have been passed by BOTH Houses** in the form in which it was passed by the Legislative Assembly for the second time.
