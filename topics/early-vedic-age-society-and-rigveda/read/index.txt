12:Tce2,The Early Vedic epoch is reconstructed from the internal evidence of the **Rigveda Samhita**:

### 1. Structure of the Rigveda:
- **10 Mandalas (Books), 1,028 Suktas (Hymns), 10,600 Verses.**
- **The Family Books (Oldest Core: Mandalas 2 to 7):**
  * Mandala 2: Sage **Gritsamada** (Shaunaka clan)
  * Mandala 3: Sage **Vishvamitra** (contains the celebrated **Gayatri Mantra** dedicated to the solar deity *Savitri*)
  * Mandala 4: Sage **Vamadeva** (contains agricultural references)
  * Mandala 5: Sage **Atri**
  * Mandala 6: Sage **Bharadvaja**
  * Mandala 7: Sage **Vashistha** (narrates the Battle of the Ten Kings)
- **Later Additions:** Mandala 1, Mandala 8 (Kanva clan), Mandala 9 (entirely dedicated to **Soma Pavamana**), and Mandala 10 (contains the *Purusha Sukta* and *Nasadiya Sukta*).

### 2. Rigvedic River Geography (The Sapta Sindhu):
The Rigveda’s **Nadi-stuti Sukta** (Mandala 10.75) catalogs the sacred rivers of the northwest:

| Rigvedic Ancient Name | Modern River Name | Rigvedic Importance & Frequency of Mention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sindhu** | **Indus** | Most frequently mentioned river in the Rigveda; celebrated for its immense water volume and commercial importance. |
| **Sarasvati** | **Ghaggar-Hakra** (dried) | Most sacred river; praised as **Naditama** (*"best of rivers"*), *Devitama* (*"best of goddesses"*), and *Matritama* (*"best of mothers"*). |
| **Vitasta** | **Jhelum** | River of northwestern Punjab. |
| **Asikni** | **Chenab** | Major Punjab river. |
| **Parushni** | **Ravi** | Historic river on whose banks the **Battle of the Ten Kings (*Dasarajna*)** was fought. |
| **Vipas** | **Beas** | River crossed and praised by Sage Vishvamitra. |
| **Shutudri** | **Sutlej** | Easternmost river of the Punjab plains. |
| **Yamuna** | **Yamuna** | Mentioned only **three times** in the Rigveda. |
| **Ganga** | **Ganges** | Mentioned only **once** in the entire Rigveda (showing the Aryans had not yet colonized the Gangetic plains). |
| **Kubha / Krumu / Suvastu** | **Kabul / Kurram / Swat** | Rivers of Afghanistan, proving early Indo-Iranian geographical contact. |

### 3. Cattle-Centric Socioeconomic Lexicon:
| Rigvedic Term | Literal Meaning | Applied Socio-Economic Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gau** | Cow | The central measure of wealth, medium of exchange, and divine gift (*Dana*). |
| **Gopati** | Lord / Protector of cattle | The tribal king / Rajan. |
| **Gaveshana / Gavishti / Gosu** | Search for cows | Warfare / tribal battles between rival clans. |
| **Godhuli** | Dust raised by cattle returning at sunset | Measurement of time (evening / twilight). |
| **Gavyuti** | Distance cattle can graze / heard calling | Measurement of physical distance. |
| **Duhitri** | One who milks the cows | Daughter of the household. |
| **Gomat** | Possessor of extensive cattle herds | Wealthy person / cattle-rich householder. |
| **Aghanya** | "Not to be killed / slaughtered" | Ritual epithet for the sacred cow. |
| **Nishka** | Gold neck ornament / bullion piece | Used alongside cows as a preliminary standard of value in barter transactions. |
| **Ayas** | Copper / Bronze | Generic metal term; Early Rigvedic people knew copper/bronze, but **did not know Iron** (iron was *Syama Ayas*, introduced in Later Vedic times). |13:T450,The Early Vedic economic mechanism operated through pastoral mobility and kin-based redistribution:

```
[Pastoral Transhumance across Sapta Sindhu River Meadows]
  --> [Cattle Accumulation -> Status & Inter-Tribal Cattle Raids (*Gavishti*)]
  --> [Supplementary Slash-and-Burn Subsistence -> Cultivation of *Yava* (Barley)]
  --> [Absence of Land Ownership & Absence of Regular Taxation]
  --> [Voluntary Offerings (*Bali*) to Rajan -> Sacrificial Redistribution in *Vidatha*]
```

### 1. Limited Role of Agriculture:
Agriculture in the Rigveda was strictly secondary to pastoralism. Only one grain—**Yava** (barley)—is mentioned. Fields were tilled using light wooden ploughs (*Langala* / *Sira*) drawn by oxen. Land was held collectively by the clan (*Vis* / *Grama*), and there was no private ownership or sale of land.

### 2. Barter and Value Accounting:
There were no metallic coins. Transactions were conducted by evaluating commodities against a standard unit of **cows** or **Nishka** (gold neck rings/discs). For example, a fine horse or chariot was valued at "ten cows" or "five nishkas".14:T4b1,### Critical Examiner Traps:
- **Trap 1 (Ganga vs Sarasvati in Rigveda):**
  * **Sarasvati** is the MOST REVERED river (*Naditama*).
  * **Ganga** is mentioned **ONLY ONCE** in the Rigveda (Mandala 10.75).
  * **Yamuna** is mentioned **THRICE**.
  * If an examiner claims "The Ganga was the central holy river of the Early Rigvedic people", the statement is **strictly FALSE**.
- **Trap 2 (Metal Knowledge - Ayas):** In the early Rigveda, the word **Ayas** refers to **copper or bronze**, NOT iron. Iron is called *Syama Ayas* (black metal) or *Krishna Ayas* and appears only in Later Vedic literature (c. 1000 BCE).
- **Trap 3 (Family Books):** The oldest Mandalas of the Rigveda are **Mandalas 2 to 7** (Family Books). Mandalas 1 and 10 are the latest chronological layers.
- **Trap 4 (Nature of Warfare):** Early Vedic wars (*Gavishti*) were fought for **cattle and pasture lands**, NOT for annexing vast territorial empires or collecting fixed agricultural revenue.
- **Trap 5 (Gayatri Mantra Location):** The Gayatri Mantra (*"Tat Savitur Varenyam..."*) was composed by Sage **Vishvamitra** and is located in **Mandala 3, Sukta 62, Verse 10** of the Rigveda, dedicated to the sun god **Savitri**.15:Tb8f,The political structures of the Early Vedic age were founded on kinship and assembly oversight:

### 1. Popular Tribal Assemblies:
| Tribal Assembly | Composition & Social Character | Core Functions & Powers | Gender Participation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vidatha** | The **oldest folk assembly** of the tribe; universal community gathering. | Secular, religious, military, and redistributive functions; equal division of war booty, food, and cattle among clans. | **Active participation by women** (*participated in debates and hymns*). |
| **Samiti** | General assembly of the entire tribe (*Vis* / *Jana*); presided over by the **Ishana** / *Pati*. | Political election, re-election, and deposition of the Rajan; deliberation on national defense and tribal policy. | Attended by all free tribesmen. |
| **Sabha** | Council of distinguished elders, noble warriors (*Sujatas*), and learned sages. | Judicial court of justice (settling murder, theft, gambling disputes) and advisory council to the Rajan. | Women attended in the Early Vedic period (called **Sabhavati**). |
| **Gana** | Assembly or troop of armed tribal warriors. | Military operations and tactical raids; led by a *Ganapati* / *Jyeshtha*. | Exclusively warrior men. |

### 2. Key Officials of the Rajan:
- **Purohita:** Chief spiritual advisor, ritual priest, and war-spell chanter (e.g., Vashistha and Vishvamitra).
- **Senani:** Leader of the tribal militia during cattle expeditions (there was **no standing army**; all able-bodied tribesmen mobilized as militia).
- **Gramani:** Head of the village / battle unit (*Vraja*).
- **Spas:** Spies / secret watchmen monitoring tribal conduct and border movements.
- **Duta:** Envoys / diplomatic messengers between rival tribes.
- **Vrajapati:** Officer in charge of pastures and cattle enclosures.

### 3. The Battle of the Ten Kings (*Dasarajna War*):
- **Textual Location:** Rigveda, **Mandala 7 (Suktas 18, 33, 83)**, composed by Sage **Vashistha**.
- **Location of Battle:** On the banks of the **Parushni River (modern Ravi)** in Punjab.
- **The Combatants:**
  * **Side A:** King **Sudas** of the **Tritsu-Bharata** tribe (advised by **Sage Vashistha**).
  * **Side B:** A confederacy of **10 tribes** organized by **Sage Vishvamitra** (who was replaced by Sudas as royal priest) and led by King **Purukutsa** of the Purus.
  * **The 10 Confederacy Tribes:**
    1. *5 Major Aryan Tribes (Panchajana):* **Puru**, **Yadu**, **Turvasha**, **Anu**, **Druhyu**.
    2. *5 Non-Aryan / Frontier Tribes:* **Alina**, **Paktha**, **Bhalanas**, **Shiva**, **Vishanin**.
- **Outcome & Historical Impact:**
  * King Sudas decisively defeated the 10-king confederacy when the Parushni river flooded the enemy lines.
  * Established the supremacy of the **Bharata tribe** in northern India.
  * Later, the victorious Bharatas merged with the defeated Purus to form the **Kuru** realm in the Ganga-Yamuna Doab during the Later Vedic period.16:T470,### Critical Examiner Traps:
- **Trap 1 (Vidatha vs Sabha vs Samiti):**
  * **Vidatha:** The OLDEST assembly; performed redistributive and religious functions; open to women.
  * **Samiti:** General political folk assembly that ELECTED the king; presided over by *Ishana*.
  * **Sabha:** Smaller elite judicial body of elders.
- **Trap 2 (Dasarajna River):** The Battle of the Ten Kings took place on the **Parushni (Ravi)** River, NOT on the Sindhu, Sarasvati, or Ganga!
- **Trap 3 (The Priests in Dasarajna):** 
  * Sage **Vashistha** was the priest of the winning King **Sudas** (Bharatas).
  * Sage **Vishvamitra** was the priest of the losing 10-King Confederacy.
  Examiners frequently swap these two sages!
- **Trap 4 (Nature of Bali):** In the *Early Vedic* period, **Bali was voluntary**. In the *Later Vedic* period, Bali became a **compulsory, regular tax**.
- **Trap 5 (Standing Army Myth):** The Rajan had **NO permanent standing army**. The military consisted of the armed citizenry organized in kinship bands (*Vrata, Gana, Sardha*). Standing armies appeared only in the 6th century BCE with Magadha (Bimbisara *Seniya*).17:T449,If you stepped into a Rigvedic settlement, you would be astonished by how different it was from later conservative orthodox India:

1. **Fluid Social Identity:** There were **no rigid castes**. A family could live under one roof where the father was a doctor, the mother ground corn, and the son composed sacred poetry. The four-varna hierarchy did not exist in daily life; society was divided broadly into *Aryas* (Vedic clans) and *Dasas/Dasyus* (indigenous groups).
2. **Intellectual Freedom for Women:** Young women were educated, received the sacred initiation thread (*Upanayana*), chose their own husbands in public *Swayamvaras*, married in adulthood, and composed philosophical hymns that became part of the holy Rigveda (**Brahmavadinis**).
3. **Conversing with Nature (Henotheism):** When they looked at the thundercloud, fire, river, or dawn, they did not build temples or stone idols. They gathered around the household sacred fire, offered milk and barley, and chanted magnificent poetry to **Indra** (thunder/war), **Agni** (fire), and **Varuna** (the cosmic moral order of *Rita*).18:Te28,The social structure and religious pantheon of the Early Vedic era were organized as follows:

### 1. Social Units of Kinship Hierarchy:
| Social Unit | Head of the Unit | Description & Structural Nature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kula / Griha** | **Kulapa / Grihapati** | The basic family unit; patriarchal, multi-generational household. |
| **Grama** | **Gramani** | A collection of related families / mobile encampment (*Vraja*); also functioned as a fighting unit. |
| **Vis** | **Vispati** | The clan / canton; intermediate social organization composed of several Gramas. |
| **Jana** | **Gopati / Rajan** | The entire tribe (e.g., Bharata Jana, Puru Jana); highest political and kinship unit. |
| **Rashtra** | — | Concept of "territory/state" was rudimentary and did not crystallize until the Later Vedic period. |

### 2. Status of Women & Female Scholars (Brahmavadinis):
- **High Social Standing:** Women had full rights to the *Upanayana Samskara* (sacred thread initiation), studied Vedic recitations, and attended the *Sabha* and *Vidatha*.
- **Absence of Social Evils:** No child marriage (marriage occurred post-puberty at 16–18 years), no *Sati* (widow burning), no *Purdah* (veil system).
- **Widow Rights:** Widows were permitted to remarry, especially to the deceased husband's younger brother via the levirate custom of **Niyoga** to bear progeny.
- **Brahmavadinis (Female Hymn Composers of Rigveda):**
  * **Lopamudra** (wife of Sage Agastya; composed hymns in Mandala 1)
  * **Apala** (composed hymns in Mandala 8 dedicated to Indra)
  * **Ghosha** (cured of leprosy by the Ashvins, composed hymns in Mandala 10)
  * **Vishwavara** and **Sikata** (composed sacrificial hymns).
  *(Note: Gargi and Maitreyi belong to the Later Vedic Upanishadic period).*

### 1. The Philosophical Concept of Rita:
*Rita* was the ultimate cosmic, moral, and physical order governing the universe—ensuring the sun rose daily, rivers flowed to the sea, seasons rotated, and humans acted with moral truth (*Satya*). Varuna was the sovereign guardian (*Ritasya Gopa*) who strictly punished violations of Rita.

### 2. Transactional Nature of Yajna (Sacrifice):
Sacrifices in the Early Vedic age were simple, domestic, and non-violent. There were no expensive public animal slaughters or monopolistic priestly rituals.
