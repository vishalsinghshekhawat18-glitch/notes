11:T682,### Grounds for Proclamation (Article 352(1)):
1. **War** (Formal declaration of war with another country)
2. **External Aggression** (Hostile acts without formal war declaration)
3. **Armed Rebellion**
• **External Emergency:** Proclaimed on grounds of War or External Aggression.
• **Internal Emergency:** Proclaimed on ground of Armed Rebellion.
• **Territorial Scope (42nd CAA 1976):** Can be declared for the entire country or restricted to a specific part of India.

### Historical Invocations in India:
1. **October 1962 – January 1968:** Chinese aggression.
2. **December 1971 – March 1977:** Indo-Pakistan war (Bangladesh liberation).
3. **June 1975 – March 1977:** Declared on ground of *"internal disturbance"* by Indira Gandhi government.

### Comprehensive Safeguards by 44th CAA 1978:
1. **Substituted "Armed Rebellion" for "Internal Disturbance"**: Eliminated the vague political pretext used in 1975.
2. **Written Advice of Cabinet:** President can proclaim emergency ONLY upon the **written communication of the Union Cabinet** (not PM alone).
3. **Approval Window:** Reduced from 2 months to **1 Month**.
4. **Approval Majority:** Elevated from Simple Majority to **Special Majority** in each House (majority of total membership + not less than 2/3rd present and voting).
5. **Periodic Renewal:** Operates for **6 months at a time**; requires fresh Special Majority resolution every 6 months for continuation.

1. **Article 356:** The President is satisfied (on **Governor's report OR *suo motu***) that the state government cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution.
2. **Article 365:** A State fails to comply with or give effect to any **lawful direction from the Union Executive**.

### Parliamentary Approval & Duration:
• **Approval Window:** Must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within **2 Months** from date of issue.
• **Majority Required:** **Simple Majority** in each House (unlike Art 352 which requires Special Majority!).
• **Initial Validity:** Valid for **6 months** once approved.

Inter-State Council (Article 263) — Constitutional Body
* **Constitutional Basis:** Article 263 empowers the President to create the Council if public interest is served.
* **Establishment:** Set up on **May 28, 1990** upon the recommendation of the Sarkaria Commission.
* **Composition:**
  - **Chairman:** Prime Minister of India.
  - **Members:** Chief Ministers of all States and UTs with Assemblies, Administrators of UTs without Assemblies, and 6 Union Cabinet Ministers nominated by the PM.
* **Standing Committee:** Chaired by the Union Home Minister with 5 Union Cabinet Ministers and 9 Chief Ministers.
* **Mandate:** Inquiring into disputes between States, investigating subjects of common interest, and deliberating policy coordination.

### 2. Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act 1956) — Statutory Bodies
* **Status:** Created under Part III of the *States Reorganisation Act 1956* (NOT a constitutional body).
* **Five Zones:** Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, and Southern.
* **Leadership:**
  - **Chairman:** Union Home Minister (common chairman for all five Zonal Councils).
  - **Vice-Chairman:** Chief Ministers of member States by annual rotation.
* **North Eastern Council (NEC):** Created separately under the *North Eastern Council Act 1971* covering the 8 North-Eastern states (Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura, and Sikkim added in 2002).

### 3. National Integration Council (NIC)
* **Origin:** Constituted in **1961 by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru** following the National Integration Conference.
