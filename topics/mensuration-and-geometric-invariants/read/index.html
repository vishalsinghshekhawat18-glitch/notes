Plane Geometry Invariants & Optimization Principles

2D Mensuration in banking examinations focuses on Area, Perimeter, Inscribed/Circumscribed Circles, and Pathway Deductions.

Core geometric invariants include:

  1. Isoperimetric Optimization Theorem: For any fixed perimeter PPP, the geometric figure enclosing the Maximum Area is ALWAYS a Circle. Among 4-sided figures of fixed perimeter, a Square maximizes area.

Core Principle

3D Topology & Conservation Invariants

3D Solid Mensuration evaluates Volume, Curved Surface Area (CSA / LSA), Total Surface Area (TSA), and Geometric Transformations.

Three foundational theorems govern 3D problems in Banking Mains:

  1. Volume Conservation in Melting/Recasting: Melting one or more solids into new shapes STRICTLY CONSERVES TOTAL VOLUME (∑Vinitial=∑Vfinal\sum V_{\text{initial}} = \sum V_{\text{final}}∑Vinitial​=∑Vfinal​), but Total Surface Area (TSA) ALWAYS CHANGES.
  2. Cutting Invariant: Slicing a 3D solid across a plane leaves Volume unchanged, but increases Total Surface Area by twice the cut cross-sectional area (DeltaTSA=+2AcutDelta\text{TSA} = +2A_{\text{cut}}DeltaTSA=+2Acut​).
  3. Dimensional Scaling Multiplier: Multiplying all linear dimensions by scale factor kkk scales:
    • Linear parameters (radius, height, diagonal) by k1k^1k1.
    • Surface Area parameters (CSA, TSA, base area) by k2k^2k2.
    • Volume parameters by k3k^3k3.
