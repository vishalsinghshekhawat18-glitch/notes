RBI Regulation & Banking Supervision

Statutory and regulatory powers of the Reserve Bank of India: Banking Regulation Act 1949, RBI Act 1934, bank licensing under Section 22, regulatory directions under Section 35A, board supersession under Section 36AAA, promoter ownership limits, voting rights caps, and Fit & Proper governance norms.

RBI Regulation & Banking Supervision · Concept 1 of 2

Statutory & Regulatory Architecture: Banking Regulation Act, 1949, Licensing, Ownership Caps & Board Governance

The comprehensive statutory framework governing commercial banking regulation in India: the interplay between the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 and the RBI Act, 1934. Covers bank licensing requirements under Section 22, broad binding direction powers under Section 35A, board supersession and officer removal under Section 36AA/36AAA, statutory shareholder voting caps (26% in private banks under Section 12), promoter shareholding dilution glidepaths (15-year 26% cap), and "Fit and Proper" criteria for bank directors.

Intuition & Context

Why Banks Cannot Be Governed Like Ordinary Corporations

If a smartphone manufacturer or restaurant goes bankrupt, only its shareholders and bondholders lose money. The general public is unharmed.

However, in a commercial bank, over 90%90%90% of total funding comes from public depositors, not equity shareholders. If a bank engages in reckless, self-serving lending to promoter-owned companies, millions of innocent households face financial ruin, and the entire national payments system freezes.

Therefore, banking law treats banks as quasi-public trusts. The Reserve Bank of India is granted statutory police powers under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 to inspect books, supersede rogue management boards, cap promoter voting power, and issue binding orders without needing prior court approval.

Core Principle

Key Regulatory Pillars of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949

The statutory authority of the RBI is codified across several key provisions of the BR Act:

1. Licensing & Entry (Section 22)

  • Mandatory license issued by RBI to commence banking operations.
  • Continuous compliance: RBI can cancel a license if the bank fails to pay depositors or breaches statutory net worth requirements.

2. Regulatory Directions (Section 35A)

  • The most powerful operational tool in Indian banking regulation.
  • Allows RBI to issue legally binding directives across interest rate benchmarks (EBLR), fee caps, cyber security, customer compensation, and priority sector mandates.

3. Management & Board Control (Sections 36AA & 36AAA)

  • Section 36AA: Power of RBI to remove any Chairman, Managing Director, Chief Executive Officer, or director of a bank who acts against depositor interest.
  • Section 36AAA: Power of RBI to supersede the entire Board of Directors of a bank and appoint an Administrator for up to 12 months (extendable up to 3 years), as seen in the rescue of YES Bank (2020).

4. Ownership & Voting Caps (Section 12)

  • Voting Rights Cap: Capped at 26%26%26% for any individual shareholder to prevent hostile takeovers and corporate concentration.
  • Promoter Shareholding Cap: Promoters must dilute equity to 26%26%26% within 15 years of commencement of business.

Fit and Proper Due Diligence

Every director must satisfy formal criteria regarding integrity, reputation, track record, financial soundness, absence of criminal convictions or regulatory debarment, and lack of conflict of interest with major borrowers.

Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Section 35A vs Section 42: Section 35A is in the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (Power to issue directions); Section 42 is in the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 (Cash Reserve Ratio / Scheduled Bank criteria).
  • Trap 2: Maximum Voting Rights Cap: The maximum statutory voting rights cap in private banks is 26% (raised from 10% by BR Amendment Act 2012).
  • Trap 3: MD/CEO Tenure Cap: The maximum continuous tenure for an MD/CEO or Whole-Time Director (WTD) in a private sector bank is 15 years, and the maximum statutory age limit is 70 years.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Sections 22, 35A, 36AA, 36AAA, 26% voting cap, and corporate governance circulars.
  • IIBF DBF, SBI/IBPS PO & UPSC APFC: Licensing conditions, board supersession powers, and fit & proper norms.

Banking Regulation Act Statutory Matrix:

  1. Licensing & Entry: Section 22 (RBI License requirement; capital, solvency, business model scrutiny).
  2. Regulatory Directives: Section 35A (Binding directions across lending rates, priority sector, fees, cybersecurity).
  3. Supervisory Enforcement:
    • Section 35: Power of inspection and audit.
    • Section 36AA: Removal of managerial and executive personnel.
    • Section 36AAA: Supersession of Board of Directors and appointment of Administrator.
    • Section 45: Schemes of reconstruction, compulsory amalgamation, or moratorium.
  4. Corporate Governance Rules (2021 RBI Master Directions):
    • Voting Rights Cap: 26%26%26% (Section 12(2)).
    • Promoter Shareholding: Maximum 26%26%26% of voting equity after 15 years.
    • Executive Limits: MD/CEO & WTD max tenure 15 years; max age 70 years; 3-year cooling-off period.

RBI Regulation & Banking Supervision · Concept 2 of 2

Banking Supervision & Enforcement: CAMELS Rating Model, Risk-Based Supervision (RBS) & The Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework

The institutional framework through which the Reserve Bank of India monitors financial health, identifies emerging vulnerabilities, and enforces corrective remedies in commercial banks. Covers the supervisory oversight of the Board for Financial Supervision (BFS), the CAMELS rating framework (Capital adequacy, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Systems & controls), the forward-looking Risk-Based Supervision (RBS) model, and the mandatory Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework based on Capital (CRAR/CET1), Asset Quality (Net NPA), and Leverage thresholds.

Intuition & Context

Regulation vs Supervision: Writing the Traffic Rules vs Driving the Patrol Car

To understand central banking, distinguish between two often-confused functions:

  1. Banking Regulation (Rule-Making): Drafting the laws and prudential boundaries (e.g., "All banks must maintain 9% capital adequacy and classify 90-day overdue loans as NPAs").
  2. Banking Supervision (Policing & Enforcement): Inspecting bank records, evaluating loan books, assessing IT systems, and taking punitive action if a bank breaks the rules.

Supervision acts as the diagnostic medical check-up of the financial system—detecting financial sickness early before a bank collapses into insolvency.

Core Principle

The CAMELS Framework & Risk-Based Supervision (RBS)

The RBI evaluates the soundness of commercial banks using two integrated methodologies:

1. The CAMELS Supervisory Rating Framework

  • C (Capital Adequacy): Capacity of equity capital to absorb unexpected loan default shocks.
  • A (Asset Quality): Level of NPAs, slippages, sector concentration, and adequacy of provisioning.
  • M (Management Soundness): Competence of board, internal audit controls, compliance, and corporate governance.
  • E (Earnings & Profitability): Quality of Net Interest Margin (NIM), Return on Assets (RoA), and sustainability of profits.
  • L (Liquidity): Adequacy of liquid assets to meet sudden depositor run-offs (Liquidity Coverage Ratio / LCR).
  • S (Systems & Controls / Sensitivity to Market Risk): Robustness of IT infrastructure, cyber security, and interest rate risk defense.

2. Risk-Based Supervision (RBS / SPARC)

Historically, supervision was backward-looking (checking whether past transactions complied with rules). Under Risk-Based Supervision (Supervisory Program for Assessment of Risk and Capital - SPARC), the RBI assesses forward-looking inherent risks and the bank's risk-management governance.

Mechanism & Operational Rule

The Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework (Revised 2022)

When a bank's financial metrics deteriorate, the RBI does not wait for bankruptcy. It places the bank under the Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework—a legally binding quarantine that automatically restricts risky activities:

Mandatory vs Discretionary Corrective Actions

  • Mandatory Actions (Automatic Trigger):
    • Restriction on Dividend Distribution: Bank cannot pay dividends to shareholders or remit profits abroad.
    • Restriction on Branch Expansion: Domestic and overseas branch expansion frozen.
    • Promoter Capital Injection: Promoters/Govt legally mandated to infuse fresh equity.
    • Management Compensation Cap: Restrictions on executive bonuses and director fees.
  • Discretionary Actions (RBI Discretion): Curbs on lending to high-risk corporate sectors, staff recruitment freezes, special audit inspections, or forcing a merger under Section 45.
Exam Application & Traps

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Indicators in Revised PCA (2022): The revised PCA framework tracks THREE indicators: (1) Capital (CRAR/CET1), (2) Asset Quality (Net NPA), and (3) Leverage. Profitability (Return on Assets / RoA) was REMOVED as a trigger indicator in the 2021 revision.
  • Trap 2: Gross vs Net NPA in PCA: PCA triggers are based on Net NPA ratio (ge6.0%ge 6.0%ge6.0%), NOT Gross NPA.
  • Trap 3: Does PCA apply to NBFCs?: In December 2021, RBI introduced a separate PCA framework for NBFCs (Middle, Upper, and Top Layers), effective October 2022.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: CAMELS components, Revised PCA 2022 triggers (Net NPA 6%, CET1 thresholds), and BFS composition.
  • IIBF DBF, SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Definitions of CAMELS, purpose of PCA, and mandatory restrictions under PCA.

🏛️ 5-Minute Architecture & Key Revision

Supervisory & PCA Architectural Matrix:

  1. Supervisory Body: Board for Financial Supervision (BFS - 1994; Chaired by RBI Governor, Dy Governors as ex-officio, 4 Central Board directors).
  2. CAMELS Pillars: C (CRAR), A (NPA/Slippages/PCR), M (Governance/Internal Controls), E (NIM/RoA), L (LCR/Maturity mismatches), S (IT/Cyber/Market Risk).
  3. Revised PCA Framework (2022):
    • Primary Indicators: Capital (CET1/CRAR), Asset Quality (Net NPA), Leverage (Tier 1 Leverage).
    • Threshold 1: Net NPA ≥6.0%\ge 6.0%≥6.0% OR CET1 <8.0%< 8.0%<8.0% (Dividend ban, branch restrictions).
    • Threshold 2: Net NPA ≥9.0%\ge 9.0%≥9.0% OR CET1 <6.5%< 6.5%<6.5% (Mandatory capital infusion, expansion curbs).
    • Threshold 3: CET1 <5.5%< 5.5%<5.5% (Discretionary resolution under Section 45 / amalgamation).
