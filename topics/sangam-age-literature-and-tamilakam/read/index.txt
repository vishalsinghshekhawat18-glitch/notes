16:T998,The word *Sangam* refers to an academy or assembly of Tamil poets established under the royal patronage of the **Pandya kings of Madurai**.

### 1. The Three Sangams (Muchangam Tradition)

| Sangam Assembly | Traditional Seat | Patron Dynasty | Key Surviving Texts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First (Mudal Sangam)** | Ten-Madurai (submerged) | Early Pandyas | *No surviving literature*; attended by gods and legendary sages (Agastya). |
| **Second (Idai Sangam)** | Kapatapuram (submerged) | Early Pandyas | **Tolkappiyam** (sole surviving work; grammar, poetics, sociology). |
| **Third (Kadai Sangam)** | Northern Madurai (Modern Madurai) | Historical Pandyas | **Ettuttokai** (8 Anthologies), **Pattuppattu** (10 Idylls), **Patinenkilkanakku** (18 Minor Works). |

### 2. The Five Ecological Landscapes (Aintinai)

| Tinai (Landscape) | Physical Geography | Primary Deity | Human Occupation | Associated Mood / Emotion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kurinji** | Hilly / Mountainous | **Murugan** (Seyon) | Hunting & Honey collection | Secret union of lovers (*punartal*) |
| **Mullai** | Forest / Pastoral pastures | **Mayon** (Krishna/Vishnu) | Cattle rearing & Dairy farming | Patient waiting of wife (*iruttal*) |
| **Marudam** | Fertile riverine plains | **Vendan** (Indra) | Wet-rice agriculture | Wifes sulking / infidelity quarrels (*udal*) |
| **Neydal** | Coastal / Littoral tracts | **Varunan** (Sea God) | Fishing & Salt manufacturing | Anxious pining for sea-farer (*irankal*) |
| **Palai** | Arid / Parched wasteland | **Kotravai** (War Goddess) | Banditry, raiding & waylaying | Painful separation of lovers (*pirital*) |

### 1. Major Commercial Ports of Ancient South India

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Tondi** | Near Kadalundi, Kerala | Cheras | Secondary western port for spices and timber. |
| **Korkai** | Tamraparni estuary, Tuticorin, TN | Pandyas | Renowned world center of deep-sea pearl fisheries (*muttukkulittal*). |

```18:T450,The massive influx of Roman coinage fundamentally shaped peninsular Indian economic structures:

1. **Numismatic Hoards & Slashing**: Hundreds of hoards containing thousands of Roman gold (*aurei*) and silver (*denarii*) coins have been unearthed across peninsular India (e.g., Coimbatore, Madurai, Kottayam, Nagarjunakonda). Many coins feature deliberate punch-marks or chisel cuts across the Roman emperors face, indicating they circulated in India as **bullion by weight** rather than sovereign fiat currency.
2. **The Yavana Presence**: The term *Yavana* (originally Ionian Greeks) expanded in Sangam texts to encompass all Westerners (Greeks, Romans, Arabs). Yavanas served as palace bodyguards, engineering watchmen, ship captains, and wine merchants residing in specialized coastal enclaves (*Yavanar Irukkai*).
3. **Decline of the Roman Connection**: By the 3rd century CE, internal crises in the Roman Empire, inflation, and the rise of the Sassanian Empire in Persia disrupted the direct Red Sea maritime
