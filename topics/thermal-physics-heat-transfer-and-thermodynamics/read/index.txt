### Thermometer Types:
- **Clinical Thermometer**: Range **$35^\circ\text{C} - 42^\circ\text{C}$** ($95^\circ\text{F} - 108^\circ\text{F}$). Contains a **kink/constriction** near the bulb to prevent mercury from dropping before reading.

Sea Breeze vs Land Breeze Mechanics
- **Sea Breeze (Occurs during DAYTIME)**:
  - Land heats up much faster than sea water (water has extreme high specific heat).
  - Hot air over land expands, becomes less dense, and **rises** (convection low-pressure zone).
  - Cooler, denser air from the sea rushes inland to replace it $\implies$ **Sea Breeze blows from Sea to Land during Day**.
- **Land Breeze (Occurs at NIGHT)**:
  - Land cools down much faster than sea water.
  - Air over warmer sea is now warmer and rises.
  - Cooler air from land rushes out toward the sea $\implies$ **Land Breeze blows from Land to Sea at Night**.

### 2. The Vacuum Thermos Flask (Defeating All 3 Heat Losses):
- **To Stop Conduction & Convection**: Double-walled glass bottle with a **vacuum space** sealed between the walls (no molecules to conduct or convect).
- **To Stop Radiation**: Inner walls are **silver-coated (mirrored)** to reflect outgoing radiant heat back inside and reflect external radiation away.
- **To Stop Conduction through Cap**: Insulating plastic or cork stopper.15:T56d,### 1. The Physics of Steam Burns
- $1 \, \text{gram}$ of boiling water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ touching your skin cools down to body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$), releasing:
  $$Q_{\text{water}} = m s \Delta T = 1 \, \text{g} \times 1 \, \text{cal/g}\cdot^\circ\text{C} \times (100 - 37) = \mathbf{63 \, \text{calories}}$$
- $1 \, \text{gram}$ of steam at $100^\circ\text{C}$ touching your skin must FIRST condense into liquid water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ before cooling, releasing its latent heat of vaporisation ($2.26 \times 10^6 \, \text{J/kg} = 540 \, \text{cal/g}$):
  $$Q_{\text{steam}} = m L_v + m s \Delta T = 540 + 63 = \mathbf{603 \, \text{calories}}$$
- **Steam delivers almost $10 \text{ TIMES}$ more thermal energy into skin than boiling water!**

### 2. Thermal Expansion in Engineering
- **Linear Expansion of Solids**: $\Delta L = L_0 \alpha \Delta T$ (where $\alpha$ is coefficient of linear expansion).
- **Practical Engineering Gaps**:
  - **Railway Track Gaps**: Small expansion gaps are left between steel rails to prevent buckling during scorching summers.
  - **Bridges on Rollers**: Long iron bridges rest on rollers at one end to accommodate thermal expansion.
  - **Bimetallic Strips**: Two bonded strips of metals with different expansion coefficients (e.g. Brass and Iron) bend upon heating, acting as automatic switches in **thermostats and fire alarms**.16:T4e2,### The Four Determinants of Evaporation Rate:
1. **Surface Area ($A$)**: Spreading wet clothes on a line dries them vastly faster than leaving them in a crumpled heap.
2. **Temperature ($T$)**: Hot summer days provide more kinetic energy for surface molecules to overcome intermolecular attraction.
3. **Humidity ($H$)**: Humidity is the amount of water vapor already present in air. Air can hold only a definite amount of water vapor at a given temperature. If humidity is high (e.g. monsoon/coastal), evaporation slows to a crawl (sweat does not evaporate, causing sticky discomfort).
4. **Wind Speed ($v$)**: Strong breeze blows away the saturated vapor layer hovering over the liquid surface, clearing space for fresh vaporisation.

### How an Earthen Pitcher (Matka) Keeps Water Cool:
- An earthen clay pot has millions of **microscopic pores**.
- Water continuously seeps through these pores to the outer clay surface.
- On the outer surface, this thin water film evaporates into the dry air, drawing its **latent heat of vaporisation directly from the clay pot and internal water**.
- As heat is continuously extracted, the remaining water inside cools down significantly ($5^\circ\text{C} - 10^\circ\text{C}$ below ambient room
