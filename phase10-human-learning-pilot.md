## 3. Stage C: Immediate Teach-Back Results

*Protocol*: Closed-book verbal explanation immediately following reading. Scored 0–2 across 5 dimensions (Max 10 per question).

| # | Teach-Back Prompt | Learner's Unedited Verbal Response | Score (out of 10) | Evaluation Breakdown |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **TB1** | *"What is inflation?"* | *"Inflation is when the average price level across a whole basket of goods keeps rising over time, which makes the purchasing power of your ₹100 note drop. It's not just one tomato getting expensive after a storm."* | **10 / 10** | Concept (2), Causal (2), Scope (2), Example (2), Terminology (2). Perfect mental model. |
| **TB2** | *"Explain inflation → disinflation → deflation."* | *"Inflation is prices rising. Disinflation is prices still rising, but at a slower speed—like going from 8% to 4%, so the price index goes from 100 to 108 to 112.3. Deflation is when prices actually drop below zero, like negative inflation."* | **10 / 10** | Reconstructed the speedometer analogy and numerical index ladder flawlessly. |
| **TB3** | *"Why can a repo-rate hike reduce inflation?"* | *"When RBI raises the Repo rate, banks have to pay more to borrow, so they raise their lending rates like EBLR on home and car loans. People face higher EMIs, so they borrow and spend less. This cools down total demand and brings prices down, if inflation was caused by high demand."* | **9 / 10** | Clear transmission chain; correctly added the demand-pull scope condition. |
| **TB4** | *"Why might a repo-rate hike fail to solve an onion supply shock?"* | *"Because hiking interest rates doesn't grow more onions. It cools general demand, but the onion shortage is a physical supply bottleneck. That needs the government's supply lever—like releasing buffer stocks from FCI godowns or cutting import taxes."* | **10 / 10** | Perfect application of the "Two Hands of Policy" boundary. |
| **TB5** | *"CPI and WPI measure different things. Explain the difference."* | *"CPI is published by NSO with base 2012, measures retail prices for consumers, includes services, and has ~46% food weight. | **TB6** | *"Why can inflation affect debtors and creditors differently?"* | *"If you took a fixed-rate loan, unexpected inflation helps you because you repay in rupees that buy less. But if you have a floating-rate home loan, your bank raises your EMI, so you don't gain. And creditors lose because their fixed return buys fewer goods."* | **10 / 10** | Mastered the fixed vs floating nuance. |
| **TB7** | *"Why isn't inflation automatically a regressive tax on everyone?"* | *"It hurts poor people and daily wage workers the most because they spend over 50% on food and have no assets. But farmers who sell surplus crops can gain from higher prices, and government workers get Dearness Allowance to cover the price rise."* | **10 / 10** | Nuanced conditional distributional reasoning. |
