## 2. Scenario-by-Scenario Evaluation Record

### Scenario 1: Food Supply Shock with Stable Core
* **Prompt**: *"A severe localized drought causes a 35% collapse in onion and tomato harvest, driving headline food inflation to 12%, while Core CPI remains steady at 3.8%. Should the RBI aggressively hike the Repo rate? Explain why or why not, and identify the correct policy tools."*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"No, RBI should not aggressively hike the Repo rate because hiking rates cannot grow more onions. This is a structural cost-push/skewflation shock in perishable food. Raising interest rates would only hurt general economic growth and increase EMIs without fixing the crop loss. The right response is the government's supply lever—releasing buffer stocks under OMSS, importing onions, and cracking down on hoarding at mandis."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Mastered the Two Hands of Policy and supply-side boundary.

### Scenario 2: Falling Inflation Rate with Higher Price Level
* **Prompt**: *"Headline CPI inflation drops from 8% in Year 1 to 4% in Year 2. A consumer claims: 'The government is lying because prices in my local market are higher than last year.' How would you explain what actually happened?"*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"The consumer is confusing Disinflation with Deflation. Think of inflation as a speedometer. In Year 1, prices jumped from ₹100 to ₹108 (an 8% jump). In Year 2, inflation slowed to 4%, so the price index rose from ₹108 to ₹112.3. Prices did not fall; they are at an all-time high of ₹112.3, but they grew at a slower speed. This is the Base Effect at work."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Perfect application of the 3-step Base Effect ladder and speedometer analogy.

### Scenario 3: Unexpected Inflation on Fixed-Rate Borrowing
* **Prompt**: *"An entrepreneur borrows ₹50 lakh on a 5-year fixed-rate commercial loan at 8.0%. Unexpected generalized inflation surges from 4% to 11%. Who benefits and who loses between the borrower and the lending bank? Why?"*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"The entrepreneur (borrower) benefits because the loan is at a fixed nominal rate. The borrower repays the bank in rupees that have lost real purchasing power due to 11% inflation. The lending bank loses because its real return is negative ($r \approx 8.0\% - 11.0\% = -3.0\%$). This gain happens because the inflation was unexpected and the contract was fixed."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Accurately integrated the exact Fisher effect and fixed-debt scope conditions.

### Scenario 4: Cash-Rich Corporation & Monetary Transmission
* **Prompt**: *"A large cash-rich FMCG company has zero bank debt and finances all its factory expansion from retained cash earnings. The RBI hikes the Repo rate by 150 bps. Is this monetary policy tightening completely irrelevant to this corporation? Explain the mechanism."*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"No, it is not irrelevant. While the direct borrowing channel doesn't affect the company (it has no bank debt), monetary policy affects it indirectly through consumer demand. Commercial banks will raise floating rates on home loans (EBLR), increasing mortgage EMIs for urban households. Families with higher monthly EMI burdens will cut back on discretionary FMCG spending, lowering the company's retail sales."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Demonstrated profound grasp of indirect demand channels and transmission heterogeneity.

### Scenario 5: Fiscal Bracket Creep
* **Prompt**: *"A salaried employee receives an 8% annual salary increment purely to match an 8% rise in the general cost of living. The government only raises statutory income tax exemption slabs by 2%. What happens to the worker's real purchasing power and actual tax burden?"*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"Even though the worker's pre-tax real purchasing power is unchanged (salary +8%, prices +8%), the worker suffers from fiscal bracket creep. Because tax brackets only adjusted by 2%, a larger portion of the worker's nominal salary gets pushed into a higher tax bracket. As a result, the worker pays a higher percentage in taxes and their post-tax real income actually declines, while the government gains unlegislated tax revenue."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Reconstructed the progressive tax bracket creep mechanism flawlessly.

### Scenario 6: Service Cost Surge with Flat WPI
* **Prompt**: *"Private hospital room tariffs and school tuition fees rise by 15% across major Indian cities, but the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) registers 0.0% inflation. Is there a calculation error in WPI? Explain."*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"No, there is no calculation error. The current WPI series (Base 2011-12) covers only physical commodities (Manufactured products, Primary articles, Fuel) and excludes services entirely by index design. Hospital fees and school tuition are consumer services, so they appear in the Consumer Price Index (CPI), but have 0% weight in WPI."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Mastered index scope and services exclusion without falling into the false physical rule.

### Scenario 7: Cost-Push Shock & Pricing Power
* **Prompt**: *"Global electricity and industrial diesel tariffs rise by 25%. Firm A holds a monopoly patent on life-saving pharmaceuticals, while Firm B manufactures generic plastic buckets in a cut-throat local market. Why will their final price responses differ?"*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"Because Cost-Push pass-through is conditioned by pricing power and demand elasticity. Firm A has high pricing power and low demand elasticity (patients must buy the medicine), so it can pass 100% of the energy cost hike into higher drug prices. Firm B operates in a highly competitive market where consumers will switch to rival bucket brands, so Firm B must absorb the cost shock in lower profit margins or seek input substitutes."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Applied the input substitutability and market pricing power qualification cleanly.

### Scenario 8: CPI vs WPI Divergence
* **Prompt**: *"In a given quarter, headline CPI inflation rises to 6.8% while WPI inflation falls to -1.2% (deflation). Can both numbers be statistically correct? Give two structural reasons."*
* **Learner's Unedited Reasoning**:
  > *"Yes, both can be completely correct due to structural basket differences:\n1. Food weight: Food carries a massive ~46% weight in CPI compared to only ~24% in WPI. If domestic agriculture suffers a monsoon shock, CPI spikes.\n2. Manufacturing and Global Commodities: WPI is dominated by manufactured products (~64%) and fuel (~13%). If global crude and metal prices crash, WPI enters deflation while domestic food pressures keep retail CPI elevated."*
* **Auditor Assessment**: **PASS (10/10)** — Complete structural understanding of Indian price index dynamics.
