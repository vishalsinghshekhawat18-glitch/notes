## 1. What, Where, How and When? (Historical Methodology & Geography)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> History is reconstructed through primary sources (manuscripts, inscriptions, archaeology) and geographical physical constraints.

### 🧠 Visual Mind Map & Structural Diagram

🗺️ Early Human Settlements & Geographical Anchors

1. Narmada Valley

Earliest hunter-gatherers (Hathnora Homo erectus fossil found by A.K. Sonakia).

2. Sulaiman & Kirthar Hills

First farming (~8000 BP): Wheat & Barley cultivation, rearing sheep, goats, cattle (Mehrgarh).

3. Garo Hills & Vindhyas

First Rice cultivation north of Vindhyas (Mahagara & Koldihwa, UP).

### ⚡ 1. Beginner Jargon Unpack

• **Manuscripts:** Handwritten records on palm leaves (*Tala-patra*) or birch bark (*Bhurja-patra* grown in the Himalayas). Vulnerable to insects/decay.
• **Inscriptions:** Writing carved onto hard surfaces (stone, metal plates, temple walls). Durable primary historical evidence (*Epigraphy*).
• **Archaeology:** Scientific study of human history through material remains (*artifacts, bones, pottery, coins*).

### 📖 2. Layer 1: Source Material & Geographical Settling Matrix

| Historical Region | Timeline | Key Agricultural / Human Landmark | Archeological Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Narmada River** | ~100,000+ BP | Food gathering & hunting communities | Hathnora Skull fossil (*Narmada Man*) |
| **Sulaiman & Kirthar (NW)** | ~8000 BP (~6000 BCE) | Wheat & Barley farming; Mud brick houses | Mehrgarh (Balochistan) |
| **Garo Hills & Vindhyas** | ~7000–5000 BP | Earliest Rice Cultivation in India | Koldihwa & Mahagara (Belan Valley) |
| **Indus & Tributaries** | ~4700 BP (2700 BCE) | Early urban civilization (Harappan cities) | Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal |
| **Ganga Valley & Son** | ~2500 BP (500 BCE) | Second Urbanization; Rise of Magadha | Pataliputra, Rajgir fort walls |

### 🧠 3. Layer 2: Underlying Reasoning ("The Why")

Why did early urban cities emerge along the **Indus River (2700 BCE)** first, but along the **Ganga Valley (500 BCE)** much later?

• **Indus Valley:** Arid/semi-arid floodplain required light clearing; alluvial soil was easily tilled with wooden plows.
• **Ganga Valley:** Dense, humid tropical forests required **Iron Axes and Iron Plowshares** (available only post-1000 BCE in Later Vedic Age) to clear the dense jungle and till heavy clay soil.

### 🧮 4. Layer 3: Worked Application (Chronological Diagnostic Scenario)

**Scenario:** An archaeologist unearths a site with palm-leaf manuscripts and copper plate land grants (*Tamra-patra*). Determine the likely era.

**Step-by-Step Resolution:**

• Palm-leaf manuscripts survived mostly in South Indian temples or dry regions, dating from ~1st Millennium CE onward.
• Copper plate land grants (*Tamra-patra*) became widespread under the Guptas, Vakatakas, and Cholas (4th–12th Century CE).
• The site belongs to the **Early Medieval or Classical Period**, NOT the Harappan or Vedic Period (where writing was on seals or orally transmitted *Sruti*).

### 🏛️ 5. Concrete Anchor (Primary Historical Record)

**Rosetta Stone & Asokan Pillar Inscriptions:** Asoka used Prakrit in Brahmi/Kharosthi scripts and Aramaic/Greek in NW frontiers (Kandahar inscription) to communicate royal edicts directly to citizens.

### ⚖️ 6. Debates & Controversies

**Origin of the Name 'India' vs 'Bharat':**

• *The Controversy:* 'India' comes from the Indus (*Sindhu* in Sanskrit; *Indos* in Greek; *Hind* in Iranian). 'Bharat' comes from the Rigvedic tribe *Bharatas* residing in the NW.
• *Phase 2 Core Question:* "Geography dictates the historical trajectory of subcontinent urbanization." Discuss.

• **Tier 2 (Applied Scenario):** Explain why birch bark manuscripts were predominantly produced in the Himalayan region.

## 4. In the Earliest Cities (Harappan Civilisation - 2600–1900 BCE)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Harappan urban planning featured burnt-brick grid layouts, advanced drainage, specialized craftsmanship, and standardized weights.

### 🧠 Visual Mind Map & Structural Diagram

🗺️ Harappan Urban Architecture & Major Sites

Urban Division: Citadel (West, raised, public/administrative) + Lower Town (East, larger, residential grid).

Mohenjo-daro: Great Bath (bitumen waterproofing), Granary, Dancing Girl bronze statue, Priest King.

Lothal (Gujarat): Artificial Brick Dockyard (Tidal basin), Rice husk remains, Bead-making factory.

Dholavira (Rann of Kutch): Unique 3-tier town planning (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town), 10-sign Wooden Signboard, Water Reservoirs.

Kalibangan (Rajasthan): Ploughed field surface (wooden plow grooves), Fire altars, no drainage in lower town.

### ⚡ 1. Beginner Jargon Unpack

• **Citadel:** Raised western platform constructed on mud-brick foundations, housing public structures like granaries and assembly halls.
• **Steatite Seals:** Soft stone carved with animal motifs (Unicorn, Pashupati Mahadeva, Humped Bull) and undeciphered logo-syllabic script (written right-to-left *Boustrophedon*).
• **Chert Weights:** Binary weight system (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 up to 640) for trade; decimal system for higher units.

### 📖 2. Layer 1: Major Harappan Sites & Findings Matrix

| Site Name | Location | Key Archeological Discoveries | Unique Structural Feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Harappa** | Ravi River (Punjab, Pakistan) | 6 Granaries in a row, Coffin burial (H-Cemetery), Red sandstone torso | First excavated site (Dayaram Sahni 1921) |
| **Mohenjo-daro** | Indus River (Sindh, Pakistan) | Great Bath, Great Granary, Bronze Dancing Girl, Steatite Priest King | Largest city; burnt brick covered drains |
| **Lothal** | Bhogava River (Gujarat) | Tidal Brick Dockyard, Double burial, Ivory scale, Bead factory | Port city connecting Persian Gulf trade |
| **Kalibangan** | Ghaggar River (Rajasthan) | Pre-Harappan & Harappan ploughed field, Fire Altars, Camel bones | Decorative paved bricks; no underground drains |
| **Dholavira** | Luni River (Kutch, Gujarat) | 3-part layout (Citadel, Middle, Lower), Water Harvesting Reservoirs | Large stone signboard with 10 signs |
| **Rakhigarhi** | Ghaggar-Hakra (Haryana) | Largest IVC site in India; DNA extraction from skeleton | Granary & terracotta wheel remains |
| **Chanhudaro** | Indus River (Sindh) | Bead-making workshop, Inkpot, Lipstick, No Citadel! | Only city without a Citadel |
| **Banawali** | Fatehabad (Haryana) | Terracotta model of plow, Barley grains | Radial street pattern |

### 🧠 3. Layer 2: Underlying Reasoning ("The Why")

Why was the **Harappan Drainage System** superior to contemporary Mesopotamian and Egyptian civilizations?

• Every house was connected to street drains built with burnt bricks mortar-sealed with gypsum and bitumen.
• Drains had inspection manholes at regular intervals for cleaning—demonstrating civic hygiene and centralized municipal authority over 4500 years ago!

### 🧮 4. Layer 3: Worked Application (Diagnostic Trade Scenario)

**Scenario:** A merchant seal found at Susa (Mesopotamia) features a humped bull and Harappan script symbols alongside a text mentioning *Meluhha*.

**Step-by-Step Resolution:**

• Mesopotamian texts record trade with three intermediate lands: *Dilmun* (Bahrain), *Magan* (Oman coast), and *Meluhha* (Indus Valley).
• The presence of Harappan seals in Ur, Kish, and Susa proves long-distance maritime trade via the Persian Gulf.
• *Meluhha* was the Mesopotamian name for the Harappan civilization.

### 🏛️ 5. Concrete Anchor (Artifact Study)

**Pashupati Seal (Mohenjo-daro):** Proto-Shiva seated in yogic posture surrounded by 4 animals: Elephant, Tiger, Rhinoceros, Buffalo, and two Deers at his feet.

### ⚖️ 6. Debates & Controversies

**Decline of the Harappan Civilisation (~1900 BCE):**

• *The Controversy:* Wheeler proposed 'Aryan Invasion' theory based on skeletons at Mohenjo-daro. Modern consensus rejects this, favoring **Climate Change, Tectonic River Course Shifts (Drying of Saraswati/Ghaggar)**, and monsoon weakening.
• *Phase 2 Core Question:* "The Harappan civilization was not destroyed by external conquest, but collapsed due to ecological desiccation." Evaluate.

### ❓ 7. 3-Tier Practice & Retention Section

• **Tier 1 (Recall MCQ):** Which Harappan site is the ONLY one divided into THREE distinct administrative parts? (A: Lothal, B: Kalibangan, **C: Dholavira**, D: Chanhudaro) [Correct: C].
• **Tier 2 (Applied Scenario):** Explain how standardized brick ratio (1:2:4) across 1500 km proves centralized Harappan governance.
• **Tier 3 (Mains Prompt):** *'Evaluate the urban planning, water management, and civic architecture of the Harappan civilization and their relevance to modern smart cities.'*.

## 28. How, When and Where: Historiography & Sources of Modern Indian History

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The study of modern Indian history requires critically analyzing diverse historiographical schools—Colonial, Nationalist, Marxist, and Subaltern—to decode administrative records, vernacular literature, and oral testimonies without imperialist bias.

### 📚 1. Major Schools of Modern Indian Historiography

| Historiographical School | Prominent Historians | Core Thesis & Ideological Lens | Critical Limitations Flagged |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Colonial / Imperialist** | James Mill (*History of British India*, 1817), Vincent Smith, Mountstuart Elphinstone. | Depicted pre-colonial India as backward, stagnant, and fractured; portrayed British rule as a benevolent "Civilising Mission" bringing the rule of law and modern institutions. | Justified colonial exploitation; ignored economic de-industrialisation; divided Indian history communally into "Hindu", "Muslim", and "British" periods. |
| **Nationalist** | Dadabhai Naoroji, R.C. Dutt, K.P. Jayaswal, R.C. Majumdar. | Challenged colonial assumptions; exposed economic drain of wealth; highlighted ancient democratic traditions, anti-colonial solidarity, and national unity. | Tended to romanticize ancient and medieval institutions; underplayed caste, agrarian, and subaltern class contradictions. |
| **Marxist** | Rajni Palme Dutt (*India Today*, 1940), A.R. Desai (*Social Background of Indian Nationalism*), Bipan Chandra. | Interpreted modern history through the lens of dialectical materialism, class struggle, modes of production, and structural economic exploitation by colonial capital. | Occasionally overemphasized economic determinism at the expense of religious, cultural, and regional psychological drivers. |
| **Subaltern Studies** | Ranajit Guha, Dipesh Chakrabarty, Partha Chatterjee. | Shifted focus from colonial elites and bourgeois nationalist leadership to "politics of the people"—peasants, adivasis, workers, and marginalized castes. | Can fragment the broader anti-imperialist movement into localized, disjointed episodes. |

### 🏛️ 2. Primary Archival & Epistemological Sources

• **Official Colonial Archives:**
  - *National Archives of India (NAI, New Delhi):* Holds records of the East India Company (EIC) from 1748 onward, Home Department proceedings, Foreign & Political Department files.
  - *State Archives:* Kolkata, Mumbai, Chennai (Presidency records), Punjab State Archives (Sikh kingdom and annexation records).
  - *Judicial Records:* Mayor's Courts (1726), Supreme Court of Calcutta (1774), and High Court proceedings documenting property disputes and criminal trials.
• **Non-Official & Vernacular Sources:**
  - Private letters, diaries, travelogues (*e.g. Bishop Heber, Victor Jacquemont*).
  - Vernacular press (*Bengal Gazette, Sambad Kaumudi, Rast Goftar, Kesari, Hindu Patriot*).
  - Oral narratives and folk songs (*ballads of the 1857 revolt, tribal songs of Birsa Munda and Sidhu-Kanhu*).

## 29. From Trade to Territory: European Penetration & British Conquest (1757–1856)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The British East India Company transformed from a commercial maritime joint-stock trading enterprise into a paramount military and administrative power through the systematic annexation of regional successor states using financial diplomacy, divide-and-rule, and institutional mechanisms like Subsidiary Alliance and Doctrine of Lapse.

### ⚔️ 1. European Arrival Sequence & Decisive Clashes

```
  Portuguese (1498) ──► Dutch (1605) ──► English (1600/1608) ──► Danes (1616) ──► French (1664)
```

• **Portuguese:** Vasco da Gama arrived at Calicut (1498, received by Zamorin). Francisco de Almeida initiated the **"Blue Water Policy"** (*Cartaz system* for maritime naval monopoly). Alfonso de Albuquerque captured Goa from Sultan of Bijapur (1510).
• **Elimination of Rivals:**
  - *Battle of Swally (1612):* British naval fleet under Thomas Best defeated Portuguese, securing royal permission to open factory at Surat (1613).
  - *Battle of Bedara (1759):* British defeated the Dutch in Bengal, crushing Dutch political ambitions in India.
  - *Three Carnatic Wars (1746–1763):* Anglo-French rivalry. Decisive **Battle of Wandiwash (1760)**: British General Eyre Coote routed the French under Count de Lally. **Treaty of Paris (1763)** restricted French to unfortified trading posts (Pondicherry, Chandernagore, Mahe, Karaikal).

### 🏛️ 2. The Conquest of Bengal: Foundation of British Empire

| Battle / Event | Date & Location | Key Belligerents | Decisive Historical Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Battle of Plassey** | **June 23, 1757**<br>(Nadia, Bengal) | Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal) vs. Robert Clive (EIC). | Treachery of Mir Jafar (Commander-in-Chief), Rai Durlabh, and Jagat Seth led to Nawab's defeat. EIC installed Mir Jafar as puppet Nawab; acquired 24 Parganas zamindari. |
| **Battle of Buxar** | **October 22, 1764**<br>(Bihar) | Combined Forces of: Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh), and Shah Alam II (Mughal Emperor) vs. **Major Hector Munro (EIC)**. | Decisive military triumph over the Mughal sovereign. Established British military supremacy across Northern India. |
| **Treaty of Allahabad** | **August 1765** | Signed by Robert Clive with Shah Alam II and Shuja-ud-Daulah. | Mughal Emperor granted **Diwani Rights (Civil revenue collection)** of **Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa** to EIC in return for an annual tribute of ₹26 Lakh. Awadh paid ₹50 Lakh war indemnity. |
| **Dual System of Government** | **1765–1772**<br>(Introduced by Clive) | Bifurcated governance: **Diwani** (revenue & civil justice) with EIC; **Nizamat** (police & criminal justice) with the puppet Nawab. | Complete division of power from responsibility. Devastated Bengal's economy; catalyzed the catastrophic Great Bengal Famine of 1770 (one-third population perished). Abolished by Warren Hastings in 1772. |

### 🛡️ 3. Instruments of Colonial Expansion

#### A. Policy of Ring Fence (Warren Hastings, 1772–1785)
• Aimed at creating buffer states around Company territories to defend against Afghan invaders and Marathas (e.g. defending Awadh's frontiers to insulate Bengal).

#### B. Subsidiary Alliance System (Lord Wellesley, 1798–1805)
*An ingenious diplomatic-military trap compelling Indian rulers to surrender external sovereignty in exchange for British protection:*
• **Core Conditions:**
  1. Indian ruler must disband native army and maintain a permanent British contingent at their own expense (paying fixed cash subsidy or ceding territory).
  2. A British Resident stationed at the native court; no European employed without British consent.
  3. No foreign alliances, negotiations, or wars with other states without British mediation.
• **Chronological Order of States Entering Subsidiary Alliance:**
  $$\text{Hyderabad (1798 \& 1800)} \longrightarrow \text{Mysore (1799)} \longrightarrow \text{Tanjore (1799)} \longrightarrow \text{Awadh (1801)} \longrightarrow \text{Peshwa Baji Rao II (Treaty of Bassein, 1802)} \longrightarrow \text{Bhonsle (1803)} \longrightarrow \text{Scindia (1804)}$$

#### C. Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie, 1848–1856)
• Stated that if an Indian ruler of a dependent state died without a natural biological male heir, his adopted son could inherit personal property but **NOT the state sovereignty**, which lapsed to the paramount British Crown.
• **Chronological Order of States Annexed under Doctrine of Lapse:**
  $$\text{Satara (1848)} \longrightarrow \text{Jaitpur \& Sambalpur (1849)} \longrightarrow \text{Baghat (1850)} \longrightarrow \text{Udaipur (1852)} \longrightarrow \text{Jhansi (1853)} \longrightarrow \text{Nagpur (1854)}$$
• *Exception Note:* **Awadh (1856)** was annexed NOT under Doctrine of Lapse, but on the fabricated grounds of **"internal misgovernance and chronic maladministration"** based on the Outram Report under Nawab Wajid Ali Shah.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 29:**
> 1. **First State under Subsidiary Alliance:** **Hyderabad (1798)** was the first state to sign Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance; Dupleix (French) was the first to conceptualize the subsidy model.
> 2. **Awadh's Annexation:** Awadh was **NOT annexed by Doctrine of Lapse**; it was annexed in **1856 on charges of misgovernance**.

## 30. Ruling the Countryside: Land Revenue Systems & Agrarian Transformation

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The colonial state commodified land and commercialized agriculture to maximize unrequited revenue extraction, replacing customary community rights with rigid private property regimes that impoverished the Indian peasantry and institutionalized rural usury.

### 🌾 1. The Three Colonial Land Revenue Settlements

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 COLONIAL LAND REVENUE SYSTEMS COMPARISON                    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. PERMANENT SETTLEMENT (1793) : 19% of British India (Bengal, Bihar, OR)   │
│ 2. RYOTWARI SYSTEM (1820)     : 51% of British India (Madras, Bombay, AS)  │
│ 3. MAHALWARI SYSTEM (1822)    : 30% of British India (NWFP, Punjab, CP)    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Parameter | Permanent Settlement (Zamindari) | Ryotwari System | Mahalwari System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Introduced By & Year** | **Lord Cornwallis (1793)**<br>(Plan designed by Sir John Shore) | **Thomas Munro & Alexander Read (1820)** | **Holt Mackenzie (1822)**<br>(Reformed by Bentinck & Bird, 1833) |
| **Geographical Area** | Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Northern Karnataka, Varanasi (**~19% of British India**). | Madras, Bombay Presidencies, parts of Assam and Coorg (**~51% of British India**). | North-Western Provinces, Punjab, Central Provinces, parts of Gangetic Valley (**~30%**). |
| **Settlement Made With** | **Zamindars** recognized as absolute owners of land. | Directly with the **Ryot (Cultivator)**. | The **Mahal (Village community / Estate)** collectively. |
| **Revenue Assessment** | **Fixed permanently in perpetuity**.<br>EIC Share: **10/11th**; Zamindar Share: **1/11th**. | Periodically reassessed (every 20–30 years). High demand: **50% to 60%** of gross produce. | Periodically revised; village headman (*Lambardar*) collected revenue. Demand set at **66% (later reduced to 50%)**. |
| **Consequences on Peasantry** | • **Sunset Law:** Zamindari auctioned if revenue not paid before sunset on fixed date.<br>• Led to sub-infeudation (*Patni taluqs*) and rack-renting. | • Extreme over-assessment; peasant reduced to tenant of the state.<br>• Failure to pay resulted in immediate land confiscation. | • Village headman exploited position.<br>• Inability to pay led to dispossession and moneylender domination. |

### 📉 2. Forced Commercialisation of Agriculture & Its Structural Fallouts

• **Shift from Food Crops to Cash Crops:** Peasants compelled to cultivate commercial crops for British industrial factories—**Indigo** (for textile dyeing), **Opium** (smuggled into China to balance British tea trade), **Raw Cotton** (Lancashire mills), and **Jute**.
• **Breakdown of Village Self-Sufficiency:** Peasantry forced into cash economy to pay fixed land taxes in cash, regardless of drought, floods, or harvest failure.
• **The Debt-Trap Cycle:** Peasant borrowed cash from moneylenders (*Mahajans / Sowcars*) at usurious interest (25%–100%) $\to$ Default led to mortgage and alienation of ancestral land $\to$ Peasant reduced from landowning cultivator to landless agricultural labourer.
• **Recurring Famines:** Commercialization without technological investment caused devastating famines (Orissa Famine 1866, Great Madras Famine 1876–78, Bengal Famine 1943).

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 30:**
> 1. **Largest Area Covered:** The **Ryotwari system covered the largest area (~51%)** of British India, NOT the Permanent Settlement (~19%).
> 2. **Sunset Law:** Directly linked to the **Permanent Settlement of 1793** (if the zamindar did not deposit the stipulated tax by sunset on the appointed date, his estate was confiscated and auctioned).

> Long before the organized national movement, marginalized rural communities, dispossessed feudal nobility, and indigenous tribes mounted fierce, localized rebellions against colonial revenue extortion, forest restrictions, and police corruption.

## 32. Forest Society & British Colonialism: Environmental Destruction & Tribal Displacement

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The British colonial state appropriated Indian forest wealth for imperial naval shipbuilding and railway expansion, legally criminalizing traditional tribal livelihoods through repressive statutory forest enactments.

### 🌲 1. Imperial Thirst for Timber & Forest Enclosures

• **Royal Navy Demand:** Depletion of English oak forests forced the British to exploit Indian teak forests (Malabar, Central India) to construct warships for imperial naval supremacy.
• **Railway Track Sleepers:** From the 1850s, railway track construction required enormous quantities of timber (approx. 1,760 to 2,000 wooden sleepers required per mile of track). Forests were recklessly clear-felled.
• **Dietrich Brandis:** German forestry expert appointed the first **Inspector General of Forests in India (1864)**. Founded the **Imperial Forest Service (1867)** and the **Imperial Forest Research Institute at Dehradun (1906)**.

### ⚖️ 2. The Statutory Enclosure Acts

| Statute | Core Provisions | Impact on Indigenous Communities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Forest Act of 1865** | First statutory assertion of government monopoly over Indian forests. | Extinguished customary tribal sovereignty; state claimed power to declare any land covered with trees as government forest. |
| **Indian Forest Act of 1878** | Classified all forests into **three statutory tiers**: (1) *Reserved Forests*, (2) *Protected Forests*, (3) *Village Forests*. | **Reserved Forests:** State exercised absolute monopoly; all customary tribal grazing, fuel gathering, and produce collection criminalized. |
| **Indian Forest Act of 1927** | Consolidated all forest laws; empowered Forest Officers with extensive police powers. | Further restricted minor forest produce rights; established severe penalties for unauthorized timber cutting or cattle grazing. |

• **Banning of Shifting Cultivation (*Swidden / Jhum*):** Colonial foresters viewed shifting agriculture (*Jhum in Northeast, Podu in Andhra, Bewar/Dahiya in Central India*) as destructive and wasteful of commercial timber. Its prohibition devastated tribal food security, forcing tribes into bonded plantation and mine labour.

> 2. **First Inspector General of Forests:** **Dietrich Brandis** (German expert), NOT a British civil servant.

## 33. Weavers, Iron Smelters & Factory Owners: De-Industrialisation & Colonial Economic Critique

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> British economic policy systematically converted India from an exporter of high-value manufactured textiles into an unrequited exporter of raw agricultural commodities and a captive market for Manchester manufactured goods, a process scientifically exposed as the "Drain of Wealth".

### 🧵 1. The Mechanics of Colonial De-Industrialisation

• **One-Way Free Trade:** Britain imposed exorbitant import tariffs (up to 70%–80%) on Indian handloom textiles entering Britain to protect its domestic infant industries, while British factory-made cotton goods entered India with near-zero customs duties.
• **Destruction of Urban Artisans:** Influx of cheap, machine-made cotton yarn and fabric from Lancashire and Manchester ruined traditional spinning and weaving centers (Dhaka, Murshidabad, Surat, Madurai).
• **Lord William Bentinck's Famous Remark (1834):** *"The misery hardly finds a parallel in the history of commerce. The bones of the cotton-weavers are bleaching the plains of India."*
• **Ruralisation of the Economy:** Displaced artisans had no modern domestic factories to absorb them; they were forced back onto land as impoverished tenant farmers, driving up agrarian land pressure and pauperism.

### 💸 2. Dadabhai Naoroji & The "Drain of Wealth" Theory

• **Pioneering Exposure:** First formulated by **Dadabhai Naoroji** in his paper *"Poverty of India"* (1867) and his landmark treatise **"Poverty and Un-British Rule in India" (1901)**.
• **Core Definition:** A unilateral transfer of national wealth from India to Britain for which India received no material, economic, or financial return whatsoever.
• **Components of the Drain:**
  1. **Home Charges:** Expenses incurred in Britain by the Secretary of State for India, including salaries, pensions, and leave allowances of India Office staff, British military officers, and diplomatic missions abroad.
  2. **Guaranteed Return on Railways:** British investors guaranteed an unearned **5% return on capital** out of Indian tax revenues, regardless of operating loss or efficiency ("private enterprise at public risk").
  3. **Interest on Public Debt:** Servicing colonial public debts incurred by the British Crown to finance imperial wars in Burma, China, and Afghanistan.
  4. **Profits of Foreign Capital:** Repatriation of profits by British managing agency houses, shipping cartels, and banking syndicates.
• **Prominent Economic Nationalists:** **Romesh Chunder (R.C.) Dutt** (*The Economic History of India*), **Dinshaw Wacha**, **G.V. Joshi**, and **Mahadev Govind Ranade**.

### 🏭 3. Rise of Modern Indigenous Industry & Colonial Factory Acts

• **Pioneering Enterprises:**
  - *Cotton Mill:* First successful modern cotton textile mill set up in **Bombay (1854)** by **Cowasjee Nanabhoy Davar**.
  - *Jute Mill:* First jute mill founded in **Rishra (near Calcutta, 1855)** by George Acland.
  - *Iron & Steel:* **Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO)** founded at Jamshedpur (1907) by **J.N. Tata** (commenced production 1911).
• **Colonial Factory Acts:**
  - **First Factory Act (1881, Lord Ripon):** Prohibited employment of children under 7 years; fixed working hours for children aged 7–12 at max **9 hours per day** with 1 hour daily rest and 4 monthly holidays. (Did not apply to tea/coffee plantations; applied only to factories with 100+ workers).
  - **Second Factory Act (1891, Lord Lansdowne):** Fixed working hours for women at max **11 hours per day** (with 1.5 hr rest); banned night work for women; raised child minimum age to 9 years (working hours 9–14 years capped at 7 hrs/day). Mandated weekly holiday for all workers.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 33:**
> 1. **Home Charges Exclusion:** Home Charges did **NOT include commercial purchases of food grains**; they consisted of India Office expenses, pensions, debt interest, and guaranteed railway dividends.
> 2. **First Factory Act 1881:** Focused **primarily on child labour**, NOT adult male or female working hours.

## 34. When People Rebel: The Great Revolt of 1857 & The Government of India Act 1858

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Revolt of 1857 was the culmination of a century of simmering civil, economic, and religious discontent against British exploitation. Though crushed militarily, it terminated Company rule and transformed British imperial policy into a cautious strategy of social conservatism and military segregation.

### 🔥 1. Catalysts of the Rebellion

• **Political Causes:** Dalhousie's aggressive **Doctrine of Lapse** (Jhansi, Satara, Nagpur); humiliation of the Mughal Emperor (eviction from Red Fort ordered); reckless **annexation of Awadh (1856)** which alienated the "nursery of the Bengal Army" (75,000 sepoys hailed from Awadh).
• **Economic Causes:** Ruin of peasantry through extortionate settlements; loss of rent-free *Inam* lands investigated by the Inam Commission (1852, 20,000 estates confiscated); destruction of traditional handicrafts.
• **Socio-Religious Fears:** Christian missionary proselytisation; **Religious Disabilities Act (Lex Loci Act, 1850)** allowing Hindu converts to Christianity to inherit ancestral property; Sati Abolition (1829) and Widow Remarriage Act (1856) perceived as foreign assault on religious order.
• **Military Discontent:**
  - **General Service Enlistment Act, 1856 (Lord Canning):** Mandated sepoys to serve overseas across the "black water" (*Kala Pani*), which caused caste loss for high-caste Brahmins and Rajputs.
  - Denial of foreign service allowance (*Batta*) when serving in Sindh and Punjab; racial insults by British officers.
• **Immediate Spark:** The introduction of the **Enfield P-53 Rifle**, requiring sepoys to bite off the cartridge greased with cow (sacred to Hindus) and pig fat (repugnant to Muslims).

### ⚔️ 2. Outbreak, Leadership Centers & British Suppression

```
  Barrackpore (29 March: Mangal Pandey) ──► Meerut (10 May: Mutiny Breaks Out) ──► Delhi (11 May: March to Red Fort)
```

| Storm Center | Rebel Leaders | British Commander Who Suppressed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Delhi** | **General Bakht Khan** (Actual commander) & Emperor **Bahadur Shah II (Zafar)**. | **John Nicholson** (killed), **Hudson** (shot princes at Delhi Gate), Sir Archdale Wilson. |
| **Kanpur** | **Nana Saheb (Dhondu Pant)**, **Tantia Tope (Ramachandra Pandurang)**, Azimullah Khan. | **Sir Colin Campbell** (Nana Saheb fled to Nepal; Tantia Tope captured & hanged in 1859). |
| **Lucknow** | **Begum Hazrat Mahal** and her minor son **Birjis Qadir**. | **Sir Colin Campbell** and Sir James Outram. |
| **Jhansi** | **Rani Lakshmibai (Manikarnika)** (Joined forces with Tantia Tope at Gwalior). | **Sir Hugh Rose** (Remarked: *"Here lay the woman who was the only man among the rebels"*). |
| **Jagdishpur (Bihar)** | **Kunwar Singh** (80-year-old zamindar) and brother Amar Singh. | **William Taylor** and Vincent Eyre. |
| **Bareilly (Rohilkhand)** | **Khan Bahadur Khan**. | Sir Colin Campbell. |
| **Faizabad** | **Maulvi Ahmadullah** (Fierce religious orator; killed in combat). | Sir Colin Campbell. |
| **Allahabad & Banaras** | **Maulvi Liaquat Ali**. | **Colonel James Neill** (Infamous for brutal atrocities). |

### 📜 3. Transformative Constitutional Overhaul: Government of India Act, 1858

Passed on **August 2, 1858** ("An Act for the Better Government of India"):
1. **End of Company Rule:** Liquidated the East India Company; transferred all governing powers directly to the **British Crown (Queen Victoria)**.
2. **Abolition of Dual Government:** Abolished the **Court of Directors** and **Board of Control** created by Pitt's India Act (1784).
3. **Secretary of State for India:** Created a new Cabinet-rank minister in the British Government—the **Secretary of State for India**—vested with absolute authority, assisted by a 15-member advisory **Council of India**.
4. **Viceroy of India:** Governor-General redesignated as **Viceroy** when acting as the Crown's diplomatic representative to princely states. **Lord Canning** became the **first Viceroy of India**.
5. **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (Nov 1, 1858, Allahabad Darbar):**
   - Read out by Lord Canning; promised to respect the dignity and rights of native princes.
   - Declared an end to further territorial annexation; guaranteed complete freedom of religion; granted general amnesty to all rebels except those convicted of murder of British subjects.
6. **Peel Commission (1859) Military Restructuring:**
   - Enforced policy of **"Divide and Rule"** in the armed forces.
   - Categorized Indian communities into **"Martial"** (Punjabi Sikhs, Gorkhas, Pathans who remained loyal) and **"Non-Martial"** (Awadh, Bihar Brahmins/Rajputs who rebelled).
   - Fixed European-to-Indian troop ratios at **1 : 2 in Bengal Army** and **1 : 3 in Madras and Bombay Armies**. Artillery placed exclusively in British hands.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 34:**
> 1. **First Viceroy:** **Lord Canning** was the last Governor-General of the Company and the **first Viceroy under the Crown**.
> 2. **Peel Commission:** Reorganized the army along **caste and regional lines** to prevent cross-regimental solidarity, creating communal segregation in army barracks.

## 35. Colonialism and the City: Urban Transformation, Railways & Infrastructure

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Colonial urbanisation reversed traditional Indian urban geography by draining interior manufacturing centers and establishing heavily fortified Presidency port capitals designed for maritime commodity extraction and military garrison control.

### 🏙️ 1. De-Urbanisation vs. Colonial Port Urbanisation

• **De-Urbanisation of Historic Centers:** Traditional royal and manufacturing urban capitals (**Murshidabad, Dacca, Agra, Thanjavur**) declined precipitously due to the collapse of native princely courts and destruction of handicraft guilds.
• **Rise of the Presidency Cities:** Growth of **Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras** as multi-functional colonial metropolises:
  - *Civil Lines & Cantonments:* Segregated, sanitary, low-density European quarters sharply demarcated from crowded, unserviced native quarters ("White Town" vs. "Black Town").
  - *Hill Stations:* Established as sanitariums and strategic military cantonments (Shimla, Darjeeling, Ooty); **Shimla** became the official summer capital of British India in 1864 under Sir John Lawrence.

### 🚂 2. Dalhousie's Infrastructural Trinity

*Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) laid the technical foundations of colonial infrastructure to tighten military surveillance and expedite raw material transport:*

| Infrastructural Line | Launch Year & Route | Imperial & Commercial Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Passenger Railway** | **16 April 1853**<br>(Bombay to Thane, 34 km) | Three locomotives (*Sultan, Sindh, Sahib*). Built under the "Guarantee System" (guaranteed 5% investor yield). Transported raw cotton to ports and moved troops to frontiers. |
| **Electric Telegraph** | **1853**<br>(Calcutta to Agra, 800 miles) | Pioneered by Dr. William O'Shaughnessy. Revolutionized imperial military intelligence; played a vital role in suppressing the 1857 Revolt (*"The electric telegraph saved India"*). |
| **Modern Postal System** | **Post Office Act, 1854** | Replaced courier post with uniform postage rate of **half-anna (1/2 anna)** for letters throughout India, irrespective of distance. Introduced first paper postage stamps. |
| **Public Works Department** | **1854** | Separated civil engineering and road/canal construction from military control; created dedicated civilian PWD. |

• **New Delhi Capital Construction (1911–1931):** Capital shifted from Calcutta to Delhi in **1911** by Lord Hardinge II. Master-planned by British architects **Sir Edwin Lutyens** and **Sir Herbert Baker** in monumental classical-imperial style (Viceroy's House / Rashtrapati Bhavan, Kingsway / Kartavya Path, Secretariat, and Council House / Parliament).

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 35:**
> 1. **First Railway Line in Eastern India:** Ran from **Howrah to Hooghly (1854)**, shortly after the Bombay-Thane line.
> 2. **Postage Uniformity:** The Post Office Act of 1854 established a uniform rate of **half-anna for anywhere in India**, ending distance-graduated rates.

## 36. Civilising the 'Native', Educating the Nation: Western Education & The Colonial Press

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Colonial education aimed to produce English-educated native clerks and administrators loyal to British tastes, but inadvertently provided Indian intellectuals with the modern liberal tools of constitutionalism, democracy, and anti-colonial resistance.

### 🎓 1. The Orientalist-Anglicist Debate & Macaulay's Minute

• **Orientalist Phase (Pre-1830s):** Warren Hastings founded **Calcutta Madrassa (1781)** for Islamic law; Jonathan Duncan founded **Sanskrit College at Varanasi (1791)**; William Jones founded **Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784)**. Aimed to govern Indians according to their own customary laws.
• **The Anglicist-Orientalist Controversy (1830s):** Heated conflict within the Committee of Public Instruction over spending the ₹1 Lakh annual education grant mandated by the Charter Act of 1813.
• **Lord Macaulay's Minute on Indian Education (Feb 2, 1835):**
  - Fiercely championed English medium and European science.
  - Famously declared: *"A single shelf of a good European library was worth the whole native literature of India and Arabia."*
  - **Downwards Filtration Theory:** Educate a select aristocratic native upper class; modern ideas and European knowledge would naturally filter down to the masses.
  - **Goal:** To create a class of persons *"Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste, in opinions, in morals, and in intellect"*.
  - Enacted via **English Education Act of 1835** by Lord William Bentinck.

### 📜 2. Key Education Commissions in British India

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    CHRONOLOGY OF COLONIAL EDUCATION POLICY                  │
│                                                                             │
│  • Wood's Despatch (1854)       : "Magna Carta of English Education"        │
│  • Hunter Commission (1882)     : Focus on Primary & Secondary Education    │
│  • Raleigh Commission (1902)    : Indian Universities Act 1904              │
│  • Saddler Commission (1917)    : 10+2+3 School-Degree Framework            │
│  • Hartog Committee (1929)      : Primary Education Quality & Consolidation │
│  • Wardha Scheme (1937)         : Nai Talim (Zakir Husain Committee)        │
│  • Sargent Plan (1944)          : Post-War Universal Literacy Plan          │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

• **Wood's Despatch (1854):** Issued by Charles Wood (President of Board of Control).
  - Recommended establishing dedicated Departments of Public Instruction in all provinces.
  - Proposed establishing Universities on London model at **Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras (all 3 founded in 1857)**.
  - Advocated vernacular primary schools $\to$ Anglo-vernacular high schools $\to$ affiliated colleges. Grants-in-aid to private schools.
• **Wardha Scheme of Basic Education (*Nai Talim*, 1937):** Formulated at Wardha Conference under Mahatma Gandhi; detailed by **Dr. Zakir Husain Committee**. Emphasized learning through productive manual crafts (spinning, weaving, agriculture) in the child's mother tongue for ages 7–14.

### 📰 3. Development of the Colonial Press & Censorship Acts

• **Pioneers:** **James Augustus Hicky** founded **The Bengal Gazette (1780)** in Calcutta (seized in 1782 for criticizing Hastings).
• **Censorship of Press Act, 1799 (Lord Wellesley):** Imposed pre-censorship during wartime against French threat.
• **Licensing Regulations, 1823 (John Adams):** Starting or using a press required a government license; used to shut down Raja Ram Mohan Roy's Persian weekly **Mirat-ul-Akbar**.
• **Press Act of 1835 (Metcalfe Act):** **Charles Metcalfe** repealed the oppressive 1823 licensing rules; celebrated as the **"Liberator of the Indian Press"**.
• **Vernacular Press Act, 1878 (Lord Lytton) — "The Gagging Act":**
  - Directed specifically against vernacular newspapers spreading nationalist dissent (e.g. *Som Prakas, Amrita Bazar Patrika*).
  - Empowered District Magistrates to force printers/publishers to sign security bonds promising not to print seditious material; magistrate's decision was final with **no appeal to courts**.
  - *Circumvention:* **Amrita Bazar Patrika** (edited by Sisir Kumar Ghosh) turned into a complete English-language newspaper overnight to escape the Act!
  - **Repealed in 1881 by Lord Ripon**.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 36:**
> 1. **Magna Carta of Indian Education:** **Wood's Despatch of 1854**, NOT Macaulay's Minute of 1835.
> 2. **Metcalfe's Title:** **Charles Metcalfe** earned the title *"Liberator of the Indian Press"*, while **Lord Lytton** passed the notorious *"Gagging Act"*.

## 37. Women, Caste and Reform: The 19th-Century Socio-Religious Renaissance

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The 19th-century reform movements sought to purge Indian society of superstitious corruptions, child marriage, caste oppression, and female subjugation by synthesizing rationalist humanism with re-interpreted indigenous scriptural traditions.

### 🌸 1. Pioneer Reformers & The Emancipation of Women

• **Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772–1833) — "Father of Modern Indian Renaissance":**
  - Founded **Atmiya Sabha (1814)** and **Brahmo Sabha (1828)** (renamed **Brahmo Samaj** in 1830).
  - Advocated monotheism; rejected idol worship, polytheism, and priestly intermediaries; translated Vedas and Upanishads into Bengali.
  - Crusade against Sati: Convinced Lord William Bentinck to enact **Regulation XVII of 1829**, declaring Sati illegal and punishable as culpable homicide.
  - Published *Sambad Kaumudi* (Bengali) and *Mirat-ul-Akbar* (Persian).
• **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820–1891):**
  - Principal of Sanskrit College, opened it to non-Brahmin students.
  - Used ancient Vedic texts to prove widows could remarry; petition led to the passage of the landmark **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856 (Act XV of 1856)**.
  - Secretary of **Bethune School (Calcutta, 1849)**, the pioneer institution for women's higher education.
• **Brahmo Samaj Splits:**
  - *1866 Split:* **Adi Brahmo Samaj** (under Debendranath Tagore, conservative Vedic focus) vs. **Brahmo Samaj of India** (under Keshub Chandra Sen, dynamic all-faith inclusive).
  - *1878 Split:* Keshub's followers broke away to found the **Sadharan Brahmo Samaj** (Anand Mohan Bose, Shibnath Shastri) when Keshub married his minor 13-year-old daughter to the Prince of Cooch Behar violating his own Native Marriage Act.
• **Other Women Reform Milestones:**
  - **Pandita Ramabai:** Founded **Arya Mahila Samaj (1881)** and **Sharada Sadan (1889)** in Bombay to shelter young Hindu widows; wrote *The High-Caste Hindu Woman*.
  - **Savtiri Bai Phule:** Opened India's first school for lower-caste girls in Pune (1848); pioneered widow care and infant adoption center (*Balhatya Pratibandhak Griha*).
  - **Age of Consent Act, 1891:** Raised minimum age of marriage for girls from 10 to **12 years** (vigorously opposed by B.G. Tilak as colonial interference in religion).
  - **Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929 (Sarda Act):** Fixed minimum marriage age at **14 years for girls and 18 years for boys**.

### 🕉️ 2. Socio-Religious Revivalism vs. Reformism

| Movement | Founder & Year | Core Doctrine & Social Program |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arya Samaj** | **Swami Dayanand Saraswati (1875, Bombay)** | • Slogan: *"Go Back to the Vedas"*; book: *Satyarth Prakash*.<br>• Infallibility of original Vedas; rejected caste by birth, idol worship, and child marriage.<br>• **Shuddhi Movement:** Reconverting Hindus who had converted to Islam/Christianity.<br>• *1893 Split:* College Party (DAV Colleges, English curriculum, non-veg allowed: Lala Lajpat Rai) vs Gurukul Party (Gurukul Kangri, Haridwar 1902, Vedic curriculum, strict veg: Swami Shraddhanand). |
| **Prarthana Samaj** | **Atmaram Pandurang (1867, Bombay)** | • Supported by Keshub Chandra Sen, guided by Justice **M.G. Ranade** and R.G. Bhandarkar.<br>• Focused on social action rather than theological dogmas: inter-caste dining, widow remarriage, upliftment of women and depressed classes. |
| **Ramakrishna Mission** | **Swami Vivekananda (1897, Belur Math)** | • Inspired by mystic Ramakrishna Paramahamsa.<br>• Synthesis of Advaita Vedanta with social service (*"Service to Daridra Narayana is service to God"*).<br>• World Parliament of Religions, Chicago (1893); emphasized physical strength, spiritual unity, and practical Vedanta. |
| **Theosophical Society** | **Madame Blavatsky & Col. H.S. Olcott (1875, New York $\to$ Adyar 1882)** | • Popularized by **Annie Besant** (joined 1889).<br>• Promoted Hindu revivalism, karma, and reincarnation; founded Central Hindu College at Varanasi (1898), which became Banaras Hindu University (BHU, 1916 under Malaviya). |

### ✊ 3. Anti-Caste & Subaltern Justice Movements

• **Satyashodhak Samaj (Truth Seekers' Society, 1873, Maharashtra):**
  - Founded by **Jyotirao Govindrao Phule**.
  - Championed the cause of *Shudras* and *Ati-Shudras*; attacked Brahminical priestly monopoly; rejected the intermediary role of priests in marriages.
  - Landmark text: **Gulamgiri (Slavery, 1873)**, dedicated to the American movement to abolish Negro slavery.
• **SNDP Yogam (Sri Narayana Dharma Paripalana Yogam, 1903, Kerala):**
  - Founded by **Sri Narayana Guru** among the oppressed Ezhava community.
  - Consecrated Shiva temple at Aruvippuram (1888) defying Brahmin monopoly.
  - Slogan: *"One Caste, One Religion, One God for Man"*.
• **Self-Respect Movement (1925, Tamil Nadu):**
  - Founded by **E.V. Ramasamy Naicker (Periyar)**.
  - Radical anti-Brahmin, atheist, rationalist crusade; published journal *Kudi Arasu*; solemnized marriages without Brahmin priests (*Self-Respect Marriages*).
• **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's Dalit Liberation Campaign:**
  - Founded **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924)** (Motto: *"Educate, Agitate, Organise"*).
  - **Mahad Satyagraha (March 1927):** Asserted right of untouchables to draw water from the public Chawdar tank in Mahad.
  - Burned **Manusmriti** on December 25, 1927 (*Manusmriti Dahan Din*).
  - Founded *Samata Sainik Dal* (1927), Independent Labour Party (1936), and All India Scheduled Castes Federation (1942).

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 37:**
> 1. **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act 1856:** Drafted under **Lord Dalhousie**, but enacted and passed under **Lord Canning**.
> 2. **Gulamgiri Dedication:** Jyotirao Phule dedicated his book *Gulamgiri* to the **abolitionists of the American Civil War**, drawing a direct parallel between American slaves and Indian low castes.

## 38. The Changing World of Visual Arts, Literature & Nationalist Iconography

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Colonial rule transformed Indian art from royal court patronage to Western academic realism, which in turn sparked a nationalist aesthetic counter-revolution that revived indigenous traditions, miniature techniques, and spiritual symbolism as visual weapons of anti-imperial resistance.

### 🎨 1. Evolution of Art Traditions in Colonial India

| Art Movement / School | Key Figures | Distinguishing Characteristics | Nationalist / Political Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Company Painting (Kampani Kalam)** | Indian court painters employed by EIC officials (Patna, Murshidabad, Tanjore). | Hybrid of Indian miniature precision and European watercolour realism, linear perspective, and chiaroscuro (shadows). Documented Indian flora, fauna, trades, and festivals. | Reflected colonial gaze and scientific documentation for imperial governance rather than indigenous aesthetic expression. |
| **Kalighat Paintings** | Rural scroll painters (*patuas*) migrating to Kalighat Temple, Calcutta (mid-19th C). | Bold, sweeping brushstrokes, cheap paper, watercolours. Satirical depictions of Anglicized Bengali babus, corrupt priests, hypocritical babus, and assertive modern women. | Earliest urban popular mass art expressing subaltern social critique and vernacular anti-colonial mockery of pseudo-Western elites. |
| **Western Academic Realism** | **Raja Ravi Varma** (Travancore, Kerala; 1848–1906). | Mastered Western oil painting and European academic realism (anatomy, light, three-dimensional depth) while illustrating Indian mythological epics (Mahabharata, Ramayana). | Established a **Lithographic Printing Press** at Ghatkopar, Bombay (1894), democratizing art; his portrayals of Saraswati, Lakshmi, and Shakuntala created modern pan-Indian visual iconography. |
| **Bengal School of Art (Revivalist)** | **Abanindranath Tagore**, **E.B. Havell**, Nandalal Bose. | Rejected Western oil realism as materialistic; revived Mughal, Rajasthani miniature traditions, and Ajanta frescoes; developed the Japanese "wash" technique (introduced by Okakura Kakuzo). | Anti-colonial cultural nationalism; sought an "authentic Asian/Indian aesthetic"; rejected colonial cultural inferiority complexes. |

### 🇮🇳 2. Nationalist Iconography & Patriotic Literature

• **Bharat Mata Iconography:**
  - **Abanindranath Tagore (1905):** Painted *Bharat Mata* during the Swadeshi Movement as an ascetic, spiritual figure (four arms holding Vedic scriptures, sheaves of paddy, prayer beads, and white cloth), symbolizing *shiksha, anna, diksha, and vastra*.
  - **Evolution:** Later transformed in the 1920s–30s by popular calendar art into an armed warrior goddess riding a lion, draped in the tricolor flag.
• **Patriotic Literature & National Anthem:**
  - **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay:** Serialized novel *Anandamath* (1882) based on the Sanyasi Rebellion; composed **"Vande Mataram"** (originally written in 1875), which became the official hymn of the anti-colonial resistance.
  - **Rabindranath Tagore:** Composed *Jana Gana Mana* (first sung at 1911 Calcutta INC Session; adopted as National Anthem in 1950) and *Amar Shonar Bangla* (composed during 1905 anti-partition struggle; became Bangladesh national anthem).
  - **Subramania Bharati:** Spearheaded Tamil patriotic renaissance through fiery revolutionary poems (*Swadesha Geethangal*).
  - **Muhammad Iqbal:** Authored *Tarana-e-Hindi* (*"Saare Jahan Se Achha"*, 1904) celebrating composite Indian civilization before later drifting towards separate Muslim nationhood.
• **Architectural Shifts: Imperial vs Indigenous:**
  - *Indo-Saracenic Architecture:* Synthesis of Gothic revival with Mughal, Hindu, and Islamic domes, chhatris, and arches (e.g. Gateway of India, Victoria Terminus by F.W. Stevens, Madras High Court).
  - *New Delhi Construction (1911–1931):* Designed by **Edwin Lutyens** and **Herbert Baker**; monumental neo-classical design engineered to project imperial permanence and authoritarian dominance.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 38:**
> 1. **Vande Mataram Singing First Time:** First sung at an Indian National Congress session in **1896 (Calcutta Session)**, presided over by **Rahimtulla M. Sayani**, NOT in 1905.
> 2. **Raja Ravi Varma's Kaiser-i-Hind:** Awarded the Kaiser-i-Hind Gold Medal in 1904 by Lord Curzon for his contributions to fine arts.

## 39. The Making of the National Movement (1870s–1918): Moderates, Extremists & Swadeshi

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Indian National Movement progressed from elite constitutional agitation (Moderates) demanding administrative share to mass mobilization (Extremists) demanding self-rule (*Swaraj*), catalyzed by Lord Curzon's reactionary partition of Bengal and institutionalized via the Swadeshi movement.

### 🏛️ 1. Pre-Congress Political Associations (1866–1884)

```
  East India Association (1866, London) ──► Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870) ──► Indian Association (1876, Calcutta) ──► Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884) ──► Bombay Presidency Association (1885)
```

• **East India Association (London, 1866):** Founded by **Dadabhai Naoroji** to lobby British MPs and public opinion regarding Indian grievances.
• **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870):** Founded by **M.G. Ranade**, G.V. Joshi, and S.H. Chiplunkar; served as a bridge between peasants and government during the 1875 Deccan Riots.
• **Indian Association of Calcutta (1876):** Founded by **Surendranath Banerjee** and **Anand Mohan Bose**; agitated against the reduction of ICS civil service examination age from 21 to 19 (1877) and the Vernacular Press Act (1878); organized the **All India National Conference** (1883 & 1885), the direct precursor to INC.
• **Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884):** Founded by M. Veeraraghavachariar, G. Subramania Iyer, and P. Anandacharlu.
• **Bombay Presidency Association (1885):** Founded by the "Triumvirate" — **Pherozeshah Mehta, K.T. Telang, and Badruddin Tyabji**.

### ⚖️ 2. Moderate Phase (1885–1905) vs. Extremist Phase (1905–1918)

| Feature | Moderate Phase (1885–1905) | Extremist / Militant Nationalist Phase (1905–1918) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Leading Personalities** | W.C. Bonnerjee, Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Dinshaw Wacha, Surendranath Banerjee. | **Lal-Bal-Pal** (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal), Aurobindo Ghosh, Ashwini Kumar Dutta. |
| **Ideological Base** | British liberalism, faith in British sense of justice and fair play; believed British connection was historically beneficial at that stage. | Indigenous civilizational pride; belief that British rule was intrinsically exploitative and incompatible with Indian national survival. |
| **Ultimate Goal** | Colonial self-government / administrative reform within the British Empire; entry into legislative councils and civil services. | **Swaraj (Complete Self-Rule)**: *"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it"* (B.G. Tilak). |
| **Methods of Struggle** | **3Ps:** Prayers, Petitions, and Protests; constitutional delegations; writing memorandums, press articles, and legislative speeches. | **Passive Resistance, Swadeshi & Boycott**, national education, social boycott of foreign goods, mass picketing, non-cooperation. |
| **Social Base** | Narrow, urban educated middle-class elites, lawyers, landlords, and merchants. | Expanded to urban lower-middle classes, students, women, shopkeepers, and vernacular youth. |
| **Monumental Achievement** | Pioneered the **Economic Critique of Colonialism**; secured the Indian Councils Act of 1892; created a unified pan-Indian political platform. | Transformed nationalism into a passionate mass movement; instilled self-reliance (*Atmashakti*); smashed myth of British invincibility. |

### 🔥 3. Partition of Bengal (1905) & The Swadeshi Movement

• **Curzon's Motive:** Announced July 1905; came into effect **October 16, 1905**. Official pretext: administrative convenience (Bengal population: 78 million). **Real political motive:** Divide Bengal along communal lines (Muslim-majority East Bengal & Assam with capital at Dacca; Hindu-majority West Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa with capital at Calcutta) to strangle the nerve-center of Indian nationalism.
• **Day of Mourning (October 16, 1905):** People observed fasts, sang *Vande Mataram*, tied *Rakhi* on each other's wrists as a symbol of unbreakable brotherhood, and took ritual bath in the Hooghly.
• **Core Techniques of Resistance:**
  - *Boycott of Foreign Goods:* Burning of Manchester textiles and Liverpool salt.
  - *Swadeshi Enterprises:* Indigenous textile mills, soap and match factories (P.C. Ray established **Bengal Chemicals & Pharmaceutical Works**; V.O. Chidambaram Pillai launched **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company** at Tuticorin).
  - *National Education:* National Council of Education set up (August 15, 1906); Bengal National College opened with **Aurobindo Ghosh** as its first principal.
  - *Samitis / Corps of Volunteers:* **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti** (founded by Ashwini Kumar Dutta in Barisal) mobilized rural masses through cultural performances and social service.

### ⚡ 4. Political Splits & Colonial Counter-Offensive

• **Foundation of All India Muslim League (1906):** Founded at Dacca under the leadership of **Aga Khan, Nawab Salimullah of Dacca, and Mohsin-ul-Mulk** to champion separate Muslim political interests and counter Congress influence.
• **Surat Split (December 1907):**
  - Session presided over by **Rash Behari Ghosh** (after Moderates blocked Tilak's or Lajpat Rai's candidacy).
  - Rifts over extending Boycott beyond Bengal and to legislative councils erupted into open violence; Moderates expelled Extremists, weakening the national movement for a decade.
• **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909):**
  - Increased legislative council membership (non-official majority in provinces, official majority at Centre).
  - Introduced the pernicious system of **Separate Electorates for Muslims** (legalized communalism; Lord Minto called the *"Father of Communal Electorate"*).
• **Annulment of Partition (1911):** At the **Delhi Durbar of 1911** (attended by King George V and Queen Mary), Lord Hardinge II announced the **annulment of Bengal's partition**, the separation of Bihar and Orissa from Bengal, and the **shifting of India's capital from Calcutta to Delhi**.
• **Home Rule Movement (1916):**
  - *Tilak's Home Rule League (April 1916):* Headquartered at Poona; worked in Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, and Berar. Operated through newspapers *Mahratta* (English) and *Kesari* (Marathi).
  - *Annie Besant's Home Rule League (September 1916):* Headquartered at Adyar (Madras); covered the rest of India (including Bombay city). Newspapers: *New India* and *Commonweal*.
• **Lucknow Pact (1916):**
  - Presided over by **Ambica Charan Mazumdar**.
  - **Reunion of INC:** Extremists readmitted to Congress due to efforts of Annie Besant and Tilak.
  - **Congress-League Concordat:** Congress controversially accepted the Muslim League's demand for **Separate Electorates** in exchange for a joint demand for provincial self-government from the British Raj.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 39:**
> 1. **INC First Session Location:** Originally scheduled at **Poona**, but shifted to **Bombay** (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College) due to a sudden outbreak of **cholera** in Poona. Attended by **72 delegates**; W.C. Bonnerjee was president; A.O. Hume was general secretary.
> 2. **Safety Valve Theory:** Put forward by Lala Lajpat Rai (Extremist) and later R.P. Dutt (Marxist), alleging Hume founded Congress under Viceroy Dufferin's guidance to release brewing anti-British mass rebellion. Modern research (Bipan Chandra) disproves this: early leaders used Hume as a "lightning conductor" to prevent early state repression.

## 40. Nationalism in India: The Gandhian Upsurge & Revolutionary Movements (1915–1934)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Mahatma Gandhi transformed Indian anti-colonialism from an elite legislative dialogue into an unprecedented mass movement through the philosophy of Satyagraha (*truth-force* and *ahimsa*), operating synchronously alongside radical underground revolutionary heroism to dismantle the moral authority of the British Empire.

### 🧘 1. Gandhi's Ideology & Initial Experimental Laboratory (1915–1918)

• **Return to India:** Gandhi arrived in Bombay on **January 9, 1915** (celebrated as *Pravasi Bharatiya Divas*); spent his first year touring India on the advice of his political guru, **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**.
• **Three Early Local Satyagrahas:**

```
  Champaran Satyagraha (1917) ──► Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918) ──► Kheda Satyagraha (1918)
  (First Civil Disobedience)       (First Hunger Strike)            (First Non-Cooperation)
```

1. **Champaran Satyagraha (Bihar, 1917):**
   - Agitated against European indigo planters enforcing the oppressive **Tinkathia System** (farmers compelled to grow indigo on 3/20th of their land).
   - Invited by local peasant leader **Rajkumar Shukla**; joined by Rajendra Prasad, J.B. Kripalani, and Mahadev Desai.
   - Result: Tinkathia abolished; planters agreed to refund 25% of illegally extorted money.
2. **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (Gujarat, March 1918):**
   - Dispute between cotton mill owners and workers over the withdrawal of the **Plague Bonus**. Mill owners offered 20% hike; workers demanded 50%.
   - Gandhi undertook his **first fast unto death** in India; helped negotiate a **35% wage hike**. Supported by Anusuya Sarabhai.
3. **Kheda Satyagraha (Gujarat, June 1918):**
   - Crops failed in Kheda; under the Revenue Code, if yield was less than 25% (*one-fourth*), peasants were entitled to full tax remission, but the colonial state insisted on full collection.
   - Gandhi and **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** organized peasants to withhold revenue payment.
   - Result: Government issued secret instructions that revenue be recovered only from those who could pay.

### 🩸 2. Rowlatt Satyagraha, Jallianwala Bagh & The Non-Cooperation Movement

• **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act (Rowlatt Act, March 1919):** Empowered the colonial police to arrest anyone without warrant and detain suspects for up to **two years without trial** (*"No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal"*).
• **Rowlatt Satyagraha (April 6, 1919):** Gandhi launched an all-India *hartal*, his first nationwide mass political intervention.
• **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (April 13, 1919, Baisakhi Day):**
  - Brigadier-General **Reginald Dyer** ordered troops to block the narrow entrance and fire without warning on an unarmed peaceful gathering at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar.
  - People had gathered to protest the arrest of leaders **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal**.
  - Official Hunter Committee stated 379 dead; Indian National Congress inquiry estimated over 1,000 dead.
  - **Repercussions:** Rabindranath Tagore renounced his **Knighthood**; Gandhi renounced his **Kaiser-i-Hind** gold medal. Michael O'Dwyer (Lt. Governor of Punjab who endorsed the shooting) was assassinated in London in 1940 by **Sardar Udham Singh**.
• **Khilafat & Non-Cooperation Movement (NCM, 1920–1922):**
  - *Khilafat Issue:* Turkish Ottoman Sultan (Caliph of Islam) dismembered by the Treaty of Sevres after WWI. Khilafat Committee formed by **Ali Brothers (Shaukat Ali & Mohammad Ali)**, Maulana Azad, and Hasrat Mohani. Gandhi forged a historic Hindu-Muslim anti-imperialist joint front.
  - *Nagpur Session of INC (December 1920):* Presided over by **C. Vijayaraghavachariar**. Endorsed Non-Cooperation program; restructured Congress into a mass party with a 15-member Working Committee (CWC) and Provincial Congress Committees organized on a linguistic basis (*four-anna annual membership*).
  - *Programs:* Surrender of colonial titles, boycott of government schools, law courts, foreign cloth, and upcoming elections under the GoI Act 1919; promotion of Khadi, hand-spinning (*Charkha*), and national schools (Kashi Vidyapeeth, Jamia Millia Islamia).
  - *Chauri Chaura Incident (February 4, 1922, Gorakhpur, UP):* A crowd set fire to a police station, killing 22 policemen. Gandhi, adhering strictly to non-violence, abruptly withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement through the **Bardoli Resolution (February 12, 1922)**.

### 💣 3. Revolutionary Nationalism: Phase I & Phase II

| Phase & Organization | Key Leaders | Daring Actions & Historic Significance | Ideological Transformation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase I (1900–1915): Early Militant Groups** | • **Anushilan Samiti** (Barindra Ghosh, Pramod Mitter, 1902).<br>• **Jugantar** (Bagha Jatin, 1906).<br>• **Abhinav Bharat** (V.D. Savarkar, 1904).<br>• **Ghadar Party** (Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, 1913, San Francisco). | • Muzaffarpur Bomb Case (1908): Khudiram Bose & Prafulla Chaki targeted Magistrate Kingsford.<br>• Alipore Conspiracy Case (1908): Aurobindo Ghosh defended by C.R. ### 🌊 4. Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934) & Round Table Conferences

```
  Simon Commission (1927) ──► Nehru Report (1928) ──► Lahore Session (1929) ──► Dandi March (1930) ──► Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) ──► Poona Pact (1932)
```

• **Simon Commission (1927):** Statutory commission under Sir John Simon to review GoI Act 1919. Boycotted universally because it was an **all-white body with zero Indian members**. Lala Lajpat Rai succumbed to head injuries from police lathi charges led by J.A. Scott in Lahore.
• **Nehru Report (1928):** First Indian attempt to draft a written constitution (chaired by **Motilal Nehru**). Demanded **Dominion Status**, joint electorates with reserved seats for minorities, fundamental rights (universal adult suffrage, equal rights for women), and linguistic provinces. Rejected by Jinnah, who countered with his **Fourteen Points**.
• **Lahore Session (December 1929):** Presided over by **Jawaharlal Nehru**. Passed the historic **Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence)** resolution. Decided to launch Civil Disobedience and celebrate **January 26, 1930** as the first **Independence Day** (origin of Republic Day date).
• **Salt Satyagraha & Dandi March (March 12 – April 6, 1930):**
  - Gandhi set out from Sabarmati Ashram with **78 chosen followers** to walk 240 miles to the coastal village of **Dandi**.
  - On **April 6, 1930**, Gandhi broke the Salt Law by picking up a lump of natural salt, igniting a countrywide wave of illegal salt manufacture and refusal to pay land revenue/chaukidari tax.
  - *Other Theatres:* **C. Rajagopalachari** led salt march from Trichinopoly to Vedaranyam (Tamil Nadu); **K.   - *North-West Frontier Province (NWFP):* **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (Frontier Gandhi)** led the non-violent volunteer brigade **Khudai Khidmatgars (Red Shirts)**. Royal Garhwal Rifles under Chandra Singh Garhwali refused to fire on unarmed Pathan demonstrators in Peshawar.
• **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact, March 5, 1931):**
  - Signed between Viceroy Lord Irwin and Gandhi. Congress agreed to suspend Civil Disobedience and attend the **Second Round Table Conference** in London.
  - Government agreed to release non-violent political prisoners, return confiscated land, and permit peaceful picketing of liquor and foreign cloth shops.
• **Karachi Session of INC (March 1931):** Presided over by **Sardar Patel**. Endorsed the Gandhi-Irwin Pact; passed two historic resolutions drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru: **Resolution on Fundamental Rights** and **National Economic Programme** (state control of key industries and railways).
• **Communal Award (August 1932) & Poona Pact (September 1932):**
  - British Prime Minister **Ramsay MacDonald** announced the Communal Award, granting **separate electorates to Depressed Classes (Dalits)**.
  - Gandhi went on a fast unto death in Yerwada Jail (Poona), arguing separate electorates would permanently sever Dalits from Hindu society.
  - **Poona Pact (September 24, 1932):** Signed between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya (on behalf of caste Hindus). Abandoned separate electorates for Dalits in exchange for **increasing reserved seats in provincial legislatures from 71 to 147** under joint electorates.

## 41. Rebels, Left Movements, Peasant & Working-Class Mobilization

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Indian freedom struggle was not a monolithic bourgeois campaign; radical peasant sabhas, trade unions, socialist cadres, and tribal rebellions continually pushed the Indian National Congress leftward, institutionalizing agrarian reform and workers' rights as non-negotiable national objectives.

### 🔨 2. Rise of the Left: Communists, Socialists & Trade Unions

• **Communist Movement:**
  - **Tashkent Formation (1920):** Formed in Tashkent by **M.N. Roy**, Abani Mukherji, and others following the Second Congress of the Comintern.
  - **Kanpur Formation (December 1925):** Formal establishment of the **Communist Party of India (CPI)** on Indian soil at the Kanpur Conference chaired by Singaravelu Chettiar; Satyabhakta was the organizer.
  - **British Repression (Conspiracy Trials):**
    - *Peshawar Conspiracy Cases (1922–1927)*.
    - *Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case (1924):* Tried M.N. Roy, Muzaffar Ahmad, Shaukat Usmani, and S.A. Dange.
    - *Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929–1933):* 31 labour leaders (including three British communists: Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley, Lester Hutchinson) arrested and tried for organizing railway strikes; brought worldwide attention to the Indian socialist movement.
• **Congress Socialist Party (CSP, 1934):**
  - Formed within the Indian National Congress in 1934 at Patna/Bombay by young radical nationalists disillusioned with Gandhi's suspension of Civil Disobedience.
  - Key Founders: **Jayaprakash Narayan (General Secretary)**, **Acharya Narendra Deva (President)**, Ram Manohar Lohia, Achyut Patwardhan, and Minoo Masani.
  - Objective: Transform the Congress into a genuine anti-imperialist socialist vehicle without splitting the nationalist front.
• **Trade Union Movement:**
  - **All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC, 1920):** Founded at Bombay in 1920. First President: **Lala Lajpat Rai**; First General Secretary: **Dewan Chaman Lall**.
  - *AITUC 1929 Split (Nagpur Session):* Presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru; split between moderate constitutionalists (N.M. Joshi formed National Trade Union Federation) and radical communist elements.

### 🏹 3. Major 20th-Century Tribal Resistance

• **Rampa Rebellion / Manyam Rebellion (1922–1924, Godavari Agency, Andhra Pradesh):**
  - Led by **Alluri Sitarama Raju** (a charismatic non-tribal ascetic revered as an avatar with miraculous powers).
  - Mobilized Koya and Konda Dora tribes against the draconian **Madras Forest Act of 1882**, which banned traditional *podu* (shifting cultivation) and compelled tribals into forced unpaid labour (*muta system*) for road building.
  - Masterminded daring guerrilla raids on colonial police stations (Chintapalli, Krishnadevipeta) to seize firearms; Raju was captured and martyred in May 1924.

## 42. Constitutional Deadlocks, War Crisis & Towards Independence (1935–1947)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The final decade of colonial rule was defined by the structural failure of British constitutional concessions (GoI Act 1935, Cripps Mission), the unyielding mass explosion of the Quit India movement, and the aggressive communal polarization engineered by the Muslim League, ending inexorably in a bloody Partition and Independence.

### 🏛️ 1. Government of India Act 1935 & 1937 Provincial Elections

• **Salient Provisions of GoI Act 1935:**
  - *All-India Federation:* Comprising British Indian provinces and Princely States (never came into operation because Princely States refused to join).
  - *Provincial Autonomy:* Abolished Dyarchy in provinces; introduced responsible parliamentary government in 11 provinces.
  - *Dyarchy at the Centre:* Reserved subjects (Defence, External Affairs, Ecclesiastical affairs, Tribal areas) administered solely by Governor-General; Transferred subjects administered with council of ministers.
  - *Bicameralism:* Introduced in 6 out of 11 provinces (Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces).
  - *Establishment of Institutions:* Federal Court (1937, predecessor to Supreme Court), Reserve Bank of India (RBI Act 1934, commenced operations 1935), and Federal Public Service Commission.
• **1937 Provincial Elections:**
  - Congress swept elections, forming absolute majorities in 5 provinces (Madras, United Provinces, Central Provinces, Bihar, Orissa) and coalition ministries in Bombay, Assam, and NWFP.
  - Muslim League won less than 25% of seats reserved for Muslims, exposing its weak popular base at that time.
• **Resignation of Congress Ministries (October–November 1939):**
  - Viceroy Lord Linlithgow unilaterally declared India at war with Nazi Germany in WWII without consulting Indian political leaders or provincial legislatures.
  - Congress ministries resigned in protest across all provinces.
  - Jinnah and the Muslim League celebrated Congress resignations on **December 22, 1939** as the **"Day of Deliverance"** (*Yaum-e-Tashakkur*).
• **Lahore Resolution / "Pakistan Resolution" (March 1940):** Muslim League session at Lahore formally adopted the resolution demanding geographically contiguous Muslim-majority areas in North-Western and Eastern zones be grouped to constitute *"Independent States in which the constituent units shall be autonomous and sovereign"*.

### ⏳ 2. Wartime Constitutional Offers & Individual Satyagraha

• **August Offer (August 1940):** Announced by Viceroy Linlithgow. Offered Dominion status as the objective, expansion of Viceroy's Executive Council, and a post-war constituent assembly. **Fatal Flaw:** Declared that no future constitution would be adopted without the consent of minority communities (effectively giving the Muslim League a veto over Indian constitutional progress). Rejected by Congress and League.
• **Individual Satyagraha (October 1940):**
  - Launched by Gandhi to assert the democratic right to freedom of speech against imperialist war mobilization without provoking mass disorder during wartime.
  - **First Satyagrahi:** **Acharya Vinoba Bhave** (Pavnar Ashram, Maharashtra).
  - **Second Satyagrahi:** **Jawaharlal Nehru**.
  - **Third Satyagrahi:** **Brahma Datt**.
• **Cripps Mission (March 1942):**
  - Headed by Sir Stafford Cripps to secure Indian cooperation in WWII against advancing Japanese forces.
  - Offered Dominion Status after the war, a Constituent Assembly elected by provincial legislatures, and **the right of any province to refuse the new constitution and form a separate union with Britain**.
  - Gandhi termed the offer a *"Post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"*.

### 🔥 3. Quit India Movement (August 1942)

```
  AICC Gowalia Tank Meeting (Aug 8, 1942) ──► Operation Zero Hour (Aug 9) ──► Underground Radio & Prati Sarkars ──► Mass Repression
```

• **Launch & Slogan:** August 8, 1942 at Gowalia Tank Maidan (August Kranti Maidan), Bombay. Gandhi delivered his historic call: **"Do or Die"** (*Karo ya Maro*).
• **Operation Zero Hour (August 9, 1942):** British colonial police arrested all top Congress leaders (Gandhi detained at Aga Khan Palace, Pune; CWC members jailed at Ahmednagar Fort).
• **Leaderless Mass Explosion:**
  - Students, peasants, and workers paralyzed transport and communications, uprooted railway lines, and hoisted tricolor on government buildings.
  - **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted the Indian National Flag at Gowalia Tank on August 9.
• **Underground Networks:**
  - **Usha Mehta** operated the secret, clandestine **Congress Radio** transmitter from Bombay, broadcasting uncensored nationalist news until arrested in November 1942.
  - Active underground leaders: Ram Manohar Lohia, Jayaprakash Narayan (escaped from Hazaribagh Jail), Achyut Patwardhan, Sucheta Kripalani.
• **Parallel Governments (*Prati Sarkars*):**
  1. *Ballia (UP, August 1942):* Established under **Chittu Pandey**; released all arrested Congressmen.
  2. *Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal, Dec 1942 – Aug 1944):* **Tamluk Jatiya Sarkar** set up armed volunteer corps *Vidyut Vahini* and famine relief. Matangini Hazra (73-year-old peasant woman) was shot dead holding the tricolor.
  3. *Satara (Maharashtra, 1943–1945):* Longest-lasting parallel government under **Nana Patil** and **Y.B. Chavan**; organized *Toofan Sena*, people's courts (*Nyayadan Mandals*), and banned usury.

### ⚔️ 4. INA, RIN Mutiny & The March to Independence (1943–1947)

• **Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj):**
  - *Origin:* First conceived by **Mohan Singh** (British Indian Army captain captured by Japanese in Malaya) with support of Japanese Major Fujiwara and Rash Behari Bose (1942).
  - *Netaji's Leadership:* **Subhas Chandra Bose** escaped from house arrest in Calcutta (1941) via Afghanistan and Germany, arrived in Singapore via submarine (July 1943). Reorganized INA; established the **Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind Sarkar)** on **October 21, 1943** in Singapore.
  - *Regiments & Slogans:* Brigades named Gandhi, Nehru, Azad, and the women's unit **Rani of Jhansi Regiment** (commanded by **Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan/Sahgal**). Slogans: *"Jai Hind"*, *"Dilli Chalo"*, and *"Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom"*.
  - *Red Fort Trials (November 1945):* Joint court-martial of three INA officers—**Prem Kumar Sehgal (Hindu), Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (Sikh), and Shah Nawaz Khan (Muslim)**—at Red Fort, Delhi. Defended by Congress legal team (**Bhulabhai Desai, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Asaf Ali, and Jawaharlal Nehru**). Triggered pan-Indian mass unrest across communities; sentences were remitted by Commander-in-Chief Claude Auchinleck.
• **Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny (February 18–23, 1946):**
  - Ratings (sailors) of **HMIS Talwar** in Bombay struck work protesting racial discrimination, unpalatable food, and abuse by British officers. Led by B.C. Dutt (who scrawled *"Quit India"* on HMIS Talwar).
  - Spread to 78 ships, 20 shore establishments, and 20,000 ratings in Karachi, Calcutta, and Madras. Hoisted Congress, Muslim League, and Communist flags jointly on ship masts.
  - Ended after surrender brokered on February 23, 1946 by **Sardar Patel and M.A. Jinnah**.
• **Cabinet Mission (March–June 1946):**
  - Members: Lord Pethick-Lawrence (Secretary of State), Sir Stafford Cripps, and A.V. Alexander.
  - **Key Proposal:** Rejected Pakistan; proposed a loose three-tier federation:
    - Central Government limited to Foreign Affairs, Defence, and Communications.
    - Provinces grouped into three sections: **Section A** (Hindu-majority: Madras, Bombay, UP, CP, Bihar, Orissa), **Section B** (Muslim-majority NW: Punjab, NWFP, Sindh), **Section C** (Muslim-majority East: Bengal, Assam).
    - Constituent Assembly to be elected by provincial assemblies.
• **Direct Action Day & Partition Catastrophe (1946–1947):**
  - Following disputes over compulsory provincial grouping, Jinnah called for **"Direct Action Day" (August 16, 1946)**, triggering horrific communal butchery (*Great Calcutta Killings*), which spread to Noakhali, Bihar, and Punjab.
  - **Interim Government (September 2, 1946):** Jawaharlal Nehru sworn in as Vice-President of Viceroy's Executive Council; Muslim League joined in October (Liaquat Ali Khan became Finance Minister, deliberately obstructing government functioning).
  - **Mountbatten Plan (3rd June Plan 1947):** Viceroy Lord Mountbatten announced the partition of British India into India and Pakistan, partition of Bengal and Punjab, and immediate transfer of power on **August 15, 1947**.
  - **Radcliffe Boundary Commission:** Sir Cyril Radcliffe drew boundary lines dividing Punjab and Bengal.
  - **Indian Independence Act, 1947:** Passed by British Parliament on **July 18, 1947**; abolished the office of Secretary of State and Governor-General's veto power; established two independent Dominions: India and Pakistan.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 42:**
> 1. **Quit India Resolution Date & Venue:** Passed by AICC on **August 8, 1942** at **Gowalia Tank, Bombay**, under the presidency of **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**, NOT Jawaharlal Nehru.
> 2. **Cabinet Mission & Pakistan Demand:** Cabinet Mission explicitly **REJECTED** the demand for a sovereign Pakistan, proposing instead a united Indian federation with provincial grouping.

## 43. India After Independence (1947 Onward): Integration, Constitution & Nation Building

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Post-colonial Indian state-building required simultaneously solving three existential crises: integrating 565 sovereign princely states into a unified republic, framing a democratic secular constitution amidst partition trauma, and accommodating diverse ethno-linguistic aspirations without balkanizing the nation.

### 🗺️ 1. Integration of Princely States (1947–1950)

• **The Crisis:** Under the Indian Independence Act 1947, British paramountcy lapsed (*Crown relations ceased*). All 565 princely states were given the option to join India, join Pakistan, or remain independent, threatening the balkanization of India.
• **The Architects:** **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (Minister of States / Home Minister) and **V.P. Menon** (Secretary, Ministry of States).
• **The Strategy:**
  - Appealed to patriotism and geopolitical contiguity.
  - Offered the **Instrument of Accession (IoA)** (ceding only Defence, Foreign Affairs, and Communications to the Union) and guaranteed hereditary titles with a tax-free **Privy Purse** (abolished later by the 26th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971).
• **Three Recalcitrant States:**

| State | Ruler & Demographics | Challenge / Crisis | Resolution Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Junagadh** | Muslim Nawab (Muhammad Mahabat Khan III); predominantly Hindu population. | Nawab acceded to Pakistan in August 1947 despite no geographical contiguity. Mass popular uprising broke out (*Aarzi Hukumat* formed by Samaldas Gandhi). | Nawab fled to Karachi. Indian administration took over; conducted a **Plebiscite in February 1948** (99.9% voted overwhelmingly to join the Indian Union). |
| **Hyderabad** | Muslim Nizam (Mir Osman Ali Khan); 85% Hindu population; landlocked within India. | Nizam signed a one-year **Standstill Agreement** (Nov 1947) while secretly expanding arms and patronizing the fanatical paramilitary militia **Razakars** (led by Kasim Razvi), who unleashed terror on the population and communist-led peasant rebels. | On **September 13, 1948**, India launched **Operation Polo** (police action). Major General J.N. Chaudhuri entered Hyderabad; Nizam surrendered on September 17, 1948; acceded to India. |
| **Jammu & Kashmir** | Hindu Maharaja (Hari Singh); Muslim-majority population. | Maharaja sought independent neutrality; signed Standstill Agreement with Pakistan (India deferred signing). In October 1947, Pakistan engineered an armed tribal invasion (*Operation Gulmarg* led by Pathan tribesmen and Pakistani army regulars). | Maharaja signed the **Instrument of Accession on October 26, 1947**. Indian airborne troops landed in Srinagar on October 27, repelling invaders. Conflict halted by UN-brokered ceasefire on January 1, 1949 (creating the Line of Control). Led to Article 370. |

### 📜 2. Framing of the Constitution & Key Institutional Pillars

```
  Constituent Assembly (Dec 1946) ──► Objectives Resolution (Dec 1946) ──► Drafting Committee (Aug 1947) ──► Adoption (Nov 26, 1949) ──► Enforcement (Jan 26, 1950)
```

• **Constituent Assembly Timeline:**
  - First Meeting: **December 9, 1946** (Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha temporary chairman; **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** elected permanent President on Dec 11; **H.C. Mookerjee** and **V.T. Krishnamachari** elected Vice-Presidents).
  - **Objectives Resolution:** Moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru on December 13, 1946** (unanimously adopted on January 22, 1947); became the preamble of the Constitution.
  - **Drafting Committee:** Appointed on August 29, 1947, chaired by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** ("Father of the Indian Constitution"). Sir B.N. Rau served as Constitutional Advisor.
  - Took **2 years, 11 months, and 18 days** to complete; adopted on **November 26, 1949** (*Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas*); fully enforced on **January 26, 1950**.
• **Major Constitutional Committees & Chairpersons:**
  - *Drafting Committee:* Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.
  - *Union Powers Committee & Union Constitution Committee:* Jawaharlal Nehru.
  - *Provincial Constitution Committee & Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights and Minorities:* Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.
  - *Rules of Procedure Committee & Steering Committee:* Dr. Rajendra Prasad.

### 🌐 3. Linguistic Reorganisation of States

• **Dhar Commission (June 1948):** Chaired by S.K. Dhar. Recommended reorganization of states on **administrative convenience rather than linguistic basis**.
• **JVP Committee (December 1948):** Comprising **Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, and Pattabhi Sitaramayya**. Formally rejected language as the primary basis for state reorganization, citing post-partition national unity.
• **Catalyst — Martyrdom of Potti Sreeramulu (1952):** Veteran Gandhian Potti Sreeramulu undertook a fast unto death demanding a separate Telugu state; died on the 58th day (December 15, 1952). Violent unrest forced the Union government to carve out **Andhra State** (first linguistic state) from Madras State on **October 1, 1953** (capital at Kurnool).
• **Fazl Ali Commission / States Reorganisation Commission (SRC, 1953–1955):**
  - Members: **Justice Fazl Ali (Chairman)**, **K.M. Panikkar**, and **H.N. Kunzru**.
  - Broadly accepted language as the basis of reorganization, but firmly rejected the doctrine of *"One Language, One State"*.
  - Led to the enactment of the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** and the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956**, abolishing the old Part A, B, C, D states classification and creating **14 States and 6 Union Territories**.

### 🏗️ 4. Post-Independence Foreign Policy, Planning & Tribal Policy

• **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) & Panchsheel:**
  - *Panchsheel (Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence):* Signed between India (Nehru) and China (Premier Zhou Enlai) on **April 29, 1954** (mutual respect for territorial integrity, non-aggression, non-interference, equality, and peaceful coexistence).
  - *Bandung Conference (1955) & Belgrade Summit (1961):* Founded NAM along with President Tito (Yugoslavia), Nasser (Egypt), Sukarno (Indonesia), and Nkrumah (Ghana) to remain neutral during Cold War superpower rivalries.
• **Planned Industrialisation:**
  - Planning Commission established in March 1950 (extra-constitutional, advisory body; Nehru first chairman).
  - *First Five-Year Plan (1951–1956):* Based on the **Harrod-Domar Model**; focused on agriculture, irrigation (Bhakra Nangal, Damodar Valley dams), and price stability.
  - *Second Five-Year Plan (1956–1961):* Based on the **Nehru-Mahalanobis Model**; prioritized rapid industrialization, heavy capital goods industries, and public sector dominance (Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela steel plants).
• **Nehru's Tribal Panchsheel (1952):** Guided by anthropologist **Verrier Elwin**. Dictated that tribals should develop along the lines of their own genius; tribal rights in land and forests should be respected; administration should be trained from among tribals without over-administering tribal areas.

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 43:**
> 1. **First Linguistic State:** **Andhra State (1953)** was created from Madras State *before* the States Reorganisation Commission submitted its report in 1955. It merged with Telangana to form Andhra Pradesh in 1956.
> 2. **Privy Purses Abolition:** Abolished by Indira Gandhi via the **26th Constitutional Amendment Act of 1971**, NOT immediately after 1947 or in the 1950 Constitution.

## 44. History, Sport and Clothing: Decolonization of Culture & Sartorial Politics

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Colonial hegemony was exercised not only through guns and revenue codes, but also through cultural institutions—sports and clothing—which Indian society simultaneously appropriated, challenged, and weaponized to forge a decolonized national identity.

### 🏏 1. Sport & Social Hierarchy: The History of Indian Cricket

• **Colonial Origin & Racial Exclusivity:**
  - Cricket was introduced into India by British military officers and civil servants as a tool of the "Civilising Mission" to teach Victorian virtues of discipline, fair play, and imperial order.
  - First Indian club: **Oriental Cricket Club (Bombay, 1848)**, founded by the affluent Parsi merchant community.
• **Evolution of Tournaments (Communal / Gymkhana System):**
  1. *Bombay Triangular:* Parsis, Europeans, and Hindus (after Hindu Gymkhana was founded in 1890).
  2. *Bombay Quadrangular (1912):* Muslims added following the foundation of Islam Gymkhana.
  3. *Bombay Pentangular (1937):* "The Rest" added (including Indian Christians and Anglo-Indians).
• **Palwankar Baloo — Subaltern Anti-Caste Pioneer:**
  - **Palwankar Baloo** (a Dalit from the Chamar caste) was a left-arm spin bowler whose extraordinary talent forced the upper-caste Hindu Gymkhana to select him in the late 1890s.
  - Upper-caste players initially refused to dine with him at tea intervals (he was given tea in disposable clay cups outside the pavilion).
  - His brilliant performances led to his captaincy brother, **Palwankar Vithal**, being named captain of the Hindu Gymkhana in 1923, striking an early cultural blow against untouchability decades before mass political mobilization.
• **Nationalist Critique of Pentangular:**
  - In December 1940, **Mahatma Gandhi strongly condemned the Pentangular tournament**, arguing that communal team selections fostered sectarian divisions. The tournament was officially abolished in 1946 and replaced by the secular inter-provincial **Ranji Trophy** (inaugurated in 1934–35, named after K.S. Ranjitsinhji).

### 🧵 2. Clothing & Swadeshi Sartorial Politics

| Cultural Aspect | Colonial Imposition / Policy | Indigenous & Nationalist Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sumptuary Rules & Turban / Shoe Disputes** | British colonial officials insisted Indians remove their shoes (*Shoe Respect Rule*) before entering courts or British offices, while Europeans kept their shoes on. Indians were permitted to keep their turbans on. | Indian elites fiercely resisted. In 1862, Manockjee Cowasjee Entee (Parsi assessor at Surat) refused to take off his shoes, prompting the Bombay government to formalize rigid shoe etiquette regulations. |
| **Swadeshi & Boycott of Manchester Textiles** | Industrial revolution transformed India from the world's greatest textile exporter into a raw-cotton supplier and captive market for cheap Lancashire and Manchester cloth. | **Burning of foreign cloth** became a core public spectacle in the 1905 Swadeshi movement and 1920–22 Non-Cooperation Movement. Wearing indigenous handspun cloth was defined as an ethical and patriotic duty. |
| **Mahatma Gandhi's Sartorial Revolution** | British-trained lawyers and colonial elites wore three-piece European suits, high stiff collars, and ties, visually alienating themselves from rural India. | Gandhi evolved his attire: European suit (London) $\to$ Kathiawadi turban & dhoti (South Africa) $\to$ **Loincloth / Khadi Dhoti (Madurai, September 1921)**. The coarse handspun Khadi and *Charkha* (spinning wheel) became sacred emblems of rural self-reliance (*Swavalamban*), class solidarity, and non-violent economic resistance. |
| **National Cap (*Gandhi Topi*)** | British police banned the white Khadi cap during Civil Disobedience; wearers faced dismissals and arrests. | Became the universal uniform of freedom fighters across India, unifying disparate regional headgear into a single egalitarian national identity. |

> 2. **Ranji Trophy Inception:** The Ranji Trophy was founded in **1934**, well before independence, following meetings of the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI, established 1928).

## 45. Peasants, Farmers & Agrarian Transformation: Famines to Reforms

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> British colonial land-revenue policies commercialized Indian agriculture without technological capitalization, binding peasants to global commodity price swings and usurious moneylenders, generating structural rural indebtedness and cataclysmic famines.

### 🌾 1. Forced Commercialisation & The Triangle of Rural Distress

• **Structural Shift:** Pre-colonial Indian agriculture was oriented towards food crops for domestic and village subsistence. The colonial state forced monetization (cash revenue demands) and promoted cash crops for imperial industry:
  - *Opium:* Forced cultivation in Bihar and Malwa; exported to Qing China by the EIC to finance British tea imports, leading to the Anglo-Chinese Opium Wars (1839–1860).
  - *Indigo:* Cultivated under oppressive *Nij* and *Ryoti* systems; indigo planters forced advances (*dadon*) on ryots, precipitating the **Indigo Revolt (1859–60)** in Bengal.
  - *Cotton:* American Civil War (1861–1865) blocked Southern American cotton supply to Lancashire mills; British turned to the Deccan. Raw cotton prices skyrocketed, creating a temporary boom. When the war ended in 1865, demand crashed abruptly, trapping Deccan ryots in heavy debt to *Marwari* and *Gujarati* sahukars, directly triggering the **Deccan Riots of 1875**.
• **Agrarian Usury & Indebtedness:**
  - High fixed cash land revenue payable on inflexible dates (*Sunset Law*).
  - Peasant had to borrow from moneylenders at exorbitant interest rates (25% to 50%).
  - Introduction of British procedural contract law (*Civil Procedure Code 1859*) enabled moneylenders to acquire court decrees, attach peasants' land, bullocks, and standing crops.

### 💀 2. Colonial Famines & The Evolution of Famine Codes

```
  Orissa Famine (1866) ──► Great Famine (1876-78) ──► Strachey Commission (1880) ──► Bengal Famine (1943)
```

| Famine Episode | Viceroy / Period | Human Loss & Root Causes | Administrative Inquest & Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Orissa Famine (1866)** | Sir John Lawrence | Over 10 lakh deaths (one-third of Orissa's population). Drought compounded by administrative negligence and official adherence to free-market non-intervention. | **Campbell Commission (1866):** Criticized laissez-faire dogma; recommended state responsibility for famine relief and irrigation development. |
| **Great Famine of 1876–1878** | **Lord Lytton** | 50 to 80 lakh deaths across Madras, Mysore, Hyderabad, and Bombay. While millions starved, Lytton exported record quantities of wheat to Britain and held the lavish **Imperial Delhi Durbar (1877)**. | **Strachey Commission (1880):** First comprehensive famine commission under Sir Richard Strachey. Recommended creation of **Famine Code (1883)**, Famine Relief Fund, suspension/remission of land revenue, and public relief works (*Temple Wage*). |
| **Famines of 1896–1897 & 1899–1900** | Lord Elgin II / Lord Curzon | Affected Central Provinces, Bombay, and Punjab; severe bubonic plague concurrent with famine. | • **Lyall Commission (1898):** Recommended expanding scope of relief to purdanashin women and craftsmen.<br>• **MacDonnell Commission (1901):** Appointed by Curzon; advocated "moral strategy" (early distribution of *taccavi* loans, non-official participation, and creation of Agricultural Banks). |
| **Great Bengal Famine of 1943** | **Lord Linlithgow** / Lord Wavell | **30 lakh (3 million) deaths**. Man-made catastrophe triggered by Japanese occupation of Burma (halting rice imports), British "Denial Policy" (seizing boats and rice stocks in coastal Bengal), wartime military inflation, and catastrophic Churchillian diversion of grain shipping away from India. | **Woodhead Commission (1945):** Documented monumental administrative failure and food rationing breakdowns. Exposed by nationalist journalist Ian Stephens in *The Statesman*. |

### 🌱 3. Post-Independence Agrarian Reforms & Land Legislation

• **Abolition of Intermediaries (Zamindari Abolition):**
  - First major agrarian reform passed in the 1950s (UP, Bihar, Madras, West Bengal).
  - Over 20 million tenants were brought into direct contact with the state; eliminated absentee landlords, though loopholes allowed zamindars to retain massive tracts under the pretext of *"personal cultivation"* (*Khudkasht*).
• **Tenancy Reforms:** Enacted to regulate rent (capped at 20% to 25% of gross produce), ensure security of tenure, and confer ownership rights on cultivating tenants.
• **Land Ceiling Legislation:**
  - First phase (1960s) fixed ceilings on individual holdings; largely subverted through fictitious transfers (*benami* transfers) to relatives, dead ancestors, and domestic servants.
  - Second phase (post-1972) fixed ceilings based on family as a unit (10 to 18 acres for irrigated double-cropped land; up to 54 acres for dry land).
• **Bhoodan & Gramdan Movements:**
  - Initiated by **Acharya Vinoba Bhave** on **April 18, 1951** at **Pochampally village (Telangana)**.
  - Landowner V. Ramachandra Reddy donated 100 acres to landless Dalits.
  - *Bhoodan:* Voluntary donation of one-sixth of land by landlords for redistribution to the landless.
  - *Gramdan:* Entire village land pooled and held communally (first Gramdan: Mangroth village, UP, 1953).

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 45:**
> 1. **First Famine Code:** The first official Famine Code was formulated in **1883** based on the recommendations of the **Strachey Commission of 1880**, NOT the Campbell Commission.
> 2. **Pochampally Milestone:** The Bhoodan movement originated at **Pochampally** (then in Andhra Pradesh, now in Yadadri Bhuvanagiri district, Telangana).

## 46. Pastoralists in the Modern World: Enclosure, Sedentarisation & Criminalisation

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Colonial land policies viewed mobile pastoralism as economically unproductive and politically subversive, systematically enclosing grazing commons, imposing movement permits and grazing taxes, and branding mobile communities as hereditary criminals under the Criminal Tribes Act.

### 🐑 1. Major Pastoral Communities of the Indian Subcontinent

```
  Mountains: Gujjar Bakarwals (J&K) / Gaddis (HP) / Bhotiyas (Uttarakhand)
  Deserts & Plains: Raikas (Rajasthan) / Maldharis (Kutch, Gujarat)
  Plateaus: Dhangars (Maharashtra) / Gollas & Kurumas (Karnataka & AP)
```

| Region / Ecology | Pastoral Community | Seasonal Transhumance Cycle | Economic Specialisation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Jammu & Kashmir** | **Gujjar Bakarwals** | High alpine meadows (*Bugyals*) in summer; descend to low foothills of Siwalik range in winter. | Pastoral nomads rearing sheep and goats; live in *koths* (stone and mud huts). |
| **Himachal Pradesh** | **Gaddis** | Spend winter in low hills of Kangra; cross Rohtang Pass into Lahaul-Spiti in summer. | Shepherds; trade wool, ghee, and livestock products. |
| **Uttarakhand** | **Bhotiyas, Gujjars, Kinnauris** | Seasonal transhumance between Siwalik winter pastures (*Bhabhar*) and high altitude alpine grasslands (*Bugyals*). | Weaving woollen shawls, trading across Himalayan passes with Tibet (until 1962). |
| **Rajasthan (Thar Desert)** | **Raikas (Rebaris)** | Marwar region; combine rain-fed cultivation with seasonal camel and sheep migration during dry season towards Malwa (MP) and Haryana. | Specialized camel breeders and sheep-wool pastoralists; revered deity *Pabuji* (protector of camels). |
| **Maharashtra (Deccan Plateau)** | **Dhangars** | Stay in dry central plateau during monsoon (sow bajra); migrate to fertile, wet coastal **Konkan** in post-monsoon dry season. | Shepherds and blanket weavers; Konkani rice farmers welcomed them because sheep manure fertilized the fields after Kharif harvest. |
| **Karnataka & Andhra Pradesh** | **Gollas, Kurumas, Kurubas** | Migrate between dry scrub interiors and moist forested tracts based on monsoon rains. | Gollas herd cattle; Kurumas and Kurubas rear sheep and goats and weave coarse woolen blankets (*kamblis*). |
| **Gujarat (Kutch & Saurashtra)** | **Maldharis** (Rabari, Bharwad, Jat) | Inhabit Banni grasslands; migrate inland during severe desert droughts. | Indigenous dairy breeders; bred world-famous Gir cattle, Banni buffalo, and Kankrej cattle. |

### 🔒 2. Colonial Enclosure & The Destruction of Pastoral Livelihoods

• **Colonial Prejudices:**
  - British officials regarded uncultivated land as "waste" because it yielded neither land revenue nor commercial timber (*Waste Land Rules* enacted from mid-19th C).
  - Mobile communities were viewed with deep suspicion because they were difficult to monitor, census, and tax.
• **Mechanisms of Pastoral Dispossession:**
  1. *Forest Acts (1865, 1878, 1927):* Grazing was completely banned in **Reserved Forests**; strictly regulated via expensive seasonal entry permits in **Protected Forests**.
  2. *Tirni (Grazing Tax):* Introduced in the 1850s–1860s; pastoralists had to pay a tax on every single animal grazing on government pastures. Right to collect tax was often auctioned to ruthless private revenue contractors.
  3. *Agricultural Enclosure:* Expanding canal colonies (especially in Punjab) converted millions of acres of traditional grazing lands (*barani*) into settled private wheat fields.

### ⛓️ 3. The Criminal Tribes Act (CTA, 1871) & Denotified Tribes (DNT)

• **The Criminal Tribes Act of 1871:**
  - One of the most draconian legislations in colonial history.
  - Colonial state declared entire nomadic communities, wandering pastoralists, petty traders, and entertainers (over 200 communities, including **Banjaras, Sansis, Kanwars, Yerukulas, and Lodhas**) as **"criminals by birth and heredity"**.
  - *Draconian Restrictions:* Forced to register at local police stations; prohibited from moving outside designated settlement colonies without police permits; required to report daily for roll-calls; summarily arrested on suspicion without warrant.
• **Post-Independence Repeal & The DNT Status:**
  - The Criminal Tribes Act was formally **repealed in August 1952** on the recommendations of the **Ayyangar Committee (1949–50)**.
  - These communities were reclassified as **"Denotified Tribes" (DNTs)** or *Vimukta Jatis*.
  - Continued stigmatization: Despite legal denotification, DNTs faced severe police harassment under Habitual Offenders Acts (1952).
  - **National Commissions:**
    - *Balkrishna Renke Commission (2008):* Highlighted that 98% of DNTs are landless and 57% lack housing.
    - *Bhiku Ramji Idate Commission (2018):* Recommended setting up a permanent commission for DNTs, conducting a nationwide caste-based census of DNTs, and creating a dedicated Development and Welfare Board (DWBDNC).

> 🎯 **Top Civil Services Traps for Chapter 46:**
> 1. **Bugyal vs Bhabhar:** **Bugyals** are high-altitude summer alpine meadows in the Himalayas (above 10,000–12,000 feet); **Bhabhar** is the dry, porous boulder-strewn foothill belt where streams disappear underground in winter. Pastoralists like Bhotiyas move *from* Bhabhar in winter *to* Bugyal in summer.
> 2. **Denotified Tribes Repeal Year:** The Criminal Tribes Act was repealed in **1952**, not in 1947 at the time of independence. August 31 is celebrated by DNT communities as *Vimukti Divas* (Liberation Day).

## 47. RAS History Ch 1: 6 UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan & Military Architecture

### 🏰 Inscription of 6 Hill Forts of Rajasthan (UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2013)

At the 37th Session of the World Heritage Committee in Phnom Penh (Cambodia, June 2013), **6 Hill Forts of Rajasthan** were collectively inscribed as a serial UNESCO World Heritage Site:

1. **Chittorgarh Fort (Mewar — Largest Fort in India):**
   • **Geomorphology:** Built on the **Mesa Plateau (616m elevation)** by Chitrangada Mori; surrounded by Gambhiri and Berach rivers.
   • **Three Historic Jauhars (Shakas):**
     - **1st Jauhar (1303):** Rani Padmini & Rawal Ratan Singh against **Alauddin Khilji** (Gora and Badal fought bravely; Khilji renamed fort *Khizrabad*).
     - **2nd Jauhar (1535):** Rani Karnavati (sent Rakhi to Humayun) against **Bahadur Shah of Gujarat** (Rawat Bagh Singh led defense).
     - **3rd Jauhar (1568):** Led by Phool Kanwar against **Mughal Emperor Akbar** (Jaimal Rathore and Patta Sisodia fought to martyrdom; Akbar erected their elephant statues at Agra fort gate).
   • **Monuments:Vijay Stambha** (9 stories, 122 ft, built by Maharana Kumbha to commemorate victory over Mahmud Khilji in Battle of Sarangpur 1437; architect Jaita, Napa, Punja); **Kirti Stambha** (7 stories, dedicated to 1st Jain Tirthankara Adinath by merchant Jija Shah).
2. **Kumbhalgarh Fort (Rajsamand — The Inaccessible Fort):**
   • **Architect & Wall:** Built by **Maharana Kumbha** (1443–1458) under chief architect **Mandan**; encircled by a **36 km continuous defensive perimeter wall** (2nd longest continuous wall in the world after the Great Wall of China; width allows 4 horsemen to ride abreast).
   • **Katargarh (The Eye of Mewar):** Upper fortified citadel housing Kumbha's palace and the **birthplace of Maharana Pratap (9 May 1540 at Badal Mahal)**.
3. **Ranthambore Fort (Sawai Madhopur — The Forest/Hill Fort):**
   • **Legacy:** Famous for Chauhan ruler **Hammir Dev Chauhan** ("Hammir Hath"); besieged by **Alauddin Khilji in 1301** (1st historic Jauhar / *Jal Jauhar* of Rajasthan led by Rani Ranga Devi; Amir Khusro noted *"Today the bastion of infidelity has become the home of Islam"*).
   • **Monuments:** Unique **Trinetra Ganesha Temple**; 32-Pillared Cenotaph (Battis Khambon ki Chhatri); Padmala Lake.
4. **Gagron Fort (Jhalawar — Masterpiece of Water Fort / Jal Durg):**
   • **Location:** Surrounded on three sides by the confluence of **Ahu and Kali Sindh rivers**; has **zero foundation** (rests directly on solid natural bedrock).
   • **Two Jauhars:** 1st in 1423 under Achaldas Khichi against Hoshang Shah of Mandu (recorded in *Achaldas Khichi ri Vachanika* by Shivdas Gadan); 2nd in 1444 under Palhansi against Mahmud Khilji.
5. **Amber Fort (Jaipur — Rajput-Mughal Fusion):**
   • **Architecture:** Built by **Raja Man Singh I (1592)** on Cheel ka Teela; contains **Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace)**, Diwan-i-Aam, Diwan-i-Khas, Kesar Kyari garden in Maota Lake; houses the idol of **Shila Devi** (brought by Man Singh from Jessore, Bengal).
6. **Jaisalmer Fort / Sonar Qila (Swarn Durg — Living Desert Fort):**
   • **Construction:** Founded by Bhati Rajput ruler **Rao Jaisal (1156)** on Trikuta Hill; built of yellow sandstone with **interlocking stone joints (zero mortar/lime)**; home to 25% of city population.
   • **2.5 Jauhars (Dhai Shaka):** 1st under Alauddin Khilji (Rawal Jait Singh); 2nd under Feroz Shah Tughlaq (Rawal Duda); **0.5 (Half) Jauhar in 1550** under Rawal Lunkaran against Afghan ruler Amir Ali (Rajput warriors fought to death, but women could not perform Jauhar in time, hence counted as half Jauhar).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Exam Angle → 🔥 HIGH — RPSC RAS Top Invariants:

2. **Half-Jauhar (Ardh Shaka):** Occurred in Jaisalmer in 1550 under Rawal Lunkaran against Amir Ali of Kandahar.

## 48. RAS History Ch 2: Rajasthan Miniature Painting Schools (Mewar, Kishangarh, Bundi & Kota)

### 🎨 The 4 Principal Schools of Rajasthani Painting (Anand Coomaraswamy Framework)

Anand Kentish Coomaraswamy categorized Rajasthani painting in his seminal 1916 book *"Rajput Painting"*. The painting styles are classified into 4 distinct schools:

1. **Mewar School (The Mother School of Rajasthani Art):**
   • **Chawand Style (Origin):** Developed during Maharana Pratap’s reign at Chawand; painter **Nisardin** painted the famous *Ragamala* set in **1605** during Maharana Amar Singh I.
   • **Udaipur Golden Era:** Under Maharana Jagat Singh I (1628–1652); established a dedicated painting department called **Chitaron ki Ovari** (also known as *Tasveera ro Karkhano*); master painter **Sahibdin** illustrated *Ragamala (1628)*, *Geet Govind*, and *Arsha Ramayana*.
   • **Nathdwara Style (Pichwai Art):** Centered on Shrinathji temple; famous for **Pichwai cloth paintings** depicting Krishna Leela behind the deity idol; women painters: **Kamala and Ilaichi**.
2. **Marwar School (Jodhpur, Bikaner, Kishangarh):**
   • **Kishangarh Style & Bani Thani:** Golden age under **Raja Sawant Singh (pen name Nagridas)**; court artist **Nihal Chand** painted **Bani Thani** (court singer Rasik Bihari) with elongated fish-like eyes, arched brows, sharp nose, and transparent veil.
   • **Eric Dickinson’s Tribute:** Art critic Eric Dickinson designated Bani Thani as the **"Mona Lisa of India"**; Government of India issued a 20-paise postage stamp on Bani Thani in 1973.
   • **Bikaner Style:** Famous for **Usta Kala** (intricate gold embossing on camel leather by Usta craftsmen) and **Matheerna Kala** (fresco paintings of religious themes on wet plaster).
3. **Hadoti School (Bundi & Kota):**
   • **Bundi Style (Bird & Nature Specialization):** World-famous for rich depictions of wildlife, lush vegetation, water ponds, and monsoon peacocks; **Chitrashala (Picture Gallery)** built by Maharao Ummed Singh is called a *"Paradise of Wall Paintings"*.
   • **Kota Style (Hunting Scenes):** Characterized by dynamic hunting expeditions (*Shikar scenes*) where **women and queens are depicted hunting tigers and wild beasts** alongside kings.
4. **Dhundhar School (Jaipur, Alwar, Shekhawati):**
   • **Shekhawati Haveli Frescoes:** Known globally as the **"Open Air Art Gallery of Rajasthan"** for exquisite fresco paintings (*Arayash technique / Morakasi*) across Havelis in Mandawa, Nawalgarh, and Fatehpur.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Exam Angle → 🔥 HIGH — RPSC RAS Visual Markers:

2. **Bird & Animal Dominance:** **Bundi Style**.
3. **Bani Thani Painter & Patron:** Painter is **Nihal Chand**; King is **Sawant Singh (Nagridas)**; Termed "Mona Lisa of India" by **Eric Dickinson**.

## 49. RAS History Ch 3: Rajasthan Peasant Revolts (Bijolia, Begun) & Prajamandal Movements

### 🌾 Peasant Revolts & Prajamandal Freedom Movement in Rajasthan

1. **Bijolia Peasant Movement (Bhilwara — 1897 to 1941):**
   • **Significance:** Longest non-violent organized peasant movement in world history (**44 years**, 1897–1941) against the Jagirdars of Bijolia (Thikana of Mewar).
   • **Cess Exploitation:** Farmers burdened with **84 different types of cesses (*Lag-Bag*)**; in 1903, Rao Krishna Singh imposed **Chawri Kar** (₹5 tax per daughter's marriage); in 1906, Rao Prithvi Singh imposed **Talwar Bandhai** (succession cess).
   • **Three Operational Phases:**
     - **Phase 1 (1897–1915):** Led locally by **Sadhu Sitaram Das**, Nanji Patel, and Thakari Patel.
     - **Phase 2 (1916–1927 - Golden Phase):Vijay Singh Pathik (Bhup Singh)** took leadership; established **Uparmal Panch Board** (1917, Sarpanch: Manna Patel) and newspaper *Pratap* (published from Kanpur by Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi); supported by Manikya Lal Verma (wrote motivational song *Panchhida*).
     - **Phase 3 (1927–1941):** Led by Jamnalal Bajaj and Haribhau Upadhyaya; successfully concluded with land rights restored in 1941 by Mewar Prime Minister Sir T. Vijayaraghavachari.
2. **Begun Peasant Movement (Chittorgarh — 1921 to 1923):**
   • **Leadership:** Led by **Ramnarayan Chaudhary** at Menal (Chittorgarh); Rawat Anoop Singh of Begun signed the **Bolshevik Agreement** with peasants (later annulled by Mewar Maharana).
   • **Govindpura Firing (13 July 1923):** Commissioner Trench opened fire on peaceful farmer gathering; **Rupa ji Dhakad and Kripa ji Dhakad** were martyred.
3. **Prajamandal Movement Master Chronology:**
   • **Objective:** Established to secure responsible governance under the aegis of native princely rulers and abolish feudal cesses (*Lata-Kunta, Begar*).
   • **Key Prajamandals & Founders:**
     - **1931 (1st in Rajasthan):Jaipur Prajamandal** (Founded by Kapurchand Patni; reorganized in 1936 by Jamnalal Bajaj and Hiralal Shastri).
     - **1931:Bundi Prajamandal** (Kanti Lal).
     - **1934:Marwar Prajamandal** (Jai Narayan Vyas, Bhanwarlal Saraf).
     - **1936:Bikaner Prajamandal** (Magharam Vaidya at Calcutta).
     - **1938:Mewar Prajamandal** (Manikya Lal Verma, Balwant Singh Mehta as 1st President).
     - **1939:Kota Prajamandal** (Pandit Nayanuram Sharma, Abhay Ali).
     - **1944:Dungarpur Prajamandal** (**Bhogilal Pandya**, revered as *"Gandhi of Vagad"*).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap:**
> 🎯 Exam Angle → 🔥 HIGH — RPSC RAS Top Traps:
1. **First Prajamandal:** **Jaipur Prajamandal (1931)** was the 1st in Rajasthan.
2. **Prajamandals Founded Outside Native States:**
   • **Bikaner Prajamandal (1936):** Founded in **Calcutta** by Magharam Vaidya.
   • **Sirohi Prajamandal (1939):** Founded in **Bombay** by Gokulbhai Bhatt ("Gandhi of Rajasthan").
   • **Bharatpur Prajamandal (1938):** Founded in **Rewari (Haryana)** by Gopi Lal Yadav.
