## 2. Core Central Welfare Schemes (PMJDY, PMJJBY, PMSBY, PMMY, PM SVANidhi)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The JAM Trinity (Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, Mobile) serves as the core delivery infrastructure for subsidized term life insurance, accident coverage, micro-credit, and street vendor livelihood protection.

• **PMJDY (Jan Dhan Yojana):** Launched Aug 28, 2014 under Ministry of Finance (DFS). Universal banking access with Zero balance BSBDA account. Free RuPay debit card with **₹2 Lakh** accidental death cover. Overdraft facility up to **₹10,000** (age 18–65, no condition up to ₹2,000).
• **PMJJBY (Life Insurance):** Launched May 9, 2015. Age: **18–50 years**. Risk cover: **₹2 Lakh** for death due to ANY cause. Annual premium: **₹436** (auto-debited). Life coverage terminates at age 55.
• **PMSBY (Accident Insurance):** Launched May 9, 2015. Age: **18–70 years**. Risk cover: **₹2 Lakh** for accidental death/total permanent disability; **₹1 Lakh** for partial disability. Annual premium: **₹20** (auto-debited).
• **PMMY (MUDRA Yojana):** Non-farm micro-enterprise loans through banks/MFIs refinanced by MUDRA:
  - *Shishu:* Loans up to **₹50,000**
  - *Kishore:* Loans from **₹50,000 to ₹5 Lakh**
  - *Tarun:* Loans from **₹5 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh**
  - *Tarun Plus (Budget 2024):* Raised to **₹20 Lakh** for entrepreneurs who previously repaid Tarun loans.
• **PM SVANidhi (Street Vendors):** Nodal Ministry: **MoHUA** (implemented via SIDBI). Collateral-free working capital loan: 1st Tranche **₹10,000** (1 yr); 2nd Tranche **₹20,000**; 3rd Tranche **₹50,000**. Interest subvention: **7.0% per annum**. Digital transaction cashback up to ₹1,200/year.
• **PM Vishwakarma:** Nodal: MSME + MSDE + DFS. Outlay **₹13,000 Crore** for 18 traditional craft trades. Collateral-free enterprise loan up to **₹3 Lakh** (Tranche 1: ₹1L, Tranche 2: ₹2L) at concessional **5% interest**. Toolkit incentive ₹15,000 digital voucher; training stipend ₹500/day.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 2:**
> - **Trap:** Believing MUDRA lends money directly to borrowers. *Reality:* MUDRA is a **refinancing NBFC**; commercial banks, RRBs, and Microfinance Institutions issue the actual loans.

## 3. D-SIBs & Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Targets

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs) are institutions deemed "Too Big To Fail" requiring additional capital surcharges, while Priority Sector Lending mandates targeted credit delivery to vulnerable and productive sectors of the economy.

### 🏛️ 1. Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs)
- Designated annually by the RBI under the D-SIB Framework (since 2014) based on size, interconnectedness, substitutability, and complexity:
  1. **State Bank of India (SBI):** Bucket 4 $\rightarrow$ Additional Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) requirement: **0.80%** of Risk-Weighted Assets (RWAs).
  2. **HDFC Bank:** Bucket 2 $\rightarrow$ Additional CET1 requirement: **0.40%** of RWAs.
  3. **ICICI Bank:** Bucket 1 $\rightarrow$ Additional CET1 requirement: **0.20%** of RWAs.

### 📊 2. Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Master Matrix

| Bank Category | Overall PSL Target (% of ANBC or CEOBE) | Agriculture Target | Micro Enterprises Target | Weaker Sections Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Domestic Commercial Banks** | **40%** | **18%** (10% for Small/Marginal Farmers) | **7.5%** | **12%** |
| **Foreign Banks ($\ge 20$ branches)** | **40%** | 18% (10% for SMFs) | 7.5% | 12% |
| **Foreign Banks ($< 20$ branches)** | **40%** | No sub-targets (can achieve in any PSL sector) | No sub-target | No sub-target |
| **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)** | **75%** | 18% (10% for SMFs) | 7.5% | 15% |
| **Small Finance Banks (SFBs)** | **75%** | 18% (10% for SMFs) | 7.5% | 12% |
| **Primary Urban Co-op Banks (UCBs)** | **75%** (phased up to March 2026) | NA | 7.5% | 12% |

- *PSL Shortfall Penalty:* Shortfall must be deposited into the **Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)** managed by NABARD, or similar funds with SIDBI, MUDRA, or NHB at penal interest rates below the repo rate.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 3:**
> - **Trap:** Assuming all foreign banks have identical PSL mandates. *Reality:* Foreign banks with **20 or more branches** have the exact same sub-targets as domestic scheduled commercial banks; foreign banks with **less than 20 branches** have an overall 40% target with **no mandatory sub-targets**.

## 4. DAY-NRLM SHG Credit Framework & Subvention (FY 2025–26)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana - National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM) mobilizes rural poor women into Self-Help Groups (SHGs) linked to institutional bank credit through structured interest subventions and collateral waivers.

• **Nodal Lead Bank:** **Indian Bank** appointed as the Nodal Bank by the Ministry of Rural Development for administering interest subventions across public and private sector banks.
• **Interest Subvention Structure:**
  - For loans up to **₹3 Lakh:** Banks lend at **7% per annum** to women SHGs; the Central Government provides an **Interest Subvention of 4.5%**, reducing the effective cost to the group.
  - Prompt Repayment Incentive (PRI): An additional **3% subvention** for prompt repayment, bringing the effective interest rate down to **4.0% per annum**.
  - For loans between **₹3 Lakh and ₹5 Lakh:** Banks lend at commercial rates; Government provides an interest subvention equal to the difference between the bank's lending rate and 7% (capped at **5.0% subvention**).
• **Collateral Waiver & Credit Guarantee (CGFMU):**
  - No collateral and no margin requirement for loans up to **₹10 Lakh**.
  - For loans from **₹10 Lakh to ₹20 Lakh:** No collateral, covered under the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU); maximum margin charged by bank is capped at **10%**.
• **Revolving Fund (RF) vs Capital Subsidy:**
  - Revolving Fund of **₹20,000 to ₹30,000** provided per eligible SHG.
  - **Zero Capital Subsidy:** DAY-NRLM does not provide any capital subsidy; it relies strictly on credit linkages and interest subventions.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 4:**
> - **Trap:** Expecting a capital asset subsidy under DAY-NRLM. *Reality:* DAY-NRLM explicitly **prohibits capital subsidies**; support is delivered purely through Revolving Funds, Community Investment Funds (CIF), and interest subventions.

## 5. Deep Dive: RBI Central Board & Governance Architecture

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Reserve Bank of India is governed by a Central Board of Directors constituted under Section 8 of the RBI Act, 1934, balancing executive leadership with government nominees and independent professional directors.

• **Composition of the Central Board (Maximum 21 Members):**
  - **Governor:** 1 Governor appointed under Section 8(1)(a) (Current: Shaktikanta Das).
  - **Deputy Governors:** Not more than **4 Deputy Governors** appointed under Section 8(1)(a). (Four portfolios: Monetary Policy, Banking Regulation, Financial Markets/Supervision, Information Technology/Currency).
  - **Government Nominee Directors:** **2 Directors** nominated by the Central Government under Section 8(1)(d) (typically Secretary, Department of Financial Services, and Secretary, Department of Economic Affairs).
  - **Non-Official Independent Directors:** **10 Directors** nominated by the Central Government under Section 8(1)(c) representing diverse economic sectors.
  - **Local Board Representatives:** **4 Directors**, one each elected from the four Local Boards (Northern [New Delhi], Southern [Chennai], Eastern [Kolkata], Western [Mumbai]) under Section 8(1)(b).
• **Statutory Meetings & Quorum:**
  - Central Board must meet at least **6 times in each year** and at least **once in each quarter** (Section 13).
  - Quorum for a Board meeting is at least **3 Directors**, of whom at least one must be the Governor or a Deputy Governor authorized to act on his behalf.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 5:**
> - **Trap:** Believing the RBI Act limits the Governor's term to 3 years. *Reality:* Section 8(4) of the RBI Act states the Governor and Deputy Governors hold office for a term **not exceeding 5 years**, as the Central Government may fix upon appointment, and are eligible for re-appointment.

## 6. Economic Survey 2025–26 & Union Budget 2026–27 Highlights

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Macroeconomic fiscal policy navigates the glide path to reduce the Fiscal Deficit below 4.5% of GDP while expanding capital expenditure (Capex) to crowd-in private corporate investment.

• **Fiscal Deficit Glide Path (FRBM Compliance):**
  - FY 2024–25 (Revised Estimates): **4.9% of GDP**.
  - FY 2025–26 (Budget Estimates): **4.4% of GDP** (successfully breaking below the targeted 4.5% threshold).
  - Objective: Stabilize the Debt-to-GDP ratio on a declining trajectory post-2026.
• **Effective Capital Expenditure (Capex Push):**
  - Central Government Capex allocation scaled past **₹11.11 Lakh Crore** (~3.4% of GDP), emphasizing railways, national highways, multi-modal logistics (PM GatiShakti), and defence indigenization.
• **Key Economic Survey Diagnostic Insights:**
  - Real GDP growth projected in the range of **6.5% to 7.0%**.
  - Headline retail inflation maintained within the RBI's target band of $4\% \pm 2\%$.
  - Current Account Deficit (CAD) contained below 1.5% of GDP, backed by robust services exports and strong remittance inflows.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 6:**
> - **Trap:** Confusing Fiscal Deficit with Primary Deficit. *Reality:*
>   $$\text{Primary Deficit} = \text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Interest Payments}$$
>   Primary deficit measures the government's borrowing needs excluding the interest liability on past accumulated debt.

## 8. Exam Revision Corner (Banking & SEBI Reforms)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Capital market and banking regulations focus on reducing settlement settlement cycle latency (T+0), lowering mutual fund barriers (MF Lite), tightening speculative derivatives trading, and establishing Central KYC registries.

• **T+0 Rolling Settlement & Instant Settlement:** SEBI introduced optional **T+0 settlement** (same-day settlement by 4:30 PM) for 25 liquid scrips, laying the pathway towards real-time instantaneous settlement.
• **Mutual Fund Lite (MF Lite):** Relaxed regulatory framework for passively managed index funds and ETFs; lower net worth and compliance overheads to foster retail penetration in Tier 2/3 cities.
• **Equity Derivatives (F&O) Regulatory Restructuring:** Increased minimum contract value from ₹5 Lakh to **₹15 Lakh** (phased up to ₹20 Lakh); restricted weekly index options expiry to only **one benchmark index per exchange**; mandatory upfront margin collection for option buyers.
• **Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR):** Managed by **CERSAI**; issues unique 14-digit KYC Identifier (KIN), eliminating repetitive KYC verification across banks, mutual funds, and insurance firms.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 8:**
> - **Trap:** Who manages the Central KYC Registry? *Reality:* CKYCR is managed and operated by **CERSAI** (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India), not the RBI or SEBI directly.

## 9. Exam Revision Corner (Budget & Survey)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Union Budget classifies receipts into Revenue and Capital, balancing developmental expenditure against constitutional debt ceilings.

• **Four Key Deficit Formulas:**
  1. $$\text{Revenue Deficit} = \text{Revenue Expenditure} - \text{Revenue Receipts}$$
  2. $$\text{Effective Revenue Deficit} = \text{Revenue Deficit} - \text{Grants for Creation of Capital Assets}$$
  3. $$\text{Fiscal Deficit} = \text{Total Expenditure} - (\text{Revenue Receipts} + \text{Non-debt Capital Receipts})$$
  4. $$\text{Primary Deficit} = \text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Interest Payments}$$
• **Article 112:** Annual Financial Statement (the term "Budget" does not appear anywhere in the Constitution of India).
• **Guillotine:** Parliamentary device used on the last day of the discussion on Demands for Grants, putting all remaining undiscovered demands to vote without debate.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 9:**
> - **Trap:** Assuming Effective Revenue Deficit is part of the original FRBM Act, 2003. *Reality:* Effective Revenue Deficit was introduced by amendment via the **Finance Act, 2012** on the recommendation of the Dr. Parthasarathi Shome Committee.

## 10. Exam Revision Corner (Insurance & Bima Trinity)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> IRDAI's strategic blueprint "Insurance for All by 2047" is anchored by the three pillars of the Bima Trinity: an affordable bundled product (Bima Vistar), a decentralized female distribution network (Bima Vahak), and a digital marketplace (Bima Sugam).

• **Bima Sugam:** An end-to-end digital public infrastructure protocol for buying, servicing, and settling insurance claims across life, health, and general policies.
• **Bima Vistar:** An all-in-one comprehensive bundled micro-insurance product offering parametric life, accident, and property/crop risk cover for rural households.
• **Bima Vahak:** Dedicated women-centric distribution channel at the Gram Panchayat level to drive last-mile financial literacy and policy uptake.
• **Insurance Penetration vs Insurance Density:**
  - **Insurance Penetration:** Ratio of premium underwritten in a given year to GDP (expressed as percentage: $\frac{\text{Premium}}{\text{GDP}} \times 100$).
  - **Insurance Density:** Ratio of premium to total population (expressed in USD per capita: $\frac{\text{Premium}}{\text{Population}}$).

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 10:**
> - **Trap:** Confusing Insurance Penetration with Insurance Density. *Reality:* Penetration is a percentage of **GDP**; Density is per capita spend in **Dollars ($)**.

## 11. Exam Revision Corner (MDBs & Forecasts)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs) and credit rating agencies monitor India's potential output through capital formation, demographic dividends, and external debt vulnerability metrics.

• **India's Sovereign Credit Rating:** Standard & Poor's (BBB- with Positive outlook), Moody's (Baa3 with Stable outlook), Fitch (BBB- with Stable outlook); currently pegged at the lowest investment grade.
• **Voting Power in AIIB (Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank):**
  - China is the largest shareholder (26.6% voting power).
  - **India is the second largest shareholder (7.6% voting power)** and the single largest recipient of AIIB project financing.
• **New Development Bank (NDB / BRICS Bank):**
  - Equal voting rights (20% each) among the five founding BRICS members; no single member holds veto power; HQ in **Shanghai, China**.

> - **Trap:** Assuming India has veto power in AIIB. *Reality:* Only **China** holds de facto veto power in AIIB as major decisions require a 75% supermajority and China controls over 26% of the voting share.

## 12. Exam Revision Corner (RBI Monetary Tools)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Quantitative monetary policy instruments regulate the overall volume of credit (CRR, SLR, Repo, MSF, SDF), whereas Qualitative (selective) tools direct credit into specific priority channels (margin requirements, moral suasion).

• **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF):** Operationalized in April 2022 under amended Section 17 of the RBI Act; uncollateralized liquidity absorption tool (RBI absorbs excess bank liquidity **without providing government securities as collateral**); forms the floor of the LAF corridor.
• **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF):** Penal borrowing window for scheduled commercial banks to borrow overnight funds against approved SLR securities up to their dipping limit; forms the ceiling of the LAF corridor.
• **Open Market Operations (OMO):** Outright purchase (injects liquidity) or sale (absorbs liquidity) of government securities by the central bank in the secondary market.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 12:**
> - **Trap:** Does the RBI provide collateral when absorbing funds under SDF? *Reality:* **NO**. The revolutionary feature of SDF is that it allows the RBI to absorb unlimited liquidity **without pledging any government securities**.

## 15. Exam Revision Corner (Static Banking Invariants & Committees)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Modern Indian banking architecture was established by landmark statutory committees: Hilton-Young (RBI), Narasimham (Financial Systems), Urjit Patel (Monetary Policy Committee), and Nachiket Mor (Differentiated Banks).

• **Landmark Banking Committees & Historical Invariants:**
  - **Hilton Young Commission (1926):** Recommended the creation of the Reserve Bank of India.
  - **Narasimham Committee I (1991) & II (1998):** Recommended reduction in CRR/SLR, introduction of prudential norms (NPA classification, capital adequacy), and bank mergers.
  - **Urjit Patel Committee (2014):** Recommended the transition to Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT), adopting CPI as headline inflation metric, and creating the **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**.
  - **Nachiket Mor Committee (2014):** Recommended the licensing of differentiated banks (**Payments Banks and Small Finance Banks**).
  - **Bimal Jalan Committee (2019):** Economic Capital Framework (ECF) governing RBI's surplus transfer and contingency risk buffer.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 15:**
> - **Trap:** Did the Narasimham Committee recommend creating Payments Banks? *Reality:* **NO**. Small Finance Banks and Payments Banks were recommended by the **Nachiket Mor Committee** in 2014.

## 16. Health, Social Justice & Education (104 Schemes)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The human capital safety net spans secondary/tertiary hospitalization assurance (PM-JAY), foundational school literacy (NIPUN Bharat), and female life-cycle protection (Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, Mission Shakti).

### 🏥 1. Social Sector Core Schemes Matrix

## 21. Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) Corridor Visualised

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) corridor brackets overnight interbank call money rates between the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF - uncollateralized floor) and the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF - penalized ceiling), centered around the Policy Repo Rate.

```
                         THE OPERATIONAL LAF CORRIDOR
   ▲ Interest Rate (%)
   │
   ├────── 6.75% ── Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) & Bank Rate [CEILING]
   │                (Repo + 25 bps: Emergency borrowing against SLR dipping)
   │
   ├────── 6.50% ── POLICY REPO RATE [CENTER / ANCHOR]
   │                (Key policy signaling rate; liquidity injection via G-Secs)
   │
   ├────── 6.25% ── Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) [FLOOR]
   │                (Repo - 25 bps: Uncollateralized liquidity absorption)
   │
   └────── 3.35% ── Fixed Reverse Repo Rate (Passive instrument)
```

• **Corridor Width:** Symmetrical **50 basis points (0.50%)** width, anchored 25 bps above (MSF) and 25 bps below (SDF) the Policy Repo Rate.
• **Bank Rate Alignment:** Under Section 49 of the RBI Act, the **Bank Rate is automatically aligned with the MSF Rate** and changes dynamically with it.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 21:**
> - **Trap:** Is Bank Rate equal to the Repo Rate or MSF Rate? *Reality:* Bank Rate is pegged identically to the **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) rate** (Repo + 25 bps), NOT the Policy Repo Rate.

## 26. Monetary Policy Instruments Deep Dive (Direct vs Indirect Tools)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Direct instruments rely on administrative mandates to set reserve ratios or balance sheet caps (CRR, SLR, Refinance), whereas indirect instruments utilize price-based market interventions (Repo, OMO, SDF) to influence interbank liquidity.

• **Direct Instruments:**
  1. **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR):** Mandatory liquid cash reserves maintained with RBI. No floor and no ceiling under current RBI Act (statutory limits removed in 2006).
  2. **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR):** Reserves maintained by banks themselves in liquid assets (cash, gold, dated G-Secs). Maximum statutory ceiling is **40%** under Section 24 of Banking Regulation Act, 1949.
• **Indirect Instruments:**
  1. **Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF):** Daily repo and reverse repo auctions and standing facilities.
  2. **Open Market Operations (OMO):** Outright purchase/sale of G-Secs.
  3. **Market Stabilisation Scheme (MSS):** Initiated in 2004 to sterilize massive foreign exchange capital inflows through the issuance of Treasury Bills and dated securities.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 26:**
> - **Trap:** What is the statutory minimum floor for CRR under the RBI Act? *Reality:* There is **NO statutory minimum floor or ceiling** for CRR; the RBI has full discretion to fix any rate following the 2006 amendment to the RBI Act.

## 29. Negotiable Instruments (NI) Act, 1881 Core Provisions

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 governs transferable legal credit instruments—Promissory Notes, Bills of Exchange, and Cheques—protecting the holder in due course and criminalizing cheque bounces under Section 138.

• **Core Statutory Definitions:**
  - **Promissory Note (Section 4):** An unconditional undertaking in writing signed by the maker to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person, or to the bearer. (Contains **2 parties**: Maker and Payee; bank notes/currency notes are excluded).
  - **Bill of Exchange (Section 5):** An unconditional order in writing signed by the maker directing a person to pay a certain sum of money. (Contains **3 parties**: Drawer, Drawee, Payee).
  - **Cheque (Section 6):** A bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker, payable always on demand; includes electronic image of a truncated cheque and a cheque in electronic form.
• **Section 138 (Cheque Bounce / Dishonour):**
  - Criminal offense for dishonour of cheque due to insufficiency of funds.
  - Notice must be issued within **30 days** of receipt of information from the bank. Drawer has **15 days** to pay; if unpaid, complaint filed within 1 month.
  - Penalties: Imprisonment up to **2 years**, or fine up to **twice the amount of the cheque**, or both.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 29:**
> - **Trap:** Is a currency note a Promissory Note under the NI Act? *Reality:* **NO**. Section 4 explicitly excludes currency notes and bank notes from the definition of a Promissory Note under the Negotiable Instruments Act.

## 32. Overseas Account Terminology & Money Supply Aggregates (Nostro, Vostro, Loro)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> International correspondent banking utilizes Latin-derived relational account nomenclatures to settle cross-border trade transactions in foreign and domestic currencies.

• **Nostro Account (*"Ours with you"*):** An account maintained by a domestic Indian bank (e.g., SBI) with a foreign bank (e.g., Citibank, New York) in foreign currency (USD).
• **Vostro Account (*"Yours with us"*):** An account maintained by a foreign bank (e.g., HSBC, London) with a domestic Indian bank (e.g., SBI, Mumbai) in Indian Rupees (INR).
  - *Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVA):* Mechanism allowing international trade settlement in Indian Rupees (INR) with partner nations like Russia, Sri Lanka, and Mauritius.
• **Loro Account (*"Theirs"*):** A third-party account referenced by a bank settling through a correspondent bank's account (*"settling payment via their account with them"*).

## 35. RBI Money Supply Equations & Reserve Money Dynamics

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Reserve Bank of India compiles money supply aggregates classified according to degree of liquidity, from narrow monetary base (M0) to broad money (M3).

• **Standard Monetary Aggregates:**
  - **M0 (Reserve Money / High-Powered Money / Monetary Base):**
    $$M_0 = \text{Currency in Circulation} + \text{Bankers' Deposits with RBI} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$$
  - **M1 (Narrow Money - Most Liquid):**
    $$M_1 = \text{Currency with the Public} + \text{Demand Deposits with Banking System} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$$
  - **M2:**
    $$M_2 = M_1 + \text{Post Office Savings Bank Deposits}$$
  - **M3 (Broad Money - Most Frequently Tracked Metric):**
    $$M_3 = M_1 + \text{Time Deposits with the Banking System}$$
  - **M4:**
    $$M_4 = M_3 + \text{Total Post Office Deposits (excluding National Savings Certificates)}$$
• **Money Multiplier ($m$):**
  $$m = \frac{M_3}{M_0}$$
  The money multiplier reflects the credit creation capacity of commercial banks given reserve ratios.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 35:**
> - **Trap:** Is Time Deposit included in Narrow Money ($M_1$)? *Reality:* **NO**. $M_1$ includes **Demand Deposits only**; Time Deposits are included in Broad Money ($M_3$).

## 36. SEBI Capital Markets Overhaul (T+0, MF Lite, F&O Restructuring, CAS)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> SEBI's regulatory framework protects retail retail participants against market manipulation, expands transparency in institutional trading, and establishes Consolidated Account Statements.

• **T+0 Settlement Mechanism:** Launched on optional basis alongside T+1; trades executed between 9:15 AM and 1:30 PM settle same day by 4:30 PM, reducing counterparty settlement risk.
• **Consolidated Account Statement (CAS):** Combined monthly single financial statement issued by depositories (NSDL and CDSL) capturing an investor's mutual fund units, demat shares, corporate bonds, and REITs/InvITs under a single PAN.
• **SEBI Equity F&O Reforms:** Minimum derivative contract value raised from ₹5 Lakh to **₹15 Lakh** (scaling up to ₹20 Lakh); weekly index option expiries limited to one benchmark index per exchange; intraday monitoring of position limits.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 36:**
> - **Trap:** Who issues the Consolidated Account Statement (CAS)? *Reality:* CAS is generated collaboratively by the two depositories (**NSDL and CDSL**) and mutual fund RTAs (CAMS/KFintech) based on the investor's unique Permanent Account Number (PAN).

## 37. The Bima Trinity — IRDAI's Three-Pillar Inclusion Strategy

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> The Bima Trinity establishes a universal, digital, gender-empowered distribution model to achieve national insurance saturation.

• **The Three Pillars Visualized:**
  1. **Bima Sugam (Digital Public Rail):** A single electronic marketplace enabling paperless onboarding, policy issuance, endorsement, porting, and automated claims settlement across all insurers.
  2. **Bima Vistar (Comprehensive Product):** An affordable, standardized, bundled parametric micro-insurance product providing fixed payouts for hospital cash, accidental injury, natural death, and property damage without requiring tedious documentation.
  3. **Bima Vahak (Field Force):** A dedicated grassroots cadre of women agents deployed in every Gram Panchayat to interface directly with rural households, educate women, and assist in claims processing.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 37:**
> - **Trap:** What is the defining characteristic of Bima Vistar payouts? *Reality:* Bima Vistar utilizes **parametric (trigger-based) design**; upon a defined event occurring (e.g., certified death or hospital admission), a pre-fixed benefit amount is paid immediately without detailed loss assessment audits.

## 38. Rural Livelihood Security & Employment Architecture (MGNREGS / Asset Systems)

> 🧠 **Key Concept — First-Principles Core Truth**
> Rights-based rural employment guarantees under MGNREGS are fortified by statutory unemployment allowances, mandatory digital attendance (NMMS), Aadhaar-based payments (ABPS), and GIS-monitored asset durability.

• **Statutory Features of MGNREGA, 2005:**
  - Guarantees **100 days of unskilled wage employment** per financial year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer for manual work.
  - Employment must be provided within **15 days** of submitting application; failure to do so obligates the State to pay a daily **Unemployment Allowance** (not less than 1/4th of wage rate for first 30 days, and 1/2 of wage rate thereafter).
  - Works must be situated within **5 km radius** of the village; if beyond 5 km, an additional **10% extra wage** is mandatory to cover travel and living expenses.
• **Technological Governance Rails:**
  - **National Mobile Monitoring System (NMMS):** Mandatory twice-a-day geo-tagged digital attendance of workers at worksites.
  - **Aadhaar-Based Payment System (ABPS):** Wages routed directly to the Aadhaar-linked bank accounts of authenticated workers.
  - **Social Audit Units (SAUs):** Mandatory social audits conducted through the **Gram Sabha** at least once every six months under Section 17.

> 🎯 **Exam Anchor & Trap for Note 38:**
> - **Trap:** Who pays the Unemployment Allowance if work is not provided within 15 days under MGNREGS? *Reality:* The **State Government** is legally liable to pay the Unemployment Allowance from its own state budget; the Central Government funds the 100% unskilled wage and 75% material costs only when work is actually provided.
