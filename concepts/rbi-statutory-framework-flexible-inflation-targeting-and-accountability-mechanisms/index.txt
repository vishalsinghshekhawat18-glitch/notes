12:T52c,The Finance Act, 2016 amended the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 by inserting **Chapter III-F (Sections 45Z to 45ZO)**, creating the modern monetary framework:

### 1. The Preamble Mandate
The amended preamble of the RBI Act states:
> *"...to regulate the issue of Bank notes and keeping of reserves with a view to securing monetary stability in India and generally to operate the currency and credit system of the country to its advantage; and whereas it is essential to have a modern monetary policy framework to meet the challenge of an increasingly complex economy; and whereas the primary objective of the monetary policy is to maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth."*

### 2. Setting the Target (Section 45ZA)
- Determined by the **Central Government in consultation with the Reserve Bank** once every **5 years**.
- **Nominal Anchor**: Headline Consumer Price Index (CPI-Combined).
- **Numerical Parameter (2021–2026 Notification)**:
  $$\text{Target Inflation} = 4.0\%$$
  $$\text{Tolerance Band} = 4.0\% \pm 2.0\% \implies [2.0\% \text{ to } 6.0\%]$$
- *Why "Flexible"?*: If a rigid $4.0\%$ target were enforced, any temporary crop disruption would force violent interest rate hikes that crush employment. The $\pm 2\%$ band provides monetary flexibility to absorb supply shocks.13:T5b4,The RBI Act establishes strict statutory accountability if the central bank breaches the sovereign target:

```
                  STATUTORY FAILURE DEFINITION (Section 45ZN)
                                      │
       Average Headline CPI Inflation breaches Tolerance Band ( > 6% or < 2%)
                                      │
                     FOR THREE CONSECUTIVE QUARTERS
                                      │
                                      ▼
                  MANDATORY REPORT TO CENTRAL GOVERNMENT
      ┌───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
      ▼                               ▼                               ▼
(a) Reasons for Failure     (b) Proposed Remedial Actions    (c) Estimated Timeline to
                                                                 Achieve 4% Target
```

- In Q1 (Jan-Mar 2022), Q2 (Apr-Jun 2022), and Q3 (Jul-Sep 2022), average CPI inflation in India remained above $6.0\%$ due to the Russia-Ukraine geopolitical supply shock and global commodity surge.
