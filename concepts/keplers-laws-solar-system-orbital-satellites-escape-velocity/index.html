Kepler's Laws, Solar System Architecture, Orbital Mechanics, Satellites & Escape Velocity

The laws governing planetary orbits, solar system composition, satellite orbital velocities, geostationary orbits, and the escape speed from gravitational wells.

Intuition & Context

How a Satellite Stays in the Sky Without Falling Down

Imagine firing a cannonball horizontally from the peak of a high mountain. Gravity curves it down to Earth. If you fire it faster, it lands farther away. If you fire it at exactly $7.9 , \text{km/s}$ ($28,000 , \text{km/h}$), the rate at which the cannonball falls towards Earth exactly matches the rate at which the Earth's spherical surface curves away beneath it! The cannonball is in perpetual free fall around Earth—it has become an artificial Satellite.

Kepler's Three Laws & Solar System Architecture

Kepler's Three Laws of Planetary Motion

Kepler's Law Formal Statement Physical / Mathematical Mechanism
First Law: Law of Orbits Every planet moves in an elliptical orbit with the Sun located at one of the two foci. Ellipse eccentricity (eee). Sun is at one focus; the other focus is empty space.
Second Law: Law of Areas The radius vector joining the planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time (dA/dt=constantdA/dt = \text{constant}dA/dt=constant). Direct consequence of Conservation of Angular Momentum (L=mvr=constL = m v r = \text{const}L=mvr=const). Planet moves fastest at Perihelion (closest to Sun) and slowest at Aphelion (farthest from Sun).
Third Law: Law of Periods The square of the orbital period (TTT) is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis (rrr): T2∝r3\mathbf{T^2 \propto r^3}T2∝r3. T2=(4π2GM)r3T^2 = \left(\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}\right) r^3T2=(GM4π2​)r3. Solar System Architecture & Key Astronomical Facts:

  • Inner Rocky / Terrestrial Planets: Mercury (smallest, no atmosphere), Venus ("Morning/Evening Star", hottest planet due to 96% CO296% , CO_296%CO2​ runaway greenhouse, rotates retrograde East-to-West), Earth, Mars ("Red Planet" due to iron oxide).
  • Asteroid Belt: Millions of rocky remnants orbiting between Mars and Jupiter.
  • Outer Gas Giants: Jupiter (largest planet, Great Red Spot storm, moons Ganymede/Europa), Saturn (spectacular ring system, Titan moon).
  • Ice Giants: Uranus (rotates on its side), Neptune (coldest, farthest).

3. Geostationary vs Polar Satellite Orbits

  • Geostationary Orbit (GEO):
    • Altitude: ≈35,786 km\mathbf{\approx 35,786 , \text{km}}≈35,786km (often rounded to $36,000 , \text{km}$).
    • Time Period: 24 Hours (equal to Earth's rotation period).
    • Direction: West to East (same as Earth rotation).
    • Position: Parked directly over the Equator.
    • Application: Satellite TV (INSAT), telecommunications, continuous weather monitoring.
  • Polar / Low Earth Orbit (LEO):
    • Altitude: $500 - 800 , \text{km}$; Time period: ≈90−100 minutes\approx 90 - 100 , \text{minutes}≈90−100minutes.
    • Orbits North-South over poles while Earth spins beneath.
    • Application: Earth Remote Sensing (IRS), military reconnaissance, mapping.

  4. Eclipses & Tides:
    • Solar Eclipse: Moon passes between Sun and Earth (New Moon day).

    • Spring Tides (Highest tides): Sun, Moon, and Earth in straight line (Syzygy: Full/New Moon).
    • Neap Tides (Lowest tides): Sun and Moon at right angles (90∘90^\circ90∘) relative to Earth (Quarter Moons).
