Agricultural Price Policy: Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP), Mandated Crops & FRP for Sugarcane

The statutory mechanisms, price determination rationale, and institutional framework of India's agricultural price support policy. Dissects the **Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)**: established in 1965 (originally as the Agricultural Prices Commission) as an attached office of the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare to recommend agricultural price policy. Analyzes the **Minimum Support Price (MSP)** architecture: announced by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) prior to each sowing season for **22 mandated crops** (7 cereals, 5 pulses, 7 oilseeds, and 3 commercial crops: cotton, raw jute, copra) plus the **Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP)** for sugarcane (governed by the Sugarcane [Control] Order, 1966 under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955). Contrasts statutory FRP with State Advised Prices (SAP) and analyzes the **PM-AASHA** (Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanraksan Abhiyan) umbrella scheme.

Intuition & Context

The Price Floor: Why the State Sets a Minimum Support Price

Agricultural markets suffer from extreme Cobweb Price Cycles (the "Cobweb Theorem"):\n- In Year 1, onion prices are high (₹60/kg). Farmers see high profits and plant massive onion acreage in Year 2.\n- In Year 2, a bumper harvest causes an extreme market glut. Prices collapse to ₹3/kg! Farmers suffer devastating losses and abandon onions in Year 3.\n- In Year 3, a massive shortage occurs, and prices skyrocket back to ₹80/kg!\n\nTo prevent this wild volatility from bankrupting farmers, the Government acts as a "Buyer of Last Resort" by setting a Minimum Support Price (MSP):\n- Before the seeds are even sown, the Government guarantees: "No matter how huge the harvest, the state will buy your crop at not less than this floor price."\n- This provides income insurance and encourages farmers to invest in crop production.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps\n- Trap 1: Who Approves MSP?: CACP recommends MSP, but the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) chaired by the Prime Minister approves and announces it.\n- Trap 2: Total Number of Mandated Crops: MSP is announced for 22 mandated crops plus the Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) for Sugarcane (Total = 22 + 1 = 23 crops).\n- Trap 3: Is MSP a Legal Right?: NO. Under current Indian law, MSP is an administrative executive policy, not a statutorily enforceable right under an Act of Parliament (unlike FRP for sugarcane, which is statutorily backed under the Essential Commodities Act).\n\n### Cross-Exam Focus\n- NABARD Grade A (ARD & ESI): CACP origin (1965, L.K.
