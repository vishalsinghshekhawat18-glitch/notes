11:T561,To master BoP accounting, one must understand two core rules:

### 1. The Credit vs Debit Rule
| Entry Type | Economic Event | Sign | Concrete Real-World Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **CREDIT (+)** | Inflow of foreign currency / Claims on foreigners | $+$ | • Merchandise Exports (textiles, pharmaceuticals)\n• Services Exports (software, BPO, tourism in India)\n• Inward Remittances from diaspora\n• Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) inflows\n• External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) raised abroad |
| **DEBIT (-)** | Outflow of foreign currency / Payments to foreigners | $-$ | • Merchandise Imports (crude oil, electronics, gold)\n• Services Imports (shipping, foreign consulting)\n• Indian tourists spending abroad\n• Outward FDI / Indian acquisitions of foreign companies\n• Repayment of foreign loan principal |

### 2. The Economic Principle of Residency
BoP measures transactions based on **Economic Residency**, NOT nationality or citizenship!
- **Resident**: Any individual, enterprise, or institution whose center of predominant economic interest lies within the domestic territory of a country (typically residing/operating for 1 year or more).
- *Example*: A US citizen working and living in Bengaluru for 3 years is an **Indian Resident** for BoP purposes. A transaction between him and a US firm is a cross-border BoP transaction.12:T63c,Under the IMF BPM6 standard adopted by the Reserve Bank of India, BoP is divided into three distinct accounts:


### The Universal Accounting Identity
$$\text{Current Account Balance (CAB)} + \text{Capital Account Balance (KAB)} - \text{Net Financial Account (FA)} + \text{Errors \& Omissions (EO)} = 0$$
*Note on Presentation*: Under modern BPM6, the Financial Account records net acquisition of financial assets minus net incurrence of liabilities. In India's traditional RBI format, the Capital Account and Financial Account are traditionally grouped together under the broad header **"Capital Account"**.13:T493,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: BoP Deficit vs BoP Accounting Identity**: "BoP is always in balance, so a country can never have a BoP crisis." -> *False*: BoP always balances as an *accounting identity* because central bank reserve depletion or emergency IMF loans enter as balancing items. An "economic BoP deficit" means autonomous debits exceed autonomous credits, forcing the RBI to drain foreign exchange reserves.
- **Trap 2: Residency vs Citizenship**: Foreign embassies located in New Delhi are treated as **non-resident extraterritorial entities**. When the US Embassy pays an Indian security guard, it is an export of services (Credit item) in India's BoP.
- **Trap 3: Errors and Omissions (E&O)**: Because BoP data is collected from thousands of disparate banking and customs sources, credits never exactly match debits in practice. E&O is the statistical balancing plug.
