| Process / Destination | Units Lost to Space | Units Absorbed by Atmosphere | Units Absorbed by Earth Surface |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Planetary Albedo (Reflected to Space)** | **35 units** (27 clouds + 6 scattering + 2 ground) | 0 | 0 |

### Temperature Inversion: Normal vs. Inverted Gradient
- **Normal Environmental Lapse Rate (ELR):** Temperature drops at $6.5^\circ\text{C}$ per $1,000\text{ m}$ ($1^\circ\text{C}$ per $165\text{ m}$). Warm air near surface, cold air aloft.
- **Temperature Inversion:** Warm air overlies cold air ($dT/dz > 0$).
  * *Radiation Inversion (Ground/Surface):* Rapid terrestrial radiative cooling on cold, clear, calm winter nights.
  * *Air Drainage Inversion (Valley Inversion):* Dense cold air rolls down mountain slopes under gravity and pools in the valley floor, leaving hillsides warmer (used for apple orchards and coffee plantations).

### Geodesy, Earth Motions & Orbital Mechanics (NCERT Benchmarks)

#### 1. Planetary Graticule: Latitudes & Thermal Zones
- **Equator ($0^\circ$):** Largest parallel circle; divides Earth into Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
- **Tropic of Cancer ($23^\circ 30' \text{N}$)** and **Tropic of Capricorn ($23^\circ 30' \text{S}$):** Delimit the **Torrid Zone** (receives maximum insolation where midday sun is overhead at least once a year).
- **Arctic Circle ($66^\circ 30' \text{N}$)** and **Antarctic Circle ($66^\circ 30' \text{S}$):** Delimit the **Temperate Zones** ($23.5^\circ - 66.5^\circ$, moderate angle of sun rays) and **Frigid Zones** (beyond $66.5^\circ$, sun never rises far above horizon; extreme cold).

#### 2. Earth Motions: Rotation, Revolution & Solstices
- **Axial Inclination:** Earth's axis is tilted $23^\circ 30'$ from the vertical perpendicular, making an angle of $66^\circ 30'$ with its orbital plane.
- **Circle of Illumination:** The great circle that divides day from night across the globe. *Crucial trap: It does NOT coincide with Earth's axis due to the $23.5^\circ$ tilt.*
- **Summer Solstice (June 21):** Northern Hemisphere tilted toward Sun. Direct vertical rays hit Tropic of Cancer ($23.5^\circ \text{N}$). Longest day & shortest night in Northern Hemisphere; 24-hour continuous daylight beyond Arctic Circle.
- **Winter Solstice (December 22):** Southern Hemisphere tilted toward Sun. Direct vertical rays hit Tropic of Capricorn ($23.5^\circ \text{S}$). Longest day in Southern Hemisphere; continuous 24-hour night beyond Arctic Circle.
- **Equinoxes (March 21 & September 23):** Direct vertical rays hit Equator ($0^\circ$). Equal day and night ($12\text{h} / 12\text{h}$) across the entire planet. Neither pole tilted toward Sun.

#### 3. Longitudes & Local Time Calculation Formula
- Earth rotates $360^\circ$ in 24 hours $\to 15^\circ \text{ longitude} = 1 \text{ hour} \to 1^\circ \text{ longitude} = 4 \text{ minutes}$.
- **Prime Meridian ($0^\circ$ at Greenwich, UK):** Baseline for Universal Time Coordinated (UTC / GMT). Places east are ahead ($+\text{time}$); places west are behind ($-\text{time}$).
- **Indian Standard Time (IST):** Fixed along standard meridian $82^\circ 30' \text{E}$ (passing through Mirzapur / Prayagraj, UP):
  $$82.5^\circ \times 4\text{ min} = 330\text{ minutes} = +5\text{ hours } 30\text{ minutes ahead of GMT (UTC+5:30)}.$$
- **Longitudinal Span of India:** From $68^\circ 7' \text{E}$ (Gujarat) to $97^\circ 25' \text{E}$ (Arunachal Pradesh) $\approx 29^\circ \approx 30^\circ$, creating an approximate **2-hour local sunrise time difference** ($30^\circ \times 4\text{ min} \approx 120\text{ min}$) between Arunachal Pradesh and Gujarat.
- **International Date Line ($180^\circ$):** Zigzags through Bering Strait and Pacific island groups to avoid splitting calendar dates. Crossing westward advances 1 calendar day; crossing eastward steps back 1 calendar day.13:T440,### Governing Radiative Physics
1. **Wien's Displacement Law:**
   $$\lambda_{max} = \frac{b}{T}$$
   Solar surface temperature ($T \approx 5778\text{ K}$) emits peak radiation in the visible spectrum ($\lambda_{max} \approx 0.5\ \mu\text{m}$, shortwave). Earth surface ($T \approx 288\text{ K}$) emits peak radiation in the thermal infrared spectrum ($\lambda_{max} \approx 10\ \mu\text{m}$, longwave).
2. **Stefan-Boltzmann Law:**
   $$E = \sigma T^4$$
   Radiation emitted increases with the 4th power of absolute temperature.
3. **Atmospheric Window:** Wavelengths between $8\ \mu\text{m}$ and $12\ \mu\text{m}$ pass through atmosphere with minimal absorption by greenhouse gases, allowing direct nocturnal radiation to space.

### Pollution Trapping in Radiation Inversions
During winter high-pressure anticyclones, calm air and radiative cooling form a stable shallow inversion layer. Convective thermals cannot breach the warm cap, trapping particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}, PM_{10}, SO_2, NO_x$) near the ground to form dense winter smog (common in the Indo-Gangetic Plains / Delhi).14:T47b,### Frequent Examiner Traps
1. **Albedo Numbers Trap:** Planetary albedo is **35%** (not 51%). Clouds account for the largest share (**27 units**), atmospheric scattering **6 units**, and ground ice/snow **2 units**.
2. **Atmosphere Heating Medium:** The atmosphere is heated predominantly from **BELOW by longwave terrestrial radiation**, NOT directly from above by incoming shortwave solar rays (atmosphere absorbs only 14% of solar radiation directly).
3. **Valley Inversion Agricultural Siting:** Fruit orchards (apples in Himachal Pradesh, coffee in Brazil) are planted on **valley slopes/hillsides**, NOT on valley floors, to escape freezing frost damage caused by nocturnal cold air drainage.
4. **Inversion Conditions:** Cloudy skies prevent inversion because clouds absorb and re-radiate terrestrial heat back to Earth. Inversion strictly requires **CLEAR skies** for unhindered radiative cooling.
