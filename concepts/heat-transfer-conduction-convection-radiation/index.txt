11:T40f,### The Three Heat Transfer Modes Compared

| Feature | Conduction | Convection | Radiation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Medium Requirement** | **Mandatory solid/dense medium**. | **Mandatory fluid (liquid or gas)**. | **NO medium required** (propagates in vacuum). |
| **Material Transport?** | **NO bulk motion** (vibrations only).| **YES, actual bulk mass movement**. | **NO material motion** (EM photon waves). |
| **Speed of Propagation**| Slowest mode. | Moderate speed. | **Fastest mode** (Speed of light: $3 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s}$). |
| **Direction of Flow** | Any direction along temperature gradient.| Primarily **vertically upward** (buoyancy).| Straight lines in all directions ($360^\circ$). |
| **Master Examples** | • Metal cooking pan handle heating up.<br>• Woollen clothes trapping air insulators. | • Sea Breeze and Land Breeze.<br>• Water boiling in a kettle.<br>• Chimneys and room heaters. | • Sunlight warming the Earth across vacuum space.<br>• Heat felt near a campfire or room radiator. |12:T437,### 1. Sea Breeze vs Land Breeze Mechanics
- **Sea Breeze (Occurs during DAYTIME)**:
  - Land heats up much faster than sea water (water has extreme high specific heat).
  - Hot air over land expands, becomes less dense, and **rises** (convection low-pressure zone).
  - Cooler, denser air from the sea rushes inland to replace it $\implies$ **Sea Breeze blows from Sea to Land during Day**.
- **Land Breeze (Occurs at NIGHT)**:
  - Land cools down much faster than sea water.
  - Air over warmer sea is now warmer and rises.
  - Cooler air from land rushes out toward the sea $\implies$ **Land Breeze blows from Land to Sea at Night**.

### 2. The Vacuum Thermos Flask (Defeating All 3 Heat Losses):
- **To Stop Conduction & Convection**: Double-walled glass bottle with a **vacuum space** sealed between the walls (no molecules to conduct or convect).
- **To Stop Radiation**: Inner walls are **silver-coated (mirrored)** to reflect outgoing radiant heat back inside and reflect external radiation away.
- **To Stop Conduction through Cap**: Insulating plastic or cork
