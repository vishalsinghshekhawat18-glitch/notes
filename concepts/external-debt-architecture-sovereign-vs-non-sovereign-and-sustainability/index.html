External Debt Architecture: Sovereign vs Non-Sovereign Debt, Currency Mismatch & Debt Sustainability Metrics

The structural taxonomy, maturity profiling, and risk diagnostic framework of national external debt. Explains the critical distinction between Sovereign External Debt (government borrowings from multilateral institutions, bilateral donors, and sovereign bonds) and Non-Sovereign External Debt (commercial ECBs, NRI deposits, and trade credits raised by private Indian enterprises). Evaluates key vulnerability metrics: Debt-to-GDP ratio (~18%–20%), Debt Service Ratio (~5%–7%), Short-term Debt to Total Debt (~18%–20%), and Forex Reserves to External Debt cover (~95%–105%). Analyzes the "Original Sin" of borrowing in foreign currency and balance-sheet currency mismatch.

Intuition & Context

The Currency Trap: Why Borrowing in Dollars Destroys Weak Companies

Imagine an Indian airline borrows US \USD 100 Millionillion from an American bank at a cheap 4%4%4% interest rate to buy aircraft. At the time of borrowing, the exchange rate is ₹70 / USD. The airline owes ₹700 Crore.

Two years later, global financial markets panic, and the Rupee depreciates to ₹85 / USD.

What happened to the airline's debt?

  • The airline still owes US \USD 100 Millionillion to the US bank.
  • But in Indian Rupees, its debt has automatically exploded from ₹700 Crore to ₹850 Crore—an instant loss of ₹150 Crore, even though the airline did nothing wrong!
  • Worse, the airline sells its flight tickets in Indian Rupees, but must repay the loan in US Dollars.

This deadly trap is called Currency Mismatch (or "Original Sin"): earning revenue in domestic currency while owing debt in foreign currency. If unhedged, currency depreciation can bankrupt even profitable domestic firms.

Critical Structural Parameters of India's External Debt

  1. Currency Composition: US Dollar-denominated debt is the largest (sim54\sim 54\% - 56\%sim54), followed by Indian Rupee-denominated debt (sim29\sim 29\% - 31\%sim29), SDRs (sim5\sim 5\% - 6\%sim5), Yen, and Euro.
    • Advantage of Rupee Debt: Foreign holding of Indian G-Secs/Bonds is Rupee-denominated; the foreign investor bears the exchange rate risk, shielding India from currency mismatch!
  2. Maturity Profile: Long-term debt (original maturity >1> 1>1 year) comprises sim80\sim 80\% - 82\%sim80; Short-term debt by original maturity is sim18\sim 18\% - 20\%sim18.
  3. Debt-to-GDP Ratio: India's external debt-to-GDP ratio is sim18.5\sim 18.5\% - 19.5\%sim18.5, among the lowest across G20 and emerging market peers.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Public Debt vs External Debt: Examiners often conflate the two. Public Debt is total debt owed by the government (domestic + external, sim82\sim 82\%sim82 of GDP, mostly domestic). External Debt is debt owed to non-residents by everyone (government + private corporates, sim19\sim 19\%sim19 of GDP). Over 9595\%95 of India's government debt is domestic, NOT external!
  • Trap 2: Is Rupee Debt Owed to Foreigners External Debt?: YES. Under IMF definition, external debt is determined by the Residency of the creditor, not currency. If a foreign fund in London buys Rupee G-Secs, it is part of India's External Debt.
  • Trap 3: Largest Component of External Debt: Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) form the single largest component (sim32\sim 32\% - 34\%sim32), followed by NRI Deposits (sim23\sim 23\% - 24\%sim23).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Debt service ratio formula, currency composition percentages, ECB hedging mandates, and rollover risk.
  • UPSC APFC, SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Distinguishing sovereign from non-sovereign external debt and safe DSR thresholds.
