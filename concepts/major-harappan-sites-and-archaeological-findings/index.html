Major Harappan Archaeological Sites and Landmark Excavation Findings

Exhaustive site-by-site catalog of diagnostic artifacts, structures, and cultural discoveries across Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal, Kalibangan, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Banawali, and Chanhudaro.

Intuition & Context

Conceptual Framework: Reading the Material Record of the Indus Metropolis

Because the Indus script remains undeciphered, the entire political, spiritual, and technological story of the Harappans must be reconstructed from the material artifacts excavated by archaeologists over the past century.

Think of each major Harappan city as a specialized regional capital with its own diagnostic signature:

  • Mohenjo-daro was the artistic and monumental hub (Bronze Dancing Girl, Priest-King, Pashupati seal, Great Bath).
  • Harappa was the agricultural and mortuary capital (R-37 cemetery, 12-unit granaries, red sandstone torso).
  • Lothal was the international maritime gateway (Tidal dockyard, Persian Gulf seal, rice husks, double burials).
  • Kalibangan was the agro-ritual center of the Sarasvati basin (Criss-cross ploughed field, row of fire altars, camel bones).
  • Dholavira was the stone-fortified water metropolis (10-symbol signboard, stadium, massive stone reservoirs).
  • Rakhigarhi is the largest urban site in India (DNA breakthroughs, extensive cemetery, granary).
  • Chanhudaro was the industrial manufacturing hub (Bead factory, inkpot, bronze toy cart, lipsticks, zero citadel).

How It Works / Mechanism

Spatial Specialization and Inter-Site Material Exchange Workflows

The diversity of finds reflects a coordinated division of labor and craft specialization across the Harappan civilization:

[Raw Material Hubs: Nageshwar (Shells), Ganeshwar (Copper), Badakhshan (Lapis)]
  --> [Industrial Processing Centers: Chanhudaro & Lothal (Beads, Cast Metals)]
  --> [Metropolitan Commercial Markets: Mohenjo-daro & Harappa]
  --> [Agricultural Producers: Kalibangan (Mustard/Gram), Banawali (Barley)]
  --> [Export Ports: Lothal, Sutkagendor, Balakot -> Mesopotamian Trade]

Specialized Production Centers:

  • Bead-Making Technology: Chanhudaro and Lothal possessed specialized furnaces to heat carnelian stones to achieve deep red tones, drilling them using micro-drills made of hardened ernestite (a dense chert drill).
  • Shell-Working Industry: Coastal stations at Balakot and Nageshwar exclusively harvested marine conch shells to mass-produce bangles, ladles, and inlays traded thousands of miles inland to Harappa and Mohenjo-daro.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, High-Yield Site Mappings, and Diagnostic Findings

Critical Examiner Traps:

  • Trap 1 (Ploughed Field vs Terracotta Plough):
    • The ploughed field surface with criss-cross furrows was discovered at Kalibangan (Rajasthan).
    • The complete terracotta toy model of a plough was discovered at Banawali (Haryana). Do NOT interchange them!
  • Trap 2 (Rice Remains): Evidence of rice husks / impressions was found at Lothal and Rangpur in Gujarat. Harappan core agriculture was based on wheat and barley, making rice a regional Gujarat exception.
  • Trap 3 (The Signboard): The famous 10-symbol giant signboard was found at Dholavira (Gujarat), NOT Mohenjo-daro.
  • Trap 4 (Absence of Grid Pattern): If asked which Mature Harappan site lacked a strict grid pattern and featured radial streets, the answer is Banawali (Haryana).
  • Trap 5 (Horse Remains at Surkotada): Surkotada in Gujarat yielded purported horse bones/teeth, but archaeologists (like B.B. Lal and Richard Meadow) debate whether they belong to the domesticated true horse (Equus caballus) or the wild ass (Equus hemionus khur). Harappan culture was fundamentally NOT a horse-centered civilization.
