Rajasthan Socio-Demographic Profile: Census 2011 Baseline, NFHS-5 Health Dynamics & Gender Disparity Economics

The empirical analysis, spatial variations, and demographic indicators of human development in Rajasthan. Analyzes: (1) Census 2011 Demographic Baseline (Population 6.85 Crore, Decadal Growth Rate 21.44%, Sex Ratio 928 females/1000 males, Child Sex Ratio [0-6] 888, Overall Literacy Rate 66.11% with Male Literacy 79.19% vs Female Literacy 52.12% generating a severe 27.07% Gender Literacy Gap), (2) NFHS-5 (2019-21) Vital Health Trends (Total Fertility Rate [TFR] = 1.9, Infant Mortality Rate [IMR] = 38/1000 live births, Institutional Deliveries = 84.3%, Child Nutrition: Stunting 31.8%, Wasting 19.7%, Underweight 29.4%), and (3) Gender Economics & Institutional Initiatives (Beti Bachao Beti Padhao pilot in Jhunjhunu, Indira Gandhi Matritva Poshan Yojana, and Udan Scheme for menstrual hygiene).

Intuition & Context

The Jhunjhunu Turnaround: Transforming Gender Economics in the Desert

In 2011, the desert district of Jhunjhunu in northern Rajasthan was in the headlines for a tragic reason: it had one of the worst Child Sex Ratios in India (just 837 girls per 1,000 boys).

Why were daughters missing in a state with rich cultural heritage?

  • Deep-rooted patriarchal succession preferences.
  • Misuse of portable ultrasound machines for illegal sex determination.
  • Massive female literacy deficits (rural female literacy in southern Rajasthan was below 40%).

Between 2015 and 2020, Jhunjhunu became the national testbed for Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (BBBP):

  1. District officials installed active GPS and digital tracking on every single ultrasound machine.
  2. Informant decoys and monetary rewards caught corrupt ultrasound clinics.
  3. The district celebrated the birth of baby girls by planting trees and naming village gates after high-scoring schoolgirls.
  4. By 2018, Jhunjhunu's Sex Ratio at Birth jumped to over 950 girls per 1,000 boys, earning national recognition from Prime Minister Narendra Modi in March 2018 when the nationwide expansion of BBBP was launched right from Jhunjhunu.

Rajasthan's social journey proves that human development is not just about building clinics; it requires changing deep cultural mindsets.

How It Works / Mechanism

Institutional Gender & Maternal Welfare Programs in Rajasthan

1. The Indira Gandhi Matritva Poshan Yojana (IGMPY)

  • Pioneering Mechanism: While Central PM Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY) historically provided cash for the first child, Rajasthan launched IGMPY to provide ₹6,000 cash in 5 installments to pregnant and lactating mothers for the second child.
  • Objective: Prevent low birth weight, combat high child stunting/wasting, and disincentivize gender-biased female foeticide for second pregnancies.

2. The i-Start and Udan Menstrual Hygiene Scheme

  • Udan Scheme (Free Sanitary Napkins): Universal distribution of free sanitary napkins to adolescent girls and women (ages 10–45) through schools, Anganwadi centers, and public health networks, removing a primary cause of female adolescent school dropouts.
  • Jan Aadhar Integrated Card: The Jan Aadhar card designates the senior-most female of the household (18+ years) as the official Head of the Family, routing all state cash and non-cash subsidies into her personal bank account, empowering female intra-household financial agency.

Examiner Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Highest vs Lowest Sex Ratio Districts in Census 2011:
    • Overall Sex Ratio Highest: Dungarpur (994) (NOT Jaipur or Udaipur).
    • Overall Sex Ratio Lowest: Dholpur (846).
    • Child Sex Ratio (0-6) Highest: Banswara (934) (Tribal district with egalitarian gender norms).
    • Child Sex Ratio Lowest: Jhunjhunu (837).
  • Trap 2: Highest vs Lowest Literacy Districts:
    • Highest Literacy (Overall & Female): Kota (76.6% / 65.9%).
    • Lowest Literacy (Overall & Female): Jalore (54.9% / 38.5%).
    • Highest Male Literacy: Jhunjhunu (86.9%).
    • Lowest Male Literacy: Pratapgarh (69.5%) and Banswara.
  • Trap 3: Decadal Population Growth Champion: The district with the highest population growth rate (2001-2011) was Barmer (32.5%), NOT Jaisalmer (31.8%). The lowest was Ganganagar (10.0%).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RPSC RAS (Mains Paper 1 & Prelims): Mandatory core topic. 2-mark, 5-mark, and 10-mark questions on Rajasthan Census 2011 exact percentages, NFHS-5 health indicators, gender literacy gaps, and district rankings.
  • UPSC APFC / Civil Services: Tribal demographic indicators in southern Rajasthan, sex ratio at birth turnaround, and maternal nutrition programs.
  • NABARD Grade A (ESI & ARD): Rural literacy, female labor participation, and rural health metrics in Rajasthan.
  • SBI / IBPS PO: Basic Rajasthan demographic stats and state welfare models.
