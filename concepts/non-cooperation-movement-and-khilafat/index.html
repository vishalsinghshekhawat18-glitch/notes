The Gandhian Mass Movements (1917–1942) · Concept 2 of 4

The First Pan-Indian Mass Upsurge: Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922), The Khilafat Alliance & Chauri Chaura

The unprecedented forging of Hindu-Muslim unity and mass mobilization against colonial rule: The Khilafat Question (dismemberment of Ottoman Caliphate by Treaty of Sevres, Ali Brothers - Shaukat Ali and Mohammad Ali, Maulana Azad); Special Calcutta Session (Sept 1920) and Nagpur Session (Dec 1920, C. Vijayaraghavachariar president: Congress goal transformed to Swaraj through peaceful means, linguistic provincial congress committees, ₹1 crore Tilak Swaraj Fund); Programme of Non-Cooperation (surrender of titles, boycott of government schools, law courts, foreign cloth, picketing of liquor shops); massive participation of students, women, and peasants (Eka and Awadh Kisan Sabhas); Baba Ramchandra; and the abrupt withdrawal of the movement following the Chauri Chaura incident (Feb 5, 1922, 22 policemen burnt alive in Gorakhpur), leading to the Bardoli Resolution and Gandhi's imprisonment.

Intuition & Context

The Convergence of Two Torrents: Unprecedented Hindu-Muslim Solidarity

How did Mahatma Gandhi transform the Indian National Congress from an elite, English-speaking debating society into a roaring, pan-Indian mass movement that terrified the British Empire in 1920?

The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922) was made possible by the confluence of three distinct rivers of anti-colonial fury:

  1. The Punjab Wrongs: Nationwide outrage over the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and the whitewashing Hunter Commission report.
  2. The Constitutional Betrayal: Deep dissatisfaction with the hollow Government of India Act 1919 and post-war economic misery (hyper-inflation, plague, crop failure).
  3. The Khilafat Question: The humiliation of the Ottoman Sultan (who was revered as the Khalifa or spiritual head of Sunni Muslims worldwide) following the dismemberment of Turkey by the Allies through the Treaty of Sèvres (1920).

Gandhi recognized this as a once-in-a-century historical opportunity to fuse Hindu and Muslim aspirations into an indomitable anti-imperialist front: "An opportunity of uniting Hindus and Muslims as would not arise in a hundred years". By bringing the entire organizational weight of the Congress behind the Khilafat Committee, Gandhi forged the first truly pan-Indian mass upsurge across religion, region, and class.

Core Principle

The Architecture of Non-Cooperation: From Nagpur Resolutions to Bardoli Retreat

1. The Twin Congress Sessions of 1920

  • Special Calcutta Session (September 1920):
    • Presided over by Lala Lajpat Rai.
    • Gandhi moved the historic Non-Cooperation resolution. Despite initial opposition from stalwarts like C.R. Das, Bipin Chandra Pal, and Annie Besant (who feared council boycotts and radicalization), the resolution was passed with an overwhelming majority.
  • Regular Nagpur Session (December 1920) — The Watershed Moment:
    • Presided over by C. Vijayaraghavachariar.
    • C.R. Das dramatically dropped his opposition and himself moved the main Non-Cooperation resolution!
    • Radical Structural Transformation of Congress:
      1. Goal Redefined: The objective of the Congress was altered from self-government within the British Empire through constitutional means to "the attainment of Swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means".
      2. Working Committee Established: Created a 15-member Congress Working Committee (CWC) to act as a permanent, year-round executive steering body.
      3. Linguistic Provincial Committees: Reorganized Provincial Congress Committees (PCCs) on a linguistic basis (demolishing colonial administrative boundaries and taking politics to the regional vernacular).
      4. Mass Membership: Reduced annual membership fee to 4 annas (25 paise), opening Congress doors to peasants, artisans, and industrial workers.
      5. Tilak Swaraj Fund: Announced following the demise of Bal Gangadhar Tilak (died August 1, 1920, the day NCM formally began); aimed to collect ₹1 Crore to finance the movement, which was oversubscribed within months!

2. The Comprehensive Programme: Boycott & Constructive Work

Aspect Boycott / Negative Programme ("Destructive Work") Constructive / Positive Programme ("Swadeshi Nation-Building")
Titles & Honours Surrender of imperial titles, honours, and honorary magistrate posts (Gandhi surrendered Kaiser-i-Hind, Boer War, and Zulu War medals). Promotion of national self-respect and popular civic discipline (Satyagraha).
Education Boycott of government and aided schools and universities. Thousands of students walked out under Subhas Bose, Zakir Husain, and Lala Lajpat Rai. Establishment of National Educational Institutions: Jamia Millia Islamia (Aligarh, later Delhi), Kashi Vidyapeeth, Gujarat Vidyapith, Bihar Vidyapeeth, Bengal National University.
Judiciary Boycott of British law courts by lawyers and litigants. Eminent lawyers surrendered fabulously lucrative practices: Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Saifuddin Kitchlew. Setting up of informal Panchayats and Arbitration Boards to resolve civil and criminal disputes outside British courts.
Economic & Material • Boycott of foreign cloth and imported consumer goods.
• Public bonfires of foreign textiles across Indian cities.
• Picketing of government liquor shops and toddy stores (cutting colonial excise revenues).		 • Universal adoption of hand-spun and hand-woven Khadi.
• Introduction of the Charkha (spinning wheel) into every household as a symbol of economic self-sufficiency.
• Collection of funds for Tilak Swaraj Fund.
Social Reforms Boycott of legislative council elections under the Government of India Act 1919 (mass voter abstention). Eradication of Untouchability, upliftment of oppressed castes, and strict maintenance of communal Hindu-Muslim brotherhood.

3. Regional Outbursts & Subaltern Mobilization

The Non-Cooperation Movement was not merely an urban middle-class protest; it triggered volcanic subaltern peasant uprisings across the country:

  • Awadh Peasant Movement (UP): Led by Baba Ramchandra (a wandering sanyasi who carried the Ramcharitmanas); organized peasants against illegal cesses (Nazrana) and evictions (Bedakhali). Awadh Kisan Sabha formed in Oct 1920 with Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • Eka Movement (1921, UP): Led by Madari Pasi in Hardoi, Bahraich, and Sitapur; peasants pledged to pay only recorded rents and refuse forced labour (Begar).
  • Mappila (Moplah) Rebellion (1921, Malabar, Kerala): Muslim tenant cultivators rose in armed rebellion against oppressive upper-caste Hindu landlords (Janmis) and British police. Initially aligned with Khilafat, it unfortunately acquired tragic communal overtones.
  • Assam & Bengal Strikes: Massive tea-garden coolie walkouts in Assam and the legendary strike on the Assam-Bengal Railway and river steamer services organized under the leadership of J.M. Sengupta.
  • Akali Movement (Punjab): Peaceful agitation to liberate historical Sikh Gurudwaras from corrupt, pro-British hereditary priests (Mahants), leading to the Sikh Gurdwaras Act 1925.

4. Chauri Chaura & The Abrupt Retreat (February 1922)

  • The Incident (February 4/5, 1922):
    • At Chauri Chaura, a village in Gorakhpur district, United Provinces (UP), police fired upon a peaceful procession of Congress-Khilafat volunteers protesting liquor sales and food prices.
    • An enraged crowd of peasants retaliated, chased the police into the police station, locked the doors, and set it ablaze. 22 policemen were burnt alive.
  • The Bardoli Resolution (February 12, 1922):
    • Horrified by the eruption of violence, Gandhi immediately summoned the Congress Working Committee at Bardoli and unilaterally called off the entire Non-Cooperation Movement.
    • Gandhi maintained that Indians were not yet spiritually trained in non-violence, and that allowing the movement to become violent would give the British armed state the moral and military justification to crush it with artillery.
  • National Shock and Division:
    • Nationalist leaders were devastated. Subhas Chandra Bose called the withdrawal a "national calamity". Jawaharlal Nehru and Motilal Nehru expressed deep dismay from prison.
    • Taking advantage of the retreat, the British government arrested Gandhi on March 10, 1922. In a historic trial, Judge C.N. Broomfield sentenced him to 6 years imprisonment.
    • The collapse of NCM fractured the Hindu-Muslim alliance, led to the disintegration of the Khilafat movement (especially after Mustafa Kemal Atatürk abolished the Ottoman Caliphate entirely in 1924), and spawned the formation of the Swaraj Party in 1923 by C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru.

The Sociological Mechanism of Non-Cooperation: Demystifying Colonial Invincibility

How Non-Cooperation Pierced the "Colonial Consent"

Colonial rule never depended solely on British bayonets; it rested upon the psychological cooperation and perceived indispensability of the Raj in the minds of the colonized:

Colonial Power=Military CoercionVoluntary Native Compliance\text{Colonial Power} = \frac{\text{Military Coercion}}{\text{Voluntary Native Compliance}}Colonial Power=Voluntary Native ComplianceMilitary Coercion​

  1. The Principle of Withdrawing Consent:

    • In Hind Swaraj (1909), Gandhi wrote: "The English have not taken India; we have given it to them. They are not in India because of their strength, but because we keep them. If we cease to cooperate, British rule will collapse in a year."
    • By refusing to attend courts, schools, council chambers, and revenue offices, Non-Cooperation demonstrated in practice that the British Raj was an illusion maintained by Indian collaboration.

  2. The Eradication of Fear (Nirbhayata):

    • As Jawaharlal Nehru remarked in The Discovery of India, the supreme gift of Gandhi in 1920 was fearlessness. Going to jail (Jail Bharo), previously considered a catastrophic social stigma, was transformed into an exalted badge of patriotic honour.

Spatial & Strategic Dynamics:

  1. The Congress-Khilafat Coalition: Uniting nationalist agitation with the Pan-Islamic Khilafat issue created unprecedented Hindu-Muslim fraternal unity across North and South India.
  2. Grassroots Peasant Autonomy: Rural uprisings (UP Kisan Sabhas, Rampa forest rebellion, Moplah revolt) often broke past Gandhi's strictly non-violent strictures, transforming the movement into an agrarian revolution against zamindars.
  3. The Chauri Chaura Crossroads: By abruptly suspending the movement after the burning of the Gorakhpur police station, Gandhi preserved his absolute commitment to satyagraha while frustrating radical youth and triggering the formation of the Swaraj Party.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

High-Frequency Traps, Chronological Sequences & Examiner Pitfalls

⚠️ Top 5 High-Frequency Traps for UPSC & State PCS

  1. The 1920 Sessions Presidency Trap:

    • Special Calcutta Session (Sept 1920): Presided over by Lala Lajpat Rai (passed NCM resolution).
    • Nagpur Regular Session (Dec 1920): Presided over by C. Vijayaraghavachariar (ratified NCM and transformed Congress constitution).
    • Trap: Thinking Gandhi or Motilal Nehru presided over the 1920 Nagpur session!

  2. The Chauri Chaura Date and Location:

    • Date: February 4/5, 1922 (UP state records observe Feb 4; historical texts cite Feb 5).
    • Location: Gorakhpur District, United Provinces (UP), NOT Bihar or Bengal.
    • Withdrawal Site: Formally called off by the CWC meeting at Bardoli (Gujarat) on February 12, 1922 (hence known as the Bardoli Resolution).

  3. Tilak's Role in NCM:

    • Trap: Believing Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak led or opposed the Non-Cooperation Movement in action.
    • Fact: Tilak passed away on August 1, 1920, the very morning the movement was formally launched. The Tilak Swaraj Fund was established in his honour.

  4. Peasant Leadership Attribution:

    • Awadh Kisan Sabha (1920): Led by Baba Ramchandra along with Jawaharlal Nehru.
    • Eka Movement (1921): Led by Madari Pasi in Hardoi, Bahraich, and Sitapur.
    • Trap: Conflating Baba Ramchandra with the Eka movement.
