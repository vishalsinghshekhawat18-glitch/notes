11:T408,### 1. Mathematical Proof of Momentum Conservation
Consider two colliding balls $A$ and $B$ (masses $m_1, m_2$) with initial velocities $u_1, u_2$ colliding for time $t$:
- Force on $A$ by $B$: $F_{AB} = m_1 \left(\frac{v_1 - u_1}{t}\right)$
- Force on $B$ by $A$: $F_{BA} = m_2 \left(\frac{v_2 - u_2}{t}\right)$
- By Newton's Third Law ($F_{AB} = -F_{BA}$):
$$m_1 \left(\frac{v_1 - u_1}{t}\right) = -m_2 \left(\frac{v_2 - u_2}{t}\right)$$
$$m_1 v_1 - m_1 u_1 = -(m_2 v_2 - m_2 u_2)$$
$$m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 \quad \implies \quad \vec{P}_{\text{initial}} = \vec{P}_{\text{final}}$$

### 2. Recoil Velocity of a Gun
Before firing, total momentum $= 0$. After firing:
$$P_{\text{final}} = (M_{\text{gun}} \cdot V_{\text{recoil}}) + (m_{\text{bullet}} \cdot v_{\text{bullet}}) = 0$$
$$V_{\text{recoil}} = -\frac{m_{\text{bullet}} \cdot v_{\text{bullet}}}{M_{\text{gun}}}$$
*(Negative sign indicates recoil is backward. Because $M_{\text{gun}} \gg m_{\text{bullet}}$, recoil velocity is much smaller than bullet
