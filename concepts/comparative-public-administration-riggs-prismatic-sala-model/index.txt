12:T4fb,To grasp Fred Riggs' Prismatic-Sala model, observe a Tehsil office in rural Jaisalmer or Barmer.

On the wall hangs a state-of-the-art **Apna Khata / E-Mitra digital portal** and citizen's charter guaranteeing digitised land mutation within 7 days. This is Western, diffracted administrative law.

Yet when an illiterate villager approaches the counter, the formal digital token is ignored. The matter moves only when the villager approaches a local *mukhiya* (village elder) who belongs to the same caste *biradari* as the revenue inspector. The patwari accepts tea, discusses village kinship, and then processes the file. This embodies Riggs' core concepts:
1. **Formalism:** A complete gap between the legal rule on paper (statutory 7-day e-delivery) and ground reality (kinship intervention).
2. **Overlapping:** The modern computer terminal physically coexists with medieval caste and patronage networks.
3. **Heterogeneity:** An Indian Administrative Service (IAS) officer holding an Oxford degree works in the same building where the water-bearer is bound by semi-feudal deference.
4. **Sala Bureaucracy:** The office does not operate as an impersonal Weberian office; it functions as a **'Sala'**, where decisions are negotiated via familial loyalty and social power.13:Tc4b,Fred Riggs rejected the unilinear Western assumption that Anglo-American administrative models could be transplanted directly into post-colonial developing nations without analyzing their socio-cultural environment (**Ecology**).

### 1. The Evolutionary Continuum: Fused, Prismatic, and Diffracted
Riggs used the optical metaphor of a beam of white light passing through a prism:
- **Fused Society (White Light):** Undeveloped, traditional agrarian society (e.g., Imperial China, Siam, pre-modern feudal Rajputana). A single structure (such as the King or tribal council) performs all political, economic, religious, and judicial functions. Little or no division of labor.
- **Prismatic Society (Inside the Prism):** Transitional, developing society (e.g., post-colonial India, Southeast Asia). Modern specialized structures have been imported constitutionally, but traditional social habits persist, creating refraction and collision.
- **Diffracted Society (Dispersed Spectrum / Rainbow):** Advanced industrial society (e.g., USA, Western Europe). High degree of functional specialization where each structure performs a specific, differentiated function.

### 2. The Three Cardinal Prismatic Traits
1. **Heterogeneity:** The simultaneous coexistence of radically different viewpoints, technologies, and social practices. Modern multi-specialty hospitals and MRI machines coexist with village faith healers; bullet trains coexist with camel carts; high-tech civil servants work alongside caste-bound local functionaries.
2. **Formalism:** The degree of divergence between prescription and practice—between what is written in statutes and what actually happens. High formalism means laws and rules exist on paper, but officials routinely ignore them or apply them selectively.
3. **Overlapping:** The extent to which formally differentiated modern structures are permeated by traditional, undifferentiated structures. Differentiated bodies (parliaments, courts, civil service commissions) exist, but decisions are determined by caste (*biradari*), family, religion, and patronage.

### 3. Structural Comparison: Chamber, Sala, and Office

| Feature | Fused Society (Chamber) | Prismatic Society (Sala) | Diffracted Society (Office) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Social Stratification** | Ascriptive (Caste, hereditary status) | Poly-communalism / Clects (Competing religious/caste cliques) | Universalistic (Class, professional achievement) |
| **Administrative Bureau** | **Chamber** (Personal royal household) | **Sala** (Bureaucratic quarter influenced by patrimony) | **Office** (Rational, impersonal Weberian bureau) |
| **Recruitment Basis** | Pure family loyalty and royal favor | Mixed: Formal exams de jure, nepotism & bribery de facto | Strict meritocracy and open competitive examination |
| **Economic Arena** | Traditional Redistribution / Feudal tribute | **Bazaar-Canteen** (Price indeterminacy, subsidies for insiders) | Pure Market Mechanism (Supply and demand equilibrium) |
| **Normative Order** | Sacred Traditionalism | **Poly-normativism** (Clash between modern law and ancient custom) | Secular Legal-Rationalism |14:T5ca,Riggs demonstrated that in a Prismatic society, market forces do not operate freely. ### The Sala Feedback Loop of Inefficiency:
1. **Selectivism:** Officials enforce laws strictly against political opponents or poor citizens, but selectively exempt members of their own caste, family, or political patrons.
2. **Poly-normativism:** Officials and citizens experience intense cognitive dissonance because they must outwardly profess adherence to modern constitutional values while simultaneously fulfilling deep traditional kinship obligations.
3. **Administrative Ineffectiveness:** Because the bureaucracy is absorbed in self-preservation, patronage distribution, and status enhancement, actual developmental implementation stalls.15:T4bf,### High-Yield RPSC RAS Traps & Scoring Insights:
- **Formalism Definition (2 Marks):** In RPSC RAS, do not write 'Formalism means following too many formal procedures'. That is a fatal error (which defines Red Tape). **Formalism is the degree of discrepancy between formally enacted laws/rules on paper and actual administrative practices on the ground.**
- **Overlapping vs. Heterogeneity (2 Marks / 5 Marks):**
  * **Heterogeneity:** Coexistence of different systems and stages of development side-by-side (e.g., modern cities and backward tribal hamlets).
  * **Overlapping:** Traditional structures intruding into and operating through modern administrative forms (e.g., caste networks dictating transfer postings inside a government department).
- **Riggs' Subsystems (2 Marks):** Administrative bureau = **Sala**; Economic subsystem = **Bazaar-Canteen**; Social groups = **Clects** (clique + sect).
- **Critique of Riggs (5 Marks):** Riggs is heavily criticized for: (1) Excessive, opaque jargon (clects, polynormativism, sala); (2) A pessimistic, deterministic Western bias viewing developing nations as inherently flawed; (3) Overlooking positive developmental successes in Asian tiger economies and India.16:T494,### Architectural Breakdown: Fred Riggs' Prismatic-Sala Model
- **Ecological Approach:** Administrative behavior is shaped organically by environment (ecology) and vice versa.
- **Structural-Functional Typology:**
  * Fused (Chamber): Traditional agrarian, unspecialized structures performing multiple functions.
  * Prismatic (Sala): Transitional developing society; refracted, overlapping, and formalistic.
  * Diffracted (Office): Advanced industrial, functionally specific, meritocratic, and universalistic.
- **The Prismatic Triad:**
  1. *Heterogeneity:* Unequal simultaneous existence of modern and archaic systems.
  2. *Formalism:* Wide chasm between de jure constitutional prescriptions and de facto implementation.
  3. *Overlapping:* Modern administrative shells penetrated by traditional 'clects' (caste, kinship cliques).
- **The 'Sala' Administrative Subsystem:**
  * Poly-normativism: Coexistence of modern legal codes with traditional obligations.
  * Nepotism & Selectivism: Laws applied strictly to outsiders, waived for insiders.
