The Gandhian Mass Movements (1917–1942) · Concept 4 of 4

The August Kranti: The Quit India Movement (1942), Parallel Governments & Total Mass Mobilization

The climactic final mass rebellion of the Gandhian era: Failure of the Cripps Mission (March 1942, "post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"); Japanese advance to the borders of Assam; All-India Congress Committee (AICC) meeting at Gowalia Tank Ground, Bombay; adoption of the Quit India Resolution (August 8, 1942); Gandhi's immortal call to the nation: **"Do or Die" (*Karo ya Maro*)**; Operation Zero Hour (midnight Aug 8–9 arrest of entire top Congress leadership including Gandhi, Nehru, Patel); spontaneous leaderless mass eruption across India; underground resistance network (Aruna Asaf Ali hoisting the flag at Gowalia Tank, Ram Manohar Lohia, Jayaprakash Narayan escaping Hazaribagh jail, Usha Mehta running Congress Secret Underground Radio); establishment of **Parallel Governments (*Prati Sarkar*)**: Ballia (Chittu Pandey, first parallel govt), Tamluk (Jatiya Sarkar in Midnapore, Bengal, Matangini Hazra martyrdom), and Satara (Nana Patil, longest running Prati Sarkar); and violent British state repression involving machine-gun strafing from aircraft.

Intuition & Context

The August Eruption: From Disciplined Satyagraha to Uncompromising Open Rebellion

In August 1942, Mahatma Gandhi took a radical, unprecedented departure from his earlier philosophies of cautious, incremental mass mobilization. In 1922, he had abruptly halted the Non-Cooperation Movement over the solitary death of 22 policemen at Chauri Chaura. In 1930, he meticulously scripted every step of the Salt Satyagraha to maintain non-violent discipline.

Why, then, in August 1942, did Gandhi proclaim to the nation: "Leave India to God. If that is too much, then leave her to anarchy... I am not going to be satisfied with anything short of complete freedom. Here is a mantra, a short one, that I give you: Do or Die (Karo ya Maro). We shall either free India or die in the attempt"?

The catalysts for this historic transformation were urgent and existential:

  1. The Cripps Mission Debacle (March–April 1942): Sir Stafford Cripps brought an offer of post-war Dominion Status, constitutional provisions for provinces to secede, and zero immediate transfer of defence power. Gandhi famously dismissed it as a "post-dated cheque on a crashing bank". The failure proved British insincerity.
  2. The Looming Japanese Invasion: Imperial Japanese forces had swept through Southeast Asia, capturing Malaya, Singapore, and Burma (Rangoon fell in March 1942), reaching the borders of Assam. The British practiced a scorched-earth policy in coastal Bengal (confiscating country boats and rice supplies, paving the way for the horrific 1943 Bengal Famine). Gandhi recognized that British presence was an invitation for Japan to invade India.
  3. Total National Readiness: Popular frustration, war inflation, and soaring food shortages reached a boiling point. Gandhi understood that if the leadership did not channel this fury, the nation would sink into demoralized passive slavery.

When the British launched Operation Zero Hour before dawn on August 9, arresting the entire Congress Working Committee, they assumed the movement would die in the womb. Instead, without leaders, without instructions, and without a central high command, 300 million people rose in the fiercest open rebellion the British Raj had faced since 1857.

Core Principle

The August Kranti Matrix: Cripps Failure, Underground Networks & Parallel Governments

1. Key Triggers & The Gowalia Tank Declaration

  • Wardha Resolution (July 14, 1942): The Congress Working Committee met at Wardha and authorized Gandhi to take charge of a non-violent mass movement.
  • AICC Gowalia Tank Meeting (August 7–8, 1942): The All-India Congress Committee met at Gowalia Tank Ground (now August Kranti Maidan) in Bombay.
    • Quit India Resolution: Moved by Jawaharlal Nehru and seconded by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel. Passed on August 8, 1942.
    • The Call to Action: Gandhi delivered his historic 2-hour speech proclaiming "Karo ya Maro" (Do or Die). Specific instructions were issued:
      1. Government servants: Do not resign, but declare your allegiance to the Congress.
      2. Soldiers: Do not leave the army, but refuse to fire on your fellow countrymen.
      3. Students: Give up studies if you can maintain firmness until independence.
      4. Peasants: If zamindars are pro-government, pay no rent; if anti-government, pay agreed mutual rent.
      5. Princely States rulers: Accept the sovereignty of your own people, not the Paramount Power.
  • Operation Zero Hour (Pre-dawn, August 9, 1942):
    • British police arrested Gandhi (imprisoned at Aga Khan Palace, Poona alongside Kasturba Gandhi and Mahadev Desai, both of whom died there).
    • The entire Congress Working Committee (Nehru, Patel, Azad, Rajendra Prasad) was arrested and locked in Ahmednagar Fort and Bankipore jail (Patna).
    • Congress was declared an unlawful association, its funds frozen and offices seized.

2. The Three Parallel Governments (Prati Sarkars) of 1942

The institutional apex of the 1942 movement was the establishment of independent parallel administrations that overthrew colonial courts, police stations, and revenue collectors:

Parallel Government (Prati Sarkar) Region & Province Key Leaders & Historic Role Distinct Administrative Features
Ballia (First Parallel Govt) Ballia, Eastern United Provinces (UP) Chittu Pandey (the "Tiger of Ballia") Lasted for about a week in August 1942. Stormed the local jail, released all arrested Congressmen, and established an independent district people's administration before military reconquest.
Tamluk (Jatiya Sarkar) Tamluk, Midnapore district, Bengal Ajoy Mukherjee, Sushil Kumar Dhara, Satish Chandra Samanta Functioned from Dec 17, 1942 to Sept 1944. Maintained an armed volunteer corps called Vidyut Vahini (Lightning Corps), ran arbitration courts (Nyayadan Mandals), distributed relief during the 1943 famine. 73-year-old Matangini Hazra was shot dead by police while holding the Congress tricolour aloft.
Satara (Prati Sarkar) Satara district, Maharashtra Nana Patil ("Krantisinha Nana Patil") and Y.B. Chavan Longest-running parallel government (mid-1943 to 1945/46). Formed volunteer guerrilla bands (Toofan Sena). Ran village grain banks, banned moneylender debt contracts, and operated people's courts (Nyayadan Mandals) that meted out swift justice against British informants (known as Patri Sarkar for beating informants' feet).

3. The Underground Network & Defiant Leadership

Leader / Actor Specific Underground Role & Historic Contribution
Aruna Asaf Ali Known as the "Heroine of 1942" / Grand Old Lady of the Independence Movement. Defied police cordons and unfurled the National Flag at Gowalia Tank Ground on August 9, 1942 right after top leaders were jailed; remained underground throughout.
Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) Staged a daring Diwali-night escape from the high-security Hazaribagh Central Jail (Bihar) along with Ramnandan Mishra and Yogendra Shukla; organized the Azad Dasta (guerrilla resistance force) in the Terai jungles of Nepal.
Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Regular broadcaster on the clandestine radio, wrote pamphlets providing ideological direction, mobilized youth across UP and Bombay.
Dr. Usha Mehta Established and operated the clandestine Secret Congress Radio ("Broadcasting from somewhere in India on 42.34 metres") with Babubhai Khakar and Vithalbhai Jhaveri from August to November 1942, when she was betrayed and arrested.
Sucheta Kripalani Coordinated the central relief and underground communication network connecting underground cells across provinces.

4. Spectrum of Opposition to Quit India (UPSC High-Frequency Focus)

Not all political organizations supported the 1942 August rebellion:

  • The Muslim League (M.A. Jinnah): Opposed the movement entirely; feared a Hindu-dominated transfer of power; urged Muslims to stay away and coined the slogan "Divide and Quit".
  • The Hindu Mahasabha (V.D. Savarkar): Boycotted the movement, ordering its members to stay at their administrative posts and join the armed forces to get military training.
  • The Communist Party of India (CPI): Boycotted the movement. Following Nazi Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union (Operation Barbarossa, June 1941), the CPI changed its characterization of WW2 from an "Imperialist War" to a "People's War", aligning with the British-Soviet Allied war effort.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: Opposed the movement, criticizing it as untimely, irresponsible, and detrimental to national defence; served as Labour Member on the Viceroy's Executive Council.
  • C. Rajagopalachari: Resigned from the Congress Working Committee because he opposed launching a mass campaign during wartime and favoured accepting the League's demand for Pakistan to build a united front.
How It Works / Mechanism

Tactical Evolution of the 1942 Rebellion: The Three Phases of Mass Insurgency

The Three Distinct Phases of the August Movement

[Phase 1: Urban Mass Eruption (Aug 1942)]
       │ (Hartals, student strikes, clashes in Bombay, Calcutta, Ahmedabad)
       ▼
[Phase 2: Rural Guerrilla War & Parallel Govts (Late Aug–End 1942)]
       │ (Destruction of railways, telegraph lines, post offices; Ballia, Tamluk, Satara)
       ▼
[Phase 3: Underground Cell Resistance & Sabotage (1943–1944)]
         (Secret Congress Radio, Azad Dasta, Jayaprakash Narayan, Lohia)

  1. Phase 1: Urban Mass Eruption (First few weeks of August 1942):

    • Spontaneous general strikes (hartals), street battles, and student demonstrations in Bombay, Delhi, Calcutta, Patna, and Ahmedabad.
    • Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO) workers at Jamshedpur went on a 13-day patriotic strike refusing to produce steel for the British war effort. Textile mills in Ahmedabad remained shut for over 3 months ("Ahmedabad Stalingrad").

  2. Phase 2: Peasant Guerrilla War & Sabotage of Infrastructure (Mid-August to late 1942):

    • Movement shifted to rural hinterlands, particularly in Eastern UP, Bihar, Midnapore, and Coastal Orissa.
    • Systematic destruction of the apparatus of British colonial communication: over 250 railway stations destroyed or damaged, 500 post offices attacked, 150 police stations burned, and thousands of telephone/telegraph lines severed.
    • Bihar became the storm centre; British authority evaporated across entire districts (Azamgarh, Ballia, Ghazipur, Munger, Darbhanga).

  3. Phase 3: Underground Resistance (1943 to 1944):

    • Organized by sophisticated underground networks supplying funds, bombs, detonators, and underground literature.
    • British state deployed unprecedented terror: aircraft machine-gunned crowds from the air (in Patna, Bhagalpur, Nadia, Talcher, and Gorakhpur); collective fines exceeding ₹90 Lakh were extorted from villages; public whipping was legalized; over 10,000 freedom fighters were killed or executed.
    • Gandhi's 21-Day Fast (Feb 1943): When Viceroy Linlithgow demanded that Gandhi condemn the mass violence, Gandhi turned the moral tables, refusing to condemn popular fury and laying the entire guilt on the "leonine violence" of the British state.

Spatial & Strategic Dynamics:

  1. Severing Imperial Communications: The initial fury targeted railway tracks, telegraph wires, and post offices along the vital East Indian Railway connecting Delhi, Patna, and Calcutta—vital for Allied supply lines to the Burma front.
  2. The Parallel Governments (Prati Sarkars): In rural pockets where British authority collapsed (Ballia, Tamluk, Satara), peasants established autonomous administrative republics with independent revenue, policing, and welfare boards.
  3. The Terai Guerrilla Sanctuary: JP Narayan's daring escape from Hazaribagh Jail enabled the construction of armed underground networks (Azad Dasta) in the dense jungles of the Nepal border.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

High-Frequency Examiner Traps & Historiographical Insights

Traps Frequently Tested in Competitive Examinations

  1. The Slogan Authorship Trap (#1 UPSC/State PSC Trap!):

    • Trap: Believing Mahatma Gandhi coined the phrase "Quit India".
    • Truth: The slogan "Quit India" was coined by Yusuf Meherally, an Indian socialist and trade union leader who was then Mayor of Bombay! (He also coined the immortal slogan "Simon Go Back" in 1928). Gandhi enthusiastically approved and adopted the phrase.

  2. The Matangini Hazra Martyrdom Trap:

    • Matangini Hazra (known affectionately as Gandhi Buri or "Old Lady Gandhi", aged 73) was martyred on September 29, 1942 during the march on the Tamluk police station in Midnapore, Bengal. She was shot three times by British police while chanting Vande Mataram and holding the Indian flag high.

  3. Parties Opposing Quit India:

    • If an exam question asks which organizations supported Quit India, remember that Muslim League, Hindu Mahasabha, Communist Party of India, and Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) did NOT participate.
    • The movement was predominantly driven by the Congress rank-and-file, Congress Socialists (CSP), peasants, students, and independent nationalist masses.

  4. Longest vs Shortest Parallel Governments:

    • Shortest: Ballia (Chittu Pandey, barely 7 to 10 days).
    • Longest: Satara Prati Sarkar (Nana Patil & Y.B. Chavan, lasted nearly 3 years, from mid-1943 until 1945–46).
