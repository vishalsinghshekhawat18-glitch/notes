Committees of the Constituent Assembly & The Drafting Committee

The division of labor in the Assembly across 8 major committees and numerous minor committees, with deep focus on the 7-member Drafting Committee chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar appointed on August 29, 1947.

Intuition & Context

Intuitive Mental Model: Committees of the Constituent Assembly & The Drafting Committee

Think of Committees of the Constituent Assembly & The Drafting Committee as a fundamental structural safeguard in a constitutional democracy. Without this explicit mechanism, the balance between state power and individual freedom would collapse into arbitrary exercise of authority. In everyday governance, this provision ensures that public functionaries operate under defined legal boundaries, guaranteeing predictability, accountability, and the rule of law across the republic.

Core Principle

The Functional Division of Labor in Constitution-Making

Rather than debating complex constitutional clauses in a 300-member plenary session, the Constituent Assembly appointed specialized committees to draft specific constitutional domains. These specialized drafts were then consolidated, scrutinized, and legally formulated by the Drafting Committee.

How It Works / Mechanism

Functional Grouping of the 8 Major Committees & Sub-Committees

Understanding the committee architecture requires seeing who handled what type of constitutional work:

1. National Governance & Federal Architecture (Chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru)

• Union Powers Committee: Demarcated the scope of Central legislative powers and federal balance.
• Union Constitution Committee: Designed the structure of the Union Executive, Parliament, and Judiciary.
• States Committee: Negotiated accession and integration modalities with Princely States.

2. State Governance, Rights & Minorities (Chaired by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel)

• Provincial Constitution Committee: Designed State/Provincial executives and legislative structures.
• Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities & Tribal/Excluded Areas: A massive 54-member body that operated through crucial sub-committees:

  • Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee: J.B. Kripalani (Drafted the civil liberties charter of Part III)
  • Minorities Sub-Committee: H.C. Mukherjee (Drafted safeguards for religious and linguistic minorities)
  • North-East Frontier Tribal Areas & Assam Excluded Areas Sub-Committee: Gopinath Bardoloi (Drafted the framework for the 6th Schedule)
  • Excluded and Partially Excluded Areas (Other than Assam) Sub-Committee: A.V. Thakkar (Drafted the framework for the 5th Schedule)

3. Assembly Governance & Procedural Rules (Chaired by Dr. Rajendra Prasad)

• Rules of Procedure Committee: Formulated parliamentary procedure and voting mechanisms.
• Steering Committee: Managed the legislative timetable, business agenda, and committee sequencing.
• (Also chaired the Finance & Staff Committee and the Ad Hoc Committee on the National Flag).

4. Synthesis & Formal Drafting (Chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar)

• Drafting Committee (Appointed Aug 29, 1947): Scrutinized the initial constitutional draft prepared by Constitutional Advisor Sir B.N. Rau and formulated the official Draft Constitution of India.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Exception Clauses & High-Yield Pitfalls

Critical Traps & Multi-Statement Pitfalls for Aspirants:\n\n1. Article & Nomenclature Confusion: Examiners frequently confuse identical sounding powers across Union and State levels (e.g. Art 123 vs Art 213; Art 72 vs Art 161). Verify whether the authority belongs to the President or Governor.\n\n2. Absolute vs Qualified Language Trap: Beware of statements containing absolute words like "solely", "without exception", or "unconditional". Most constitutional powers are subject to ministerial aid and advice (Art 74/163) or judicial review (Basic Structure doctrine).\n\n3. Constitutional vs Statutory Distinction: Do not confuse bodies created by constitutional articles (e.g. UPSC, Finance Commission, ECI) with statutory watchdogs created by ordinary Acts of Parliament (e.g. NGT, CVC, Lokpal, NHRC).\n\n4. Appointment vs Removal Asymmetry: Remember high-yield asymmetries—such as State PSC members being appointed by the Governor but removable only by the President after a Supreme Court inquiry under Article 317.
