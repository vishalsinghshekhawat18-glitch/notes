11:T447,A bank can be completely solvent (possessing high positive net worth and capital adequacy) but still collapse within 48 hours due to a sudden liquidity run (as observed during the Silicon Valley Bank and Northern Rock collapses). Why? Because a bank's assets (long-term mortgages, infrastructure loans) are illiquid and locked in multi-year maturities, while its liabilities (savings accounts, corporate call deposits) are callable on demand. Basel III addressed this existential maturity mismatch by introducing three distinct guardrails: (1) **LCR (30-day survival kit)**: Ensuring the bank has an emergency cash/G-Sec stockpile (HQLA) sufficient to withstand 30 days of aggressive customer deposit withdrawals; (2) **NSFR (1-year structural match)**: Forcing banks to fund 10-year loans with stable 3-to-5-year term deposits rather than fragile overnight interbank call borrowings; and (3) **Leverage Ratio (Non-risk-based reality check)**: Preventing banks from building massive balance sheets loaded with 'zero-risk-weighted' assets that could fail if risk models misjudge market realities.12:T8ad,### 1. The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{Stock of High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days}} \ge 100\%$$

- **HQLA Level 1 (0% haircut):** Cash, excess CRR, Government Securities within mandatory SLR permitted by RBI under **Facility to Avail Liquidity for Liquidity Coverage Ratio (FALLCR)** and **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)** carve-outs.
- **HQLA Level 2A (15% haircut):** Qualifying non-financial corporate bonds rated AA- or higher, sovereign debt with 20% risk weight.
- **HQLA Level 2B (50% haircut):** Qualifying non-financial corporate bonds rated BBB- to A+, common equities included in major indices.
- **Cap:** Level 2 assets cannot exceed 40% of total HQLA, and Level 2B cannot exceed 15%.

### 2. The Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)

$$\text{NSFR} = \frac{\text{Available Stable Funding (ASF)}}{\text{Required Stable Funding (RSF)}} \ge 100\%$$

- **ASF (Numerator):** Regulatory capital, preferred stock, liabilities with effective maturity $\ge 1$ year (100% ASF factor); stable retail demand and term deposits $<1$ year (90–95% ASF factor); less stable retail deposits (90%); wholesale funding from non-financial corporates (50%).
- **RSF (Denominator):** Assets requiring stable funding. Cash/G-Secs (0% RSF); residential mortgages (65% RSF); standard corporate loans $<1$ year (50% RSF); unrated corporate loans $\ge 1$ year and NPAs (100% RSF).

### 3. The Basel III Leverage Ratio

$$\text{Leverage Ratio} = \frac{\text{Tier 1 Capital}}{\text{Total Exposure (On-Balance Sheet + Off-Balance Sheet + Derivatives)}} \times 100$$

| Standard / Metric | Scope / Horizon | Statutory Minimum (Commercial Banks) | Statutory Minimum (D-SIBs) | Core Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)** | 30 Calendar Days | **≥ 100%** | **≥ 100%** | Acute short-term liquidity shock survival |
| **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)** | 1 Year Structural Horizon | **≥ 100%** | **≥ 100%** | Structural asset-liability maturity matching |

To prevent double-counting while ensuring robust LCR compliance, RBI established a specific carve-out mechanism:

```
[Total Government Securities Portfolio (SLR)]
                    ↓
[Excess SLR above Mandatory Statutory Level -> Included 100% in Level 1 HQLA]
                    ↓
[Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) Carve-Out (up to 2% of NDTL) -> Level 1 HQLA]
                    ↓
[Facility to Avail Liquidity for LCR (FALLCR) Carve-Out -> Level 1 HQLA]
                    ↓
[Total Mandatory SLR Permitted for LCR = MSF Carve-Out + FALLCR Carve-Out]
                    ↓
[Total Level 1 HQLA = Cash + Excess CRR + Excess SLR + MSF Carve-out + FALLCR Carve-out]
```

1. **Level 1 HQLA Compilation:** Banks calculate eligible sovereign assets including unencumbered excess SLR securities, cash balances with RBI above minimum CRR, and government securities within mandatory SLR permitted under MSF and FALLCR.
2. **Cash Outflow Stress Modelling:** The bank projects 30-day cash outflows applying regulatory run-off rates (e.g., stable retail deposits = 5% run-off rate; operational wholesale deposits = 25%; non-operational wholesale deposits = 100%).
3. **Cash Inflow Modelling:** 30-day contractual inflows are calculated and capped at **75% of gross outflows** to guarantee a minimum net outflow floor.
4. **Supervisory Monitoring:** Daily LCR data is submitted to RBI. If LCR drops below 100%, the bank must immediately notify RBI's Department of Supervision and execute a board-approved Liquidity Restoration Plan.14:T43c,### Basel III Liquidity & Leverage Framework Architecture

1. **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR: ≥ 100%)**
   - **Time Horizon:** 30 calendar days of acute liquidity stress.
   - **Numerator:** High-Quality Liquid Assets (Level 1 [0% haircut], Level 2A [15% haircut], Level 2B [50% haircut]). Level 2 capped at 40% of total.
   - **SLR Carve-Outs:** MSF (up to 2% NDTL) + FALLCR.
   - **Denominator:** Total Net Cash Outflows (Total Outflows minus Inflows capped at 75% of outflows).

2. **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR: ≥ 100%)**
   - **Time Horizon:** 1 year ongoing structural resilience.
   - **Numerator (ASF):** Capital and liabilities weighted by stability factor (0% to 100%).
   - **Denominator (RSF):** Assets and off-balance sheet exposures weighted by funding requirement factor (0% to 100%).

3. **Leverage Ratio (Non-Risk-Weighted Backstop)**
   - Formula: $\text{Tier 1 Capital} / \text{Total Exposure} \ge 3.50\%$.
   - D-SIBs Floor: $\ge 4.00\%$.
   - Total Exposure includes gross on-balance sheet assets, derivatives exposures, and off-balance sheet
