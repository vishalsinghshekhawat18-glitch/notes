11:T752,Following the landmark monetary policy reform on **April 8, 2022**, the RBI restructured the LAF corridor to its modern, symmetric format:

```
                      THE LAF INTEREST RATE CORRIDOR
                                    │
  ▲  MSF Rate = Bank Rate (Repo Rate + 25 bps) ─── CEILING (Emergency Overnight Borrowing)
  │                                                 (Collateral: G-Secs dipped from SLR)
  │
  │  POLICY REPO RATE (Benchmark Policy Anchor) ── CENTER (Target for WACR)
  │                                                 (Collateral: Unencumbered G-Secs)
  │
  ▼  SDF Rate (Repo Rate - 25 bps) ────────────── FLOOR (Uncollateralized Absorption)
                                                    (No G-Sec collateral required)
```

### The Core Instruments
1. **Policy Repo Rate (Center)**:
   - The rate at which RBI injects liquidity against unencumbered government securities (Repo = Repurchase Agreement).
2. **Standing Deposit Facility / SDF (Floor = Repo - 25 bps)**:
   - Operationalized on **April 8, 2022** under **Section 17(1A) of the RBI Act**.
   - Absorbs surplus liquidity from commercial banks **without RBI providing any G-Sec collateral**.
   - Eliminated the collateral constraint that previously limited RBI's liquidity absorption capacity.
3. **Marginal Standing Facility / MSF (Ceiling = Repo + 25 bps)**:
   - Introduced in 2011.
   - Emergency overnight borrowing window for scheduled commercial banks against **dipping into their SLR portfolio** (up to $2\%$ of NDTL) at a penalty rate.
4. **Bank Rate (Section 49, RBI Act 1934)**:
   - The rate at which RBI buys or rediscounts bills of exchange or other commercial papers.
   - Today, it is strictly aligned with the **MSF rate** and serves as the reference rate for determining penal interest on CRR/SLR shortfalls.12:T52b,A comparative analysis of the operational mechanics of each instrument:

| Feature | Policy Repo Rate | Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) | Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) | Fixed Rate Reverse Repo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Function** | Liquidity Injection | Liquidity Absorption | Emergency Injection | Legacy Absorption |
| **Transacting Parties**| RBI $\to$ Scheduled Banks | Banks $\to$ RBI | Scheduled Banks $\to$ RBI | Banks $\to$ RBI |
| **Collateral Requirement**| **Collateralized** (Unencumbered G-Secs) | **Uncollateralized** (Zero collateral) | **Collateralized** (Can dip into SLR quota) | **Collateralized** (RBI gives G-Secs) |
| **Corridor Position** | **Center** (Benchmark) | **Floor** (Repo - 25 bps) | **Ceiling** (Repo + 25 bps) | Sub-floor (discretionary) |
| **Tenure** | Overnight / 14-day | Overnight (discretionary) | Overnight | Overnight |
| **Rate Setting** | Set by **MPC** | Fixed spread to Repo | Fixed spread to Repo | Fixed spread to Repo |

### Impact on Market Rates
- When liquidity is **deficit**, banks borrow at Repo/MSF $\implies$ WACR moves toward the ceiling (MSF).
- When liquidity is **surplus**, banks park funds at SDF $\implies$ WACR moves toward the floor (SDF).
- The ultimate goal of RBI liquidity management is to align **WACR with the Policy Repo Rate**.13:T4fb,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Collateral in SDF vs Reverse Repo**: Reverse Repo requires RBI to transfer government securities as collateral to the depositing bank. **SDF is uncollateralized** (RBI gives NO securities). This was the primary reason SDF was introduced under Section 17(1A) of the RBI Act.
- **Trap 2: Dipping into SLR**: Under standard Repo, banks **cannot** use SLR-locked securities as collateral (must use unencumbered excess G-Secs). Under **MSF**, banks are legally permitted to **dip into their SLR quota** (up to a specified percentage, typically $2\%$ of NDTL).
- **Trap 3: Bank Rate vs Repo Rate**: The Bank Rate requires no collateral (rediscounting), but is currently aligned to the MSF rate and used for statutory penalty computations. The Repo Rate is collateralized and set by the MPC.
- **Trap 4: Corridor Width**: The standard symmetric LAF corridor width is **50 basis points** ($\text{SDF} = \text{Repo} - 25\text{ bps}$; $\text{MSF} = \text{Repo} + 25\text{ bps}$).
