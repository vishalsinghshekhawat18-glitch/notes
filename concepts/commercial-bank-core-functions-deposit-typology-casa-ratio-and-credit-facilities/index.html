Commercial Bank Functions: Statutory Definition, Deposit Typology, CASA Ratio & Credit Facilities

The foundational operational model of commercial banking under Section 5(b) and Section 6 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949: accepting deposits repayable on demand or otherwise and lending/investing. Examines the full deposit hierarchy (Current Account, Savings Account, Term/Fixed Deposits, Recurring Deposits), the strategic significance of the CASA ratio in determining Net Interest Margin (NIM), credit product structures (Cash Credit, Overdraft, Term Loans, Letters of Credit, Bank Guarantees), and agency/utility services.

Intuition & Context

The Dual Engine: Deposit Mobilization and Credit Allocation

A commercial bank is a financial business operating on a spread. It sources raw material (money) from depositors at a low cost of interest, packages and monitors that money, and sells it to borrowers as loans at a higher rate of interest.

The difference between the interest earned on loans/investments and the interest paid on deposits is the bank's Net Interest Income (NII). To maximize this spread without taking reckless risks, banks focus on mobilizing cheap, sticky deposits (Current and Savings Accounts / CASA) and deploying them into well-collateralized, high-yielding loan products.

Core Principle

Deposit Typology & The Strategic Architecture of CASA

Commercial bank deposit liabilities are bifurcated into Demand Deposits and Time/Term Deposits:

1. Deposit Product Hierarchy

  • Demand Deposits (Withdrawable at any time without prior notice):
    • Current Account: Maintained by businesses and corporates for high-frequency transactions. Earns 0%0%0% interest (RBI prohibits interest on current accounts, with narrow exemptions).
    • Savings Account: Maintained by households for savings and daily retail payments. Earns low nominal interest (e.g., $2.5% - 3.5% p.a.$).
  • Term / Time Deposits (Withdrawable after a contracted maturity period):
    • Fixed Deposit (FD): Lump-sum deposit locked for a fixed duration (7 days to 10 years). Earns higher interest (e.g., $6.5% - 7.5% p.a.$). Premature withdrawal incurs penal charges.
    • Recurring Deposit (RD): Fixed regular monthly installments deposited over a specified tenure.

How It Works / Mechanism

Credit Facility Structures: Funded vs Non-Funded Advances

Bank lending is structured into two fundamental categories:

Funded vs Non-Funded Mechanics

  1. Cash Credit (CC) & Overdraft (OD): Running credit limits where the borrower is charged interest only on the actual amount drawn on a daily basis, not on the entire sanctioned limit.
  2. Term Loans: The full sanctioned amount is disbursed upfront or in scheduled tranches. Interest is charged on the entire outstanding loan balance.
  3. Letters of Credit (LC): A documentary commitment issued by a bank on behalf of a buyer, assuring the seller that payment will be made provided shipping documents conform to terms. If the buyer pays, the bank disburses zero cash and pockets a fee. If the buyer defaults, the LC "devolves" into a funded liability of the bank.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Statutory Definition of Banking: Section 5(b) of the Banking Regulation Act requires BOTH accepting deposits AND lending/investing. An institution that only lends (like an NBFC) or only accepts deposits without lending (like a pure custodian) is not a bank under Section 5(b).
  • Trap 2: Interest on Current Accounts: Under RBI regulations, commercial banks are strictly prohibited from paying interest on Current Accounts (subject to specific exemptions like RRB balances or deceased depositor claims).
  • Trap 3: Funded vs Non-Funded: LCs and Bank Guarantees are non-funded facilities that appear as Contingent Liabilities (Off-Balance Sheet items), generating fee income rather than interest income.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • IIBF DBF & SBI/IBPS PO: Mastery of deposit rules, CASA ratio computation, cash credit vs overdraft, and off-balance sheet items.
  • RBI Grade B & RPSC RAS: Statutory definition under Section 5(b) BR Act, Net Interest Margin (NIM) dynamics, and cost of funds.
