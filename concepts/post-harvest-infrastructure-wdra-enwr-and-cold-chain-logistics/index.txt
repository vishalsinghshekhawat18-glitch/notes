Dissects the magnitude and economic cost of post-harvest food losses (estimated by CIPHET at ₹92,000+ Crore annually, with 5%–15% losses in grains and 15%–30% in fruits/vegetables) driven by acute shortages in integrated cold chains, modern silos, and primary pack-houses. Analyzes: (1) The Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act, 2007 (WDRA): regulating registered warehouses and creating **electronic Negotiable Warehouse Receipts (e-NWRs)** issued through digital repositories (NeRL, CCRL) enabling farmers to pledge stored produce as collateral for immediate post-harvest pledge financing from banks, preventing distress sales, (2) The **Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF)**: ₹1.00 Lakh Crore financing facility providing 3% interest subvention and CGTMSE credit guarantee for post-harvest farm-gate infrastructure, and (3) Cold-Chain Logistics and the Pradhan Mantri Kisan SAMPADA Yojana (PMKSY) for mega food parks, integrated cold chains, and agro-processing clusters.12:T4c6,In November, a farmer harvests 500 quintals of wheat. He deposits his 500 quintals of wheat into a WDRA-registered scientific warehouse.\n2. The warehouse grades the wheat and issues an **e-NWR** directly into his digital repository account (like demat shares!).\n3. The farmer presents this e-NWR to his bank on his smartphone. The bank immediately sanctions a **Pledge Loan of ₹6 Lakh** (75% of produce value) at a low interest rate.\n4. The farmer uses the ₹2 Lakh to repay his immediate debts.\n5. In March, when wheat market supplies dwindle and the price rises to ₹2,500/quintal, the farmer sells his e-NWR on the e-NAM portal with a single click, repays the bank loan, and pockets a huge profit!\n\nPost-harvest warehousing converts physical perishable crops into liquid financial collateral.13:T683,```\n                      POST-HARVEST VALUE CHAIN ARCHITECTURE\n                                       │\n     ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐\n     ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼\n1.
