Constitutional Funds & Budget Components: Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, Public Account & Charged Expenditure

The constitutional fund structure and expenditure authorization mechanisms established under Articles 112, 266, and 267 of the Constitution of India. Analyzes the Three Constitutional Funds: (1) Consolidated Fund of India (Art 266(1): all revenues, loans raised, and loan recoveries; no withdrawal without parliamentary appropriation law), (2) Contingency Fund of India (Art 267: imprest placed at the disposal of the President for unforeseen emergencies, enhanced to ₹30,000 Crore in 2021), and (3) Public Account of India (Art 266(2): funds held by government as a banker/trustee, e.g., provident funds and small savings, operated by executive action). Examines the strict distinction between Charged Expenditure (non-votable) and Voted Expenditure.

Intuition & Context

The Three Government Safes: Who Holds the Key?

Imagine the Government of India manages three distinct bank accounts:

  1. The Sovereign Master Safe (Consolidated Fund of India - Art 266(1)): Contains all taxes you pay, all government bond borrowings, and all loan recoveries. The key to this safe is held exclusively by Parliament! Not even the Prime Minister can take out one single rupee without a formal Appropriation Law passed by elected MPs.

  2. The Emergency Petty Cash (Contingency Fund - Art 267): Kept for unexpected crises (earthquakes, war, sudden pandemics). The key is held by the President of India (operated by the Finance Secretary) to release immediate relief funds without waiting months for Parliament to meet.

  3. The Public Trustee Vault (Public Account - Art 266(2)): Holds money that does not belong to the government (your Post Office Small Savings, Provident Fund / GPF, court deposits). The government acts merely as a trustee/banker; executive officers can withdraw and disburse this money without parliamentary voting.

Core Principle

Comparison of the Three Constitutional Funds

The constitutional funds matrix under Articles 266 and 267:

Fund Name Constitutional Article Source of Money Withdrawal Authorization
Consolidated Fund of India (CFI) Article 266(1) All tax revenues, non-tax revenues, market borrowings, and loan repayments Strict Parliamentary Law (Appropriation Act under Article 114)
Contingency Fund of India Article 267(1) Fixed statutory corpus of ₹30,000 Crore (enhanced in 2021) President of India (Finance Secretary); requires ex-post parliamentary replenishment
Public Account of India Article 266(2) Public moneys held in trust: National Small Savings Fund (NSSF), Provident Funds, Judicial deposits Executive Action (No parliamentary appropriation needed)

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: High Court Judges Salaries vs Pensions:
    • Salaries of High Court Judges: Charged on the Consolidated Fund of the STATE (Article 202(3)(d)).
    • Pensions of High Court Judges: Charged on the Consolidated Fund of INDIA (Article 112(3)(d)(iii))! (A classic UPSC / RPSC trap!).
  • Trap 2: Contingency Fund Corpus: The corpus was raised from ₹500 Crore to ₹30,000 Crore in Finance Act, 2021.
  • Trap 3: Voting on Charged Expenditure: Parliament can discuss charged expenditure, but the Lok Sabha CANNOT vote on it.
  • Trap 4: Public Account Withdrawals: Public Account disbursements do NOT require an Appropriation Act under Article 114.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & UPSC APFC: Article 112(3) charged list, High Court pension anomaly, and Contingency Fund rules.
  • RPSC RAS & SBI/IBPS PO: 2-mark definitions of CFI vs Public Account, Contingency Fund limit (₹30,000 Cr), and charged expenditure.
