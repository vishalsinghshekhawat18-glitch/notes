Industrial Salts: Sodium Hydroxide (Chlor-Alkali Process) & Bleaching Powder

The industrial brine electrolysis producing chlorine, hydrogen, and sodium hydroxide (Chlor-Alkali), and the synthesis, disinfectant properties, and chemical reactions of Bleaching Powder (CaOCl₂).

Intuition & Context

How Common Table Salt Powers Water Purification, Soaps, and Rocket Fuel

If you dissolve ordinary common salt (NaClNaClNaCl) in water and zap it with high-voltage electricity (The Chlor-Alkali Process), you split salt and water into three indispensable industrial pillars: Chlorine gas (which sterilizes swimming pools and makes bleaching powder), Caustic Soda (which manufactures soaps and paper), and pure Hydrogen gas (which fuels rockets and makes ammonia fertilizer for crops)!

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps: Anode vs Cathode Gas Placement & Slaked Lime State

Critical Exam Traps:

  1. Electrode Gas Placement in Chlor-Alkali:
    • ANODE (+++)   ⟹  \implies⟹ CHLORINE Gas (Cl2Cl_2Cl2​) (because negative Cl−Cl^-Cl− ions migrate to positive anode).
    • CATHODE (−-−)   ⟹  \implies⟹ HYDROGEN Gas (H2H_2H2​) (because positive H+H^+H+ ions migrate to negative cathode).
  2. Synthesis Condition for Bleaching Powder:
    • Trap: Reacting chlorine with Quicklime (CaOCaOCaO) or aqueous lime water.
    • Correct: Chlorine must be passed over DRY SLAKED LIME [Ca(OH)2Ca(OH)_2Ca(OH)2​] at 40∘C40^\circ\text{C}40∘C.
  3. Bleaching Action Principle: Bleaching powder bleaches organic colored substances through Oxidation by nascent oxygen ([O][O][O]) released in aqueous acidic reactions.
