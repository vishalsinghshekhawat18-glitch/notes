### The Mathematical Formulas
$$\text{CRAR} = \left(\frac{\text{Eligible Tier 1 Capital} + \text{Eligible Tier 2 Capital}}{\text{Total Risk-Weighted Assets (Credit + Market + Operational RWA)}}\right) \times 100$$

$$\text{Leverage Ratio} = \left(\frac{\text{Tier 1 Capital}}{\text{Total On- and Off-Balance Sheet Exposure}}\right) \times 100 \ge 3.5\%$$12:T42d,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Indian Basel III Standards are Stricter than Global Basel**: Global Basel III mandates minimum total CRAR of **8.0%** (10.5% with CCB). The **RBI mandates 9.0%** (11.5% with CCB for SCBs, and 12% for PSBs).
- **Trap 2: AT1 Bond Loss Absorption (PONV)**: Additional Tier 1 (AT1) bonds are **not risk-free fixed deposits**; under RBI Basel III norms, AT1 bonds can be permanently written down or converted to equity if the bank hits the Point of Non-Viability (PONV), as occurred during the YES Bank reconstruction in 2020.
- **Trap 3: Leverage Ratio is Non-Risk-Based**: Unlike CRAR (which divides by Risk-Weighted Assets), the **Leverage Ratio divides by Total Nominal Exposure** without applying any risk weights.
