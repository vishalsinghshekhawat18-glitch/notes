Bank Capital Adequacy & The Basel III Framework: Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA), Capital Tiers, CRAR & Buffers

The core risk-sensitive capital architecture of commercial banking under the Basel III framework (codified in RBI Master Circular on Capital Regulations). Covers Risk-Weighted Assets (Credit, Market, Operational Risk), the Capital Tiers hierarchy (Common Equity Tier 1 / CET1, Additional Tier 1 / AT1, Tier 2 / Gone-Concern Capital), minimum CRAR requirements in India (9% minimum CRAR, 11.5% with 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer / CCB; 12% for PSBs), the non-risk-based Leverage Ratio (minimum 3.5% / 4% for D-SIBs), and Countercyclical Capital Buffers (CCyB).

Intuition & Context

The Shock Absorber: Why Bank Capital Protects Depositors from Ruin

Why cannot a bank operate with 100% deposit liabilities and zero equity capital?

Suppose a bank has ₹100 Crore in deposits and ₹100 Crore in corporate loans, with ₹0 equity capital. If a single factory defaults on a ₹2 Crore loan, the bank's assets fall to ₹98 Crore. The bank is now insolvent and cannot repay its depositors in full!

Bank Capital (CRAR) is the bank's unencumbered equity shock-absorber. If the bank has ₹12 Crore in equity capital and ₹100 Crore in deposits (Total Assets = ₹112 Crore), a ₹5 Crore loan default is absorbed entirely by the equity shareholders. Depositors still get 100% of their money back. Capital adequacy regulations ensure banks hold enough equity to survive severe economic recessions without failing.

Core Principle

How It Works / Mechanism

Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) & Capital Conservation Buffers

1. Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA) Computation

Instead of treating all assets equally, each asset is multiplied by a regulatory Risk Weight (RWRWRW):

  • Cash & Sovereign G-Secs: RW=0%RW = 0%RW=0% (Risk-free).
  • Claims on Banks (AAA Rated): RW=20%RW = 20%RW=20%.
  • Prime Residential Home Loans (LTV ≤80%\le 80%≤80%): RW=35%−50%RW = 35% - 50%RW=35%−50%.
  • Standard Rated Corporate Loans: RW=20%RW = 20%RW=20% (AAA) to 150%150%150% (Unrated/D-rated).
  • Unsecured Consumer Loans / Credit Cards: RW=125%−150%RW = 125% - 150%RW=125%−150% (High risk penalty).

2. The Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB = 2.5%)

  • Mandated to be held entirely in pure Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1).
  • Built up outside periods of stress. When a bank's capital falls into the CCB band ($5.5% - 8.0%$ CET1), the bank faces automatic legal restrictions on paying dividends, share buybacks, and executive bonuses until the buffer is rebuilt.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Indian Basel III Standards are Stricter than Global Basel: Global Basel III mandates minimum total CRAR of 8.0% (10.5% with CCB). The RBI mandates 9.0% (11.5% with CCB for SCBs, and 12% for PSBs).
  • Trap 2: AT1 Bond Loss Absorption (PONV): Additional Tier 1 (AT1) bonds are not risk-free fixed deposits; under RBI Basel III norms, AT1 bonds can be permanently written down or converted to equity if the bank hits the Point of Non-Viability (PONV), as occurred during the YES Bank reconstruction in 2020.
  • Trap 3: Leverage Ratio is Non-Risk-Based: Unlike CRAR (which divides by Risk-Weighted Assets), the Leverage Ratio divides by Total Nominal Exposure without applying any risk weights.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B, SEBI Grade A & IIBF DBF: CRAR math (9% + 2.5% CCB = 11.5%), CET1 (5.5% + 2.5% = 8%), AT1 write-down rules, and leverage ratio.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Basel acronym (Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, Switzerland), 3 Pillars of Basel II/III, and CRAR definition.
