2D Geometric Invariants: Inradius-Circumradius Invariants, Isoperimetric Optimization & Pathway Algebra

Rigorous plane geometry and 2D mensuration: isoperimetric maximum-area theorems, inradius/circumradius invariants for right-angled, equilateral, and general triangles, pathway ring algebra, and 3-tier competitive problem decomposition.

Intuition & Context

Geometric Enclosure: Perimeter Constraints & Symmetrical Optimization

Imagine you are given a fixed length of flexible fencing—say exactly 40 meters. If you construct an elongated, narrow rectangle (19m by 1m), you enclose a meagre 19 m219\text{ m}^219 m2. If you square the dimensions (10 m×10 m10\text{ m} \times 10\text{ m}10 m×10 m), your enclosed area surges to 100 m2100\text{ m}^2100 m2. If you bend the fencing into a circle of circumference 40 m40\text{ m}40 m, the enclosed area expands to approximately $127.3\text{ m}^2$.

This intuitive fact forms the bedrock of the Isoperimetric Theorem: among all closed plane figures with a fixed perimeter, the shape with maximum enclosed area is strictly the circle. As polygon side count n→∞n \to \inftyn→∞, the enclosed area strictly monotonically increases.

In competitive banking and SSC examinations, 2D geometry is rarely about drawing proofs; it is about exploiting fixed ratio invariants—such as the relationship between a triangle's area, its semi-perimeter, and the radius of its inscribed or circumscribed circle, or avoiding the classic algebraic trap of overlapping intersections in internal and external pathways.

2. Systematic Pathway Algebra Framework

When a pathway of uniform width www surrounds or cuts through a rectangular field of length lll and breadth bbb:

  1. Internal Pathway (Pathway inside perimeter):
    Area=2w(l+b−2w)\text{Area} = 2w(l + b - 2w)Area=2w(l+b−2w)
  2. External Pathway (Pathway outside perimeter):
    Area=2w(l+b+2w)\text{Area} = 2w(l + b + 2w)Area=2w(l+b+2w)
  3. Central Crossroad Pathways (Two perpendicular paths intersecting in center):
    Area=w(l+b−w)\text{Area} = w(l + b - w)Area=w(l+b−w)
    (Note: The term −w2-w^2−w2 subtracts the central overlapping square common to both paths).
How It Works / Mechanism

Mathematical Derivation & 3-Tier Problem Decomposition

Derivation of the Right-Triangle Inradius Invariant

Let a right-angled triangle have legs a,ba, ba,b meeting at right angle CCC, and hypotenuse ccc. Let incircle center be III with radius rrr.

  1. Tangents from vertices to incircle are equal in length.
  2. From vertex C(90∘)C(90^\circ)C(90∘), the quadrilateral formed by CCC, the two tangent points, and center III is a square of side rrr.
  3. Thus, the tangent segments adjacent to vertex CCC are each of length rrr.
  4. The remaining segment on side aaa is (a−r)(a - r)(a−r), and on side bbb is (b−r)(b - r)(b−r).
  5. Tangents from vertex AAA to the circle have length (b−r)(b - r)(b−r); tangents from vertex BBB have length (a−r)(a - r)(a−r).
  6. The hypotenuse ccc is the sum of these two tangent segments:
    c=(a−r)+(b−r)=a+b−2rc = (a - r) + (b - r) = a + b - 2rc=(a−r)+(b−r)=a+b−2r
    $2r = a + b - c \implies r = \frac{a + b - c}{2}$
    (Q.E.D. This eliminates calculating semi-perimeter sss and Heron's formula entirely).

Examiner Traps, Confusion Matrix & Speed Heuristics

High-Frequency Examiner Traps in 2D Geometry

  1. The Pathway Direction Trap:

    • Internal path: Dimensions decrease by $2w$ (width from left/right, top/bottom): Area=2w(l+b−2w)\text{Area} = 2w(l+b-2w)Area=2w(l+b−2w).
    • External path: Dimensions increase by $2w$: Area=2w(l+b+2w)\text{Area} = 2w(l+b+2w)Area=2w(l+b+2w).
    • Trap: Confusing internal vs external leads to an error of 8w2 cm28w^2\text{ cm}^28w2 cm2.

  2. The Equilateral Incircle-Circumcircle Area Ratio Trap:

    • Inradius r=a23r = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{3}}r=23​a​; Circumradius R=a3R = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}R=3​a​.
    • Radius ratio is R:r=2:1R : r = 2 : 1R:r=2:1.
    • Trap: Examiners ask for the Area ratio, which squares the radius ratio:
      Area(R)Area(r)=(21)2=4:1\frac{\text{Area}(R)}{\text{Area}(r)} = \left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^2 = 4 : 1Area(r)Area(R)​=(12​)2=4:1
    • Shaded area between circumcircle and incircle is exactly $3 \times \text{Area of incircle}$.
