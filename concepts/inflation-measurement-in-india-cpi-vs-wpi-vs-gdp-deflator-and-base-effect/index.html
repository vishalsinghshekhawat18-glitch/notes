Inflation Measurement in India: CPI (Combined, Rural, Urban) vs WPI vs GDP Deflator, Weighting Schemas & The Base Effect

The institutional and statistical architecture of price indices in India: Consumer Price Index (CPI-Combined, Rural, Urban with Base 2012 by MoSPI/NSO), Wholesale Price Index (WPI with Base 2011-12 by DPIIT), GDP Deflator, index item basket composition, weighting bifurcations (Food weight dominance), and the statistical arithmetic of the Base Effect.

Intuition & Context

Retail Basket vs Wholesale Mandi: Why Price Indices Diverge

Why does the newspaper report that WPI inflation is −1%-1%−1% (deflation) while your monthly grocery bill rose by +6%+6%+6% (CPI inflation)?

The difference lies in who is buying and what is inside the basket:

  • Wholesale Price Index (WPI) measures the bulk price of physical goods traded between businesses at the factory gate or wholesale mandi (dominated by manufactured goods and global crude metals, with 0%0%0% services).
  • Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures the retail cost of living experienced by households, heavily dominated by food ($45.86%$ of the basket), housing, education, healthcare, and services.

When global metal and crude oil prices collapse, WPI plummets; but if domestic tomato and onion prices surge, CPI spikes.

How It Works / Mechanism

The Base Effect: Mathematical Mechanics & Distortions

Inflation is calculated as the percentage change in the price index over a 12-month period (Year-on-Year / YoY):
Inflation Ratet=(CPIt−CPIt−12CPIt−12)×100\text{Inflation Rate}t = \left(\frac{\text{CPI}t - \text{CPI}{t-12}}{\text{CPI}{t-12}}\right) \times 100Inflation Ratet​=(CPIt−12​CPIt​−CPIt−12​​)×100

The Base Effect occurs because the denominator CPIt−12\text{CPI}_{t-12}CPIt−12​ dictates the percentage change independently of month-on-month (MoMMoMMoM) price movements:

1. High Base Effect (Favourable / Deflationary Base)

  • If CPI spiked abnormally in July 2025 due to a heatwave (CPIJul 2025=180\text{CPI}_{\text{Jul 2025}} = 180CPIJul 2025​=180),
  • Then in July 2026, even if prices remain elevated (CPIJul 2026=183\text{CPI}_{\text{Jul 2026}} = 183CPIJul 2026​=183),
  • The recorded YoY inflation will be just 183−180180×100=1.67%\frac{183 - 180}{180} \times 100 = 1.67%180183−180​×100=1.67%.
  • Outcome: Inflation drops statistically even though the actual cost of living has not fallen.

2. Low Base Effect (Unfavourable / Inflationary Base)

  • If CPI collapsed in November 2025 due to a bumper harvest (CPINov 2025=160\text{CPI}_{\text{Nov 2025}} = 160CPINov 2025​=160),
  • Then in November 2026, normal seasonal prices (CPINov 2026=172\text{CPI}_{\text{Nov 2026}} = 172CPINov 2026​=172) produce an alarming recorded inflation of 172−160160×100=7.5%\frac{172 - 160}{160} \times 100 = 7.5%160172−160​×100=7.5%.
  • Outcome: Statistical inflation surges without abnormal demand pressure.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps, Base Year Parameters & PYQ Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Services in WPI: WPI does NOT include services. If an exam option states "WPI captures inflation in healthcare and transport services", it is strictly false.
  • Trap 2: RBI Target Transition: Prior to 2014, RBI used WPI for policy guidance. Following the Urjit Patel Committee Report (2014), RBI formally adopted CPI-Combined (Headline) as its sole nominal anchor for Flexible Inflation Targeting under the 2016 statutory amendments.
  • Trap 3: Housing in Rural CPI: The "Housing" sub-group ($10.07%$ weight in CPI-Combined) is surveyed and included only in Urban CPI. In Rural CPI, the weight for Housing is 0%0%0%.
  • Trap 4: Imported Goods in GDP Deflator vs CPI: CPI includes imported consumer goods (e.g., imported edible oil or electronics). The GDP Deflator completely excludes all imported goods because it only measures prices of domestically produced output.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Precise weights of CPI and WPI, Urjit Patel committee recommendations, GDP deflator calculations, and base effect.
  • UPSC APFC, RPSC RAS & Banking Exams: Compiling ministries (MoSPI vs DPIIT), base years (2012 vs 2011-12), and exclusion of services in WPI.
