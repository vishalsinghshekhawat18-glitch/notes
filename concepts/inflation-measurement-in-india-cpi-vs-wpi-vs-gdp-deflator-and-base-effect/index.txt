12:T575,The three primary measures of inflation in India are structured as follows:

| Feature | Consumer Price Index (CPI-Combined) | Wholesale Price Index (WPI) | GDP Deflator |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Compiling Agency** | **NSO, MoSPI** | **Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT** (MoCI) | **NSO, MoSPI** |
| **Current Base Year** | **2012 = 100** | **2011–12 = 100** | **2011–12 = 100** |
| **Transaction Stage** | Retail consumer purchase | First point of bulk/wholesale sale | All domestically produced goods & services |
| **Services Inclusion**| **Included** (Transport, Health, Education, Housing) | **Completely Excluded** ($0\%$) | **Fully Included** (All domestic services) |
| **Food Weight** | **$45.86\%$** (Food & Beverages) | **$24.38\%$** (Primary Food $15.26\%$ + Mfg Food $9.12\%$) | Dynamic (matches GDP consumption share) |
| **Manufactured Goods**| Low (~$20\%$ non-food goods) | **$64.23\%$** (Dominant weight) | Matches domestic manufacturing share |
| **Imported Goods** | Included (if consumed by households) | Included (raw material imports) | **Excluded** (Only domestic output) |
| **Release Frequency** | Monthly (around 12th of every month) | Monthly (around 14th of every month) | Quarterly/Annual (with GDP releases) |
| **RBI Target Index** | **Official Nominal Anchor** (Urjit Patel Committee) | Abandoned as target in 2014 | Background analytical indicator |13:T4c9,Inflation is calculated as the percentage change in the price index over a 12-month period (Year-on-Year / YoY):
$$\text{Inflation Rate}_t = \left(\frac{\text{CPI}_t - \text{CPI}_{t-12}}{\text{CPI}_{t-12}}\right) \times 100$$

The **Base Effect** occurs because the denominator $\text{CPI}_{t-12}$ dictates the percentage change independently of month-on-month ($MoM$) price movements:

### 1. High Base Effect (Favourable / Deflationary Base)
- If CPI spiked abnormally in July 2025 due to a heatwave ($\text{CPI}_{\text{Jul 2025}} = 180$),
- Then in July 2026, even if prices remain elevated ($\text{CPI}_{\text{Jul 2026}} = 183$),
- The recorded YoY inflation will be just $\frac{183 - 180}{180} \times 100 = 1.67\%$.
- *Outcome*: Inflation drops statistically even though the actual cost of living has not fallen.

### 2. Low Base Effect (Unfavourable / Inflationary Base)
- If CPI collapsed in November 2025 due to a bumper harvest ($\text{CPI}_{\text{Nov 2025}} = 160$),
- Then in November 2026, normal seasonal prices ($\text{CPI}_{\text{Nov 2026}} = 172$) produce an alarming recorded inflation of $\frac{172 - 160}{160} \times 100 = 7.5\%$.
- *Outcome*: Statistical inflation surges without abnormal demand pressure.14:T4ec,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Services in WPI**: WPI does **NOT** include services. If an exam option states "WPI captures inflation in healthcare and transport services", it is **strictly false**.
- **Trap 2: RBI Target Transition**: Prior to 2014, RBI used WPI for policy guidance. Following the **Urjit Patel Committee Report (2014)**, RBI formally adopted **CPI-Combined (Headline)** as its sole nominal anchor for Flexible Inflation Targeting under the 2016 statutory amendments.
- **Trap 3: Housing in Rural CPI**: The "Housing" sub-group ($10.07\%$ weight in CPI-Combined) is surveyed and included **only in Urban CPI**. In Rural CPI, the weight for Housing is $0\%$.
- **Trap 4: Imported Goods in GDP Deflator vs CPI**: CPI includes imported consumer goods (e.g., imported edible oil or electronics). The **GDP Deflator completely excludes all imported goods** because it only measures prices of domestically produced output.
