Microfinance & Grassroots Collectives: SHG-Bank Linkage Programme (SHG-BLP), Joint Liability Groups (JLGs) & MFIs

The economic principles, social collateral dynamics, and institutional delivery models of microfinance in India. Dissects the **Self-Help Group-Bank Linkage Programme (SHG-BLP)** launched by NABARD in 1992 (now the world's largest community-based microfinance movement): voluntary groups of 10–20 rural women pooling small thrift savings, internal lending, and graduating to collateral-free bank credit (with repayment rates consistently > 96%). Contrasts SHGs with **Joint Liability Groups (JLGs)**: informal credit groups of 4–10 small/tenant farmers using mutual cross-guarantees for individual agricultural loans. Analyzes the **RBI Harmonized Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans (2022)**: defining microfinance as collateral-free loans to households with annual income up to ₹3.00 Lakh, with a maximum debt service limit of 50% of monthly household income.

Intuition & Context

Social Collateral: How 15 Women Defeated the Moneylender

If a poor landless woman in a village asks a commercial bank for a ₹30,000 loan to buy a sewing machine, the bank rejects her because she has no land title, no gold, and no credit history.\n\nShe is forced to go to the local moneylender, who charges an extortionate interest rate of 60% per year (55\% \text{ per month}5) and traps her in perpetual debt bondage.\n\nHow does microfinance solve this fundamental market failure?\n- 15 women from the village form a Self-Help Group (SHG).\n- For 6 months, every woman saves ₹100 every week into a common box. They lend small sums to each other for emergencies and repay on time.\n- The bank observes this discipline and gives the SHG a collateral-free loan of ₹3 Lakh!\n- Why does the bank feel safe without land collateral?\n Because if one woman skips a payment, the other 14 women in her village know immediately, visit her house, and ensure she resolves it. "Peer monitoring and social solidarity" replace brick-and-mortar collateral!

Core Principle

Master Comparison: SHG vs JLG vs MFI Model

| Dimension | Self-Help Group (SHG) | Joint Liability Group (JLG) | Microfinance Institution (MFI / NBFC-MFI) |\n| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |\n| Inception & Origin | 1992 (NABARD Pilot); scaled via DAY-NRLM. | 2004-05 (NABARD Scheme). | 1990s private microfinance companies; regulated by RBI. |\n| Group Membership | 10 to 20 Members (strictly homogeneous, primarily women). | 4 to 10 Members (small/tenant farmers, oral lessees). | Individual borrowers formed into centers/groups (Grameen model). |\n| Savings Component | Mandatory Thrift Savings: Group must save internally for 6 months before seeking bank loans. | No Mandatory Savings Requirement: Purely a credit-delivery mechanism. | Savings prohibited (unless licensed as a Small Finance Bank or Universal Bank). |\n| Lending Architecture | Group-to-Individual: Bank lends a lump sum to the SHG; SHG decides internal loan allocation among members. | Individual with Joint Guarantee: Bank sanctions individual loans, but all members sign a joint mutual guarantee. | Direct institutional lending to individual women with center-level weekly/monthly meetings. |\n| Collateral Type | Social Collateral & Internal Group Pressure. | Joint Mutual Cross-Liability Guarantee. | Collateral-Free (governed by RBI 2022 Master Direction). |

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps\n- Trap 1: Household Income Ceiling: Under the 2022 RBI Master Direction, the microfinance income cap is ₹3.00 Lakh per annum uniformly for both rural and urban households (earlier it was ₹1.25L rural / ₹2.00L urban).\n- Trap 2: Maximum Debt Limit: Total monthly loan repayments (including principal and interest across all existing loans) cannot exceed 50% of the monthly household income.\n- Trap 3: SHG vs JLG Membership Size: SHG has 10 to 20 members (5 to 20 in difficult/desert/hill areas). JLG has 4 to 10 members.\n\n### Cross-Exam Focus\n- NABARD Grade A (ARD & ESI): Top-tier core topic: SHG-BLP genesis (1992), JLG mechanics (tenant farmers), DAY-NRLM interest subvention, RBI 2022 microfinance framework (₹3L income, 50% debt cap, zero prepayment penalty).\n- RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: NBFC-MFI capital requirements, harmonized microfinance framework, systemic risk in microcredit.\n- SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Women empowerment through SHGs, Rajeevika (Rajasthan Grameen Aajeevika Vikas Parishad), NRLM interest subventions.
