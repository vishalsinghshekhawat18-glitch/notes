11:T461,Consider two everyday scenarios:
1. **Scenario A (Current Account)**: You run an electronics shop in Jaipur and want to import ₹50 Lakh worth of computer chips from Taiwan. You walk into your bank with Indian Rupees, and by law, the bank **must convert your Rupees into US Dollars** at the market exchange rate. The government cannot stop you. India has **Full Current Account Convertibility**.
2. **Scenario B (Capital Account)**: You sell your ancestral land in Rajasthan for ₹20 Crore. You want to convert all ₹20 Crore into US Dollars, wire it to Zurich, and buy Swiss sovereign bonds. Can you do this freely? **No.** Under the **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)**, an Indian resident can remit a maximum of **US \$250,000 per financial year** abroad for capital investments. India has **Partial / Calibrated Capital Account Convertibility**.

Why does the RBI restrict capital transfers? Because during a panic, if all wealthy citizens converted their wealth to dollars simultaneously, the Rupee would collapse and the banking system would disintegrate (as happened in the 1997 East Asian Financial Crisis).12:T463,| Dimension | Current Account Convertibility | Capital Account Convertibility (CAC) |

### Why India Adopted a "Calibrated" Approach
The 1997 Asian Crisis (Thailand, Indonesia, South Korea) proved that premature full CAC in countries with fragile domestic banks and high short-term debt causes catastrophic economic collapse. India's cautious, calibrated liberalization (permitting non-debt FDI first, then long-term debt, while retaining prudential limits on volatile debt and retail outflows) protected India during the 1997 Asian Crisis and 2008 Global Financial Crisis.14:T471,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Is the Rupee Fully Convertible on Current Account?**: **YES.** Under Article VIII of the IMF (since August 1994), India cannot restrict payments for imports, services, or travel. The Rupee is 100% convertible on the Current Account.
- **Trap 2: Is the Rupee Fully Convertible on Capital Account?**: **NO.** The Rupee is only *partially* convertible on the Capital Account. Foreigners can invest easily in equity, but domestic citizens face retail caps (LRS US \$250,000), and foreign debt flows face strict RBI caps.
- **Trap 3: FEMA Section 5 vs Section 6**: Section 5 of FEMA guarantees freedom for Current Account transactions (unless restricted by Central Govt for security/morals); Section 6 empowers the RBI to regulate, restrict, or prohibit Capital Account transactions.
