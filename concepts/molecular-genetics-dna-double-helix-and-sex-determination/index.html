Molecular Genetics: DNA Double Helix & Chromosomal Sex Determination

The molecular substrate of heredity: Watson and Crick's DNA double helix (A=T, G≡C), the Central Dogma, and chromosomal (XX female vs XY male) versus environmental sex determination mechanisms.

Intuition & Context

Why Science Completely Clears Mothers of Blame for Having a Girl

In patriarchal societies for thousands of years, unscientific traditions unfairly blamed mothers if they gave birth to daughters instead of sons. Modern genetics exposes how absurd this is: Every single human mother can ONLY give an X-chromosome in her egg (100% X). It is the Father's sperm that carries either an X or a Y. If a father's X-sperm wins the race, the baby is a girl (XXXXXX); if his Y-sperm wins, the baby is a boy (XYXYXY). The father's sperm alone determines the baby's sex with an exact 50:50 mathematical flip of a coin!

How It Works / Mechanism

Environmental vs Chromosomal Sex Determination Spectrum

Comparative Spectrum of Sex Determination:

  1. Human Beings (XX−XYXX-XYXX−XY):
    • Female =XX= XX=XX (Homogametic), Male =XY= XY=XY (Heterogametic)   ⟹  \implies⟹ Father determines sex.
  2. Birds (ZZ−ZWZZ-ZWZZ−ZW):
    • Male =ZZ= ZZ=ZZ (Homogametic), Female =ZW= ZW=ZW (Heterogametic)   ⟹  \implies⟹ Mother determines sex!
  3. Reptiles (Temperature-Dependent):
    • Green Sea Turtles: Hot incubation nests (>31∘C>31^\circ\text{C}>31∘C) hatch 100%100%100% Females; Cool nests (<28∘C<28^\circ\text{C}<28∘C) hatch Males.

  4. Snails (Helix): Non-genetic; individuals can change biological sex dynamically.

Examiner Traps: Father Determines Sex & Bird ZW System

Top 4 Sex Determination Traps:

  1. Father's Genetic Responsibility:
    • Question: "Who genetically determines whether a human newborn will be a boy or a girl?"
    • Answer: THE FATHER (Sperm carries either X or Y chromosome).
  2. Bird Sex Determination Inversion:
    • In humans, the male is XYXYXY (heterogametic).
    • In BIRDS, the FEMALE is ZWZWZW (heterogametic) and the MALE is ZZZZZZ (homogametic)!
  3. Hydrogen Bonds in DNA:
    • Adenine and Thymine share 2 Hydrogen bonds (A=TA=TA=T).
    • Guanine and Cytosine share 3 Hydrogen bonds (G≡CG \equiv CG≡C) (DNA with high G−CG-CG−C content has a higher melting temperature).
