12:T4c6,To grasp Herbert Simon's Bounded Rationality and Satisficing, picture the District Collector of Udaipur during midnight torrential flash floods near the Ahar River.

Under classical economic theory (**Economic Man / Pure Rationality**), the Collector would:
1. Know the exact water level, structural integrity, and family count of every single flooded hut across 40 square kilometers.
2. Compute every conceivable logistical route, weather permutation, and relief packaging combination.
3. Rank all 50,000 possibilities with mathematical perfection to select the single absolute optimal solution.

In reality, the Collector faces **severe bounded rationality**:
- Incomplete and conflicting radar data.
- Snapped phone lines and road washouts.
- Limited cognitive capacity and immense time pressure.

Instead of wasting critical hours attempting to 'optimize', the Collector exercises bounded rationality: she identifies the first workable plan that mobilizes SDRF rubber boats to the two worst-hit settlements and opens high-ground schools for sheltering. The plan is not mathematically optimal, but it is **'Satisficing' (satisfactory + sufficient)**—it works and saves lives. This is Simon's **'Administrative Man'**.13:T903,Herbert Simon (Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, 1978) rejected classical administrative principles as unscientific 'proverbs' and established decision-making as the primary unit of organizational analysis.

### 1. The Fact-Value Dichotomy
Every administrative decision is an amalgam of two distinct premises:
- **Factual Premise:** Relates to observable reality, means, and cause-effect relationships. It can be empirically tested and validated as TRUE or FALSE (e.g., 'Installing drip irrigation reduces water consumption by 40%'). Simon argued that a science of administration can only be constructed on factual premises.
- **Value Premise:** Relates to ethical choices, ends, and subjective moral preferences. It can only be validated as GOOD or BAD, DESIRABLE or UNDESIRABLE, and cannot be scientifically proved (e.g., 'The state must prioritize subsidies for marginal farmers over corporate agri-businesses').

### 2. Simon's Three Stages of Decision-Making
1. **Intelligence Activity:** Scanning the internal and external environment to identify conditions that require a decision (identifying the problem).
2. **Design Activity:** Inventing, developing, and analyzing possible alternative courses of action.
3. **Choice Activity:** Evaluating the alternatives developed in the design phase and selecting the most satisfactory course of action.
*(Later added: Review Activity - monitoring and post-implementation audit).*

### 3. Economic Man vs. Administrative Man

| Dimension | Classical 'Economic Man' (Optimization) | Simon's 'Administrative Man' (Satisficing) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Model of Rationality** | Total / Objective Rationality | **Bounded Rationality** (Cognitively limited) |
| **Information Available** | Complete, perfect, and instantaneous information | Incomplete, fragmented, and asymmetrical information |
| **Alternative Evaluation** | Analyzes ALL conceivable alternatives simultaneously | Sequential heuristic search; evaluates alternatives one by one |
| **Selection Criterion** | **Optimizing / Maximizing** (The single best outcome) | **Satisficing** (Selects the first alternative meeting aspiration thresholds) |
| **View of Reality** | Operates directly upon total real-world complexity | Operates on a simplified, constructed mental model of reality |14:T5f0,Bounded Rationality occurs because of four insurmountable barriers between human cognition and total objective reality:

```
[Dynamic Environmental Complexity + High Uncertainty]
                           |
                           v
           --- THREE COGNITIVE BOTTLENECK CONSTRAINTS ---
           1. Imperfect / Asymmetric Information
           2. Human Neuro-Biological & Computational Limits
           3. Personal Values, Biases, & Time Scarcity
                           |
                           v
[Construction of a Drastically Simplified Mental Model of Reality]
                           |
                           v
[Sequential Search Strategy: Evaluate Alternative A -> Alternative B]
                           |
                           v
[SATISFICING: Selection of the First 'Good Enough' Solution]
```

### Simon's Six Typologies of Rationality
Simon demonstrated that 'rationality' is not uniform:
1. **Objectively Rational:** If it is in fact the correct behavior for maximizing given values in a given situation.
2. **Subjectively Rational:** If it maximizes attainment relative to the actual knowledge of the subject.
3. **Consciously Rational:** Where adjustment of means to ends is a conscious thought process.
4. **Deliberately Rational:** To the degree that the adjustment of means to ends is purposely brought about.
5. **Organizationally Rational:** If it is oriented to the organization's goals.
6. **Personally Rational:** If it is oriented to the individual's private goals.15:T455,### Architectural Breakdown: Herbert Simon's Decision-Making Model
- **Core Thesis:** Decision-making is the core unit of administrative science; classical principles are mere folk 'proverbs'.
- **Fact-Value Dichotomy:**
  * Facts: Empirically verifiable, true/false, relate to means and scientific administration.
  * Values: Ethical imperatives, desirable/undesirable, relate to political ends.
- **Three Decision-Making Stages:**
  1. *Intelligence Activity:* Scanning environment for problem recognition.
  2. *Design Activity:* Generating and developing potential policy alternatives.
  3. *Choice Activity:* Evaluating alternatives and selecting a satisficing path.
- **Rationality Spectrum:**
  * Objective Rationality (Economic Man): Omniscient optimization across all possibilities.
  * Bounded Rationality (Administrative Man): Cognitive and informational boundaries compel heuristic search and **Satisficing** (selecting the first 'good enough' option).
- **Types of Decisions:** Programmed (routine, repetitive, standard procedures) vs Non-Programmed (unstructured, novel, complex policy
