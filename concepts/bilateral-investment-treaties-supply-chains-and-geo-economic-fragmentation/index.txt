11:T50d,Imagine you run a multinational electronics company in 2015:
- **The Hyper-Efficient Model (Pre-2020 Globalisation)**:
  - You put 100% of your smartphone manufacturing in a single mega-city in China because labor is 10% cheaper and parts arrive in 20 minutes (**Pure Cost Efficiency**).
  - In 2020, a global pandemic locks down that single city for 6 months, and you cannot ship a single phone worldwide (**Catastrophic Supply Chain Collapse**).

- **The Resilient Model ("China + 1" / Friend-Shoring)**:
  - In 2025, you build 40% of your phones in India (backed by **Production Linked Incentive [PLI]**), 30% in Vietnam, and 30% in Mexico.
  - Your unit production cost is 2% higher, but your supply chain can survive wars, shipping choke-points, and trade embargoes (**Resilience over Efficiency**).

Similarly, when multinational investors bring billions of dollars into India to build factories, how does the government balance **protecting foreign capital** with **protecting India's sovereign right to tax companies and protect public health**?

This is why India re-engineered its **Model Bilateral Investment Treaty (Model BIT 2015)** to ensure foreign corporations cannot drag the Indian state to secret international arbitral tribunals whenever parliament passes a new public law.12:T771,### 1. India's Model BIT (2015) vs Legacy 1st-Generation BITs

| Treaty Dimension | Legacy Indian BITs (Pre-2015) | India's Model BIT (2015 Edition) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dispute Settlement (ISDS)** | Foreign investors could directly drag Indian government to international arbitration (UNCITRAL/ICSID) at any time. | **Exhaustion of Local Remedies**: Foreign investors must pursue litigation in Indian domestic courts/tribunals for **at least 5 years** before approaching ISDS. |
| **Taxation & Sovereign Rights** | Silent on taxation; led to massive arbitral awards against India (*Vodafone*, *Cairn Energy*). | **Strictly Excluded**: Taxation, government subsidies, compulsory licensing, and public health/environmental laws are immune from BIT arbitration. |
| **Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN)** | Broad MFN clauses allowed investors to "treaty-shop" favorable provisions from other bilateral treaties. | **MFN Clause Completely Abolished** to prevent backdoor legal arbitrage. |
| **Enterprise Definition** | "Asset-based" definition (any paper asset counted as foreign investment). | "**Enterprise-based**" definition (requires real commercial operations, physical assets, and substantial economic presence in India). |\n
### 2. Modern Geo-Economic Terminology Guide
- **Off-Shoring**: Moving production to low-cost foreign countries regardless of geopolitical alignment (traditional 1990s globalisation).
- **Near-Shoring**: Relocating production geographically closer to home markets (e.g. US firms moving to Mexico; European firms moving to Eastern Europe).
- **Friend-Shoring (Janet Yellen)**: Restricting sourcing and manufacturing to nations with shared democratic values and low geopolitical risk (e.g. US/European tech expanding in India).
- **Geo-Economic Fragmentation**: Splitting the global economy into rival technological, monetary, and trade blocs (e.g. US/Allies vs China/Russia).13:T6c3,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Model BIT 2015 Domestic Remedy Rule**: The Model BIT mandates that a foreign investor must exhaust domestic judicial remedies for **at least 5 years** before approaching an international ISDS arbitral tribunal.
- **Trap 2: MFN in Model BIT 2015**: The Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN) clause was **completely removed** in the 2015 Model BIT; it is NOT present.
- **Trap 3: Supply Chain Resilience Initiative (SCRI) Members**: The three founding nations of SCRI are **India, Japan, and Australia** (the Quad minus the United States).
- **Trap 4: KABIL Joint Venture Constituents**: KABIL is a joint venture of three CPSEs: **NALCO, Hindustan Copper Ltd (HCL), and Mineral Exploration Corporation Ltd (MECL)**.
