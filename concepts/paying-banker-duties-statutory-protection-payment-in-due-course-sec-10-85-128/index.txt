11:T767,### The 4 Mandatory Pillars of 'Payment in Due Course' (Section 10)
To claim statutory protection under Sections 85, 85A, 89, or 128, the paying banker must satisfy ALL four conditions:
1. **In Accordance with Apparent Tenor**: Payment must strictly match the instrument's face (e.g. not paying a post-dated cheque before its date, not paying a stale cheque after 3 months, not paying cash for a crossed cheque).
2. **In Good Faith**: Payment made honestly without collusion or fraud.
3. **Without Negligence**: Exercising standard banking prudence and reasonable care (e.g. checking signature of drawer, matching words and figures, checking apparent alterations).
4. **To the Person in Possession under Reasonable Circumstances**: No suspicious grounds to suspect the presenter's title.

### Comprehensive Comparative Matrix: Paying Banker Statutory Protections

| Instrument Type | Relevant Section | Nature of Protection | Essential Conditions for Protection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Order Cheque** | Section 85(1) | Protected against **forged endorsement** of payee / endorsee | Endorsement must purport to be regular; Payment in Due Course (Sec 10) |
| **Bearer Cheque** | Section 85(2) | **'Once a Bearer, always a Bearer'**; bank discharged upon payment to bearer | Cheque was originally drawn as bearer; bank need not examine any endorsements |

- **Trigger**: Presentation of cheque through clearing (CTS) or at branch counter.
- **Verification Standard**: Verification of drawer's signature is mandatory; verification of payee's endorsement signature is waived under Sec 85(1) if apparently regular.
- **Discrepancy Rule (Section 18)**: If amount in words and figures differs, the amount in **words** is legally payable, though banks standardly return it with memo 'Amount in words and figures differs' as a prudent measure.13:T4c2,### High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams
1. **'Once a Bearer, Always a Bearer' (Section 85(2))**: If Drawer writes a bearer cheque, and Payee writes *'Pay to Suresh'* (special endorsement) on the back, the paying bank can STILL pay cash to any bearer who walks into the bank. The paying bank is completely protected under Section 85(2). The special endorsement does not affect the paying banker.
2. **Paying Post-Dated Cheque Trap**: Paying a post-dated cheque before its stated date is **NEVER a payment in due course**. If a bank pays a PDC on the 10th when dated the 25th, and as a result another legitimate cheque drawn on the 12th bounces, the bank is liable for damages for wrongful dishonour under Section 31.
3. **Forged Drawer Signature Trap**: Section 85 protects ONLY against forged *endorsements*, NEVER against a forged *drawer's signature*. Payment of a cheque with a forged drawer signature is a complete nullity and the bank must refund the money to the customer.
4. **Counter Cash Payment of Crossed Cheque (Section 129)**: If a paying teller pays cash across the counter for a crossed cheque, statutory protection under Section 128 is completely lost, and the bank is directly liable to the true owner.14:T42d,### Architectural Matrix: Paying Banker Protections & Duties (NI Act)
- **Statutory Pillars of Protection**:
  - **Section 10**: Payment in Due Course (Apparent tenor, good faith, without negligence).
  - **Section 31**: Statutory duty to honour customer cheques (liable in damages for wrongful dishonour).
  - **Section 85(1)**: Order Cheques — immunity against forged endorsements.
  - **Section 85(2)**: Bearer Cheques — absolute immunity upon payment to bearer ('Once a bearer always a bearer').
  - **Section 85A**: Demand Drafts — immunity against forged payee endorsements.
  - **Section 89**: Material Alteration — immunity where alteration is non-apparent.
  - **Section 128**: Crossed Cheques — immunity when paid to a banker in due course.
- **Absolute Banker Liabilities (No Protection)**:
  - Forged signature of Drawer (*Bihta Cooperative Union*).
  - Payment of post-dated cheque before stated date.
  - Payment across counter of a crossed cheque (Section 129).
  - Payment after receiving Stop Payment notice, Death notice, or Garnishee
