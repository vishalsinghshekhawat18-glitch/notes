11:T40b,Consider two foreign investors putting money into India:
1. **Investor A (FDI)**: Japanese auto giant Suzuki invests ₹10,000 Crore to build a mega car manufacturing plant in Gujarat. Suzuki hires 5,000 workers, trains engineers, and builds physical machinery. Can Suzuki press a button on a smartphone and pull its ₹10,000 Crore out of India tomorrow morning? **No.** The factory is physically bolted to Indian soil. This is **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: stable, non-debt creating, and long-term.
2. **Investor B (FPI)**: A hedge fund in New York buys ₹100 Crore worth of shares in Indian IT companies on the National Stock Exchange. Three weeks later, the US Federal Reserve raises interest rates. The hedge fund clicks "Sell" on its terminal, converts Rupees to US Dollars, and leaves India in **30 seconds**. This is **Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI / "Hot Money")**: highly liquid, volatile, and reversible.

Developing nations welcome FPI for stock market liquidity, but rely on FDI for enduring industrial growth.12:T52b,| Dimension | Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) | Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) |

### 1. Non-Resident Indian (NRI) Deposit Schemes
- **Non-Resident External (NRE) Account**: Rupee-denominated account. Fully repatriable (both principal and interest). Exchange rate risk borne by the NRI depositor.
- **Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) [FCNR-B] Account**: Denominated in designated foreign currencies (USD, GBP, EUR, JPY). Fully repatriable. **Exchange rate risk borne by the Indian commercial bank**.
- **Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) Account**: Rupee-denominated for managing legitimate income earned in India (rent, pension). Restricted repatriability (up to US \\USD 1 Millionillion per financial year).

### 2. External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs)
Indian corporations borrow from foreign lenders (commercial banks, bond markets) to fund capital expenditure due to lower foreign interest rates.
