### Himalayan Arc vs. Peninsular Plateau Comparison

| Attribute | The Himalayan Mountain System | The Peninsular Plateau Shield |
|---|---|---|
| **Geological Age** | Youngest mountain chain on Earth (Tertiary/Alpine orogeny, ~50 Ma) | Archaean cratonic basement (>3.5 Ga), one of Earth's oldest shields |
| **Tectonic Stability** | Highly unstable, active neo-tectonic seismicity (Zone IV & V) | Rigid and stable; rare intra-plate seismic activity (Koyna, Latur) |
| **Lithology** | Marine sedimentary rocks (Tethyan limestone), schist, granites | Plutonic Archaean granites, gneisses, charnockites, Deccan basalt |
| **Geomorphology** | High relief, youthful topography, deep V-gorges, waterfalls, scree slopes | Senile, mature topography, broad shallow valleys, relict rounded hills |
| **Drainage System** | Antecedent, perennial, snow/glacier fed (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra) | Consequent, superimposed, seasonal rain-fed (Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery) |

### The North-to-South Morphological Transect of the Northern Plains
1. **Bhabar (8–10 km wide):** Runs parallel to Shiwalik foothills. Composed of coarse unassorted gravel and boulders deposited by torrential hill streams. Extremely high porosity $\to$ **streams sink underground and disappear from the surface**.
2. **Tarai (10–20 km wide):** South of Bhabar. Zone where subterranean streams re-emerge without distinct channels, creating an ill-drained, marshy, humid tract with dense sal forests and high biodiversity (e.g. Dudhwa, Corbett).
3. **Bhangar:** Older alluvium deposited above the flood level of rivers, forming elevated river terraces. Characterized by calcareous concretions known as **Kankar**.
4. **Khadar (Betlands):** Newer alluvium deposited annually during floods on the active floodplains. Highly fertile, fine silt, ideal for intensive cultivation.13:Tfcf,### Major Structural Thrust Faults of the Himalayas
From North to South, the Himalayan ranges are separated by regional fault lines:
1. **Indus-Tsangpo Suture Zone (ITSZ):** The actual collision boundary where the Indian Plate subducted beneath the Eurasian Plate.
2. **Main Central Thrust (MCT):** Separates the Greater Himalayas (crystalline granites/gneisses) from the Lesser Himalayas.
3. **Main Boundary Thrust (MBT):** Separates the Lesser Himalayas (sedimentary rocks) from the Outer Shiwalik hills.
4. **Himalayan Frontal Fault (HFF):** Separates the Shiwaliks from the undeformed Indo-Gangetic alluvium.

### Ghats Comparison (Western vs Eastern)
- **Western Ghats (Sahyadris):** Continuous escarpment breached only by Palghat, Thalghat, and Bhorghat passes. Average height $1,200\text{ m}$. Highest peak is **Anamudi ($2,695\text{ m}$)** on Anaimalai Hills. Causes heavy orographic rainfall on windward western slopes.
- **Eastern Ghats:** Discontinuous, highly dissected mountain chain breached by Godavari, Krishna, and Mahanadi rivers. Lower height (~$600\text{ m}$). Highest peak is **Arma Konda ($1,680\text{ m}$)** or Jindhagada ($1,690\text{ m}$).

### Maritime Straits, Channels & Island Topography (Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea)
- **Palk Strait & Gulf of Mannar:** Shallow narrow sea separating Tamil Nadu (India) from Jaffna Peninsula (Sri Lanka); Adam's Bridge (Rama Setu) submerged limestone shoals.
- **Ten Degree Channel ($10^\circ\text{ N}$):** Bisects the Andaman and Nicobar archipelago, separating **Little Andaman** (south of Andaman group) from **Car Nicobar** (north of Nicobar group); ~150 km wide oceanic waterway.
- **Duncan Passage:** Separates **South Andaman** (Rutland Island) from **Little Andaman**.
- **Grand Channel (Great Channel / 6° Channel):** Separates **Great Nicobar** (Indira Point at 6°45' N) from **Sumatra, Indonesia** (Aceh / Pulau Rondo); vital entrance to Strait of Malacca.
- **Nine Degree Channel ($9^\circ\text{ N}$):** Separates **Minicoy Island** (southernmost Lakshadweep atoll) from the main **Lakshadweep archipelago** (Kalpeni/Suheli).
- **Eight Degree Channel ($8^\circ\text{ N}$):** Separates **Minicoy Island (India)** from **the Maldives** (Ihavandhippolhu Atoll).
- **Andaman & Nicobar Morphology:** Submerged southward extension of the Tertiary Arakan Yoma fold mountains of Myanmar; highest peak is **Saddle Peak ($732\text{ m}$)** in North Andaman; **Barren Island** (only active volcano in South Asia) and **Narcondam Island** (dormant volcanic cone).
- **Lakshadweep Archipelago Morphology:** Entirely formed of biogenic **coral atolls, reefs, and submerged shoals** resting on the Chagos-Laccadive submarine ridge; Kavaratti is administrative capital; Pitti Island is an uninhabited bird sanctuary.

### Strategic Land Frontiers & Chokepoints
1. **Maitri Setu (Friendship Bridge over Feni River):** Built over the **Feni River** connecting Sabroom in South Tripura with Ramgarh in Bangladesh; provides Northeast India direct access to **Chittagong Port** (just 72 km away), turning Tripura into the 'Gateway of Northeast India' and bypassing the circuitous chicken's neck corridor.
2. **Siliguri Corridor (The Chicken's Neck):** A narrow strip of land in West Bengal, merely **20 to 22 km wide** at its narrowest, connecting the Indian mainland with the eight northeastern states; flanked by Nepal to the northwest, Bangladesh to the southeast, and Bhutan/China (Chumbi Valley / Doklam plateau) to the northeast; critical strategic lifelines (railways, pipelines, NH-27) pass through this tight bottleneck.
3. **Tawang & Arunachal Geopolitics (The McMahon Line):** Tawang district in western Arunachal Pradesh borders Tibet (China) and Bhutan; home to the historic **Tawang Monastery** (second largest Buddhist monastery in the world after Potala in Lhasa) and birthplace of the 6th Dalai Lama (Tsangyang Gyatso); China claims it under 'Southern Tibet' (*Zangnan*), disputing the **McMahon Line** delineated in the 1914 Simla Convention between British India and Tibet.14:T102f,### Frequent Examiner Traps
1. **Disappearance of Streams Trap:** Streams disappear in the **BHABAR belt** due to porous boulders and coarse gravel, and re-emerge in the **TARAI belt**. Examiners frequently invert this and claim streams vanish in the Tarai.
2. **North-South vs East-West Extent Trap:** India's North-South extent ($3,214\text{ km}$) is GREATER than its East-West extent ($2,933\text{ km}$), even though both span approximately $29^\circ-30^\circ$. Reason: meridians of longitude converge towards the poles, reducing distance per degree of longitude away from the equator, whereas parallels of latitude maintain an almost constant distance (~$111\text{ km}/^\circ$).
3. **Southernmost Point Trap:** Mainland India's southernmost point is **Cape Comorin (Kanyakumari, $8^\circ 4'\text{N}$)**, but the Republic of India's absolute southernmost point is **Indira Point (Pygmalion Point, $6^\circ 45'\text{N}$)** in the Great Nicobar island.
4. **Highest Peak Siting Trap:** The highest peak of South India and the Western Ghats is **Anamudi (2,695 m)** in Kerala (Anaimalai Hills), NOT Doddabetta (which is 2,637 m and located in the Nilgiris).
5. **Nilgiri Junction:** The Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats meet at the **Nilgiri Hills** (Blue Mountains).
6. **Duns vs Chos:** Duns are longitudinal structural valleys between Lesser Himalayas and Shiwaliks (Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun); Chos are torrential seasonal gullies eroding Shiwalik slopes in Punjab and Haryana.
7. **Tropic of Cancer & Standard Meridian (IST) Intersection Trap:** Tropic of Cancer ($23^\circ 30'\text{N}$) crosses **8 States** (Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram); Standard Meridian ($82^\circ 30'\text{E}$) crosses **5 States** (UP, MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh). The two lines intersect specifically in **Chhattisgarh** (Koriya/Surajpur district).
8. **Himalayan Regional Formations Trap:** **Karewas** are lacustrine (lake-bed) deposits in the Kashmir valley uniquely suited for **Zafran (Saffron)**; **Duars** are floodplain alluvial formations in Darjeeling/Sikkim replacing Shiwaliks (which are absent there) and ideal for tea cultivation; **Mizoram** is geologically designated as the **'Molasses Basin'** formed of unconsolidated deposits.

### Strategic Lifelines of National Economy (NCERT Class 10 Transport Matrix)
- **Highway Corridors:** Golden Quadrilateral connects Delhi-Mumbai-Chennai-Kolkata (6 lanes, NHAI); North-South Corridor connects **Srinagar (J&K) to Kanyakumari (TN)**; East-West Corridor connects **Silchar (Assam) to Porbandar (Gujarat)**.
- **Inland National Waterways (Inland Waterways Authority of India - IWAI):**
  - **NW-1:** Ganga River from **Prayagraj to Haldia** ($1,620\text{ km}$) — India's longest inland waterway.
  - **NW-2:** Brahmaputra River from **Sadiya to Dhubri** ($891\text{ km}$).
  - **NW-3:** West-Coast Canal in Kerala (**Kottapuram to Kollam**, along with Champakkara & Udyogmandal canals, $205\text{ km}$).
- **Major Sea Ports Matrix:**
  - **Kandla (Deendayal Port, Gujarat):** First post-independence tidal port developed to substitute Karachi port; serves northern industrial hinterland.
  - **Mumbai & JNPT (Nhava Sheva):** Largest natural harbor port; JNPT built as decongestion container port.
  - **Marmagao (Goa):** Accounts for ~50% of India's iron ore exports.
  - **New Mangalore (Karnataka):** Exports Kudremukh iron ore slurry.
  - **Kochi (Kerala):** Natural harbor situated at entrance of Vembanad lagoon.
  - **Visakhapatnam (AP):** Deepest landlocked, protected harbor on the east coast.
  - **Paradip (Odisha):** Specialized iron ore export hub for Jharkhand-Odisha belt.
  - **Kolkata & Haldia (WB):** Kolkata is a tidal **inland riverine port** on Hooghly requiring constant dredging; Haldia acts as its downstream subsidiary.
