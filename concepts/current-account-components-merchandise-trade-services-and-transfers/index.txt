### Numerical Example of Current Account Balance Calculation
Suppose India's external accounts in a given year show the following figures:
- Merchandise Exports ($X_g$): **+US \\USD 450 Billionillion**
- Merchandise Imports ($M_g$): **-US \\USD 700 Billionillion**
- $\\implies$ **Merchandise Trade Balance = -US \\USD 250 Billionillion** (Trade Deficit)
- Net Services Exports (Software, BPO, Travel): **+US \\USD 160 Billionillion**
- Net Primary Income (Interest/Dividend payments to foreign investors): **-US \\USD 35 Billionillion**
- Net Secondary Income (Workers' Remittances from diaspora): **+US \\USD 115 Billionillion**

$$\\begin{aligned}
CAB &= \\text{Trade Balance} + \\text{Net Services} + \\text{Net Primary Income} + \\text{Net Secondary Income} \\
&= (-250) + (+160) + (-35) + (+115) \\
&= -250 + 240 \\
&= -10 \\text{ Billion USD} \\quad (\\text{Current Account Deficit of US \\USD 10 Billionillion})
\\end{aligned}$$
*Result*: Despite a massive **US \\USD 250 Billionillion Trade Deficit**, India's actual **Current Account Deficit is only US \\USD 10 Billionillion**!13:T481,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Where Do Remittances Go?**: Remittances sent by NRIs to their families are recorded under **Secondary Income in the Current Account** (they are unrequited transfers, not loans). However, if an NRI deposits money into an **NRE/FCNR bank account** in India to earn interest, it is recorded in the **Financial/Capital Account** under Banking Capital!
- **Trap 2: Software Exports Are Invisibles**: Software exports do not pass through customs ports on physical ships; they are transmitted digitally and recorded under **Services (Invisibles) in the Current Account**.
- **Trap 3: Dividend Payments Abroad**: When an Indian subsidiary of a multinational (e.g., Samsung India) repatriates profits/dividends to its parent in Seoul, it is recorded as a **Debit entry under Primary Income in the Current Account**, NOT in the Capital Account!
