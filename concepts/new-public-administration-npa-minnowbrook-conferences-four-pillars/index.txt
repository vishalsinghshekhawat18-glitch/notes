12:T554,To understand New Public Administration (NPA), visualize the implementation of subsidized food security under the *Indira Rasoi Yojana* (hot meals at ₹8) or targeted nutrition interventions for the severely malnourished Sahariya Primitive Tribal Group in Kishanganj, Baran district.

Under classical administrative theory, the civil servant's sole duty is **Economy and Efficiency**: procure the cheapest grains at the lowest tender, distribute them at minimum operational cost, and close the ledger with zero fiscal overrun. If impoverished tribal children cannot reach the distribution center because they lack bus fare, the classical administrator shrugs—procedural efficiency was achieved.

**New Public Administration furiously rejects this callous neutrality.**
NPA asserts that administration cannot be value-neutral. The administrator must become an active **advocate for the poor and marginalized**:
1. **Relevance:** Does the meal scheme actually alleviate starvation among Sahariyas?
2. **Values:** Human dignity and the constitutional right to food supersede balance-sheet austerity.
3. **Social Equity:** The state must deliberately discriminate in favor of the most vulnerable (*distributive justice*).
4. **Change:** Administrative structures must actively demolish feudal and caste barriers rather than complacently preserving the social order.13:Tae8,NPA arose in the late 1960s when the United States was rocked by the Vietnam War, civil rights riots, racial inequality, and student protests, while mainstream academic public administration remained obsessed with sterile statistical modeling and value-neutral efficiency.

### 1. The Four Cardinal Pillars of NPA (H. George Frederickson)
1. **Relevance:** Administration must actively address real-world human crises—poverty, urban blight, social inequality, and ecological collapse—rather than hiding behind esoteric theoretical abstractions.
2. **Values:** Direct repudiation of Herbert Simon's logical positivism and the Politics-Administration dichotomy. NPA openly proclaims that public administration is fundamentally **value-laden, normative, and political**.
3. **Social Equity:** The signature contribution of NPA. Traditional administration served the organized, wealthy, and powerful elite under the guise of efficiency. NPA champions **distributive justice (Rawlsian equity)**, demanding that public administrators act as committed champions for the disadvantaged, unorganized, and marginalized.
4. **Change:** Bureaucracy is innately conservative and resistant to change. NPA insists on administrative dynamic adaptability, structural debureaucratization, and institutional transformation.
*(A fifth pillar frequently included is **Client-Orientation / Citizen Empowerment**: treating the citizen not as an administrative subject, but as a recipient with a right to co-determine public services).*

### Theoretical Lineage and Predecessors of Minnowbrook I:
1. **The Honey Report on Higher Education for Public Service (1967):** Chaired by John C. Honey; revealed widespread institutional inadequacy, shortage of funds, and lack of relevance in university public administration programs.
2. **The Philadelphia Conference on the Theory and Practice of Public Administration (1967):** Organised by James C. Charlesworth; highlighted the identity crisis of the discipline and urged greater normative focus on social problems.
3. **Minnowbrook I (1968):** Crystallized these rumblings into a formal generational movement.15:T4a8,### Architectural Breakdown: New Public Administration (NPA)
- **Genealogy & Milestones:**
  * Honey Report on Higher Education for Public Service (1967): Identified disciplinary funding and relevance deficits.
  * Philadelphia Conference (1967, Charlesworth): Urged focus on social issues.
  * Minnowbrook I (1968, Dwight Waldo): Radical, anti-positivist youth manifesto.
  * Landmark Publications: Frank Marini (1971) & H. George Frederickson (1980).
- **The Four Cardinal Pillars (Frederickson):**
  1. *Relevance:* Targeting real-world social problems rather than ivory-tower abstractions.
  2. *Values:* Rejecting value-neutrality; administration is fundamentally political and moral.
  3. *Social Equity:* The 3rd Pillar; distributive justice protecting the weak against elite capture.
  4. *Change:* Overcoming bureaucratic conservatism to drive progressive institutional transformation.
- **Minnowbrook Generational Continuum:**
  * I (1968, Waldo): Radical, anti-positivist, normative, equity-driven.
  * II (1988, Mayer): Moderate, interdisciplinary, pragmatic, acknowledging market forces.
