Electromagnets vs Permanent Magnets: Core Materials (Soft Iron vs Alnico) & Uses

The physics of temporary current-driven electromagnets vs permanent ferromagnetic magnets, hysteresis, retentivity, coercivity, and core material selection.

Intuition & Context

How a Scrapyard Crane Picks Up a 20-Ton Truck and Drops It on Command

In an automotive scrapyard, a giant metal disc on a crane hovers over a crushed 20-ton steel truck. The crane operator flips an electrical switch: the disc instantly becomes a monster magnet, lifting the 20-ton truck high into the air! The crane swings over a recycling shredder, and the operator flips the switch OFF: instantly, the magnetism vanishes to zero, and the truck drops cleanly into the shredder. If the crane used a permanent magnet, it could never let go of the truck!

Core Principle

Electromagnet vs Permanent Magnet Comparison Matrix

Comparison of Magnet Types

Dimension Electromagnet (Temporary Magnet) Permanent Magnet
Magnetism Nature Temporary: Lasts only while electric current flows. Permanent: Retains magnetism indefinitely.
Control of Strength Easily adjusted by varying current (III) or turns (NNN). Fixed strength; cannot be easily varied.
Control of Polarity North and South poles can be reversed by reversing current direction. Fixed polarity; cannot be reversed easily.
Core Material Used Soft Iron (Pure Iron / Silicon steel). Steel, ALNICO (Al-Ni-Co), Neodymium (NdFeB).
Magnetic Retentivity Very LOW Retentivity (Demagnetizes instantly when current stops). Very HIGH Retentivity (Retains strong magnetic flux).
Coercivity Very LOW Coercivity (Small energy loss in AC cycles). Very HIGH Coercivity (Resists demagnetization from heat/impact).
Master Applications Scrapyard cranes, Electric bells, Relays, MRI, Maglev, Particle accelerators. Magnetic compasses, Loudspeaker cones, Refrigerator door gaskets, Electric guitar pickups.
How It Works / Mechanism

Magnetic Hysteresis: Why Soft Iron is Used for Electromagnet Cores

The Hysteresis Loop (B−HB-HB−H Curve) Physics:

  1. Retentivity (Remanence): The magnetic flux density BBB left inside the core when external magnetizing field HHH is reduced to zero.
  2. Coercivity: The reverse magnetic field required to reduce the residual magnetization back to zero.

Why Steel is NEVER Used for Electromagnets (and Soft Iron is Never Used for Permanent Magnets):

  • Soft Iron: Has a narrow, tall hysteresis loop with extremely low coercivity   ⟹  \implies⟹ Magnetizes instantly to high saturation, and demagnetizes completely to zero the microsecond current is cut.
  • Steel / Alnico: Has high retentivity and huge coercivity   ⟹  \implies⟹ Once magnetized, it locks its magnetic domains permanently, refusing to demagnetize even when current is removed.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps: Electric Bell Mechanism & Core Material Selection

High-Yield Exam Traps:

  1. The Electric Bell Mechanism:
    • Uses an Electromagnet with a Soft Iron armature.
    • When you press the button, current flows   ⟹  \implies⟹ Electromagnet attracts the soft iron striker   ⟹  \implies⟹ Striker hits the brass gong ("Ding!").
    • The striker movement breaks the circuit contact screw   ⟹  \implies⟹ Current stops   ⟹  \implies⟹ Electromagnet loses magnetism instantly   ⟹  \implies⟹ Spring pulls striker back   ⟹  \implies⟹ Circuit reconnects   ⟹  \implies⟹ Cycle repeats rapidly producing the continuous "Brrrrring!".
    • Trap: If steel were used instead of soft iron, the striker would stick permanently to the electromagnet after the first strike and stop ringing!
  2. Alnico Composition: Aluminium (8%8%8%) +++ Nickel (14%14%14%) +++ Cobalt (24%24%24%) +++ Iron (50%50%50%).
