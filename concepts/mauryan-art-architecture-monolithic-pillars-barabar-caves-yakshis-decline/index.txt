| Artistic Category | Architectural / Sculptural Forms | Primary Sites & Examples | Key Stylistic & Aesthetic Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Court Art (Imperial Patronage)** | **Monolithic Pillars** | Sarnath, Rampurva, Lauriya-Nandangarh, Sanchi, Delhi-Topra | Monolithic shaft of **grey Chunar sandstone**; mirror-like glass polish (*Mauryan Polish*); bell-shaped inverted lotus capital; abacus with dynamic animals. |
| **Court Art (Rock-Cut Caves)** | **Ajivika Rock Caves** | **Barabar Hills** (Sudama, Lomas Rishi, Karna Chaupar) & **Nagarjuni Hills** (Gopika) | Monolithic excavation inside granite hills; barrel-vaulted ceilings; mirror-polished interior walls; earliest horseshoe-shaped **Chaitya arch** with carved elephants at Lomas Rishi. |
| **Court Art (Palaces & Stupas)** | **Pataliputra Wooden Palace** & Early Stupas | Kumrahar (Pataliputra 80-pillared hypostyle hall); Core brick stupas at Sanchi and Bharhut | Described by Megasthenes and Aelian as surpassing the Persian palaces of Susa and Ecbatana; high wooden palisade with iron nails. |
| **Popular Art (Individual / Folk Patronage)** | **Free-standing Yaksha & Yakshi Sculptures** | **Didarganj Yakshi** (Patna), **Parkham Yaksha** (Mathura), Besnagar Yakshi | Monumental, heavy, sensuous human forms carved in the round; holding fly-whisks (*Chauri*); associated with indigenous fertility, water, and tree cults. |
| **Popular Art (Terracottas & Ceramics)** | **NBPW & Terracotta Figurines** | Ahichchhatra, Kausambi, Rajghat, Pataliputra, Taxila | Glossy **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)** luxury tableware; terracotta dancing figures, mother goddesses, and animal toys. |

### The Sarnath Lion Capital: Anatomy of the National Emblem
1. **Shaft:** Monolithic polished Chunar sandstone column (broken).
2. **Lotus Base:** Bell-shaped inverted lotus (*Padma*).
3. **Abacus (Circular Drum):** Carved with four dynamic animals in continuous clockwise motion:
   - **Lion** (*Simha* - courage/royalty)
   - **Elephant** (*Gaja* - Buddha's conception)
   - **Bull** (*Vrisha* - Taurus / Buddha's zodiac)
   - **Horse** (*Kanthaka* - Buddha's Great Renunciation)
   - Separated by four **24-spoked Dharmachakras**.
4. **Crowing Apex:** Four majestic Asiatic lions seated back-to-back roaring across the four cardinal directions (originally topped by a large crowning Dharmachakra of 32 spokes, fragments in Sarnath Museum).

### Theories of Mauryan Imperial Decline: Historiographical Consensus
| Historian / Scholar | Proposed Primary Cause of Decline | Critical Historiographical Assessment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Haraprasad Sastri** | **Brahmanical Reaction / Backlash** | Over-emphasized; Shungas were Brahmanas, but Ashoka’s Dhamma was non-sectarian and did not persecute Brahmanas. |
| **Hemchandra Raychaudhuri** | **Ashoka’s Pacifist Policy** | Ashoka never disbanded the army or abolished capital punishment; but military vigor waned under successors. |
| **D.D. Kosambi** | **Severe Financial Crisis & Debasement** | Corroborated by late Mauryan punch-marked coins showing severe alloy debasement (reduced silver percentage). |
| **Romila Thapar** | **Over-Centralized Bureaucracy & Lack of National Consciousness** | Highly persuasive; the vast empire lacked institutional representative mechanisms; loyalty was to the person of the King, not the State. |
| **General Consensus** | **Weak Successors, Partition & 185 BCE Coup** | Succession disputes between Kunala, Dasharatha, and Samprati; Greek incursions; assassination of Brihadratha by Pushyamitra
