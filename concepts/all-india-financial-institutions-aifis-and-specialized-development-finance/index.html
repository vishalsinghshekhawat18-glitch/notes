All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs) & Specialized Development Finance: NABARD, SIDBI, NHB, EXIM Bank, NaBFID & IFSCA

The statutory mandates, operational models, and economic roles of specialized All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs) regulated and supervised by the Reserve Bank of India under Section 45L of the RBI Act. Covers the five core AIFIs: NABARD (NABARD Act 1981, rural & agri refinance, RIDF), SIDBI (SIDBI Act 1989, MSME credit, MUDRA), NHB (NHB Act 1987, housing finance refinance), EXIM Bank (EXIM Act 1981, cross-border trade finance), and NaBFID (National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development Act, 2021, DFI for long-term infrastructure funding), alongside the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA Act, 2019 / GIFT City unified regulator).

Intuition & Context

Development Financial Institutions: Funding What Ordinary Banks Fear

Why can't commercial banks finance a ₹50,000 Crore high-speed bullet train project or a 30-year deep-sea port?

Because commercial banks rely on short-term deposits (1 to 3 years). If a bank lends depositors' money to a 30-year highway that takes 7 years just to construct and generate toll revenue, the bank faces a massive, fatal Asset-Liability Mismatch (ALM).

Development Financial Institutions (DFIs / AIFIs) exist to solve this market failure. They do not accept retail deposits. Instead, they raise ultra-long-term capital by issuing 20–30 year sovereign-backed bonds, borrowing from multilateral agencies (World Bank, ADB), and deploying patient capital into infrastructure, agriculture, MSMEs, and international export trade.

Core Principle

The Five Apex All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs)

Detailed Institutional Matrix

  1. NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development - 1982):
    • Established under NABARD Act, 1981 based on the B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD).
    • Mandate: Apex refinance institution for agriculture, allied activities, and rural development; manages the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF); supervises RRBs and Cooperative Banks.
  2. SIDBI (Small Industries Development Bank of India - 1990):
    • Established under SIDBI Act, 1989.
    • Mandate: Promotion, financing, and development of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs); operates MUDRA (Micro Units Development & Refinance Agency) and CGTMSE.
  3. NHB (National Housing Bank - 1988):
    • Established under NHB Act, 1987 based on the C. Rangarajan High-Level Group.
    • Mandate: Apex housing finance refinancing institution (supervisory powers over HFCs transferred to RBI in 2019; NHB remains primary refinancer).
  4. EXIM Bank (Export-Import Bank of India - 1982):
    • Established under EXIM Bank Act, 1981.
    • Mandate: Financing, facilitating, and promoting India's international trade and overseas Lines of Credit (LOCs).
  5. NaBFID (National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development - 2021):
    • Established under NaBFID Act, 2021.
    • Mandate: Dedicated infrastructure DFI to fund the ₹111 Lakh Crore National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP); granted a 10-year income-tax concession and sovereign credit guarantees.
How It Works / Mechanism

IFSCA & GIFT City: India's Unified Offshore Financial Gateway

International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA Act, 2019)

Historically, if an Indian company wanted to raise foreign currency bonds or an international hedge fund wanted to trade Indian derivatives, they did business in Singapore, Dubai, or London because Indian domestic financial regulations were too rigid and taxed in INR.

To onshore this multi-billion-dollar global financial activity:

  1. GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City): Established as India's first International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) in Gandhinagar.
  2. Unified Regulator (IFSCA): Instead of offshore entities having to run between four separate regulators (RBI for foreign exchange, SEBI for stock exchanges, IRDAI for insurance, PFRDA for pensions), IFSCA acts as a single unified statutory regulator exercising the powers of all four domestic regulators within the IFSC zone.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: How many AIFIs are regulated by RBI?: There are currently FIVE AIFIs: NABARD, SIDBI, NHB, EXIM Bank, and NaBFID (NaBFID was added as the 5th AIFI in 2021 under Section 45L).
  • Trap 2: NABARD Committee: NABARD was created on the recommendations of the B. Sivaraman Committee (CRAFICARD) in 1979/1981 (NOT the Narasimham Committee!).
  • Trap 3: NHB Ownership: The RBI sold its entire 100% equity stake in NHB (and NABARD) to the Government of India in 2019 to eliminate conflict of interest (RBI cannot own the banks it regulates).
  • Trap 4: IFSCA Headquarters: The International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA) is headquartered at GIFT City, Gandhinagar, Gujarat.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • NABARD Grade A & RBI Grade B: NABARD Act 1981, Sivaraman Committee, NaBFID Act 2021, AIFI list under Section 45L, and RIDF operations.
  • SEBI Grade A, SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: SIDBI/MUDRA, EXIM Bank, NaBFID mandate, and IFSCA unified regulatory powers.
