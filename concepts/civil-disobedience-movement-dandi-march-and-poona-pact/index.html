Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934): Salt Satyagraha, Round Table Conferences, Communal Award & The Poona Pact

The second nationwide civil disobedience campaign: Lahore Congress (Dec 1929, Jawaharlal Nehru president, Purna Swaraj declaration, Jan 26, 1930 as Independence Day); Gandhi's 11-point ultimatum to Lord Irwin; Dandi March (March 12 to April 6, 1930, 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi with 78 volunteers, breaking the Salt Law); Dharasana Satyagraha (Sarojini Naidu, Webb Miller report); First Round Table Conference (1930, boycotted by INC); Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact, March 5, 1931: release of political prisoners, suspension of CDM); Karachi Congress (March 1931, Sardar Patel, Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme); Second Round Table Conference (1931, Gandhi sole INC representative, failure on communal question); Ramsay MacDonald's Communal Award (Aug 1932, separate electorates for Depressed Classes); Gandhi's fast-unto-death in Yerwada Jail; and the landmark **Poona Pact (Sept 24, 1932)** between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and caste Hindu leaders (Madan Mohan Malaviya signed for caste Hindus), abandoning separate electorates in exchange for increased reserved seats (from 71 to 147 in provincial legislatures).

Intuition & Context

The Alchemy of Salt: Mobilizing a Subcontinent Through the Most Universal Commodity

Why did Mahatma Gandhi choose common salt—a chemical compound of sodium chloride—to launch the greatest challenge to the British Empire since 1857?

When the Congress Working Committee authorized Gandhi to launch Civil Disobedience following the historic Purna Swaraj declaration at Lahore (December 1929), many senior leaders like Motilal Nehru and Jawaharlal Nehru were deeply perplexed by Gandhi's obsession with the Salt Tax. Salt seemed trivial compared to sweeping demands for constitutional sovereignty or land revenue abolition.

Yet, Gandhi's choice was a masterstroke of political genius:

  1. Universal Biological Necessity: Salt was indispensable to every living human and domestic animal. The colonial government's state monopoly and tax on salt fell with regressive cruelty on the poorest of the poor, the landless peasant, and the urban labourer.
  2. Moral Clarity vs Legal Tyranny: By taxing a gift of nature freely crystallizing along thousands of miles of coastline, the British state exposed itself as an extortionist predator. Breaking the salt law carried zero moral ambiguity; it pitted human survival against imperial revenue greed.
  3. Inclusive Non-Violent Mobilization: Manufacturing illegal salt required no arms, no capital, and no foreign training. A handful of seawater boiled on an earthen stove turned every Indian peasant, villager, and homemaker into an active constitutional rebel against the King-Emperor.

From the 240-mile march to Dandi to the brutal lathi charges at Dharasana, and ultimately to the constitutional negotiations of the Round Table Conferences and the Yerwada fast that forged the Poona Pact (1932), this era redefined Indian nationhood, caste representation, and mass defiance.

Core Principle

The Civil Disobedience Matrix: From Dandi to Poona (1929–1934)

1. Chronological Sequence: Road to Civil Disobedience

  • Lahore Session of INC (December 1929): Presided by Jawaharlal Nehru. Passed the historic Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) resolution. Decided that January 26, 1930 would be celebrated across India as Purna Swaraj Day (first Independence Day), unfurling the tricolour on the banks of the Ravi River.
  • Gandhi's 11-Point Ultimatum to Lord Irwin (January 31, 1930): Included demands of general interest (reduce land revenue by 50%, cut military expenditure by 50%, impose protective tariff on foreign cloth, abolish CID, release political prisoners) and specific bourgeois/peasant demands (reduce rupee-sterling exchange ratio to 1s 4d, reserve coastal shipping for Indians, and abolish the Salt Tax and government salt monopoly). Irwin refused to negotiate.
  • The Dandi March (Namak Satyagraha):
    • Dates: Began on March 12, 1930; reached Dandi on April 5, 1930; formally broke the Salt Law by picking up a lump of natural salt on the morning of April 6, 1930.
    • Distance & Volunteers: 240 miles (approx. 385 km) from Sabarmati Ashram (Ahmedabad) to the coastal village of Dandi (Navsari, Gujarat), accompanied by 78 handpicked satyagrahis (ranging from 18 to 61 years old).

2. Regional Spread & Leaders of Salt Satyagraha

The breaking of the salt law at Dandi triggered a nationwide chain reaction of civil disobedience across distinct geographic frontiers:

Region / Province Landmark Action / March Key Leaders & Historic Specifics
Tamil Nadu Vedaranyam Salt March C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) marched from Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) to Vedaranyam on the Tanjore coast (April 1930).
Malabar (Kerala) Payyanur Salt March K. Kelappan (the "Kerala Gandhi") marched from Calicut to Payyanur to manufacture illicit salt.
North-West Frontier Province (NWFP) Peshawar Uprising & Red Shirts Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ("Frontier Gandhi") and his volunteer organization Khudai Khidmatgars ("Servants of God"), also called the Surkh Posh (Red Shirts). The Garhwal Rifles (under Chandra Singh Garhwali) famously refused British orders to fire upon unarmed Pathan demonstrators.
Gujarat (Dharasana) Dharasana Salt Works Raid Sarojini Naidu, Imam Saheb, and Manilal Gandhi (Gandhi's son) led non-violent waves against barbed wire and steel-tipped lathis. Immortalized by American journalist Webb Miller (New Freeman).
North-East (Manipur & Nagaland) Naga National Movement Rani Gaidinliu (barely 13 years old), inspired by cousin Jadonang's Heraka movement, raised the banner of revolt; captured and sentenced to life imprisonment; given the title "Rani" by Jawaharlal Nehru.
Orissa Balasore / Inchudi Gopabandhu Choudhury and Acharya Harihar Das led coastal salt manufacturing at Inchudi.
Bengal & Bihar Anti-Chowkidari Tax Campaign Refusal to pay the hated Chowkidari tax (since salt production was impossible inland in Bihar); in Bengal, anti-Union Board agitation.

3. Diplomatic Negotiations: RTCs, Gandhi-Irwin Pact & Poona Pact

Milestone Date & Venue Key Provisions & Signatories Exam Pitfall & Analytical Significance
First Round Table Conference (RTC) Nov 1930 – Jan 1931 (London) Chaired by British PM Ramsay MacDonald. Attended by Princely States, Muslim League, Hindu Mahasabha, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Depressed Classes). Boycotted by the Indian National Congress (INC). Conference failed completely because the principal political party of India was absent.
Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact) March 5, 1931 (New Delhi) Signed between Mahatma Gandhi and Viceroy Lord Irwin: Government agreed to release all non-violent political prisoners, return confiscated lands not yet sold, permit peaceful picketing of liquor/foreign cloth shops, and allow coastal residents to make salt for domestic consumption. Congress agreed to suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement and participate in the 2nd RTC. Irwin explicitly refused two key Gandhian demands: (1) Public inquiry into police atrocities; (2) Commutation of death sentences of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru.
Karachi Congress Session March 1931 (Karachi) Presided by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel. Ratified the Gandhi-Irwin Pact despite mass grief over the execution of Bhagat Singh (March 23). Adopted two historic resolutions drafted by Nehru: Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme. First time INC defined the socio-economic contours of Swaraj (living wage, state ownership of key industries).
Second Round Table Conference Sept – Dec 1931 (London) Gandhi attended as the sole representative of the INC (alongside Sarojini Naidu, Madan Mohan Malaviya). Deadlocked on the "Minorities Question" due to demands for separate electorates by Ambedkar, Aga Khan, and Europeans. Gandhi returned empty-handed; Lord Willingdon (new Viceroy) unleashed ferocious repression; CDM was resumed (Jan 1932) and finally withdrawn in 1934.
Communal Award (MacDonald Award) August 16, 1932 Announced by British PM Ramsay MacDonald. Extended separate electorates not only to Muslims, Sikhs, and Europeans, but also to the Depressed Classes (Scheduled Castes) as a distinct minority. Gandhi viewed this as a catastrophic British imperial device to permanently divide Hindu society into hostile legislative factions.
The Poona Pact September 24, 1932 (Yerwada Jail, Poona) Gandhi launched a fast-unto-death in Yerwada Jail on Sept 20, 1932. Through intensive mediation (C. Rajagopalachari, Rajendra Prasad, M.C. Rajah), an agreement was reached between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and caste Hindu leaders. Signed by Madan Mohan Malaviya (on behalf of caste Hindus) and Dr. Ambedkar (for Depressed Classes). Abandoned separate electorates for Depressed Classes; accepted Joint Electorates with reserved seats. Seats in provincial legislatures reserved for Depressed Classes increased from 71 (under Award) to 147. In the Central Legislature, 18% of general seats were reserved.
How It Works / Mechanism

Constitutional & Representation Mechanics: Separate vs Joint Electorates & Poona Pact Voting Formula

How the Poona Pact Altered Electoral Architecture

The intellectual and constitutional debate between Mahatma Gandhi and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar at Poona was one of the most consequential turning points in modern Indian jurisprudence:

  1. The Mechanism of Separate Electorates (The MacDonald Formula):

    • Under separate electorates, a legislative constituency is divided purely along communal/caste lines.
    • Only voters belonging to the Depressed Classes could vote to elect a Depressed Class candidate.
    • Gandhi argued that this would legally segregate untouchables from mainstream Hindu society forever, perpetuating social untouchability by giving it a permanent constitutional boundary.

  2. The Mechanism of Joint Electorates with Reservation (The Poona Pact Formula):

    • Under joint electorates, the entire electorate (caste Hindus, Depressed Classes, and others) votes together.
    • However, the seat is reserved exclusively for a candidate from the Depressed Classes.
    • The Primary Election Sub-system: To ensure genuine representation, the Poona Pact devised a two-tier electoral filter:
      Depressed Class Voters Only→Primary ElectionSelect a panel of 4 candidates→General Joint ElectorateFinal Winner\text{Depressed Class Voters Only} \xrightarrow{\text{Primary Election}} \text{Select a panel of 4 candidates} \xrightarrow{\text{General Joint Electorate}} \text{Final Winner}Depressed Class Voters OnlyPrimary Election​Select a panel of 4 candidatesGeneral Joint Electorate​Final Winner
    • The candidate securing the highest votes from the entire joint community in the final election was elected.
    • Quantitative Outcome:
      • Provincial Assembly seats for Depressed Classes doubled: from 71 under the Communal Award to 147 seats under the Poona Pact (e.g., Bengal 30, Madras 30, UP 20, Central Provinces 20, Bombay 15, Bihar/Orissa 18, Punjab 8, Assam 7).
      • Central Legislative Assembly allocation: 18% of the general seats were reserved for Depressed Classes.

Spatial & Strategic Dynamics:

  1. Coastal Encirclement: Because salt was naturally manufactured along both ocean littorals, the movement ringed peninsular India from Karachi and Gujarat around to Malabar, Tamil Nadu, Orissa, and Bengal.
  2. The Inland Adaptation: In landlocked provinces (Bihar, Central Provinces, UP) where salt could not be made, satyagrahis innovated non-violent substitutes—boycotting village Chowkidari taxes and violating colonial Forest Laws.
  3. The Trans-Frontier Mobilization: The conversion of traditionally martial Pathans in the North-West Frontier Province into non-violent Khudai Khidmatgars neutralized Britain's most critical border security zone.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

High-Frequency Examiner Traps & Critical Historical Nuances

Traps Frequently Set by UPSC, State PSC & Banking Boards

  1. The Signatory Trap of the Poona Pact:

    • Trap: Stating that Mahatma Gandhi signed the Poona Pact.
    • Truth: Mahatma Gandhi NEVER signed the Poona Pact! Gandhi was fasting unto death inside Yerwada Central Jail as a prisoner. The pact was signed by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar on behalf of the Depressed Classes, and Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya on behalf of the Caste Hindus (alongside C. Rajagopalachari, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Ghanshyam Das Birla, and others).

  2. The Karachi Session (1931) Triple Anchor:

    • Presided over by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (NOT Jawaharlal Nehru).
    • However, the historic Resolution on Fundamental Rights and the National Economic Programme was drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru.
    • Occurred immediately after the execution of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru (March 23, 1931), where Gandhi was greeted with black flags by the Naujawan Bharat Sabha.

  3. Gandhi-Irwin Pact Concessions vs Refusals:

    • Trap: Believing Irwin agreed to commute Bhagat Singh's sentence or hold an inquiry into police violence.
    • Truth: Irwin categorically rejected both demands. Gandhi signed the pact without securing the commutation of Bhagat Singh's death sentence, an issue fiercely debated in Indian historiography.

  4. Regional Salt Satyagraha Chronology & Geography:

    • Vedaranyam March: Led by C. Rajagopalachari (Trichy to Vedaranyam).
    • Payyanur March: Led by K. Kelappan (Calicut to Payyanur).
    • Peshawar Incident: 2nd Battalion of 18th Royal Garhwal Rifles commanded by Chandra Singh Garhwali refused to fire on unarmed Khudai Khidmatgars.
