11:T8c4,### Core Characteristics of Negotiable Instruments
1. **Free Transferability**: Transferred by mere delivery (if bearer) or by endorsement and delivery (if order) without written assignment notice to the debtor.
2. **Title Cleansing**: Transferee in due course acquires title free from equities and defects affecting previous holders.
3. **Right to Sue in Own Name**: The holder can institute legal recovery proceedings in their own name without joining prior parties.
4. **Statutory Presumptions (Section 118)**: Presumption of consideration, date, time of transfer, order of endorsements, valid stamp, and holder in due course status.

### Comparative Analysis: Promissory Note vs Bill of Exchange

| Feature | Promissory Note (Section 4) | Bill of Exchange (Section 5) | Cheque (Section 6) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature of Mandate** | Unconditional **Promise / Undertaking** to pay | Unconditional **Order / Direction** to pay | Unconditional **Order** on a specified banker |
| **Parties Involved** | 2 Parties: Maker (Debtor) & Payee (Creditor) | 3 Parties: Drawer, Drawee, Payee | 3 Parties: Drawer, Drawee Bank, Payee |
| **Primary Liability** | Maker has **primary and absolute** liability | Drawee becomes primarily liable **only upon Acceptance**; Drawer has secondary liability | Drawee bank pays; Drawer liable upon dishonour |
| **Acceptance Requirement** | No acceptance needed (maker is already debtor) | **Acceptance is mandatory** before drawee is liable | No formal acceptance needed (payable on demand) |
| **Payable on Demand / Sight** | Can be demand or usance (time) | Can be demand or usance (time) | **Always payable on demand only** |
| **Payable to Bearer on Demand?** | **Strictly Prohibited** under Sec 31(2) RBI Act (only RBI/Govt can issue) | **Strictly Prohibited** under Sec 31(1) RBI Act (only RBI can issue) | **Permitted** (bearer cheques are legal) |
| **Protest on Dishonour** | Optional for inland notes; mandatory for foreign | Optional for inland bills; mandatory for foreign | No protest required; bank return memo suffices |
| **Notice of Dishonour** | Not required to maker; required to endorsers | Mandatory to drawer and all endorsers | Statutory notice under Sec 138 (within 30 days) |12:T5e1,The operation of Section 118 and instrument enforcement follows a strict institutional workflow:

```mermaid
graph TD
    A[Plaintiff / Holder Files Suit on Instrument] --> B[Section 118 Statutory Presumptions Triggered]
    B --> C[Court Presumes: Consideration Existed, Date is Valid, Stamp is Genuine, Plaintiff is HDC]
    C --> D{Defendant / Maker Contests}
    D -- No Probable Defense --> E[Summary Decree in Favor of Holder]
    D -- Pleads Lack of Consideration / Fraud --> F[Evidentiary Burden on Defendant]
    F --> G[Defendant Must Rebut Presumption on Preponderance of Probabilities]
    G -- Failed to Rebut --> H[Judgment for Plaintiff / Full Recovery]
    G -- Successfully Rebutted --> I[Burden Shifts Back to Plaintiff to Prove Actual Consideration]
```

- **Trigger**: Production of a signed, valid negotiable instrument in a court of law.
- **Process**: Court applies Section 118 presumptions (a) to (g) automatically without requiring initial proof of consideration or receipt of money from the plaintiff.
- **Authority**: Civil Court (Summary Suit under Order XXXVII CPC) or Criminal Court (Sec 138/139 NI Act).
- **Timeline**: Section 118 presumption operates instantaneously upon filing; defendant must discharge the evidentiary burden during trial.
- **Outcome**: If unrebutted, decree is passed immediately for principal amount plus interest at specified/notified rate (Section 80 prescribes 18% p.a. if no rate is stated).
- **Enforcement**: Attachment and execution under CPC.13:T51d,### High-Yield Traps for Banking Candidates
1. **Section 31 RBI Act vs NI Act Trap**: While Section 4 of NI Act mentions a promissory note payable to bearer, **Section 31 of the RBI Act 1934 overrides it** by stipulating that no person in India other than the RBI or Central Government can draw, accept, make, or issue any bill of exchange or promissory note payable to bearer on demand. Cheques are the sole exception.
2. **Conditional Undertaking Trap**: A document stating *'I promise to pay ₹50,000 thirty days after my marriage to Priya'* is **NOT** a promissory note because marriage is a contingent event that may never occur. However, *'I promise to pay ₹50,000 thirty days after the death of Suresh'* **IS** a valid promissory note because death is a certainty, though the exact time is uncertain.
3. **'I.O.U.' (I Owe You) Trap**: A simple acknowledgement of debt (e.g. *'Mr. B, I owe you ₹20,000'*) is NOT a promissory note because it contains an admission of debt but lacks an **express undertaking/promise to pay**.
4. **Section 118 vs Section 139 Distinction**: Section 118 applies civil presumptions (consideration, date, stamp, time) to all negotiable instruments, whereas Section 139 applies a specific presumption of debt discharge exclusively in criminal complaints under Section 138.14:T49c,### Comprehensive Legal Architecture: NI Act 1881 & Instruments
- **Core Statutory Definitions**:
  - **Section 13(1)**: Negotiable Instrument = Promissory Note, Bill of Exchange, or Cheque payable to order or bearer.
  - **Section 4**: Promissory Note = In writing, signed by maker, unconditional promise, to pay a sum of money only.
  - **Section 5**: Bill of Exchange = In writing, signed by drawer, unconditional order, directing drawee to pay sum of money only.
  - **Section 6**: Cheque = Bill of exchange drawn on specified banker, payable on demand only.
- **Statutory Presumptions (Section 118)**:
  - Consideration (valid value exchanged)
  - Date (instrument drawn on date stated)
  - Time of Acceptance (accepted within reasonable time before maturity)
  - Time of Transfer (transferred before maturity)
  - Order of Endorsements (made in order appearing on instrument)
  - Stamp (properly and sufficiently stamped)
  - HDC Status (holder is holder in due course)
- **Statutory Restrictions**:
  - **Section 31 RBI Act 1934**: Complete bar on private/commercial issuance of Promissory Notes payable to bearer on demand or Bills of Exchange payable to bearer on
