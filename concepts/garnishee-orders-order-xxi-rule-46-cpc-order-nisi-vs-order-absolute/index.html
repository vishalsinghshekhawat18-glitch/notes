Garnishee Orders: Order XXI Rule 46 CPC, Order Nisi vs Order Absolute

The judicial execution process under Order XXI Rule 46 of the Code of Civil Procedure 1908 (CPC), wherein a court attaches debts owed by a third party (the Garnishee / Bank) to a Judgment Debtor (Customer) in satisfaction of a decree obtained by a Judgment Creditor, operating in two stages (Order Nisi calling upon the bank to show cause, followed by Order Absolute directing direct payment into court), attaching only debts currently due or accruing due (debita in praesenti solvendum in futuro).

Intuition & Context

Why Courts Can Intercept Bank Accounts: The Garnishee Mechanism

Suppose 'A' wins a ₹5 Lakh court judgment against 'B', but 'B' refuses to pay. 'A' discovers that 'B' has ₹5 Lakh sitting in a State Bank of India savings account. Can the court order SBI to freeze B's account and hand the money over to 'A'?

Yes! SBI is a Garnishee (a third party holding money belonging to a debtor). Through a Garnishee Order under Order XXI CPC, the court intercepts the debtor-creditor relationship between the bank and 'B', ordering the bank to pay the court directly in satisfaction of the judgment decree.

Core Principle

Two-Stage Process: Order Nisi vs Order Absolute

The Two Sequential Phases of a Garnishee Order

[Judgment Creditor applies to Civil Court for Execution]
                           │
                           ▼
[Stage 1: ORDER NISI Served on Bank Branch]
- Interim injunction freezing the customer's account.
- Bank MUST immediately stop payments from the attached funds.
- Bank files 'Show Cause' reply stating available balance, existing liens, or zero balance.

         ┌─────────────────┴─────────────────┐
         ▼                                   ▼
[Bank Shows Cause: No Funds / Prior Lien]  [Garnishee Order Upheld]
- Court discharges the Garnishee Order.      │
                                             ▼
                                  [Stage 2: ORDER ABSOLUTE]
                                  - Final binding judicial order.
                                  - Bank pays attached funds directly into Court.
                                  - Payment gives bank 100% legal discharge.
How It Works / Mechanism

Master Checklist: What is Attachable vs NOT Attachable?

Master Table of Attachable Assets under Garnishee Order

Account / Asset Category Attachable under Garnishee? Exact Legal Reason
Savings & Current Credit Balances YES Debt currently due from bank to customer
Fixed Deposit (Matured or Not Matured) YES Debitum in praesenti, solvendum in futuro (debt existing now, payable on maturity)
Deposits received AFTER service of Order NO Garnishee attaches only funds present at the exact time of service
Joint Account (A & B) where Order is on A alone NO No mutuality of debt (Hirschorn v Evans); joint funds cannot be attached for individual debt
Partnership Account where Order is on single partner NO Firm assets belong to all partners; cannot be attached for personal judgment
Sole Proprietorship Account where Order is on owner YES Proprietor and firm are legally one and the same entity
Trust Account held by customer as Trustee NO Beneficial ownership belongs to beneficiaries, not judgment debtor
Undisbursed Overdraft / Loan Limit NO Undisbursed credit limit is NOT a debt owed by bank; it is an unavailed lending facility
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps & Specific Sum vs General Orders

Critical Examiner Traps:

  1. Fixed Deposit Not Yet Matured Trap: An un-matured Fixed Deposit IS attachable under a Garnishee order because the debt exists in the present, though payable in the future (debita in praesenti solvendum in futuro). The bank pays the court upon maturity.
  2. Joint Account Garnishee Trap: If a Garnishee Order is in the name of 'A' alone, the bank CANNOT attach a joint account of 'A and B' (Hirschorn v Evans). However, if the order is against 'both A and B', the joint account is fully attachable.
  3. Specific Amount vs Entire Account Trap: If the Garnishee Order specifies an attached amount of ₹50,000 and the account balance is ₹2,00,000, the bank attaches ₹50,000 and the customer is free to operate the remaining ₹1,50,000. If no specific sum is mentioned, the entire balance is frozen.
