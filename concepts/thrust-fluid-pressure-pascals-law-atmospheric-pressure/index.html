Thrust, Fluid Pressure, Pascal's Principle, Hydraulic Machines & Atmospheric Pressure

The physics of force distribution over area (pressure), hydrostatic pressure depth scaling, Pascal's principle in hydraulic multiplication, and atmospheric barometry.

Intuition & Context

Why Camels Have Broad Feet and Needles are Sharp

If an elephant steps on your foot with its broad, padded foot, you feel pressure, but your foot might survive. If a woman in a high-heeled stiletto heel ($1 , \text{cm}^2$ tip) steps on your foot with only $60 , \text{kg}$ weight, the concentrated pressure is immense enough to puncture your skin. In physics, it is not just how hard you push (Force), but how small an area you concentrate that force onto (Pressure =Force/Area= \text{Force}/\text{Area}=Force/Area).

Core Principle

Pressure Applications & Hydrostatic Pressure Architecture

Pressure Formula:

P=F⊥A[SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)=N/m2]P = \frac{F_{\perp}}{A} \quad \left[\text{SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)} = \text{N/m}^2\right]P=AF⊥​​[SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)=N/m2]

Practical Engineering Applications of Area Modulation:

  • Decreasing Area to Maximize Pressure:
    • Sharp knives cut vegetables effortlessly (extremely small blade edge area   ⟹  \implies⟹ huge cutting pressure with tiny hand force).
    • Pointed nails and sewing needle tips enter wood/fabric easily.
  • Increasing Area to Minimize Pressure:
    • Camels walk across desert sands without sinking because their broad, wide hooves spread weight over large area   ⟹  \implies⟹ low ground pressure.
    • Heavy battle tanks and bulldozers use wide continuous caterpillar tracks.
    • Railway tracks rest on broad concrete sleepers to prevent tracks from sinking into ground.
    • School bags have wide shoulder straps so the weight does not dig painfully into shoulders.

Pascal's Law, Hydraulic Multiplication & Barometers

1. Pascal's Principle & The Hydraulic Force Multiplier

In an enclosed hydraulic system filled with oil:
P1=P2  ⟹  F1A1=F2A2  ⟹  F2=F1(A2A1)P_1 = P_2 \implies \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2} \implies F_2 = F_1 \left(\frac{A_2}{A_1}\right)P1​=P2​⟹A1​F1​​=A2​F2​​⟹F2​=F1​(A1​A2​​)

  • If the output piston area A2A_2A2​ is $100$ times larger than input piston A1A_1A1​, applying a modest $100 , \text{N}$ force on A1A_1A1​ generates a massive $10,000 , \text{N}$ force on A2A_2A2​, lifting a heavy automobile with one foot!

2. Hydrostatic Depth Pressure

P=ρghP = \rho g hP=ρgh

  • Dams are built much thicker at the bottom than at the top because water pressure increases linearly with depth (hhh).
  • Submarines must have thick titanium hulls to withstand extreme deep-sea pressure.

3. Atmospheric Pressure & Torricelli's Barometer (1643)

  • Atmospheric pressure arises from the weight of the air column above Earth.

Patm=ρHg⋅g⋅h=(13,600 kg/m3)(9.8 m/s2)(0.76 m)=1.013×105 PaP_{\text{atm}} = \rho_{\text{Hg}} \cdot g \cdot h = (13,600 , \text{kg/m}^3)(9.8 , \text{m/s}^2)(0.76 , \text{m}) = \mathbf{1.013 \times 10^5 , \text{Pa}}Patm​=ρHg​⋅g⋅h=(13,600kg/m3)(9.8m/s2)(0.76m)=1.013×105Pa

  • Why Mercury is Used in Barometers: Mercury has extreme high density ($13.6 , \text{g/cm}^3), requiring only a compact $76 , \text{cm} column. If water were used, atmospheric pressure would support a giant water column of $10.33 , \text{metres}$ high!
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

High-Yield Exam Scenarios & Barometric Weather Predictions

Barometer Readings & Weather Forecasting:

  1. Sudden Sharp Drop in Barometer Reading: Indicates rapid arrival of a low-pressure cyclone / storm.
  2. Gradual Slow Fall in Barometer Reading: Indicates approaching rain.
  3. Gradual Rise in Barometer Reading: Indicates clearing skies / fair, dry weather.

High Altitude Physiological Traps:

  • At high altitudes (e.g. Mount Everest, Ladakh), atmospheric pressure drops significantly   ⟹  \implies⟹
    1. Water boils at a lower temperature (<100∘C< 100^\circ\text{C}<100∘C), so cooking in open pots takes much longer (hence Pressure Cookers are necessary to raise boiling point to ≈120∘C\approx 120^\circ\text{C}≈120∘C).
    2. Nosebleeds occur because high blood pressure inside nasal capillaries exceeds the low external atmospheric pressure.
    3. Fountain pens leak ink in airplanes because expanding air inside the reservoir forces ink out.
