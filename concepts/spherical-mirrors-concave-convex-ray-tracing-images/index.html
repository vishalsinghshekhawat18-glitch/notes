Spherical Mirrors (Concave & Convex): Terminology, Ray Tracing & Image Formation

The geometrical optics of curved reflecting surfaces cut from spheres, focusing properties of concave (converging) and convex (diverging) mirrors, and ray tracing rules.

Intuition & Context

The Steel Kitchen Spoon Trick: Concave vs Convex in Your Hand

Take a shiny stainless steel soup spoon: (1) Look at the inner hollow bowl (Concave surface): your face appears upside down (Real & Inverted). Bring it extremely close to your eye (<3 cm< 3 , \text{cm}<3cm, within focus): suddenly your face flips upright and massively enlarged! (2) Flip the spoon to the outer bulging side (Convex surface): your face is always right side up, tiny, and you can see the entire room behind you (Wide field of view).

How It Works / Mechanism

Ray Tracing Rules & The 4 Fundamental Ray Paths

The 4 Standard Construction Rays for Spherical Mirrors:

  1. Ray 1 (Parallel to Principal Axis): After reflection, passes through the Principal Focus (FFF) in a concave mirror (or appears to diverge from FFF in a convex mirror).
  2. Ray 2 (Through Principal Focus FFF): After reflection, emerges parallel to the principal axis.
  3. Ray 3 (Through Centre of Curvature CCC): Strikes the mirror normally (90∘90^\circ90∘ along radius) and reflects back along its own path (since ∠i=0  ⟹  ∠r=0\angle i = 0 \implies \angle r = 0∠i=0⟹∠r=0).
  4. Ray 4 (At Pole PPP): Reflects symmetrically obeying ∠i=∠r\angle i = \angle r∠i=∠r with the principal axis.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps: Rear-View Mirror Warning & Headlight Bulbs

Top Exam Traps:

  1. "Objects in the mirror are closer than they appear": This famous passenger-side warning appears on CONVEX MIRRORS. Because convex mirrors diminish objects (m<1m < 1m<1), the brain perceives small objects as being far away, when in reality the car behind is dangerously close!
  2. Concave vs Convex in Automobiles:
    • Headlights / Torches: CONCAVE Mirror (bulb placed at focus FFF to create a strong, parallel beam of light).
    • Rear-View / Side Mirrors: CONVEX Mirror (gives an erect image and wide field of view).
  3. Dentist Mirror Trap: Dentists use CONCAVE mirrors held close to teeth (object between PPP and FFF) to see a magnified, erect virtual image of tooth cavities.
