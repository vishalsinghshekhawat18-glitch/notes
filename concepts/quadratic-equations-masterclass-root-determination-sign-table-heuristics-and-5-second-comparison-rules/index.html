Intuition & Context

Decoding the Sign Table: Why You Never Need the Full Quadratic Formula in Bank Exams

In every banking preliminary and mains exam (SBI PO, IBPS PO, RRB Officer Scale 1), candidates face a standardized 5-question section: Quadratic Inequalities (xxx vs yyy). You are given two quadratic equations—one in xxx and one in yyy—and must determine whether x>yx > yx>y, x<yx < yx<y, x≥yx \ge yx≥y, x≤yx \le yx≤y, or if the relationship cannot be established (x=yx = yx=y or CND).

Candidates who mechanically calculate the full quadratic formula x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​ spend 45–60 seconds per question.

However, mathematical structure reveals that the signs of the coefficients (b,c)(b, c)(b,c) strictly dictate the signs of the roots (α,β)(\alpha, \beta)(α,β). For instance, if an equation has a positive middle term and a positive constant, both roots MUST be negative. If the other equation has positive roots, xxx is automatically strictly less than yyy without calculating a single number!

Even more powerful: whenever the constant term ccc is negative in both equations, the roots of both equations have opposite signs, which mathematically guarantees that the relationship CANNOT BE DETERMINED (x=yx=yx=y or CND) in literally zero steps!
