Scientific Conception of Work: Scalar Dot Product, Positive, Negative & Zero Work

The scientific definition of work as the scalar product of force and displacement in the direction of force, and conditions for zero, positive, and negative work.

Intuition & Context

Why Holding a 50 kg Weight for 2 Hours is "Zero Work" in Physics

If you push against a solid brick wall with all your strength for three hours until you are drenched in sweat and exhausted, everyday language says you "worked very hard." But in physics, you did EXACTLY ZERO WORK because the wall never moved (s=0s = 0s=0). Similarly, when a railway porter carries a heavy suitcase horizontally across a platform, his muscles burn, but the downward force of gravity did zero work on the bag because the displacement was strictly perpendicular (90∘90^\circ90∘) to gravity.

How It Works / Mechanism

The Porter Paradox & Orbital Centripetal Work Breakdown

1. The Railway Porter Paradox (Why W=0W = 0W=0)

  • Porter lifts suitcase upward onto his head: Positive Work (W=+mghW = +mghW=+mgh).
  • Porter holds suitcase stationary on head: Zero Work (s=0s = 0s=0).
  • Porter walks horizontally with velocity vvv:
    • Downward gravitational force: F⃗g=mgj^\vec{F}_g = mg \hat{j}Fg​=mgj^​ (vertically downward).
    • Horizontal displacement: s⃗=si^\vec{s} = s \hat{i}s=si^ (horizontal).
    • Angle between force and displacement: θ=90∘\theta = 90^\circθ=90∘.

2. Work Done by Gravitational Force on Orbiting Satellites

At every point along a circular orbit:

  • Gravitational force F⃗g\vec{F}_gFg​ acts radially inward towards Earth's centre.
  • Instantaneous displacement ds⃗d\vec{s}ds is tangential to the circular orbit.
  • Angle θ=90∘\theta = 90^\circθ=90∘ at all times   ⟹  W=∫Fg⋅ds⋅cos⁡90∘=0\implies W = \int F_g \cdot ds \cdot \cos 90^\circ = \mathbf{0}⟹W=∫Fg​⋅ds⋅cos90∘=0.
  • Consequence: Earth's gravity does zero work on the Moon; the Moon circles forever without losing mechanical energy!
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps & Fast Problem Solving

Top Work Traps in Competitive Exams:

  1. The Porter Paying Trap: In physics, the porter did zero work against gravity while walking horizontally, but he did work against internal muscle friction and air resistance.
  2. Work is a SCALAR: Even if force and displacement are in negative Cartesian directions (e.g. F=−10 NF = -10 , \text{N}F=−10N, s=−5 ms = -5 , \text{m}s=−5m), work done is W=(−10)×(−5)=+50 JW = (-10) \times (-5) = +50 , \text{J}W=(−10)×(−5)=+50J (Positive work).
  3. 1 Joule Definition: One Joule is the amount of work done when a force of $1 , \text{Newton}$ displaces an object by $1 , \text{metre}$ in the direction of the force ($1 , \text{J} = 1 , \text{N}\cdot\text{m} = 10^7 , \text{ergs}$).
