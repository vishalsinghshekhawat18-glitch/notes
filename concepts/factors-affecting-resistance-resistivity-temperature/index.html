Factors Governing Resistance (R = ρ L/A), Resistivity & Temperature Effects

The dependence of electrical resistance on conductor length, cross-sectional area, material resistivity, and the contrasting temperature coefficients of metals, alloys, and semiconductors.

Intuition & Context

Why Thick Short Extension Cords Handle Heavy Heaters While Thin Long Wires Melt

If you connect a $2000 , \text{W}$ room heater using a thin $15 , \text{metre}$ long wire, the wire gets scorching hot and may melt. But if you connect it with a thick, short $1 , \text{metre}$ industrial cable, the cable stays cool. Why? Resistance is like water pipe drag: a longer wire (LLL) forces electrons through more collisions (R∝LR \propto LR∝L), while a thicker wire (AAA) gives electrons a wider avenue to bypass each other (R∝1/AR \propto 1/AR∝1/A).

If a wire of resistance RRR is stretched to nnn times its original length (L′=nLL' = n LL′=nL):

  • Because volume is conserved (A′L′=AL  ⟹  A′=A/nA' L' = A L \implies A' = A / nA′L′=AL⟹A′=A/n):
    R′=ρL′A′=ρnLA/n=n2(ρLA)=n2RR' = \rho \frac{L'}{A'} = \rho \frac{n L}{A / n} = n^2 \left(\rho \frac{L}{A}\right) = \mathbf{n^2 R}R′=ρA′L′​=ρA/nnL​=n2(ρAL​)=n2R
  • If a wire is stretched to DOUBLE its length (2×2\times2×): R′=22R=4RR' = 2^2 R = \mathbf{4 R}R′=22R=4R (Resistance quadruples!).
  • If a wire is stretched to TRIPLE its length (3×3\times3×): R′=32R=9RR' = 3^2 R = \mathbf{9 R}R′=32R=9R.

2. Temperature Dependence across Material Classes:

  • Metals (Copper, Aluminium): RT=R0(1+αT)R_T = R_0 (1 + \alpha T)RT​=R0​(1+αT). As T↑T \uparrowT↑, metal lattice ions vibrate with larger amplitude   ⟹  \implies⟹ Electron collision frequency increases   ⟹  R↑\implies \mathbf{R \uparrow}⟹R↑.
  • Semiconductors (Silicon, Germanium): As T↑T \uparrowT↑, covalent bonds break, releasing millions of new free conduction electrons   ⟹  \implies⟹ Increased carrier density overwhelms collisions   ⟹  R↓\implies \mathbf{R \downarrow}⟹R↓ (Negative temperature coefficient).

Examiner Traps: Best Conductor & Cutting a Wire in Half

High-Yield Exam Pitfalls:

  1. The Best Electrical Conductor: SILVER (Ag) is the best conductor (lowest resistivity ρ=1.60×10−8 Ω⋅m\rho = 1.60 \times 10^{-8} , \Omega\cdot\text{m}ρ=1.60×10−8Ω⋅m), followed closely by Copper (Cu) and Gold (Au). Copper and Aluminium are used commercially for household wiring due to silver's high cost.
  2. Cutting vs Stretching a Wire:
    • If a wire of resistance RRR is cut in half (L′=L/2L' = L/2L′=L/2, area unchanged): R′=R/2R' = \mathbf{R/2}R′=R/2.
    • If a wire of resistance RRR is melted and recast/stretched to double length: R′=4RR' = \mathbf{4R}R′=4R.
