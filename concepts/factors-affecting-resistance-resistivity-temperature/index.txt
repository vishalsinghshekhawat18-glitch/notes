12:T4d9,### 1. The Wire Stretching Theorem (Constant Volume $V = A \cdot L$)
If a wire of resistance $R$ is stretched to $n$ times its original length ($L' = n L$):
- Because volume is conserved ($A' L' = A L \implies A' = A / n$):
  $$R' = \rho \frac{L'}{A'} = \rho \frac{n L}{A / n} = n^2 \left(\rho \frac{L}{A}\right) = \mathbf{n^2 R}$$
- **If a wire is stretched to DOUBLE its length ($2\times$)**: $R' = 2^2 R = \mathbf{4 R}$ (Resistance quadruples!).
- **If a wire is stretched to TRIPLE its length ($3\times$)**: $R' = 3^2 R = \mathbf{9 R}$.

### 2. Temperature Dependence across Material Classes:
- **Metals (Copper, Aluminium)**: $R_T = R_0 (1 + \alpha T)$. As $T \uparrow$, metal lattice ions vibrate with larger amplitude $\implies$ Electron collision frequency increases $\implies \mathbf{R \uparrow}$.
- **Semiconductors (Silicon, Germanium)**: As $T \uparrow$, covalent bonds break, releasing millions of new free conduction electrons $\implies$ Increased carrier density overwhelms collisions $\implies \mathbf{R \downarrow}$ (Negative temperature coefficient).
- **Heating Alloys (Nichrome = $80\% \text{ Ni} + 20\% \text{ Cr}$)**: High resistivity, very low $\alpha$, does **not oxidize (burn) even at red-hot
