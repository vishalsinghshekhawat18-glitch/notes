11:T644,Siddhartha Gautama was born c. 563 BCE in the garden grove of **Lumbini** (modern southern Nepal) to Chief Shuddhodana of the Shakya clan of Kapilavastu and Queen Maya (who died seven days after childbirth; Siddhartha was raised by foster-mother Mahaprajapati Gautami). At age 29, deeply disturbed by the 'Four Sights' (an Old Man, a Sick Man, a Corpse, and an Ascetic), he renounced his luxurious princely life (**The Great Renunciation / Mahabhinishkramana**).

### The Five Great Life Events & Their Canonical Symbols
| Life Event | Pali/Sanskrit Nomenclature | Canonical Symbol | Sacred Geographical Location |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Birth** | *Janma / Jati* | **Lotus & Bull** | Lumbini (Rummindei, Nepal) |
| **Great Renunciation** | *Mahabhinishkramana* | **Horse** (*Kanthaka*) | Kapilavastu (Piprahwa) |
| **Enlightenment** | *Sambodhi / Bodhi* | **Bodhi Tree** (*Pipal / Ficus religiosa*) | Bodh Gaya (Uruvela, on River Niranjana/Phalgu) |
| **First Sermon** | *Dhammacakkappavattana* | **Dharmachakra (Wheel of Law)** | Sarnath (Isipatana / Mrigadava near Varanasi) |
| **Final Passing** | *Mahaparinirvana* | **Stupa / Footprints** | Kushinagar (Kusinara, Malla Republic, UP) |

### The Eight Great Holy Places (*Ashtamahapratiharya*)
1. **Four Primary Sites:** Lumbini (Birth), Bodh Gaya (Bodhi), Sarnath (First Sermon), Kushinagar (Mahaparinirvana).
2. **Four Secondary Miracle Sites:** Sravasti (Twin Miracle / *Yamaka-patihariya*), Sankasya (Descent from Trayastrimsa Heaven), Rajagriha (Taming of wild elephant Nalagiri), Vaishali (Offering of honey by the monkey collective).12:T46e,### 1. Iron Metallurgy, Agricultural Surplus & The Middle Gangetic Urbanisation
The expansion of wet-rice transplantation (*Ropa*) and deep ploughing with iron ploughshares generated substantial agricultural surplus. This supported the **Second Urbanisation** (emergence of cities like Rajagriha, Kausambi, Sravasti, Varanasi, Champa).

### 2. The Rise of the Gahapatis & Merchant Discontent
- The Vedic orthodoxy branded maritime trade and usury (*Kusida*) as polluting and degraded.
- Buddhism, in contrast, praised frugal accumulation of wealth through ethical trade and endorsed investment (*Sigalovada Sutta*).
- Wealthy merchants (*Setthis*, *Gahapatis* such as Anathapindika and Mendaka) and powerful royal monarchs (Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Prasenajit) lavishly endowed Buddhist monastic dwellings (*Viharas*).

### 3. Democratic Monastic Governance (*Sangha*)
The Buddhist Sangha was structured along the democratic lines of the oligarchic *Gana-Sanghas* (such as the Vrijis/Lichchhavis), featuring open deliberation, consensus voting through wooden ballot
