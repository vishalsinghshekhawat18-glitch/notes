Customer Due Diligence (CDD), Officially Valid Documents (OVDs), CKYCR & V-CIP

The regulatory framework governing Customer Due Diligence (CDD) under RBI Master Direction - Know Your Customer (KYC) Direction, 2016, specifying the mandatory verification of identity and address using six Officially Valid Documents (OVDs), Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR) for universal digital onboarding, Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP) for contactless real-time digital onboarding, and risk-based Periodic Updation cycles (every 2 years for High Risk, 8 years for Medium Risk, and 10 years for Low Risk customers).

Intuition & Context

Operational Intuition: The 'Know Your Customer' Defensive Perimeter

When an individual opens an account at a bank, how does the institution ensure the person standing at the counter (or looking into a smartphone camera) is truly who they claim to be, and not an imposter opening a 'mule account' to siphon cybercrime proceeds? Customer Due Diligence (CDD) is the bank's front-line verification protocol. If bad actors can open anonymous or fictitious accounts, the entire financial system becomes vulnerable to untraceable fraud, ransomware ransom laundering, and terror financing. By restricting identity verification to six legally defined Officially Valid Documents (OVDs), integrating with the Central KYC Registry (CKYCR) so a citizen needs to verify KYC only once across all financial institutions, leveraging real-time geo-tagged Video-KYC (V-CIP), and periodically re-verifying customer profiles based on risk categorisation, banks establish a continuous, verifiable chain of identity and accountability.

Core Principle

Statutory Standards: Six OVDs, CKYCR, V-CIP & Re-KYC Risk Schedule

1. The Six Exhaustive Officially Valid Documents (OVDs)

Under PML Rules and RBI Master Direction on KYC, only the following six documents qualify as OVDs for individuals:

  1. Passport
  2. Driving Licence (DL)
  3. Proof of possession of Aadhaar number (Aadhaar letter/card with redacted first 8 digits / e-Aadhaar / Offline XML)
  4. Voter's Identity Card (Election Commission of India)
  5. Job Card issued by NREGA (duly signed by an officer of the State Government)
  6. Letter issued by the National Population Register (NPR) (containing details of name and address)

Note on PAN: Permanent Account Number (PAN) or Form 60 is mandatory for financial transaction verification under Rule 114B of Income Tax Rules, but PAN Card alone without address does NOT serve as an OVD for address proof.

2. Deemed OVDs for Current Address (Valid for 3 Months Only)

If the OVD does not contain the updated current address, the customer may submit Deemed OVDs (utility bill <2 months old, property tax receipt, pension payment order, accommodation allotment letter). However, the customer must submit an updated OVD with current address within 3 months.

3. Central KYC Registry (CKYCR) & V-CIP Architecture

  • CKYCR (managed by CERSAI): Assigns a 14-digit KYC Identifier (KIN). Once registered with CKYCR by any Regulated Entity, the customer can open accounts at other banks/mutual funds simply by quoting their KIN without resubmitting physical KYC documents.
  • V-CIP (Video-based Customer Identification Process): Live interactive audio-visual session. Must verify customer is physically located within Indian territory (via geo-tagging / GPS), validate live photograph with Aadhaar/PAN database, verify OVD via DigiLocker / Aadhaar OTP XML, and ensure real-time liveness detection.

4. Risk Categorisation & Periodic Updation Schedule

Customer Risk Category Indicative Client Profile Periodic Updation Frequency Operational Rules for Re-KYC
High Risk Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs), Non-Residents, Trusts/Charities, Bullion dealers, High Net-worth individuals with complex flows At least once every 2 Years Fresh CDD documents and address verification mandatory; no self-declaration shortcut for address change
Medium Risk Small business merchants, high turnover retail customers, salarised employees with unexplained large credits At least once every 8 Years Fresh OVD if details changed; self-declaration allowed if no change in KYC status
Low Risk Salaried employees with verified employers, government servants, pensioners, PMJDY basic savings accounts At least once every 10 Years Self-declaration via ATM, Internet Banking, Mobile Banking, or email accepted if no change in identity/address
How It Works / Mechanism

Operational Mechanism: V-CIP Digital Onboarding & CKYCR Ingestion Workflow

The end-to-end digital onboarding of an individual customer through V-CIP proceeds as follows:

[Customer Initiates Digital Account Opening on Bank App / Portal]
                    ↓
[Aadhaar OTP / DigiLocker Integration -> OVD Data Fetched & First 8 Digits Redacted]
                    ↓
[PAN Verification with NSDL / Income Tax Database in Real Time]
                    ↓
[Bank Official Initiates Live Real-Time Video Interaction]
  ├── GPS Geo-Tagging Confirms Customer is Within Borders of India
  ├── Liveness Detection & Facial Match against Aadhaar / PAN Photo
  └── Customer Displays Physical / Digital PAN & Answers Random Dynamic Questions
                    ↓
[Concurrent / Senior Officer Review & Audit Trail Encryption]
                    ↓
[Account Activated -> KYC Records Uploaded to CERSAI CKYCR within 3 Days]
                    ↓
[14-Digit CKYCR Identifier (KIN) Generated & Dispatched to Customer]
  1. Data Upload to CKYCR: Under RBI rules, reporting entities must upload KYC data of newly onboarded customers to the CKYCR registry within 3 days of onboarding.
  2. Small Accounts (Simplified KYC): For individuals lacking any OVD, a 'Small Account' can be opened with a recent photograph and signature/thumb impression. Limits: Aggregate credits ≤₹1,00,000\le \text{₹}1,00,000≤₹1,00,000 per year, aggregate withdrawals ≤₹10,000\le \text{₹}10,000≤₹10,000 per month, balance at any time ≤₹50,000\le \text{₹}50,000≤₹50,000. Valid for 12 months, extendable by another 12 months if proof of application for OVD is submitted.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Application & Examiner Traps

High-Yield Distinctions & Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: The Six OVDs List: Questions often include PAN Card, Ration Card, or Utility Bill as OVDs. Remember: PAN card, Ration Card, and Electricity Bills are NOT in the official list of 6 OVDs. (PAN is an IT Act requirement; utility bills are only 'deemed OVDs' for temporary 3-month address proof).
  • Trap 2: Periodic Updation Intervals: Memorize the exact numbers: High Risk = 2 years, Medium Risk = 8 years, Low Risk = 10 years. Examiner traps often swap Medium (8 years) with 5 years.
  • Trap 3: Small Account Limits: Max aggregate credits per financial year = ₹1 Lakh; Max monthly withdrawal = ₹10,000; Max balance at any point = ₹50,000; Foreign remittances are strictly prohibited in Small Accounts.
  • Trap 4: Geo-location in V-CIP: V-CIP is legally permissible only if the customer is physically present in India during the video call (confirmed via live geo-tagging). Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) residing abroad cannot complete standard V-CIP from overseas locations unless specialized NRI-compliant flows apply.
