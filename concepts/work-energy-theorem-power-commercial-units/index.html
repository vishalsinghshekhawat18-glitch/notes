Work-Energy Theorem, Power (P=W/t), Horsepower & Commercial Energy Accounting

The equivalence of net work done to kinetic energy change, time rate of energy expenditure (power), horsepower ratings, and electrical kilowatt-hour accounting.

Intuition & Context

The Difference Between Energy and Power

Imagine moving 1,000 bricks to the roof of a house. Both a human laborer and a motorized crane perform the EXACT SAME WORK (mghmghmgh) and consume the exact same amount of energy. But the laborer takes 8 hours, while the crane takes 2 minutes. The crane has vastly higher Power (P=W/tP = W/tP=W/t)—it delivers that energy at a blisteringly fast rate.

Worked Example:

A household runs four $100 , \text{W}$ electric bulbs for $5 , \text{hours/day}$ and one $1000 , \text{W}$ electric geyser for $2 , \text{hours/day}$ throughout a 30-day month:

  1. Daily Bulb Consumption: (4×100 W)×5 h=2000 Wh=2.0 kWh(4 \times 100 , \text{W}) \times 5 , \text{h} = 2000 , \text{Wh} = 2.0 , \text{kWh}(4×100W)×5h=2000Wh=2.0kWh.
  2. Daily Geyser Consumption: $1000 , \text{W} \times 2 , \text{h} = 2000 , \text{Wh} = 2.0 , \text{kWh}$.
  3. Total Daily Consumption: $2.0 + 2.0 = 4.0 , \text{kWh (units/day)}$.
  4. Monthly Consumption (30 days): $4.0 \times 30 = \mathbf{120 , \text{Units (kWh)}}$.
  5. If tariff is ₹6.00 per unit\text{\text{₹}}6.00 \text{ per unit}₹6.00 per unit: Electricity Bill=120×6=₹720\text{Electricity Bill} = 120 \times 6 = \mathbf{\text{\text{₹}}720}Electricity Bill=120×6=₹720.
