Latent Heat of Fusion/Vaporisation, Specific Heat Capacity & Thermal Expansion

The hidden heat absorbed/released during isothermal phase transitions, specific heat capacity as thermal inertia, and linear/volumetric thermal expansion.

Intuition & Context

Where Does the Heat Go When Ice Melts at 0°C?

If you place a pot of ice on a roaring gas burner with a thermometer inside, something strange happens: the thermometer stays pinned at exactly 0∘C0^\circ\text{C}0∘C for minutes until the very last sliver of ice melts, even though heat is pouring in continuously. Where did all that heat energy go? It did not increase the temperature (kinetic speed) of the molecules; it was consumed as "Latent" (Hidden) Heat to shatter the rigid crystal bonds holding ice molecules together.

  • Practical Engineering Gaps:

    • Bimetallic Strips: Two bonded strips of metals with different expansion coefficients (e.g. Brass and Iron) bend upon heating, acting as automatic switches in thermostats and fire alarms.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

High-Yield Applications of Water's High Specific Heat

Why Water's Extreme Specific Heat Capacity ($4184 , \text{J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$) Matters:

  1. Car Radiator Coolant: Water absorbs enormous amounts of engine heat with only a modest rise in its own temperature.
  2. Hot Water Bags for Pain Relief: Water retains stored heat for hours, cooling down very slowly.
  3. Maritime Climate Moderation: Coastal cities (Mumbai, Chennai) experience mild climates without extreme summer/winter temperature swings because adjacent oceans absorb/release massive heat slowly.
  4. Farmers Flooding Fields in Frost: Farmers flood crop fields with water when frost is predicted: water's high specific heat and large latent heat of freezing release vast thermal buffers, preventing delicate crop cells from freezing.
