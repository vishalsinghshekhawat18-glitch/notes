12:Tce2,The Early Vedic epoch is reconstructed from the internal evidence of the **Rigveda Samhita**:

### 1. Structure of the Rigveda:
- **10 Mandalas (Books), 1,028 Suktas (Hymns), 10,600 Verses.**
- **The Family Books (Oldest Core: Mandalas 2 to 7):**
  * Mandala 2: Sage **Gritsamada** (Shaunaka clan)
  * Mandala 3: Sage **Vishvamitra** (contains the celebrated **Gayatri Mantra** dedicated to the solar deity *Savitri*)
  * Mandala 4: Sage **Vamadeva** (contains agricultural references)
  * Mandala 5: Sage **Atri**
  * Mandala 6: Sage **Bharadvaja**
  * Mandala 7: Sage **Vashistha** (narrates the Battle of the Ten Kings)
- **Later Additions:** Mandala 1, Mandala 8 (Kanva clan), Mandala 9 (entirely dedicated to **Soma Pavamana**), and Mandala 10 (contains the *Purusha Sukta* and *Nasadiya Sukta*).

### 2. Rigvedic River Geography (The Sapta Sindhu):
The Rigveda’s **Nadi-stuti Sukta** (Mandala 10.75) catalogs the sacred rivers of the northwest:

| Rigvedic Ancient Name | Modern River Name | Rigvedic Importance & Frequency of Mention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sindhu** | **Indus** | Most frequently mentioned river in the Rigveda; celebrated for its immense water volume and commercial importance. |
| **Sarasvati** | **Ghaggar-Hakra** (dried) | Most sacred river; praised as **Naditama** (*"best of rivers"*), *Devitama* (*"best of goddesses"*), and *Matritama* (*"best of mothers"*). |
| **Vitasta** | **Jhelum** | River of northwestern Punjab. |
| **Asikni** | **Chenab** | Major Punjab river. |
| **Parushni** | **Ravi** | Historic river on whose banks the **Battle of the Ten Kings (*Dasarajna*)** was fought. |
| **Vipas** | **Beas** | River crossed and praised by Sage Vishvamitra. |
| **Shutudri** | **Sutlej** | Easternmost river of the Punjab plains. |
| **Yamuna** | **Yamuna** | Mentioned only **three times** in the Rigveda. |
| **Ganga** | **Ganges** | Mentioned only **once** in the entire Rigveda (showing the Aryans had not yet colonized the Gangetic plains). |
| **Kubha / Krumu / Suvastu** | **Kabul / Kurram / Swat** | Rivers of Afghanistan, proving early Indo-Iranian geographical contact. |

### 3. Cattle-Centric Socioeconomic Lexicon:
| Rigvedic Term | Literal Meaning | Applied Socio-Economic Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gau** | Cow | The central measure of wealth, medium of exchange, and divine gift (*Dana*). |
| **Gopati** | Lord / Protector of cattle | The tribal king / Rajan. |
| **Gaveshana / Gavishti / Gosu** | Search for cows | Warfare / tribal battles between rival clans. |
| **Godhuli** | Dust raised by cattle returning at sunset | Measurement of time (evening / twilight). |
| **Gavyuti** | Distance cattle can graze / heard calling | Measurement of physical distance. |
| **Duhitri** | One who milks the cows | Daughter of the household. |
| **Gomat** | Possessor of extensive cattle herds | Wealthy person / cattle-rich householder. |
| **Aghanya** | "Not to be killed / slaughtered" | Ritual epithet for the sacred cow. |
| **Nishka** | Gold neck ornament / bullion piece | Used alongside cows as a preliminary standard of value in barter transactions. |
| **Ayas** | Copper / Bronze | Generic metal term; Early Rigvedic people knew copper/bronze, but **did not know Iron** (iron was *Syama Ayas*, introduced in Later Vedic times). |13:T450,The Early Vedic economic mechanism operated through pastoral mobility and kin-based redistribution:

```
[Pastoral Transhumance across Sapta Sindhu River Meadows]
  --> [Cattle Accumulation -> Status & Inter-Tribal Cattle Raids (*Gavishti*)]
  --> [Supplementary Slash-and-Burn Subsistence -> Cultivation of *Yava* (Barley)]
  --> [Absence of Land Ownership & Absence of Regular Taxation]
  --> [Voluntary Offerings (*Bali*) to Rajan -> Sacrificial Redistribution in *Vidatha*]
```

### 1. Limited Role of Agriculture:
Agriculture in the Rigveda was strictly secondary to pastoralism. Only one grain—**Yava** (barley)—is mentioned. Fields were tilled using light wooden ploughs (*Langala* / *Sira*) drawn by oxen. Land was held collectively by the clan (*Vis* / *Grama*), and there was no private ownership or sale of land.

### 2. Barter and Value Accounting:
There were no metallic coins. Transactions were conducted by evaluating commodities against a standard unit of **cows** or **Nishka** (gold neck rings/discs). For example, a fine horse or chariot was valued at "ten cows" or "five nishkas".14:T4b1,### Critical Examiner Traps:
- **Trap 1 (Ganga vs Sarasvati in Rigveda):**
  * **Sarasvati** is the MOST REVERED river (*Naditama*).
  * **Ganga** is mentioned **ONLY ONCE** in the Rigveda (Mandala 10.75).
  * **Yamuna** is mentioned **THRICE**.
  * If an examiner claims "The Ganga was the central holy river of the Early Rigvedic people", the statement is **strictly FALSE**.
- **Trap 2 (Metal Knowledge - Ayas):** In the early Rigveda, the word **Ayas** refers to **copper or bronze**, NOT iron. Iron is called *Syama Ayas* (black metal) or *Krishna Ayas* and appears only in Later Vedic literature (c. 1000 BCE).
- **Trap 3 (Family Books):** The oldest Mandalas of the Rigveda are **Mandalas 2 to 7** (Family Books). Mandalas 1 and 10 are the latest chronological layers.
- **Trap 4 (Nature of Warfare):** Early Vedic wars (*Gavishti*) were fought for **cattle and pasture lands**, NOT for annexing vast territorial empires or collecting fixed agricultural revenue.
- **Trap 5 (Gayatri Mantra Location):** The Gayatri Mantra (*"Tat Savitur Varenyam..."*) was composed by Sage **Vishvamitra** and is located in **Mandala 3, Sukta 62, Verse 10** of the Rigveda,
