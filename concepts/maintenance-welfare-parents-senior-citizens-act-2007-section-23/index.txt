12:T7fa,The Act spans 32 sections categorized into 7 chapters, establishing enforceable rights, fast-track summary forums, and protective asset covenants.

### 1. Key Statutory Cast & Definitions (Section 2)
- **Senior Citizen [Section 2(h)]:** Any person who is a citizen of India and has attained the age of **60 years or above**.
- **Parent [Section 2(d)]:** Father or mother whether biological, adoptive, or step-father/step-mother, whether or not the father or the mother is a senior citizen.
- **Children [Section 2(a)]:** Son, daughter, grandson, and grand-daughter, but does **not include a minor**.
- **Relative [Section 2(g)]:** Any legal heir of the childless senior citizen who is not a minor and is in possession of or would inherit his property after his death.
- **Welfare [Section 2(k)]:** Provision for food, clothing, residence, medical attendance, and treatment.

### 2. Maintenance Adjudication vs. CrPC 125
| Parameter | Maintenance Under 2007 Act | Maintenance Under Section 125 CrPC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adjudicating Authority** | **Maintenance Tribunal** (headed by Sub-Divisional Officer / SDM). | **Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC)** or Family Court. |
| **Eligible Claimants** | Senior citizens (60+) and parents (of any age). | Parents, wives, legitimate/illegitimate minor children. |
| **Obligated Persons** | Children and grandchildren (majors); relatives inheriting estate of childless senior citizen. | Only children (and husband for wife). Grandchildren are not liable. |
| **Statutory Cap** | Originally capped at ₹10,000/month (Section 9(2)); cap removed in subsequent amendments to enable need-based maintenance. | No statutory ceiling (based on lifestyle and paying capacity). |
| **Eviction & Deed Voiding** | Yes: Section 23 can **declare gift deeds void** and order summary eviction. | No: Cannot order eviction or void registered property transfers. |
| **Legal Practitioners** | **Barred as of right** under Section 17 (Tribunal discretion required). | Full legal representation permitted. |13:T9a2,The Act devises a summary, speedy administrative pipeline to rescue neglected senior citizens.

### 1. Application & Tribunal Procedure (Sections 4–8)
- **Filing:** Application under Section 5 by senior citizen, parent, or registered organization authorized by them.
- **Interim Maintenance (Section 5(2)):** Tribunal may order monthly interim maintenance allowance during the pendency of proceedings.
- **Notice & Timelines:** Notice issued to children/relatives with a mandate to dispose of the application within **90 days** from the date of service of notice (extendable by 30 days for exceptional reasons recorded in writing).
- **Conciliation Officer (Section 6):** Tribunal may refer the matter to a Conciliation Officer who must submit findings within **1 month**.
- **Summary Procedure (Section 8):** The Tribunal holds powers of a Civil Court for summoning witnesses, enforcing attendance, and discovery of evidence.

### 2. Appellate Mechanism (Sections 15–16)
- **Appellate Tribunal (Section 15):** Constituted by State Government, presided over by the **District Magistrate (DM)**.
- **Appeal Timeline (Section 16):** Any senior citizen or parent aggrieved by an order of the Maintenance Tribunal may prefer an appeal within **60 days** from the date of the order.

### 3. Section 23: Revocation of Property Transfers
- **Deemed Fraud Provision (Section 23(1)):** If a senior citizen has transferred property (gift, deed, transfer) on the condition (express or implied) that the transferee shall provide basic amenities and physical needs, and the transferee fails/refuses, the transfer **shall be deemed to have been made by fraud or coercion or undue influence**.
- **Remedy:** The Tribunal can declare the transfer **VOID** at the option of the senior citizen, ordering immediate restoration of ownership and possession.

### 4. Penal Matrix (Sections 24 & 25)
- **Abandonment of Senior Citizen (Section 24):** Whoever, having the care or protection of a senior citizen, leaves such senior citizen in any place with the intention of wholly abandoning him/her, is guilty of a **cognizable and bailable offence**, punishable with imprisonment up to **3 months**, or fine up to **₹5,000**, or both.
- **Recovery of Maintenance (Section 14 & 5(8)):** Non-payment of maintenance without sufficient cause enables the Tribunal to issue a warrant for levying the amount, or sentence the defaulter to imprisonment up to 1 month or until payment is made.14:T871,This Act is a prime target for RPSC RAS Mains Paper III (Law). ### High-Yield Answer Blueprints
- **2-Marker Blueprint: "Under what conditions can a property transfer be declared void under Section 23 of the Senior Citizens Act, 2007?" (20 words)**
  *Model Answer:* Under Section 23(1), if property is transferred subject to the condition of providing basic amenities/physical needs, and the transferee fails to do so, the transfer is deemed made by fraud/coercion and declared void by the Tribunal.
- **5-Marker Blueprint: "Explain the constitution and powers of the Maintenance Tribunal under the Senior Citizens Act, 2007" (50 words)**
  *Model Answer:* Constituted by State Government under Section 7, presided over by Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO):
  1. **Jurisdiction:** Adjudicates maintenance applications of senior citizens (60+) and parents against major children/grandchildren or relatives.
  2. **Procedure:** Summary inquiry; exercises Civil Court powers under CPC; mandate to decide within 90 days.
  3. **Interim Orders:** Grants interim monthly maintenance and expenses.
  4. **Property Protection:** Powers under Section 23 to void conditional property transfers and direct eviction of abusive children.
  5. **No Advocates:** Section 17 bars legal practitioners as of right.

### Common Examiner Traps to Avoid
- **Trap 1:** Believing only biological parents who are 60+ can apply. Under Section 2(d), parents of ANY age (even in their 40s or 50s) can claim maintenance against major children. The 60-year threshold applies to "Senior Citizens" who claim against relatives or grandchildren.
- **Trap 2:** Confusing the Appellate Authority. The appeal does NOT lie to the High Court or Sessions Court; it lies to the **Appellate Tribunal presided over by the District Magistrate (DM)** under Section 15.
- **Trap 3:** Overlooking *Sudesh Chhikara (2022)*. The Supreme Court clarified that for Section 23 to apply, the condition to maintain must either be an express stipulation in the deed or clearly deducible from
