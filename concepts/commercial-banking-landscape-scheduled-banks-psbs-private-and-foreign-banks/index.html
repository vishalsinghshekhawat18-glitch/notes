The Commercial Banking Landscape: Scheduled Banks (Second Schedule), PSBs, Private Banks & Foreign Bank Modes

The statutory classification and structural hierarchy of commercial banks in India under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 and Banking Regulation Act, 1949. Covers Scheduled Commercial Banks (Second Schedule inclusion criteria: paid-up capital & reserves >= ₹5 Lakh and non-detrimental affairs), Public Sector Banks (Nationalized Banks and State Bank of India), Private Sector Banks (Old vs New generation post-1993/2001), and Foreign Banks (Branch mode vs Wholly Owned Subsidiary / WOS mode).

Intuition & Context

The Tiered Banking Pyramid: Why Ownership and Status Matter

Not all banks are created equal. When you see a bank branch in India, it belongs to a precise statutory tier.

A Scheduled Bank carries the gold standard of trust: it has statutory borrowing access to the RBI's discount window (Repo/MSF), can clear cheques in the national settlement system, and can accept government deposits.

Furthermore, ownership shapes operational incentives: Public Sector Banks (PSBs) historically prioritized social banking, rural branches, and priority sector credit; Private Banks focused on technological innovation, customer service, and profitability; and Foreign Banks specialized in cross-border trade finance and multinational corporate banking.

Privileges and Obligations of Scheduled Bank Status

  • Privileges: Eligible for refinancing and liquidity support under RBI's Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF - Repo/MSF/Bank Rate), automatic membership in the Clearing House, and eligibility to handle Government business.
  • Obligations: Compulsory maintenance of Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) with the RBI under Section 42(1) and submission of statutory fortnightly returns (Form A).
How It Works / Mechanism

Ownership Models: PSBs, Private Generations & Foreign WOS Mode

1. Public Sector Banks (PSBs)

  • Following major consolidation mergers (2019–2020), India has 12 Public Sector Banks (SBI + 11 Nationalized Banks).
  • Majority equity (ge51%ge 51%ge51%) is held by the President of India (Central Government).
  • Primary vehicle for sovereign welfare schemes (PMJDY, PM-SVANidhi, MUDRA).

2. Private Sector Banks: The Two Generations

  • Old Private Sector Banks: Banks that existed prior to 1969 nationalization but were not nationalized because of smaller asset size (e.g., Federal Bank, South Indian Bank, Karnataka Bank).
  • New Private Sector Banks: Licensed post-1991 reforms following the Narasimham Committee I recommendations (1993 guidelines: HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank; 2001 guidelines: Kotak Mahindra Bank, YES Bank; 2014 guidelines: Bandhan Bank, IDFC FIRST Bank).

3. Foreign Banks (Branch vs WOS Model)

  • Under RBI's 2013 roadmap, foreign banks are incentivized to convert into Wholly Owned Subsidiaries (WOS) incorporated in India. WOS entities enjoy near-national branch expansion freedom and ring-fence Indian operations from global parent contagion.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Scheduled Bank Capital Criteria: Section 42(6)(a) of the RBI Act specifies a statutory historical threshold of ₹5 Lakh for Second Schedule inclusion. (Do not confuse this statutory minimum for Second Schedule with modern licensing guidelines requiring ₹500 Crore / ₹1,000 Crore capital for starting a new bank!).
  • Trap 2: SBI vs Nationalized Banks: SBI is governed by the State Bank of India Act, 1955, whereas Nationalized Banks (PNB, BoB, Canara Bank, etc.) are governed by the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Acts, 1970/1980.
  • Trap 3: Foreign Bank PSL Target: Foreign banks with 20 or more branches have the same 40% PSL target as domestic commercial banks.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Second Schedule Section 42 criteria, Foreign Bank WOS model, and PSB merger architecture.
  • SBI/IBPS PO, NABARD & RPSC RAS: List of 12 PSBs, Old vs New private banks, and scheduled bank privileges.
