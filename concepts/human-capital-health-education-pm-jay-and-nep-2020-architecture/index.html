Human Capital Theory: Health Economics, Out-of-Pocket Expenditure, PM-JAY & National Education Policy 2020

The microeconomic foundations and institutional architecture of human capital formation in India. Analyzes: (1) Human Capital Theory (Theodore Schultz, Gary Becker, and Jacob Mincer's earnings function $\ln w = \ln w_0 + \rho s + \beta_1 x + \beta_2 x^2$), (2) Health Economics and Financing (National Health Accounts [NHA 2019-20]: Out-of-Pocket Expenditure [OoPE] at 47.1% of Total Health Expenditure, Government Health Expenditure [GHE] at 1.6% of GDP), (3) Ayushman Bharat Architecture (PM-JAY: ₹5 Lakh/family/year secondary/tertiary hospitalization for bottom 40% population + 1.6 Lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs), and (4) National Education Policy (NEP 2020: 5+3+3+4 schooling structure, NIPUN Bharat foundational numeracy, Academic Bank of Credits [ABC], and 6% of GDP public expenditure target).

Intuition & Context

The Catastrophic Clinic Visit: Why Human Capital Requires Public Investment

Imagine an auto-rickshaw driver in Jaipur earning ₹18,000 a month.

  • His daughter gets admission to an engineering college, but the family has zero savings.
  • Suddenly, his wife develops acute appendicitis and kidney stones. The private hospital demands ₹1,50,000 upfront for surgery.

Without public health insurance:

  1. The driver sells his auto-rickshaw (his only productive physical asset).
  2. He borrows ₹80,000 from a local moneylender at 5% monthly interest.
  3. His daughter drops out of college to work as a domestic helper.
  4. In one week, a single health shock permanently destroyed the family's human capital and pushed them into intergenerational poverty.

This is why economists (Gary Becker, Theodore Schultz) proved that health and education are not consumption luxuries—they are macroeconomic capital assets.

When the government provides ₹5 Lakh PM-JAY health insurance and universal public schooling, it protects households from asset liquidation and equips workers to become 5x more productive in the modern economy.

2. The Ayushman Bharat Two-Pillar Delivery Model

  • Pillar 1: Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (AAM, formerly HWCs): Over 1,60,000 primary health centers upgraded to provide Comprehensive Primary Health Care (CPHC), maternal-child care, free essential diagnostic tests, and 170+ free essential medicines at the village doorstep.
  • Pillar 2: PM-JAY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana):
    • Beneficiary Identification via Ayushman Cards (Aadhaar-seeded).
    • Covers ₹5,00,000 per family per year on a family floater basis (no cap on family size or age of members).
    • Covers 1,949 pre-notified secondary and tertiary surgical/medical packages with zero out-of-pocket payment.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: NEP 2020 5+3+3+4 Stage Breakdown: The foundational stage covers 5 years (3 years of pre-school/Anganwadi for ages 3–6, plus Grades 1 and 2 for ages 6–8). Do not confuse it with 5 years of standard elementary school.
  • Trap 2: PM-JAY Family Cap: PM-JAY has NO restriction on family size, gender, or age of members; the full ₹5 Lakh is available to the entire household on a family floater basis.
  • Trap 3: Health Expenditure GDP Target: National Health Policy 2017 targets 2.5% of GDP for public health spending; NEP 2020 targets 6.0% of GDP for public education spending.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B (ESI): Human capital economics (Schultz, Becker, Mincer), National Health Accounts metrics (OoPE at 47.1%), NHP 2017 targets, and NEP 2020 higher education reforms.
  • NABARD Grade A (ESI & ARD): Ayushman Arogya Mandirs in rural areas, primary rural schooling (NIPUN Bharat), and rural health financing.
  • RPSC RAS (Mains Paper 1 & 2): National Education Policy 2020 detailed 10-mark question; Mukhyamantri Chiranjeevi Swasthya Bima Yojana vs PM-JAY in Rajasthan.
  • UPSC APFC: Health insurance mechanisms, employer liability vs social insurance, and social sector expenditure allocations in Union Budget.
