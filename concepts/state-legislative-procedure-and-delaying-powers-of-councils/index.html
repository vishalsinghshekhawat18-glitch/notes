State Legislative Procedure: 4-Month Delaying Powers & Absence of Joint Sitting

The legislative relationship between the Vidhan Sabha and Vidhan Parishad, the maximum 4-month delaying power of the Council on ordinary bills (Articles 197–198), total absence of joint sitting in State Legislatures, and the ultimate supremacy of the Legislative Assembly.

Intuition & Context

Intuitive Mental Model: State Legislative Procedure

Think of State Legislative Procedure as a fundamental structural safeguard in a constitutional democracy. Without this explicit mechanism, the balance between state power and individual freedom would collapse into arbitrary exercise of authority. In everyday governance, this provision ensures that public functionaries operate under defined legal boundaries, guaranteeing predictability, accountability, and the rule of law across the republic.

Core Principle

Asymmetry of Power: Rajya Sabha vs Vidhan Parishad

A critical structural difference between Parliament and State Legislatures lies in the power of the upper chamber:

  1. Rajya Sabha (Federal Equal Chamber): Has equal powers with Lok Sabha on ordinary bills; if deadlock occurs, a Joint Sitting (Article 108) is summoned to resolve it.
  2. Legislative Council (Subordinate Delaying Chamber): Has NO equal powers with the Legislative Assembly on ordinary bills. The Council is only a dilatory (delaying) chamber; there is NO JOINT SITTING in State Legislatures.
How It Works / Mechanism

The 4-Month Delaying Rule for Ordinary Bills (Article 197)

When an ordinary bill passed by the Legislative Assembly is transmitted to the Legislative Council, the Council has four choices:

  1. Passes the bill as sent by the Assembly.
  2. Passes the bill with amendments to which the Assembly agrees.
  3. Rejects the bill altogether.
  4. Takes no action on the bill.

The Two-Stage Delaying Timeline:

• First Instance (3 Months): The Council can hold or reject the bill for a maximum of 3 months.
• If the Council rejects the bill, makes unacceptable amendments, or takes no action for 3 months, the Assembly may pass the bill a SECOND TIME (with or without amendments) and transmit it again to the Council.
• Second Instance (1 Month): In the second instance, the Council can hold or reject the bill for a maximum of 1 month.
• If the Council rejects the bill again or takes no action for 1 month, the Bill is deemed to have been passed by BOTH Houses in the form in which it was passed by the Legislative Assembly for the second time.
• Maximum Total Delay: 3 months (1st instance)+1 month (2nd instance)=4 Months3\text{ months (1st instance)} + 1\text{ month (2nd instance)} = \mathbf{4\text{ Months}}3 months (1st instance)+1 month (2nd instance)=4 Months max.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Exception Clauses & High-Yield Pitfalls

Critical Traps & Multi-Statement Pitfalls for Aspirants:\n\n1. Article & Nomenclature Confusion: Examiners frequently confuse identical sounding powers across Union and State levels (e.g. Art 123 vs Art 213; Art 72 vs Art 161). Verify whether the authority belongs to the President or Governor.\n\n2. Absolute vs Qualified Language Trap: Beware of statements containing absolute words like "solely", "without exception", or "unconditional". Most constitutional powers are subject to ministerial aid and advice (Art 74/163) or judicial review (Basic Structure doctrine).\n\n3. Constitutional vs Statutory Distinction: Do not confuse bodies created by constitutional articles (e.g. UPSC, Finance Commission, ECI) with statutory watchdogs created by ordinary Acts of Parliament (e.g. NGT, CVC, Lokpal, NHRC).\n\n4. Appointment vs Removal Asymmetry: Remember high-yield asymmetries—such as State PSC members being appointed by the Governor but removable only by the President after a Supreme Court inquiry under Article 317.

DISTINCTION

Bills Originating in the Council & Money Bills

Bills Originating in the Legislative Council:

• If an ordinary bill originates in the Legislative Council and is transmitted to the Legislative Assembly, and the Assembly rejects the bill, the BILL DIES IMMEDIATELY.
• There is no second passage mechanism and no joint sitting.

Money Bills in State Legislature (Article 198):

• Money Bills can be introduced ONLY in the Legislative Assembly on the prior recommendation of the Governor.
• The Legislative Council has only 14 days to make recommendations (identical to Rajya Sabha).
• The Assembly may accept or reject any recommendation; if not returned within 14 days, it is deemed passed by both Houses.
