Quantity Comparison Decision Logic: Q1 vs Q2 Algebraic Invariant Comparisons & Directional Testing

Decision frameworks for Quantity 1 vs Quantity 2 competitive problems: directional inequality preservation, algebraic subtraction techniques, boundary value testing, and avoiding redundant full calculations in Banking Mains.

Intuition & Context

Comparing Without Computing: The Relative Magnitude Principle

In modern banking mains exams (SBI PO, IBPS PO, RRB PO), Quantity Comparison (Q1Q1Q1 vs Q2Q2Q2) problems test your ability to determine whether Q1>Q2Q1 > Q2Q1>Q2, Q1<Q2Q1 < Q2Q1<Q2, Q1≥Q2Q1 \ge Q2Q1≥Q2, Q1≤Q2Q1 \le Q2Q1≤Q2, or Q1=Q2Q1 = Q2Q1=Q2 / no relation.

A novice candidate treats a Quantity Comparison question as two entirely separate, grueling arithmetic problems. They spend 90 seconds fully solving Q1Q1Q1 to get $143.75$, then spend another 90 seconds solving Q2Q2Q2 to get $138.20$, and finally conclude Q1>Q2Q1 > Q2Q1>Q2.

A seasoned quantitative thinker recognizes that comparing two mathematical quantities rarely requires finding their absolute numerical values.

Consider comparing:
Q1=1517vsQ2=1719Q1 = \frac{15}{17} \quad \text{vs} \quad Q2 = \frac{17}{19}Q1=1715​vsQ2=1917​
Instead of converting to decimals via long division, observe that both fractions are missing exactly one unit of their respective denominators:
Q1=1−217,Q2=1−219Q1 = 1 - \frac{2}{17}, \quad Q2 = 1 - \frac{2}{19}Q1=1−172​,Q2=1−192​
Since 219<217\frac{2}{19} < \frac{2}{17}192​<172​, subtracting a smaller fraction leaves a larger remainder. Therefore, Q2>Q1Q2 > Q1Q2>Q1 instantaneously in 3 seconds!

1. The Subtraction & Ratio Comparison Invariants

To compare two positive quantities AAA and BBB:

  • Difference Test:
    A−B>0  ⟺  A>BA - B > 0 \iff A > BA−B>0⟺A>B
    A−B<0  ⟺  A<BA - B < 0 \iff A < BA−B<0⟺A<B

    AB<1  ⟺  A<B\frac{A}{B} < 1 \iff A < BBA​<1⟺A<B

How It Works / Mechanism

Directional Inequality Preservation & Boundary Value Testing

The Cross-Multiplication Method for Algebraic Quantities

For fractions AB\frac{A}{B}BA​ and CD\frac{C}{D}DC​ where denominators B,D>0B, D > 0B,D>0:
AB−CD=AD−BCBD\frac{A}{B} - \frac{C}{D} = \frac{AD - BC}{BD}BA​−DC​=BDAD−BC​
Since BD>0BD > 0BD>0, the sign of the difference is strictly determined by the numerator (AD−BC)(AD - BC)(AD−BC):
AD>BC  ⟺  AB>CDAD > BC \iff \frac{A}{B} > \frac{C}{D}AD>BC⟺BA​>DC​

The Boundary Value Testing Protocol for Variable Quantities

When Q1Q1Q1 and Q2Q2Q2 contain an algebraic variable xxx subject to an inequality constraint (e.g., −1<x<1-1 < x < 1−1<x<1):

  • Do NOT test only integers! Testing x=0x = 0x=0 is insufficient because behavior changes dramatically across fractions and signs.
  • The 4-Point Boundary Test Matrix:
    1. Test a positive integer (e.g. x=2x = 2x=2)
    2. Test a positive fraction between 0 and 1 (e.g. x=0.5x = 0.5x=0.5)
    3. Test zero (x=0x = 0x=0)
    4. Test a negative fraction between -1 and 0 (e.g. x=−0.5x = -0.5x=−0.5)
    5. Test a negative integer (e.g. x=−2x = -2x=−2)

Example:

  • Q1=x2Q1 = x^2Q1=x2
  • Q2=x3Q2 = x^3Q2=x3
  • Constraint: x>−1x > -1x>−1.
    • If x=2x = 2x=2: Q1=4,Q2=8  ⟹  Q1<Q2Q1 = 4, Q2 = 8 \implies Q1 < Q2Q1=4,Q2=8⟹Q1<Q2.
    • If x=0.5x = 0.5x=0.5: Q1=0.25,Q2=0.125  ⟹  Q1>Q2Q1 = 0.25, Q2 = 0.125 \implies Q1 > Q2Q1=0.25,Q2=0.125⟹Q1>Q2.
    • Since Q1<Q2Q1 < Q2Q1<Q2 for x=2x=2x=2 but Q1>Q2Q1 > Q2Q1>Q2 for x=0.5x=0.5x=0.5, the relationship CANNOT BE DETERMINED!
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, False Assumptions & Speed Elimination

High-Yield Traps in Q1 vs Q2 Problems

  1. The Integer Assumption Trap (Fatal in Banking Mains!):

    • Unless the problem statement explicitly specifies "where xxx is an integer", never assume xxx is an integer.
    • If x>0x > 0x>0, xxx can be $0.1, 0.5, \sqrt{2}, or $100.
    • Trap: Testing only x=1,2,3x = 1, 2, 3x=1,2,3 yields a false consistent pattern that collapses when fractional values are introduced.

  2. Negative Multiplier Flip Trap:

    • When multiplying or dividing an inequality by a variable whose sign is unknown, you cannot preserve direction!
    • If k<0k < 0k<0, multiplying by kkk flips the inequality sign (A>B  ⟹  kA<kBA > B \implies kA < kBA>B⟹kA<kB).

  3. Units Discrepancy Trap:

    • Q1Q1Q1: Speed of train in km/h\text{km/h}km/h.
    • Q2Q2Q2: Speed of car in m/s\text{m/s}m/s.
    • Trap: Comparing the numerical values without converting Q2Q2Q2 by multiplying by 185\frac{18}{5}518​ (1 m/s=3.6 km/h1\text{ m/s} = 3.6\text{ km/h}1 m/s=3.6 km/h). A car moving at 25 m/s25\text{ m/s}25 m/s is moving at 90 km/h90\text{ km/h}90 km/h!
