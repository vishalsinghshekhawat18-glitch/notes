Nominal and Real Exchange Rates: NEER vs REER Indices & International Trade Competitiveness

The mathematical formulation and macroeconomic interpretation of external price competitiveness metrics. Distinguishes the bilateral Nominal Exchange Rate (NER) from the Real Exchange Rate ($RER = NER \\times \\frac{P_{\\text{domestic}}}{P_{\\text{foreign}}}$), and aggregates bilateral rates into multilateral trade-weighted indices: the Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER) and the Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER). Analyzes the RBI's 40-currency trade-weighted index, proving why a rising REER ($REER > 100$) indicates domestic currency overvaluation and a loss of export price competitiveness.

Intuition & Context

The Illusion of a Falling Rupee: Why Inflation Neutralizes Depreciation

Suppose you export leather shoes from Agra to Berlin. In Year 1, the exchange rate is ₹80 / Euro, and your shoes cost ₹8,000 to manufacture. In Germany, your shoes sell for 100 Euros.

In Year 2, the Rupee depreciates by 10%10%10% to ₹88 / Euro. You celebrate, thinking your shoes will become cheaper in Germany! But during the year, Indian inflation was 10%10%10%, so your leather, labor, and electricity costs shot up from ₹8,000 to ₹8,800.

What is your price in Germany now? ₹8,800₹88=\frac{\text{₹}8,800}{\text{₹}88} =₹88₹8,800​= 100 Euros!

Even though the Nominal Exchange Rate depreciated by 10%, your Real Exchange Rate did not change at all because domestic inflation wiped out the currency gain. Your real export competitiveness in Berlin remained completely identical.

Mathematical Formulation of NEER and REER

To eliminate bilateral distortions, central banks construct trade-weighted multilateral indices:

  • PPP: Domestic Consumer Price Index (CPI) of India.
  • PiP_iPi​: Consumer Price Index (CPI) of partner country iii.

3. Interpreting REER Benchmark Values (Base = 100)

  • REER = 100: Rupee is trading at fair real macroeconomic parity against trading partners.
  • REER > 100 (e.g., REER = 105): The Rupee is Overvalued by 5% in real terms. Indian goods are relatively more expensive abroad   ⟹  \implies⟹ Loss of export price competitiveness; imports become artificially cheaper.
  • REER < 100 (e.g., REER = 95): The Rupee is Undervalued by 5% in real terms. Indian goods are cheaper in global markets   ⟹  \implies⟹ Gain in export price competitiveness.

Why RBI Uses Consumer Price Index (CPI) for REER

Prior to 2014, the RBI calculated REER using the Wholesale Price Index (WPI). Under Governor Raghuram Rajan and the Urjit Patel Committee, the RBI shifted to CPI-based REER because CPI captures the true retail purchasing power and cost of living that drives domestic labor wages.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Higher REER is Always Good: "A rising REER means the country is doing better." -> False: A rising REER indicates that the domestic currency is becoming overvalued, making domestic exports less competitive and worsening the trade deficit!
  • Trap 2: NEER Depreciation vs REER Appreciation: A country can experience NEER depreciation (nominal Rupee falling against USD/Euro) and simultaneously experience REER appreciation if domestic inflation exceeds foreign inflation by a margin larger than the nominal depreciation.
  • Trap 3: WPI vs CPI in Modern REER: The RBI officially compiles and reports CPI-based NEER and REER with base year 2015-16 = 100.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Geometric formula of REER, 40-currency basket composition, base year, and overvaluation diagnostics.
  • UPSC APFC, SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Conceptual difference between NEER and REER, and export competitiveness implications.
