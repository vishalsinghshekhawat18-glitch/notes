World Trade Organization (WTO): Institutional Architecture, MFN, National Treatment & Covered Agreements

The legal, institutional, and economic foundations of the multilateral trading system. Traces the historic transition from the provisional General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT 1947, Geneva) to the permanent World Trade Organization (WTO, established January 1, 1995 via the Marrakesh Agreement). Explains the fundamental non-discrimination pillars: Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN - GATT Article I: non-discrimination between trading partners) and National Treatment (GATT Article III: non-discrimination between domestic and imported goods once past customs). Analyzes the "Single Undertaking" concept and surveys the core covered agreements: GATT (Goods), GATS (Services), TRIPS (Intellectual Property), and TRIMS (Investment Measures).

Intuition & Context

The Rules of the Global Trade Highway: MFN vs National Treatment

Imagine an international highway with 164 countries driving their commercial trucks:

  1. Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN) Rule (At the Toll Gate): India cannot say: "If Germany brings steel across the border, the customs toll is 5%, but if Brazil brings steel, the customs toll is 25% because we like Germany more." Under MFN, whatever lowest tariff you give to your favorite best friend, you MUST give to every single member of the WTO automatically!
  2. National Treatment Rule (Inside the City): Once German steel has paid the 5% customs duty and enters Mumbai, Maharashtra cannot pass a law saying: "Foreign steel must pay a 28% GST, while Tata Steel pays only 18% GST." Once past the customs gate, imported goods must be treated with total equality compared to domestic goods.

These two rules prevent trade wars, favoritism, and hidden protectionism across the globe.

The Four Modes of Trade in Services under GATS

  • Mode 1 (Cross-Border Supply): Service crosses border; supplier and consumer stay in their countries (e.g., Indian software firm emailing code to New York).
  • Mode 2 (Consumption Abroad): Consumer travels to supplier's country (e.g., American tourist visiting Jaipur; foreign student studying in Delhi).
  • Mode 3 (Commercial Presence): Foreign firm establishes a local branch/subsidiary (e.g., Citibank or HSBC opening bank branches in India).
  • Mode 4 (Presence of Natural Persons): Temporary cross-border movement of individual professionals (e.g., Indian software engineer traveling on an H-1B visa to deploy software on-site in California). India is the world's leading advocate for liberalizing Mode 4!
How It Works / Mechanism

Exceptions to MFN & National Treatment Rules

While MFN and National Treatment are sacred WTO principles, the WTO charter allows specific legal exceptions:

  1. GATT Article XXIV (Regional Trade Agreements): Members can form Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) or Customs Unions (e.g. India-UAE CEPA, EU) and eliminate tariffs among themselves without extending zero tariffs to other WTO members, provided the FTA covers "substantially all trade".
  2. Enabling Clause (Special & Differential Treatment [S&DT]): Developed nations can grant unilateral, non-reciprocal tariff preferences to developing and least-developed nations (e.g. Generalised System of Preferences [GSP]).
  3. General Exceptions (GATT Article XX): Trade restrictions permitted for protecting human, animal, or plant life (SPS measures), public morals, or conserving exhaustible natural resources.
  4. National Security Exception (GATT Article XXI): Actions taken in times of war or essential security interests.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: MFN Does NOT Mean Free Trade: MFN does not mean zero tariffs. It means equal tariffs: if India charges a 10% duty on mobile phones, it must charge 10% to all WTO members equally.
  • Trap 2: National Treatment Does Not Apply to Customs Tariffs: National Treatment applies ONLY to internal taxes and domestic regulations after goods cross the border. Customs tariffs at the border are explicitly allowed.
  • Trap 3: WTO is Not a UN Specialized Agency: The WTO is an independent international organization that maintains a formal cooperation agreement with the UN, but is NOT a UN specialized agency (unlike the IMF and World Bank).
  • Trap 4: Mode 4 Services: India's primary offensive interest in WTO GATS negotiations is Mode 4 (Movement of Natural Persons) to facilitate visa access for Indian IT professionals.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: GATS 4 Modes of supply, TRIMS prohibitions (local content requirements), TRIPS patent durations (20 years), and GATT Articles I, III, XXIV.
  • UPSC APFC & RPSC RAS: MFN vs National Treatment definitions, Marrakesh Agreement (1994), and GATT to WTO evolution.
