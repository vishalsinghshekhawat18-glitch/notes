11:T4cd,The two fundamental regimes of debt sustainability:

```
                      DEBT SUSTAINABILITY REGIMES
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                   ▼
REGIME 1: FAVORABLE REGIME ($g > r \implies r - g < 0$)    REGIME 2: UNSTABLE REGIME ($r > g \implies r - g > 0$)
• "The Growth Dividend / Domar Blessing"                  • "The Debt Spiral / Snowball Trap"
• Nominal GDP grows faster than interest rate             • Interest compounding outpaces economic growth
• The Debt-to-GDP ratio naturally shrinks                 • Accumulated debt grows automatically even with zero
  over time even if the budget runs a small                 new spending!
  primary deficit ($pb < 0$)!                             • To prevent default, government MUST run a
• Steady State Debt: $d^* = \frac{-pb}{g - r}$             mandatory **Primary Surplus** ($pb^* = \frac{r - g}{1+g} d$)
• Typical of high-growth developing economies (India)     • Typical of stagnant, debt-laden economies
```12:T4b7,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Domar Condition Inequality**: Debt ratio stabilizes when **$g > r$ (Growth rate is GREATER than interest rate)**. If $r > g$, the debt ratio explodes unless there is a primary surplus!
- **Trap 2: Primary Balance vs Primary Deficit**: In the debt equation, a **Primary Deficit is entered as a negative primary balance ($-pb_t$)**, which increases debt ($\Delta d > 0$). A **Primary Surplus ($pb_t > 0$)** reduces debt.
- **Trap 3: Real vs Nominal in $(r - g)$**: You can use Nominal $r$ and Nominal $g$, OR Real $r$ and Real $g$. The differential $(r - g)$ is mathematically identical because inflation cancels out in both terms!
- **Trap 4: Economic Survey 2020-21 Focus**: The Economic Survey dedicated a flagship chapter arguing that for India, **growth leads to debt sustainability**, making public investment in CapEx self-financing.

### Cross-Exam Focus
- **RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A**: Mathematical derivations of $\Delta d$, Domar steady-state debt ratio ($d^*$), and Economic Survey debt sustainability analytics.
