12:T6a2,Let us analyze what determines the demand and supply curves in the forex market:

```
Exchange Rate
(₹ / USD)
    ▲                Supply of Foreign Currency (S_USD)
    │                    /
    │                   /  (Exports, Inward Remittances, FDI, FPI)
  R1 ───────────────-───/──────
    │               \  / 
  R* ────────────────\/──────── Equilibrium Rate (e.g., ₹84 / USD)
    │                /\
  R2 ───────────────/──\──────
    │              /    \
    │             /      \ Demand for Foreign Currency (D_USD)
    │            /        \ (Imports, Outward Travel, Loan Repayments)
    └───────────┴──────────┴────────► Quantity of Foreign Currency (USD)
```

### 1. Demand for Foreign Currency ($D_{\\text{USD}}$) - Downward Sloping
- When the price of USD is high (e.g., ₹90/USD), foreign goods are expensive in India, so Indian imports drop $\\implies$ lower demand for USD.
- When the price of USD is low (e.g., ₹75/USD), imports are cheap, so import demand surges $\\implies$ higher demand for USD.

### 2. Supply of Foreign Currency ($S_{\\text{USD}}$) - Upward Sloping
- When the price of USD is high (e.g., ₹90/USD), Indian goods are cheap for foreign buyers, so Indian exports expand $\\implies$ foreign buyers supply more USD to buy Rupees.

### 3. Equilibrium Price Discovery ($R^*$)
At $R^*$, the quantity of foreign currency demanded by Indian importers exactly equals the quantity supplied by Indian exporters and foreign investors.13:T414,How macroeconomic events shift the exchange rate in a market-determined float:

| Macroeconomic Shock | Demand / Supply Curve Shift | Impact on Equilibrium Exchange Rate ($₹/USD$) | Economic Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Global Crude Oil Price Surge** | $D_{\\text{USD}}$ shifts **RIGHT** (higher import bill) | Exchange rate rises (e.g., ₹84 $\\to$ ₹88) | **Rupee Depreciates** (Imported inflation rises) |
| **Global Tech Rally (Services Exports Surge)** | $S_{\\text{USD}}$ shifts **RIGHT** (higher export earnings) | Exchange rate falls (e.g., ₹84 $\\to$ ₹81) | **Rupee Appreciates** (Exports become less competitive) |
| **US Fed Hikes Interest Rates (FPI Outflow)** | $D_{\\text{USD}}$ shifts **RIGHT** (capital exit) + $S_{\\text{USD}}$ shifts **LEFT** | Exchange rate rises sharply | **Rupee Depreciates** (Asset price pressure) |
| **Massive Sovereign FDI Inflow** | $S_{\\text{USD}}$ shifts **RIGHT** (dollar supply flood) | Exchange rate falls | **Rupee Appreciates** (RBI may intervene to buy dollars) |14:T596,### Critical Examiner Traps: The Four-Way Confusion Matrix
Examiners constantly test the difference between market-driven and administrative currency movements:

| Concept | Market Regime | Mechanism | Direction of Domestic Currency Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Appreciation** | Floating / Flexible | Market forces of demand and supply | **INCREASES** (₹84 $\\to$ ₹80 / USD) |
| **Depreciation** | Floating / Flexible | Market forces of demand and supply | **DECREASES** (₹84 $\\to$ ₹88 / USD) |
| **Revaluation** | Fixed / Pegged | Official statutory decree by Central Bank/Govt | **INCREASES** (Peg adjusted upward) |
| **Devaluation** | Fixed / Pegged | Official statutory decree by Central Bank/Govt | **DECREASES** (Peg adjusted downward) |

- **Trap 1: Direct Quotation Numbering**: Under direct quote (₹/USD), when the number goes **UP** (from ₹83 to ₹86), the Rupee is getting **WEAKER** (Depreciation), NOT stronger!
- **Trap 2: Spot vs Forward vs Swap**: Spot settlement occurs in **$T+2$ business days**. Cash/Same-day is $T+0$, Tom is $T+1$. A Forward contract is settled beyond 2 business days at an agreed rate.
