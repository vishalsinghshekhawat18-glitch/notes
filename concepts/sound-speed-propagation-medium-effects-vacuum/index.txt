13:T42d,### The Universal Medium Speed Hierarchy:
$$\mathbf{v_{\text{Solids}} > v_{\text{Liquids}} > v_{\text{Gases}}}$$

### Speed of Sound in Common Media (at $20^\circ\text{C} - 25^\circ\text{C}$):

| Medium | Physical State | Speed of Sound ($m/s$) | Physical Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Aluminium / Steel / Iron** | Solid | $\mathbf{\approx 5000 - 6420 \, \text{m/s}}$ | Enormous elastic modulus (Young's modulus $Y$). |
| **Glass (Flint)** | Solid | $\approx 3980 \, \text{m/s}$ | High rigid elasticity. |
| **Sea Water** | Liquid | $\approx 1531 \, \text{m/s}$ | Dissolved salts increase elasticity and density. |
| **Pure Fresh Water** | Liquid | $\approx 1482 \, \text{m/s}$ | Moderate bulk modulus ($K$). |
| **Air ($22^\circ\text{C}$)** | Gas | $\mathbf{\approx 344 \, \text{m/s}}$ | Compressible gas (Air at $0^\circ\text{C} = 331 \, \text{m/s}$). |
| **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)** | Gas | $\approx 267 \, \text{m/s}$ | Heavy molecular mass lowers speed. |
| **Vacuum** | Vacuum | $\mathbf{0 \, \text{m/s}}$ | **Absolute Zero — Cannot propagate!** |14:T4b8,### 1. Effect of Temperature ($T$)
Sound speed in a gas is proportional to the square root of absolute temperature:
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}} \implies v \propto \sqrt{T}$$
- In air, speed increases by $\approx \mathbf{0.61 \, \text{m/s}}$ for every $1^\circ\text{C}$ temperature rise:
  $$v_T \approx 331 + 0.61 \cdot T \, (\text{where } T \text{ is in } ^\circ\text{C})$$

### 2. Effect of Humidity (Why Sound Travels Faster on Rainy/Humid Days)
- Water vapor ($H_2O$, molar mass $= 18 \, \text{g/mol}$) is lighter than dry air (dominated by $N_2 = 28$ and $O_2 = 32$, average molar mass $\approx 29 \, \text{g/mol}$).
- As air becomes humid, light water vapor molecules displace heavier nitrogen/oxygen molecules $\implies$ **Moist air is LESS dense than dry air ($\rho_{\text{moist}} < \rho_{\text{dry}}$)**.
- Since $v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}}$, lower density yields **higher sound speed in humid air**.

### 3. The Pressure Independence Principle
- If atmospheric pressure $P$ increases, gas density $\rho$ increases in exact proportion ($P/\rho = \text{constant}$ at constant $T$).
- **Changing atmospheric pressure has ZERO effect on the speed of sound** if temperature remains
