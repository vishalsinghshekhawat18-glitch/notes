Fiscal Multiplier Theory: Spending, Tax & Balanced-Budget Multipliers & Fiscal-Monetary Coordination

The mathematical derivations, economic mechanisms, and macroeconomic interactions of Keynesian Fiscal Multipliers and Fiscal-Monetary Policy Coordination. Derives the Government Spending Multiplier ($k_G = \frac{1}{1 - MPC}$), the Tax Multiplier ($k_T = \frac{-MPC}{1 - MPC}$), and the Haavelmo Balanced-Budget Multiplier ($k_{BB} = k_G + k_T = 1$). Examines how the effectiveness of fiscal expansion depends on monetary policy response (accommodative monetary policy preventing interest rate spikes vs monetary tightening causing complete crowding out), and explores the risks of Fiscal Dominance (where massive sovereign debt forces the central bank to keep interest rates artificially low, sacrificing price stability).

Intuition & Context

Why Spending Multiplies: The Chain Reaction of the Government Rupee

Suppose the government spends ₹100 to hire a worker to pave a road.

  • The road worker earns ₹100. If her Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPCMPCMPC) is $0.80$ (80%80%80%), she spends ₹80 at the local grocery store and saves ₹20.
  • The grocer now has ₹80 of new income. He spends 80%80%80% of it (₹64) on clothes and saves ₹16.
  • The tailor now has ₹64 of new income. He spends 80%80%80% of it (₹51.20) at a restaurant.

This chain reaction continues indefinitely! The original ₹100 government spending generated total economic output of:
Total ΔY=100+80+64+51.20+⋯=1001−0.80=1000.20=₹500\text{Total } \Delta Y = 100 + 80 + 64 + 51.20 + \dots = \frac{100}{1 - 0.80} = \frac{100}{0.20} = \text{₹}500Total ΔY=100+80+64+51.20+⋯=1−0.80100​=0.20100​=₹500

A ₹100 injection generated ₹500 of national GDP (Multiplier =5= 5=5)!

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Spending Multiplier vs Tax Multiplier: The spending multiplier is ALWAYS larger than the tax multiplier by exactly 1 (kG−∣kT∣=1k_G - |k_T| = 1kG​−∣kT​∣=1). A ₹1,000 Crore spending hike stimulates more GDP than a ₹1,000 Crore tax cut!
  • Trap 2: Balanced-Budget Multiplier Value: The Balanced-Budget Multiplier (kBBk_{BB}kBB​) is ALWAYS equal to 1 (unity). (It is NOT zero!).
  • Trap 3: MPC vs MPS in Multiplier: Multiplier =11−MPC=1MPS= \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}=1−MPC1​=MPS1​. As Marginal Propensity to Save (MPSMPSMPS) increases, the multiplier becomes smaller (because more money leaks out of the spending stream into savings).
  • Trap 4: Fiscal Dominance Definition: Fiscal dominance means fiscal debt forces monetary policy to subordinate its inflation goal to sovereign debt servicing.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: Multiplier numerical calculations, Balanced budget theorem, and Sargent-Wallace Fiscal Dominance arithmetic.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Multiplier formulas (1/(1−MPC)1/(1-MPC)1/(1−MPC)), and 2-mark definitions of Balanced Budget Multiplier.
