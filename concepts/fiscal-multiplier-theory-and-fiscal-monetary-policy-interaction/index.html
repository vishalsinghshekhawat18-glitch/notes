Fiscal Multiplier Theory: Spending, Tax & Balanced-Budget Multipliers & Fiscal-Monetary Coordination

The mathematical derivations, economic mechanisms, and macroeconomic interactions of Keynesian Fiscal Multipliers and Fiscal-Monetary Policy Coordination. Derives the Government Spending Multiplier ($k_G = \frac{1}{1 - MPC}$), the Tax Multiplier ($k_T = \frac{-MPC}{1 - MPC}$), and the Haavelmo Balanced-Budget Multiplier ($k_{BB} = k_G + k_T = 1$). Examines how the effectiveness of fiscal expansion depends on monetary policy response (accommodative monetary policy preventing interest rate spikes vs monetary tightening causing complete crowding out), and explores the risks of Fiscal Dominance (where massive sovereign debt forces the central bank to keep interest rates artificially low, sacrificing price stability).

Intuition & Context

The Keynesian Consumption Function, Multiplier & The Paradox of Thrift

1. The Keynesian Consumption & Saving Functions

Keynes demonstrated that private consumption expenditure is primarily a function of disposable income:

  • The Linear Consumption Function:
    C=Cˉ+cY\mathbf{C = \bar{C} + cY}C=Cˉ+cY
    • Cˉ>0\mathbf{\bar{C} > 0}Cˉ>0 (Autonomous Consumption): Baseline survival expenditure that occurs even when income is zero (Y=0Y = 0Y=0), financed by dissaving (drawing down past savings) or borrowing.
    • c=MPC=ΔCΔY\mathbf{c = MPC = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}}c=MPC=ΔYΔC​ (Marginal Propensity to Consume): The proportion of additional income that is spent on consumption ($0 < MPC < 1$).
    • APC=CY\mathbf{APC = \frac{C}{Y}}APC=YC​ (Average Propensity to Consume): The ratio of total consumption to total income. Note that as income YYY rises, APCAPCAPC continuously declines because Cˉ/Y\bar{C}/YCˉ/Y shrinks.
  • The Linear Saving Function:
    S=Y−C=−Cˉ+(1−c)Y=−Cˉ+sY\mathbf{S = Y - C = -\bar{C} + (1 - c)Y = -\bar{C} + sY}S=Y−C=−Cˉ+(1−c)Y=−Cˉ+sY
    • −Cˉ\mathbf{-\bar{C}}−Cˉ: Dissaving at zero income.
    • s=MPS=ΔSΔY=1−c\mathbf{s = MPS = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y} = 1 - c}s=MPS=ΔYΔS​=1−c (Marginal Propensity to Save): The fraction of an extra rupee saved.
    • APS=SY\mathbf{APS = \frac{S}{Y}}APS=YS​ (Average Propensity to Save): Can be negative at low income levels where C>YC > YC>Y.

3. Investment Multiplier (kkk) & The Chain Reaction

Suppose the government or private sector makes an Autonomous Investment injection (ΔI\Delta IΔI) of ₹100 into building a highway.

  • The road worker earns ₹100. If her MPC=0.80MPC = 0.80MPC=0.80 (MPS=0.20MPS = 0.20MPS=0.20), she spends ₹80 on groceries and saves ₹20.
  • The grocer earns ₹80 of new income, spends 80%80%80% (₹64) on clothing, and saves ₹16.
  • The tailor earns ₹64 of new income, spends 80%80%80% (₹51.20) at a café, and saves ₹12.80.

k=ΔYΔI=11−MPC=1MPS=10.20=5\mathbf{k = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta I} = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}} = \frac{1}{0.20} = \mathbf{5}k=ΔIΔY​=1−MPC1​=MPS1​=0.201​=5
A ₹100 investment injection expands national GDP by ₹500!

4. The Paradox of Thrift (Keynesian Fallacy of Composition)

  • If a single household decides to save more, its individual financial security rises.
  • But if ALL households across the nation simultaneously attempt to save more (i.e. MPS↑MPS \uparrowMPS↑, MPC↓MPC \downarrowMPC↓):
    1. Collective consumption (CCC) plunges.
    2. Aggregate Demand collapses, triggering unsold inventories.
    3. Firms cut output, lay off workers, and national equilibrium income (YYY) drops precipitously.
    4. Because total income shrank, total realized savings across the economy (S=s×YS = s \times YS=s×Y) remains unchanged or even declines!
    5. What is virtue for the individual becomes economic ruin for the collective!
Core Principle

The Keynesian Multipliers & Investment Taxonomy: Mathematical Framework

The comprehensive mathematical suite of Keynesian determination of income:

1. Investment Taxonomy: Autonomous vs Induced

  • Autonomous Investment (Iˉ\bar{I}Iˉ): Independent of the level of national income (YYY). It is policy-driven, infrastructure-focused (e.g. government highways, green energy transition), and represented as a flat horizontal line on the income-expenditure graph.
  • Induced Investment: Positively correlated with national income and corporate profits. As GDP expands, capacity utilization rises, inducing firms to buy more plant and machinery.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Spending Multiplier vs Tax Multiplier: The spending multiplier is ALWAYS larger than the tax multiplier by exactly 1 (kG−∣kT∣=1k_G - |k_T| = 1kG​−∣kT​∣=1). A ₹1,000 Crore spending hike stimulates more GDP than a ₹1,000 Crore tax cut!
  • Trap 2: Balanced-Budget Multiplier Value: The Balanced-Budget Multiplier (kBBk_{BB}kBB​) is ALWAYS equal to 1 (unity). (It is NOT zero!).
  • Trap 3: MPC vs MPS in Multiplier: Multiplier =11−MPC=1MPS= \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}=1−MPC1​=MPS1​. As Marginal Propensity to Save (MPSMPSMPS) increases, the multiplier becomes smaller (because more money leaks out of the spending stream into savings).
  • Trap 4: APC Can Exceed 1, but MPC Cannot: At very low income levels (or zero income), households dissave (C>YC > YC>Y), which means APC=C/Y>1APC = C/Y > 1APC=C/Y>1 and APS=S/Y<0APS = S/Y < 0APS=S/Y<0. However, MPCMPCMPC and MPSMPSMPS can NEVER be greater than 1 or less than 0 under standard Keynesian theory ($0 < MPC < 1$).
  • Trap 5: Paradox of Thrift Core Insight: Increasing the propensity to save (MPSMPSMPS) does NOT increase total equilibrium savings in a closed economy without induced investment; it lowers equilibrium income (YYY) until savings equals autonomous investment (S=IS = IS=I).
  • Trap 6: Fiscal Dominance Definition: Fiscal dominance means fiscal debt forces monetary policy to subordinate its inflation goal to sovereign debt servicing.
