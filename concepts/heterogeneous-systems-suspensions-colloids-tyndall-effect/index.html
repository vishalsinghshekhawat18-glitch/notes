Heterogeneous Systems: Suspensions vs Colloids, Tyndall Effect & Brownian Motion

The tripartite comparative taxonomy of True Solutions, Colloids, and Suspensions, dispersed phase and dispersion medium, light scattering (Tyndall effect), and Brownian motion.

Intuition & Context

Why Milk Looks Like a Pure White Liquid but is Actually a Trap for Light

If you shine a laser pointer through a glass of clear salt water, the red beam is completely invisible inside the liquid. But if you shine the laser through a glass of water containing just two drops of milk, a glowing red illuminated cylinder lights up inside the liquid (Tyndall Effect)! Milk is not a true solution—it is an Emulsion of millions of sub-microscopic fat and protein droplets suspended in water, dancing in a violent zig-zag (Brownian Motion) that scatters light in all directions.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps: Milk of Magnesia vs Milk & Tyndall Effect

High-Yield Exam Traps:

  1. Milk vs Milk of Magnesia:
    • Milk: Is a Liquid-in-Liquid Colloid (Emulsion).
    • Milk of Magnesia (Mg(OH)2Mg(OH)_2Mg(OH)2​ in water): Is a Solid-in-Liquid Colloid (Sol) used as an antacid.
  2. Everyday Tyndall Effect Examples:
    • Sunlight streaming through a small hole into a dusty attic room.
    • Car headlights cutting through a dense winter fog beam.
    • Sunlight filtering through the mist canopy of a dense rainforest.
  3. Brownian Motion Reason: Discovered by botanist Robert Brown. Caused by the unequal, random momentum collisions of liquid water molecules bombarding the colloidal particles from all sides.
