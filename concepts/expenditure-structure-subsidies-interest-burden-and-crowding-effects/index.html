Government Expenditure: Revenue vs Capital, Subsidies & Crowding · Concept 2 of 2

Expenditure Structure: Subsidies, Interest Burden, Social Infrastructure & Crowding-In vs Crowding-Out

The macroeconomic structure and spillover mechanisms of government spending. Analyzes the composition of committed non-discretionary expenditure (Interest payments consuming ~25% of revenue receipts, Defense, Pensions), major explicit Subsidies (Food under NFSA/PMGKAY, Fertilizer, Petroleum/LPG), social infrastructure investment (health, education, Jal Jeevan), and the theoretical and empirical mechanics of Crowding-Out (fiscal deficits driving up bond yields and displacing private corporate investment) vs Crowding-In (high-quality public CapEx improving logistical efficiency and raising the private Marginal Productivity of Capital).

Intuition & Context

Crowding Out vs Crowding In: The Private Sector's Double-Edged Sword

Imagine a national banking system that has ₹10,00,000 Crore of total lendable savings this year.

  • Scenario A: Financial Crowding-Out: The government runs a massive fiscal deficit and borrows ₹9,00,000 Crore to pay for operational subsidies and salary hikes. With only ₹1,00,000 Crore left for private companies, banks hike interest rates from 8%8%8% to 14%14%14%. Private companies cancel their factory expansion plans because borrowing is too expensive (Crowded Out).

  • Scenario B: Real Crowding-In: The government borrows money to build a world-class 8-lane Expressway connecting an industrial hub to the port, reducing truck travel time from 48 hours to 10 hours. Private companies immediately rush to build warehouses, factories, and assembly plants along the highway, investing trillions in new capital (Crowded In).

The Lesson: Borrowing for consumption crowds out; investing in core infrastructure crowds in!

How It Works / Mechanism

The Fiscal Multiplier Dilemma: CapEx vs Revenue Spending

Studies by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and National Institute of Public Finance and Policy (NIPFP) demonstrate a stark difference in Fiscal Multipliers in India:

CapEx Multiplier=ΔGDPΔCapital Expenditure≈2.45−4.80\text{CapEx Multiplier} = \frac{\Delta \text{GDP}}{\Delta \text{Capital Expenditure}} \approx 2.45 - 4.80CapEx Multiplier=ΔCapital ExpenditureΔGDP​≈2.45−4.80
Revenue Expenditure Multiplier=ΔGDPΔRevenue Expenditure≈0.45−0.98\text{Revenue Expenditure Multiplier} = \frac{\Delta \text{GDP}}{\Delta \text{Revenue Expenditure}} \approx 0.45 - 0.98Revenue Expenditure Multiplier=ΔRevenue ExpenditureΔGDP​≈0.45−0.98

Why the CapEx Multiplier is 4x Higher

  1. Long-Term Asset Creation: A highway or port generated by CapEx lasts for 30 years, permanently reducing logistical friction and business costs across multiple industries.
  2. Industrial Supply Chains: Building a railway track immediately triggers massive demand for domestic steel, cement, heavy machinery, and construction labor.
  3. Revenue Outlay Leakage: Revenue spending on un-targeted cash handouts or general subsidies often dissipates in immediate consumption of imported goods or gets saved, generating little sustained productive capacity.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Largest Single Item of Union Expenditure: The single largest item of expenditure in the Union Budget is INTEREST PAYMENTS (accounting for ~₹11 Lakh Crore / over 20% of the entire budget!).
  • Trap 2: Crowding-In Mechanism: Crowding-in occurs when public investment complements private investment (e.g., roads, electricity, internet broadband), raising private returns. It does NOT mean the government buys private shares.
  • Trap 3: Food Subsidy Mechanism: Food subsidy is paid to the Food Corporation of India (FCI) to bridge the gap between the Economic Cost of wheat/rice (MSP + procurement/storage cost) and the Central Issue Price (CIP = ₹0 under PMGKAY).
  • Trap 4: Multiplier Values: In India, Capital Expenditure has a multiplier greater than 2.0, whereas Revenue Expenditure has a multiplier below 1.0.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: CapEx vs Revenue fiscal multipliers, crowding-out via G-Sec yield spikes, and FCI food subsidy accounting.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Largest budget expenditure item (Interest), major subsidies (Food, Fertilizer), and Crowding-out vs Crowding-in definitions.
