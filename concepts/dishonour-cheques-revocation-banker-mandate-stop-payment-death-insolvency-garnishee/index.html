Dishonour of Cheques & Revocation of Banker's Mandate: Stop Payment, Death, Insolvency, Garnishee & Attachment Orders

Grounds for rightful refusal and revocation of a paying banker's authority to honour cheques: customer countermand (stop payment), determination by death, insolvency, mental illness, assignment of debt, and statutory attachment freezes under Court Garnishee Orders (Order XXI CPC) and Income Tax Attachment Orders (Sec 226(3) IT Act).

Intuition & Context

When the Customer's Mandate Legally Dies

The relationship between a banker and a customer regarding cheques is that of an Agent (Bank) acting on the Mandate (Order) of the Principal (Customer).

If the customer cancels that order before it is executed (Stop Payment), the agent's authority evaporates instantly. Similarly, under agency law, if the principal dies, goes bankrupt, or becomes legally insane, the agency automatically terminates. When a bank teller receives a cheque over the counter, they must ensure that the customer's legal mandate is alive, active, and unencumbered by court orders like a Garnishee Order (issued by a civil court) or an Attachment Order (issued by the Income Tax Department).

Core Principle

Revocation of Authority & Legal Freeze Mechanisms

Grounds for Termination / Revocation of Banker's Mandate

  1. Countermand of Payment (Stop Payment): Customer instructs bank (with cheque number, date, amount, payee) not to pay. The bank must note it in CBS immediately.
  2. Notice of Customer's Death: Cheques signed by customer before death cannot be paid once authentic notice (death certificate/letter) is received. Reason: 'Drawer Deceased'.
  3. Notice of Insolvency / Bankruptcy: Individual declared insolvent or corporate undergoing CIRP liquidation under IBC 2016. Reason: 'Drawer Insolvent'.
  4. Notice of Lunacy / Insanity: When bank receives notice of judicial inquisition/medical certification of customer's insanity.
  5. Notice of Assignment: Customer assigns credit balance to a third party under Section 130 Transfer of Property Act.

Comparative Matrix: Garnishee Order vs Income Tax Attachment Order

Feature Garnishee Order (CPC Order XXI Rule 46) Income Tax Attachment Order (Sec 226(3) IT Act)
Issuing Authority Civil Court executing a decree Tax Recovery Officer (TRO) / IT Authority
Stages of Order 2 Stages: Order Nisi (Show cause) -> Order Absolute (Final payment) Single direct attachment and demand order
Scope of Attachment Attaches debts due or accruing at the moment of service Attaches existing balance PLUS subsequent deposits if specified
Joint Accounts Rule If debt is against A alone, Joint account (A & B) is NOT attached Attaches money belonging to assessee; joint account attached proportionately (presumed equal share unless rebutted)

How It Works / Mechanism

Operational Protocol: Processing Stop Payment & Garnishee Orders

    A[Event: Stop Payment / Garnishee / Death Notice Received] --> B[CBS Stop Flag / Account Lien Marked Immediately]
    B --> C{Type of Trigger?}
    C -- Stop Payment Request --> D[Verify Cheque No, Date, Amount -> Return with Memo 'Payment Stopped by Drawer']
    C -- Notice of Death / Lunacy --> E[Freeze Operations -> Return Cheques with Memo 'Drawer Deceased / Insane']
    C -- Garnishee Order Nisi --> F[Calculate Attached Amount vs Account Balance]
    F --> G{Balance > Attached Amount?}
    G -- Yes: Surplus Available --> H[Hold Attached Sum in Suspense; Allow Operations on Surplus Balance]
    G -- No: Balance <= Attached --> I[Total Account Freeze: Return All Cheques 'Refer to Drawer / Attached']
  • Trigger: Service of court notice or customer communication.
  • Process: Exact timestamping of notice receipt in bank records.
  • Authority: Civil Court (Garnishee), Tax Officer (Sec 226(3)), Drawer (Stop Payment).
  • Timeline: Effective immediately upon service on the branch manager.
  • Outcome: Protection of attached funds and return of cheques with standard Return Memo.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Joint Accounts & Legal Nuances

High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams

  1. Garnishee Order on Joint Account (A & B):
    • If the Garnishee order is against A alone, the bank CANNOT attach a joint account of A and B (because the debt is not due to A individually).
    • If the Garnishee order is against A and B jointly, the bank CAN attach both individual accounts of A and B as well as their joint account.
  2. Income Tax Attachment on Joint Account: Unlike a Garnishee Order, under Section 226(3)(iii) of the IT Act, the TRO CAN attach a joint account for individual tax dues of A (presuming equal 50% shares unless proven otherwise).
  3. Unutilized Overdraft Limit Trap: A Garnishee Order attaches only 'debts due or accruing'. An unutilized Cash Credit / Overdraft limit is a facility extended by the bank, NOT a debt owed by the bank to the customer. Therefore, unutilized OD limits CANNOT be attached.
  4. Stop Payment for Lost Cheque vs Sec 138 Trap: If a drawer issues a stop payment instruction to escape criminal liability when funds are insufficient, the Supreme Court ruled in Goa Plast (P) Ltd v. Chico Ursula D'Souza that Section 138 still applies even if the cheque was returned with memo 'Payment stopped by drawer'.
