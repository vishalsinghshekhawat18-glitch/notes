11:T6e2,### Comparative Matrix: Supplier's Credit vs Buyer's Credit

| Dimension | Supplier's Credit | Buyer's Credit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Credit Provider** | The **Overseas Supplier / Exporter** directly | An **Overseas Bank / Financial Institution** |
| **Mechanism** | Supplier ships goods on usance basis (Deferred payment / DA bills) | Overseas bank pays the supplier on shipment; importer owes overseas bank |
| **Instrument Used** | Usance Bills of Exchange / Trade LC | Trade LC / Bank Guarantee (LoUs/LoCs **banned since 2018**) |
| **Interest Cost** | Factored into invoice price or stated as usance interest | Benchmark (SOFR/EURIBOR) + Credit spread within RBI ceiling |

### Co-Acceptance of Trade Bills (Section 32 NI Act 1881)
- **Operational Nature**: When an Indian buyer accepts a usance bill drawn by a seller, the buyer's bank adds its signature as a **Co-Acceptor**.
- **Legal Effect**: The co-accepting bank assumes **joint and several primary liability** with the buyer. At maturity, the holder can demand payment directly from the bank.
- **Prudential Restrictions**: Banks can co-accept bills ONLY for genuine trade/commercial transactions backed by actual physical movement of goods (accompanied by invoices and transport documents), strictly prohibiting accommodation bills.

### RBI Regulatory Ceilings on Trade Credit (2026 Baseline Framework)
- **Non-Capital Goods (Raw Materials / Spares)**: Max **USD 150 Million** per transaction; Tenure up to **1 Year** or Operating Cycle (whichever is lower).
- **Capital Goods (Machinery / Equipment)**: Max **USD 150 Million** per transaction; Tenure up to **3 Years** (from date of shipment).
- **All-in-Cost Ceiling**: Benchmark rate (SOFR for USD) + **350 bps (3.50%)** for Trade Credits.12:T417,```mermaid
