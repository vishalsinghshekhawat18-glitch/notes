Soils of India: ICAR Classification, Pedogenesis, Agronomic Properties & Soil Degradation

Genesis, geographic distribution, mineral-organic profiles, and agricultural fertility of India's major soil groups classified by ICAR (Alluvial, Black/Regur, Red & Yellow, Laterite, Arid, Saline, Peaty, Forest) and their conservation imperatives.

Intuition & Context

The Living Skin of the Land: How Rocks Turn into Food

Soil is not dirt; it is the thin biological interface between living biology and solid geology. It takes 500 to 1,000 years to form just 1 centimeter of topsoil through the breakdown of rock by weather, water, and lichens.

  • In the North, Himalayan rivers pulverize granite into fine silty flour, refreshing the farmland every year during floods (Alluvial).
  • In the Deccan, 65-million-year-old volcanic lava decomposed into a heavy black sponge that swells when soaked and splits open into deep cracks when baked by the summer sun (Black Cotton Soil).
  • In the Western Ghats, punishing tropical monsoons wash away all soluble minerals, leaving a hard, rusty crust of iron and bauxite suitable for baking into building bricks (Laterite).

Iron Ore Mineral Taxonomy & Major Belts (NCERT Class 10)

  • Magnetite: Finest iron ore with up to 70% iron content; excellent magnetic qualities, highly valuable in the electrical industry; black in color.
  • Hematite: Most important industrial iron ore in terms of quantity consumed; 50% to 60% iron content; reddish-brown in color.
  • Limonite (40–50% Fe) & Siderite (<40% Fe / iron carbonate): Lower grade ores with higher impurities.

Four Major Iron Ore Belts of India

  1. Odisha-Jharkhand Belt: High grade hematite ore in Badampahar mines (Mayurbhanj) and Kendujhar (Odisha); in Jharkhand, hematite mined in Gua and Noamundi (Singhbhum). Feeds TISCO, IISCO, Bokaro, Rourkela.
  2. Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur Belt (Chhattisgarh-Maharashtra): Super-high grade hematite found in the Bailadila range of hills (Bastar, CG). Bailadila resembles the hump of an ox; contains 14 deposits of ultra-high grade hematite with optimal physical properties for steelmaking. Entire output exported to Japan and South Korea via Visakhapatnam port.
  3. Ballari-Chitradurga-Chikkamagaluru-Tumakuru Belt (Karnataka): Vast reserves. Kudremukh mines (Western Ghats, Chikmagalur; Kudre = horse face peak) are a 100% export unit; ore is transported as liquid slurry through an underground pipeline to the port of New Mangalore.
  4. Maharashtra-Goa Belt: Includes Goa and Ratnagiri district of Maharashtra. Ores are not of very high quality but are efficiently exploited; exported through Marmagao port.

How It Works / Mechanism

Pedogenic Pathways: Desilication, Self-Ploughing & Salinization

1. The Montmorillonite Swell-Shrink Mechanism (Black Soils)

Black soils contain high fractions of expanding $2:1$ lattice clay minerals (Montmorillonite):

  • Wet Phase: Water molecules penetrate the interlayer crystal lattice, causing the clay to expand up to 30%30%30%, making it impermeable and sticky.
  • Dry Phase: As moisture evaporates, the clay contracts violently, creating vertical tension cracks up to 1 m1\text{ m}1 m deep and 10 cm10\text{ cm}10 cm wide. Surface particles fall into these open fissures. Upon subsequent re-wetting, the internal pressure pushes soil upward, producing an internal mixing effect known as 'Self-Ploughing' or pedoturbation.

2. Tropical Laterization (Desilication)

Under heavy monsoon rains (>200 cm>200\text{ cm}>200 cm) and high temperatures (>25∘C>25^\circ\text{C}>25∘C), silica (SiO2SiO_2SiO2​) and alkali bases (Na+,K+,Ca2+Na^+, K^+, Ca^{2+}Na+,K+,Ca2+) are highly soluble and leach downward into groundwater:
\text{Silicate Minerals} + \text{Tropical Leaching} \xrightarrow{-SiO_2, -Ca^{2+}} \text{Iron & Aluminum Oxides Residue} (Fe_2O_3 \cdot nH_2O, Al_2O_3 \cdot nH_2O)
This residual sesquioxide enrichment forms a crust that hardens irreversibly upon exposure to air, making it ideal for building bricks (Latin later).

3. Soil Conservation Engineering & Agronomic Methods (NCERT Taxonomy)

  • Mulching: The bare ground between plants is covered with a protective layer of organic matter (straw, compost) to retain soil moisture.
  • Contour Barriers & Contour Ploughing: Stone, grass, or soil barriers built along contours of a hill slope; trenches catch water. Ploughing parallel to contours of a hill slope creates a natural barrier against down-slope water runoff.
  • Terrace Farming: Broad, flat steps or terraces built on steep slopes so that flat surfaces are available to grow crops, drastically reducing surface runoff and sheet erosion.
  • Intercropping: Different crops grown in alternate rows and sown at different times to protect soil from rain wash (e.g. maize + cowpea).
  • Shelterbelts (Windbreaks): In coastal and dry arid regions, rows of trees planted to check wind movement and anchor sand dunes, halting wind erosion.
  • Rock Dams: Rocks piled up to slow down the flow of water, preventing gully formation and subsequent ravine degradation.

4. Classification of Agricultural Systems (NCERT Class 8 Framework)

  • Subsistence Farming: Intensive Subsistence (small landholdings, high labour, dominant in monsoon Asia) vs Primitive Subsistence (Shifting Cultivation / Jhum / Slash-and-Burn and Nomadic Herding in dry steppes).
  • Commercial Farming: Commercial Grain Farming (wheat/maize monoculture in temperate grasslands), Mixed Farming (crops + livestock on same farm, typical in Europe/E. USA), and Plantation Agriculture (single cash crop like tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane covering vast estates; capital-intensive with processing factories on-site).
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Competitive Examination Traps & Soil Degradation Policies

Frequent Examiner Traps

  1. Red vs. Yellow Soil Color: The soil develops a reddish color due to the wide diffusion of iron in crystalline and metamorphic rocks; it turns yellow when iron occurs in a hydrated form.
  2. Self-Ploughing Characteristic: The term 'Self-Ploughing' is unique to Black Soil (Regur/Vertisols), NOT alluvial or red soils.
  3. Universal Indian Soil Deficiencies: Almost ALL Indian agricultural soils (including rich alluvial and black soils) are critically deficient in Nitrogen (NNN) and Organic Matter/Humus, while generally rich in Potash (KKK).
  4. Reclaiming Saline / Alkaline Soils: Reclaiming sodic/alkaline soils (Kallar/Usar) requires adding Gypsum (CaSO4⋅2H2OCaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2OCaSO4​⋅2H2​O), which displaces exchangeable toxic sodium (Na+Na^+Na+) with calcium (Ca2+Ca^{2+}Ca2+), followed by flushing with clean water.
  5. Shifting Cultivation Nomenclature Trap (Class 8 NCERT Essential):
    • Jhumming: North-East India (Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland)
    • Milpa: Mexico & Central America
    • Roca: Brazil
    • Ladang: Malaysia & Indonesia
    • Bewar / Dahiya / Podu: Central India / Madhya Pradesh / Andhra Pradesh.
  6. Soil Conservation Matching Trap:
    • Mulching: Covering bare ground with straw/organic matter (retains moisture).
    • Shelterbelts: Planting tree rows along coastal/desert margins (checks wind velocity).
    • Contour Ploughing: Ploughing parallel to contours (slows downhill runoff).
    • Terracing: Carving flat step terraces on steep hill slopes (curbs sheet wash).
  7. Plantation Agriculture Features: Large estates, single cash crop (Tea in Assam/Sri Lanka, Coffee in Brazil/Karnataka, Rubber in Malaysia/Kerala), highly capital-intensive with processing unit located inside or close to the plantation.
  8. Global Grasslands Matching Trap:
    • Prairies: North America (Granary of the world, chinook winds)
    • Pampas: South America (Argentina, alfalfa grass)
    • Veld: South Africa (Maize triangle, merino sheep)
    • Downs: Australia (Murray-Darling basin)
    • Steppes: Eurasia (Russia / Central Asia)
    • Llanos & Campos: Tropical Savannas (Venezuela & Brazil).
  9. Deserts: Hot vs Cold Trap: Ladakh is a high-altitude cold desert in the rain-shadow of the Greater Himalayas (very low rainfall, freezing winters, dry air), whereas the Thar is a subtropical hot desert (high daytime temperatures, intense evaporation). Both share the diagnostic threshold of annual precipitation < 25 cm.
  10. Iron Ore & Port Matching Trap:
  • Bailadila (Chhattisgarh): Super-high grade hematite exported through Visakhapatnam Port (AP) to Japan/S. Korea.
  • Kudremukh (Karnataka): Exported as slurry pipeline through New Mangalore Port (Karnataka).
  • Goa/Ratnagiri: Exported through Marmagao Port (Goa).
  1. Foreign Collaborations for Steel Plants (2nd FYP):
  • Bhilai (CG): USSR
  • Rourkela (Odisha): West Germany
  • Durgapur (WB): United Kingdom
  • Bokaro (Jharkhand): USSR (3rd/4th FYP)
  • Visakhapatnam (AP): Shore-based (no foreign collaboration needed for port locational advantage).
  1. Nuclear Coolant Trap: Narora (UP) relies on freshwater from the Upper Ganga Canal/Ganga; Rawatbhata (RJ) relies on Rana Pratap Sagar reservoir; while Tarapur, Kudankulam, and Kalpakkam rely on seawater cooling.
