12:T524,How did an introspective, London-trained barrister transform into the indisputable mass leader of the largest anti-colonial struggle in human history?

Mahatma Gandhi did not arrive in India in 1915 as an untested political philosopher. He arrived having completed a twenty-year laboratory experiment in South Africa (1893–1914):
* In South Africa, confronting severe racial discrimination against Indian indentured labourers and merchants (poll tax, Asiatic Registration Act, invalidation of non-Christian marriages), Gandhi forged the radical technique of **Satyagraha** (*Satya* = Truth, *Agraha* = Firm adherence / Soul-force).
* Influenced by Leo Tolstoy (*The Kingdom of God is Within You*), John Ruskin (*Unto This Last*), and Henry David Thoreau (*Civil Disobedience*), Gandhi rejected both the "polite mendicancy" of the Indian Moderates and the "individual terror" of the Revolutionaries.
* On the advice of his political guru, **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**, Gandhi spent his first two years in India (1915–1916) travelling in third-class train compartments across the subcontinent with his "ears open and mouth shut", absorbing the lived agony of ordinary peasants. When he finally struck, he intervened not on lofty constitutional abstractions, but on immediate, localized, visceral economic oppressions.13:T1609,### 1. The Early Interventions Comparison Table

### 2. The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (April 13, 1919)
* **Immediate Context:** Arrest of popular nationalist leaders **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal** in Amritsar under the Rowlatt Act on April 10, 1919, leading to local riots.
* **The Atrocity (April 13, 1919 - Baisakhi Day):**
  * A peaceful gathering of 20,000 men, women, and children gathered in the walled enclosure of Jallianwala Bagh to protest the arrests and celebrate Baisakhi.
  * Acting Brigadier-General **Reginald Dyer** marched in with Gurkha and Baluchi riflemen, sealed the only narrow exit, and without giving any warning, ordered his troops to fire 1,650 rounds into the trapped crowd until ammunition was exhausted.
  * Officially 379 dead; independent estimates by the Congress inquiry committee established over 1,000 dead.
* **National Repercussions:**
  * **Rabindranath Tagore** renounced his British **Knighthood** (*"The time has come when badges of honour make our shame glaring in the incongruous context of humiliation..."*).
  * Gandhi renounced his **Kaiser-i-Hind** gold medal and called off the Rowlatt Satyagraha on April 18, 1919, describing launching mass civil disobedience with an untrained populace as a **"Himalayan Blunder"**.
  * **Hunter Commission (Disorders Inquiry Committee):** British official inquiry; Dyer was censured and removed from command but exonerated of murder and hailed as the "Savior of the Punjab" by the British House of Lords and British public (who presented him a purse of £26,000 and a jewelled sword).
  * **Michael O'Dwyer:** The Lieutenant Governor of Punjab who endorsed Dyer's firing was later assassinated on March 13, 1940 at Caxton Hall, London by revolutionary **Udham Singh** (Shaheed Ram Mohammad Singh Azad).14:T4a8,### The Mechanics of Mass Conversion via Suffering

Gandhian Satyagraha operated on a psychological mechanism that paralyzed the British colonial apparatus:

1. **The Tactical Disarmament of Imperial Coercion:**
   $$\text{Colonial Hegemony} = \text{Physical Brutality} \times \text{Moral Justification ("Civilizing Mission")}$$
   * When an empire uses batons and bullets against armed rebels, it easily justifies itself as the protector of public order.
   * But when British police beat unarmed, non-violent, praying men and women who refuse to retaliate or flee, the moral justification for empire shatters in the eyes of world opinion and the British electorate itself.

2. **The Technique of the 25% Refund at Champaran:**
   * Why did Gandhi agree to a mere **25% refund** of the illegally extorted money from the Champaran planters, when the ryots demanded 100%?
   * Gandhi explained: The monetary amount was secondary; what mattered was that the European planters—who had behaved as hereditary feudal lords above the law—were forced to surrender their prestige, status, and legal invulnerability. 1. **Chronology of 1918 (The Invariable Prelims Trap):**
   * *Trap:* Thinking Kheda happened before Ahmedabad.
   * *Fact:* **Ahmedabad Mill Strike occurred in March 1918**, whereas **Kheda Satyagraha occurred from March to June 1918**. Remember the sequence: **CAKE** (Champaran 1917 $\to$ Ahmedabad 1918 $\to$ Kheda 1918).

2. **The "Firsts" in India Classification:**
   * **First Civil Disobedience (*Savinay Avagya*):** Champaran (1917)
   * **First Hunger Strike (*Bhookh Hartal*):** Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)
   * **First Non-Cooperation (*Asahyog*):** Kheda Satyagraha (1918)
   * **First Pan-Indian Mass Satyagraha:** Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)

3. **Dyer vs O'Dwyer Confusion:**
   * **Reginald Dyer:** Acting Brigadier-General who physically ordered the firing at Jallianwala Bagh on April 13, 1919.
   * **Michael O'Dwyer:** Lieutenant Governor of Punjab who approved the action and imposed martial law; he was shot dead in London in 1940 by **Udham Singh**.

4. **Hunter Commission Distinction:**
   * *Trap:* Confusing the 1882 Hunter Commission with the 1919 Hunter Committee.
   * *Fact:* The **1882 Hunter Commission** (chaired by William Wilson Hunter) investigated Indian Education. The **1919 Hunter Committee** (Disorders Inquiry Committee, chaired by Lord William Hunter) investigated the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.
