Climate Economics, Carbon Markets & Green Finance: CCTS 2023, Sovereign Green Bonds & Climate Risk Architecture

The macroeconomic mechanisms, regulatory structures, and financial instruments of climate mitigation and transition finance in India. Analyzes: (1) Conceptual Taxonomy (Carbon Tax [price instrument fixing cost per ton of $CO_2$] $\neq$ Emissions Trading Scheme / Cap-and-Trade [quantity instrument fixing total emissions cap] $\neq$ Carbon Credit / Carbon Offset [verified removal/reduction of 1 ton of $CO_2$ equivalent]), (2) India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS 2023, notified under the Energy Conservation [Amendment] Act, 2022 by Bureau of Energy Efficiency [BEE] & Ministry of Power), (3) Sovereign Green Bonds (SGrBs: Framework issued Nov 2022, raised ₹16,000 Crore in FY23 with a "Greenium" to finance clean public energy, transport, and water), (4) Climate Financial Risks (RBI Framework on Climate Risk: Physical Risk [floods, droughts destroying loan collateral] vs Transition Risk [carbon tax, stranded coal assets disrupting carbon-intensive borrowers]), and (5) Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) Regulations (SEBI Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting [BRSR] Core).

Intuition & Context

The Coal Power Plant and the Solar Farm: Why Carbon has a Dollar Price

Imagine a commercial bank in Mumbai looking at two loan applications in 2025:

  • Borrower A (A 40-year-old Coal Power Plant): They want a ₹2,000 Crore loan to expand their thermal plant.
    • Today they make money. But if the government introduces a Carbon Tax of ₹2,000 per ton of CO2CO_2CO2​ or Europe levies a Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) tax on their steel exports, the coal plant will become completely uncompetitive.
    • The factory closes 10 years early, its machinery becomes a worthless "Stranded Asset", and the bank suffers a ₹2,000 Crore bad debt default (Transition Climate Risk).
  • Borrower B (A Solar + Battery Storage Developer): They produce clean power.
    • Every megawatt-hour of clean power earns them a verified Carbon Credit (1 Carbon Credit = 1 Ton of CO2CO_2CO2​ avoided).
    • They can sell these carbon credits on India's new Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) to cement factories that need to meet their emission targets, generating extra non-debt cash flow.

This is the frontier of Climate Economics and Green Finance.

Money is being redirected across the global financial system through Sovereign Green Bonds and ESG disclosures to penalize carbon emissions and reward clean green technology.

Core Principle

Carbon Instrument Taxonomy & Sovereign Green Bond (SGrB) Architecture

1. Conceptual Distinction of Carbon Instruments

Instrument Operational Mechanism Price Determination Global & Indian Examples
1. Carbon Tax Government fixes a statutory price per ton of CO2CO_2CO2​ emissions. Quantity of emissions adjusts through market demand. Fixed by Government (e.g. USD 50/ton). Singapore Carbon Tax, Clean Energy Cess on coal (historical in India).
2. Cap-and-Trade (ETS) Government sets an aggregate quantitative cap on total national emissions and auctions/issues allowances. Determined by Market Supply & Demand on carbon exchanges. European Union ETS (EU-ETS), India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS 2023).
3. Carbon Credit (Offset) A certified certificate representing 1 metric ton of CO2CO_2CO2​ equivalent (tCO2etCO_2etCO2​e) reduced, avoided, or sequestered by a green project. Market-Driven Voluntary or Compliance Pricing. UN Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), Verra (VCS), Gold Standard, CCTS Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs).
4. Carbon Border Adjustment (CBAM) Import tariff levied on carbon-intensive imports (steel, aluminum, cement) matching domestic carbon price. Equal to difference in carbon prices between exporting and importing countries. European Union CBAM (enacted 2023, transitional phase till 2026).

2. India's Sovereign Green Bond (SGrB) Architecture

  • Target Allocation (9 Eligible Green Sectors): Renewable Energy (solar, wind, bio-mass), Clean Transportation (Metro rail, EV buses), Energy Efficiency, Water & Waste Management, Pollution Prevention, Green Buildings.
  • Strict Exclusions: Fossil fuels, nuclear power, large hydro-electric projects >25 MW> 25\text{ MW}>25 MW, direct biomass burning, and tobacco.
  • The "Greenium": The yield discount (~5–6 basis points) investors accept for holding green bonds compared to conventional sovereign debt due to high ESG demand.

2. Institutional Mechanics of the Indian Carbon Market (CCTS 2023)

  • National Steering Committee for Indian Carbon Market (NSC-ICM): Oversees overall carbon market governance.
  • Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE): Functions as the Administrator—sets greenhouse gas emission intensity targets for obligated entities and issues Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs).
  • Grid Controller of India Ltd (Grid-India): Operates the centralized Registry for tracking CCC issuance, transfers, and retirements.
  • Power Exchanges (IEX, PXIL): Conduct transparent trading of CCCs.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Carbon Tax vs Cap-and-Trade: A Carbon Tax sets the price of carbon and lets the market determine the quantity of emissions. Cap-and-Trade sets the quantity cap of emissions and lets the market determine the carbon price.
  • Trap 2: Sovereign Green Bond Exclusions: India's SGrB Framework explicitly EXCLUDES nuclear power projects and large hydroelectric projects with capacity >25 MW> 25\text{ MW}>25 MW.
  • Trap 3: Unit of a Carbon Credit: Exactly 1 Carbon Credit = 1 Metric Ton of Carbon Dioxide Equivalent ($1 \text{ tCO}_2\text{e}$) reduced, avoided, or sequestered.
  • Trap 4: CCTS Regulatory Administrator: The Administrator for the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme 2023 is the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), NOT SEBI or the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B (ESI & Finance): Climate risk transmission channels (Physical vs Transition), SGrB framework and Greenium, Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD), and Network for Greening the Financial System (NGFS).
  • SEBI Grade A (Securities & Finance): SEBI Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR Core), ESG rating provider regulations, and green debt securities.
  • NABARD Grade A (ESI & ARD): Climate change physical risk impact on Indian agriculture, agro-forestry carbon credits, and SGrB water management outlays.
  • SBI / IBPS PO: First sovereign green bond issuance amounts (₹16,000 Crore, Jan–Feb 2023), carbon credit unit definition, and CCTS parent ministry (Ministry of Power).
  • UPSC APFC: Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022 statutory provisions, carbon border tax (CBAM) impact on Indian exports, and green finance taxonomy.
