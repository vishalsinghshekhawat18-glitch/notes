11:T754,The district is the foundational territorial unit of field administration in India. The **District Collector (DC)**, an office created by Warren Hastings in 1772, integrates three primary executive roles:

1. **Revenue Collector:** Apex revenue authority in the district under the *Rajasthan Land Revenue Act 1956*, responsible for land revenue assessment, record-of-rights (*Jamabandi*), disaster relief management, and supervising the subordinate revenue hierarchy.
2. **District Magistrate (DM):** Head of the executive magistracy exercising preventive and regulatory powers under the *Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023 (BNSS)* / CrPC (e.g. Section 163 BNSS / old Section 144 CrPC, preventive detention, arms licensing, and coordinating law and order with the District Police Chief / Superintendent of Police).
3. **District Development Officer (DDO):** Coordinates developmental planning, centrally sponsored and state welfare schemes, and liaises with the Zila Parishad and District Planning Committee (Article 243ZD).

### The Grassroots Sub-Divisional & Revenue Hierarchy

```
District Collector (DM / DDO)
  └── Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO / SDM) — Head of Sub-Division
        └── Tehsildar (Executive Magistrate & Head of Tehsil)
              └── Naib Tehsildar (Sub-Tehsil In-Charge)
                    └── Revenue Inspector (RI / Kanungo / Girdawar) — Revenue Circle
                          └── Patwari (Grassroots Land Official — Patwar Circle)
```

The **Patwari** is the foundational village-level revenue official responsible for maintaining three vital registers:
* **Khatauni:** Register of land ownership and tenancy rights.
* **Khasra:** Field index register mapping land parcels, soil quality, and ownership.
* **Girdawari:** Biannual harvest and crop inspection register essential for crop loss compensation.12:T8aa,### 1. Rajasthan Guaranteed Delivery of Public Services Act 2011

Enacted on **November 14, 2011**, Rajasthan became the pioneer state in India to codify enforceable service delivery timelines across 153+ services (e.g. caste certificates, revenue records, electricity connections, birth/death registrations) across 18 departments.

* **Designated Officer:** Prescribed official responsible for delivering the service within the stipulated timeline (e.g. 7 days for a caste certificate).
* **First Appeal:** If the service is rejected or delayed, the citizen files a first appeal within 30 days to the First Appellate Authority (e.g. SDO).
* **Second Appeal & Penalty:** The Second Appellate Authority (e.g. District Collector) is empowered to impose a financial penalty ranging from **₹500 to ₹5,000** (or ₹250 per day of delay) directly recovered from the defaulting officer's salary and awarded as compensation to the aggrieved citizen.

### 2. Rajasthan Right to Hearing Act 2012 (Enacted August 1, 2012)

Complements the 2011 Act by creating a mandatory right to hearing for any administrative grievance at four progressive tiers:
1. **Gram Panchayat Level:** Public Hearing Officer (Panchayat Secretary / VDO) — Disposal within 15 days.
2. **Block / Tehsil Level:** First Appellate Authority (SDO / BDO).
3. **District Level:** Second Appellate Authority (District Collector).
4. **State Level:** State Level Public Grievance Redressal Committee.

### 3. The Sevottam Model (IS 15700:2005)

Developed by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) and standardized by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS):

| Sevottam Pillar | Focus Area | Operational Requirement |

| **Pillar 2: Public Grievance Redressal** | Receipt & Resolution | Well-publicized grievance channels, standardized acknowledgment, and time-bound disposal. |
