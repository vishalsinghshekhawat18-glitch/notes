14:T577,To understand system-wide money creation, distinguish between two types of deposits:

1. **Primary Deposits (Passive)**:
   - Cash deposited by customers from their own currency holdings into a bank account.
   - Does not increase total money supply at the instant of deposit (it merely converts currency in hand into a bank deposit).

2. **Derivative Deposits (Active)**:
   - Deposits created by the bank when it sanctions a loan or purchases an asset.
   - Expands total money supply ($M_1/M_3$) because new purchasing power has been generated.

### The Mathematical Iteration of Credit Expansion
Suppose the Legal Reserve Ratio (LRR) = $10\%$ ($r = 0.10$), and Bank A receives an initial primary deposit of ₹1,000 in cash:
- **Round 1 (Bank A)**: Retains $10\%$ (₹100) as required reserve; lends excess reserve of ₹900. The borrower pays a supplier, who deposits ₹900 into Bank B.
- **Round 2 (Bank B)**: Retains $10\%$ of ₹900 (₹90) as reserve; lends ₹810. Deposited into Bank C.
- **Round 3 (Bank C)**: Retains $10\%$ of ₹810 (₹81); lends ₹729.

$$\text{Total Deposit Creation } (\Delta D) = 1000 + 900 + 810 + 729 + \dots = 1000 \times \left(\frac{1}{1 - 0.9}\right) = 1000 \times \frac{1}{0.10} = ₹10,000$$
$$\text{Total Credit Created } (\Delta L) = 900 + 810 + 729 + \dots = ₹9,000$$
$$\text{Total Required Reserves } (\Delta R) = 100 + 90 + 81 + \dots = ₹1,000$$15:T48e,The textbook simple deposit multiplier assumes that:
1. All money lent by banks is redeposited into the banking system ($0\%$ cash leakage).
2. Banks lend out $100\%$ of excess reserves ($0\%$ excess reserve holding).
3. Creditworthy borrowers with eligible collateral are infinitely available.

### Real-World Constraints in India
- **Cash Drain / Currency Leakage ($c = C/D$)**: When borrowers withdraw a portion of loans as physical currency to make payments in the informal economy, that cash exits the banking reserve pool, truncating the expansion cycle.
- **Excess Reserves / Liquidity Preference ($e = ER/D$)**: In periods of risk aversion or recession, banks park excess funds in the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) or Reverse Repo rather than lending (e.g., post-NPA crisis risk aversion).
- **Regulatory Capital Constraints (Basel III CRAR)**: Banks cannot expand balance sheets without sufficient Tier 1 and Tier 2 capital, regardless of reserve availability.
- **Statutory Reserve Pre-emptions (CRR + SLR)**: In India, RBI locks a portion of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) into CRR (cash at RBI) and SLR (G-Secs), reducing the loanable fraction.16:T4a9,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Single Bank vs Banking System**: A single commercial bank **cannot** create deposits beyond its own excess reserves (it can only lend ₹900 from a ₹1,000 deposit). Only the **entire banking system collectively** generates the ₹10,000 deposit expansion through multiple rounds of inter-bank clearing.
- **Trap 2: CRR vs SLR Impact on Money Creation**: Both CRR and SLR constrain credit creation. However, CRR removes reserves completely into zero-interest balances at RBI, whereas SLR channels loanable funds into sovereign debt (government securities), providing interest income to banks.
- **Trap 3: Deposit Multiplier vs Money Multiplier**: The deposit multiplier ($\frac{1}{r}$) relates primary deposits to total deposits. The money multiplier ($m = \frac{1+c}{c+r}$) relates high-powered reserve money ($M_0$) to broad money ($M_3$).
