Cooperative Banking Architecture: Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs), The 3-Tier Rural Cooperative Structure & Dual Regulation Reforms

The cooperative credit architecture in India operating on the principle of mutual cooperation and democratic member control ("one member, one vote"). Examines the Urban Cooperative Banking structure (4-tiered regulatory categorization: Tier 1 to Tier 4), the Three-Tier Short-Term Rural Cooperative Credit Structure (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies / PACS at village level, District Central Cooperative Banks / DCCBs at district level, and State Cooperative Banks / StCBs at state apex), and the historical dual regulation conflict resolved by the Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020.

Intuition & Context

Democratic Banking: The Cooperative Philosophy vs Joint-Stock Capitalism

In a standard commercial bank (like SBI or HDFC Bank), voting power is dictated by capital ownership: a billionaire holding 1,000,000 shares gets 1,000,000 votes, while a retail shareholder holding 1 share gets 1 vote.

A Cooperative Bank is fundamentally different. It is established by members of a community or occupational group based on mutual self-help. In a cooperative, voting follows the democratic principle of "One Member, One Vote", regardless of how many shares a member owns. Cooperative banks mobilize local savings and reinvest them within the same local community.

Core Principle

The Three-Tier Short-Term Rural Chain

  1. Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS): Village-level grassroots units. Not regulated as banks under BR Act; deal directly with farmers for crop loans (Kisan Credit Cards).
  2. District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs): Operate at district level, federating PACS and providing banking services.
  3. State Cooperative Banks (StCBs): Apex cooperative bank at state level, linking the cooperative system to NABARD and the RBI money market.
How It Works / Mechanism

The Dual Regulation Problem & The Historic 2020 Statutory Fix

The Dual Regulation Conflict (Pre-2020)

Historically, cooperative banks suffered from a disastrous regulatory overlap:

  • Banking Functions (Licensing, CRR, SLR, Prudential Norms): Regulated by RBI under Section 56 of Banking Regulation Act.
  • Incorporation, Management, Elections, Audit, and Liquidation: Controlled by the Registrar of Cooperative Societies (RCS) under State Cooperative Societies Acts (or Central Registrar for Multi-State Coops).

The Fallout: When cooperative bank managements engaged in fraud or political crony lending (e.g., the ₹6,500 Crore Punjab and Maharashtra Cooperative / PMC Bank scam in 2019), RBI could not remove directors or restructure the board without state RCS approval.

The Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020

Passed in September 2020, this landmark amendment transformed cooperative banking governance:

  1. Powers over Management: RBI can now superscede the Board of Directors of any UCB without consulting state governments.
  2. CEO/MD Appointment: Prior RBI approval is mandatory for appointing or removing MDs and CEOs of cooperative banks.
  3. Capital Raising: UCBs can now issue equity shares, preference shares, and unsecured debentures with RBI approval to boost capital.
  4. Voluntary Amalgamation & Reconstruction (Section 45): RBI can prepare schemes of reconstruction or merger without placing the bank under a moratorium.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Are PACS Banks?: NO. Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS) are cooperative credit societies, NOT banks under the Banking Regulation Act (they cannot use the word "Bank" or issue cheques).
  • Trap 2: 2020 BR Act Amendment Scope: The 2020 amendment applies to Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs) and Multi-State Cooperative Banks. It does NOT apply to PACS or Land Development Banks.
  • Trap 3: Tiered CRAR for UCBs: Tier 1 UCBs (deposits ≤\le≤ ₹100 Cr) have a 9% CRAR requirement. Tiers 2, 3, and 4 UCBs must maintain a 12% CRAR.
  • Trap 4: PSL Target for UCBs: RBI mandated UCBs to progressively increase their Priority Sector Lending (PSL) target to 75% of ANBC (phased in by March 31, 2026).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • NABARD Grade A & RBI Grade B: 3-tier rural credit structure, Vaidyanathan Committee, N.S. Vishwanathan Committee (4 tiers of UCBs), and 2020 BR Act amendments.
  • RPSC RAS & SBI/IBPS PO: 2-mark definitions of PACS, DCCBs, StCBs, and dual regulation reforms.
