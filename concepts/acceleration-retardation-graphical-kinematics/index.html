Acceleration, Retardation & Graphical Kinematics (s-t, v-t, and a-t Curves)

The rate of change of velocity, positive and negative acceleration (retardation), and physical interpretations of kinematic graph slopes and enclosed areas.

Intuition & Context

Decoding Motion Through Slopes and Enclosed Areas

If you look at a graph of a car's motion, you can read its entire physical story without watching the car: how steep the line rises tells you how violently it is speeding up (Slope), while the total geometric area colored beneath the curve tells you the total road distance it covered (Area). This duality—Slope for rates and Area for accumulated totals—is the foundation of graphical physics.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps on Graph Questions

High-Yield Exam Pitfalls:

  1. Vertical Line Impossible on s-t Graph: A vertical line on a distance-time graph implies infinite velocity (being in multiple places at the exact same instant of time), which violates physical law.
  2. Negative Slope on s-t vs v-t:
    • Negative slope on s−ts-ts−t graph   ⟹  \implies⟹ Negative velocity (returning toward origin).
    • Negative slope on v−tv-tv−t graph   ⟹  \implies⟹ Retardation/Deceleration (slowing down).
  3. Area Below the Time Axis on v-t Graph: Area above the time axis is positive displacement; area below the time axis is negative displacement. To find total distance, add absolute areas (∣A1∣+∣A2∣|A_1| + |A_2|∣A1​∣+∣A2​∣); to find net displacement, subtract (A1−A2A_1 - A_2A1​−A2​).
