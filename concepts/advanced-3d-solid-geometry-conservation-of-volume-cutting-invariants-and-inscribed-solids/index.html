Advanced 3D Solid Geometry: Conservation of Volume, Cutting Invariants & Inscribed Solids

3D spatial geometry and solid mensuration: melting and recasting volume conservation, cutting-plane surface area expansion invariants, cylinder/cone/sphere topological properties, and maximum inscribed solids.

Intuition & Context

Physical Invariance vs Surface Area Transformation

When a goldsmith takes a heavy solid gold sphere and melts it down to produce 64 tiny identical spherical beads, two fundamental physical laws govern the transformation:

  1. The Law of Mass/Volume Conservation: Assuming zero spillage, the total volume of gold remains strictly identical: ∑Vfinal=Vinitial\sum V_{\text{final}} = V_{\text{initial}}∑Vfinal​=Vinitial​.
  2. The Non-Conservation of Surface Area: While volume remains constant, the total surface area explodes. The surface area of the 64 tiny beads combined is quadruple the surface area of the original single sphere!

Similarly, if you slice an apple into two halves with a single clean cut, its volume does not change by a single cubic millimeter. Yet, the total surface area immediately increases by exactly the area of the two newly exposed flat circular cross-sections.

Competitive examination questions in 3D mensuration (Bank PO, SSC CGL Tier 2, CAT, CSAT) exploit this duality: Volume is invariant under reformations, but Surface Area is dynamic and strictly additive under cuts.

How It Works / Mechanism

Analytical Mechanics of Solid Cuts, Melting & Recasting

1. The Cutting Invariant & Percentage TSA Increase

When any 3D solid is intersected by a cutting plane, the change in Total Surface Area is:
ΔTSA=2×Cross-Sectional Area of the Cut\Delta \text{TSA} = 2 \times \text{Cross-Sectional Area of the Cut}ΔTSA=2×Cross-Sectional Area of the Cut

Example: Bisecting a Solid Sphere into Two Hemispheres:

  • Initial Sphere: TSA0=4πr2\text{TSA}_0 = 4\pi r^2TSA0​=4πr2.
  • Cut produces two hemispherical flat faces, each of area πr2\pi r^2πr2.
  • ΔTSA=2×πr2=2πr2\Delta \text{TSA} = 2 \times \pi r^2 = 2\pi r^2ΔTSA=2×πr2=2πr2.
  • Final Combined TSA = 4πr2+2πr2=6πr24\pi r^2 + 2\pi r^2 = 6\pi r^24πr2+2πr2=6πr2.
  • Percentage Increase in TSA:
    6πr2−4πr24πr2×100=50%\frac{6\pi r^2 - 4\pi r^2}{4\pi r^2} \times 100 = 50%4πr26πr2−4πr2​×100=50%
  • If cut into 4 identical quadrants by two mutually perpendicular cuts, 4 flat faces of πr2\pi r^2πr2 each are exposed:
    Total TSA=4πr2+4(πr2)=8πr2(100% increase)\text{Total TSA} = 4\pi r^2 + 4(\pi r^2) = 8\pi r^2 \quad (100% \text{ increase})Total TSA=4πr2+4(πr2)=8πr2(100% increase)

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Longest Rod Theorems & Confusing Pairs

Critical Examiner Traps in 3D Mensuration

  1. Solid vs Hollow Hemisphere TSA Trap:

    • A solid hemisphere has a curved bowl (2πr22\pi r^22πr2) PLUS a circular base lid (πr2\pi r^2πr2). TSA=3πr2\text{TSA} = 3\pi r^2TSA=3πr2.
    • A hollow hemisphere open at top has TSA=2πr2\text{TSA} = 2\pi r^2TSA=2πr2.
    • Trap: If question states "painted on all exposed surfaces", check if it is open or closed!

  2. The Longest Rod in a Room Trap:

    • The longest pole or rod that can be placed in a rectangular room of dimensions l,b,hl, b, hl,b,h is along the 3D space diagonal, NOT the floor diagonal.
      dspace=l2+b2+h2d_{\text{space}} = \sqrt{l^2 + b^2 + h^2}dspace​=l2+b2+h2​
    • Trap: Taking floor diagonal l2+b2\sqrt{l^2 + b^2}l2+b2​ underestimates length by 15–30%.

  3. Units of Measurement Trap:

    • 1 m3=1,000 Litres=1,000,000 cm3=106 cm31\text{ m}^3 = 1,000\text{ Litres} = 1,000,000\text{ cm}^3 = 10^6\text{ cm}^31 m3=1,000 Litres=1,000,000 cm3=106 cm3.
    • 1 Litre=1,000 cm3=0.001 m31\text{ Litre} = 1,000\text{ cm}^3 = 0.001\text{ m}^31 Litre=1,000 cm3=0.001 m3.
    • In water tank filling questions, flow rate is often given in km/h\text{km/h}km/h or litres/minute\text{litres/minute}litres/minute while dimensions are in meters. Convert all parameters to meters before multiplying!
