Money Supply Aggregates: Reserve Money (M0), Narrow/Broad Money (M1, M2, M3, M4), Liquidity Aggregates & Velocity of Money

The official measurement of money supply in India based on the RBI Working Group recommendations (1977 Working Group and 1998 Y.V. Reddy Working Group). Covers Reserve Money / High-Powered Money ($M_0$), Narrow Money ($M_1$), Broad Money ($M_3$), post-1998 New Monetary Aggregates ($NM_1, NM_2, NM_3$), Liquidity Aggregates ($L_1, L_2, L_3$), and Fisher’s Velocity of Money ($V = \frac{PY}{M}$).

Intuition & Context

The Liquidity Spectrum: From Cash in Pocket to Long-Term Bank Deposits

Not all money is equally accessible for immediate spending. A ₹500 currency note in your wallet can buy groceries in 5 seconds. A demand deposit in a savings account can be spent via UPI in 10 seconds. However, a 5-year bank fixed deposit (FD) or an NBFC certificate of deposit requires formal liquidation steps.

Central banks measure the total stock of money in an economy along a liquidity spectrum, arranging assets from the most liquid (immediately spendable without conversion cost) to broad monetary stores of purchasing power. The total volume of money directly dictates spending capacity, aggregate demand, and price pressure.

Core Principle

The Official Money Supply Aggregates in India

The Reserve Bank of India compiles two major frameworks of monetary aggregates:

1. The Traditional 1977 Aggregates

  • Reserve Money (M0M_0M0​ / High-Powered Money / Monetary Base):
    M0=Currency in Circulation (CIC)+Bankers’ Deposits with RBI+"Other" Deposits with RBI\text{M}_0 = \text{Currency in Circulation (CIC)} + \text{Bankers' Deposits with RBI} + \text{"Other" Deposits with RBI}M0​=Currency in Circulation (CIC)+Bankers’ Deposits with RBI+"Other" Deposits with RBI
    (Note: Currency in Circulation includes Vault Cash with banks + Currency with the Public).
  • M1M_1M1​ (Narrow Money):
    M1=Currency with the Public (CWP)+Demand Deposits with Banking System (DD)+"Other" Deposits with RBI (OD)\text{M}_1 = \text{Currency with the Public (CWP)} + \text{Demand Deposits with Banking System (DD)} + \text{"Other" Deposits with RBI (OD)}M1​=Currency with the Public (CWP)+Demand Deposits with Banking System (DD)+"Other" Deposits with RBI (OD)
    Highest liquidity; zero investment yield.
  • M2M_2M2​:
    M2=M1+Post Office Savings Bank Deposits\text{M}_2 = \text{M}_1 + \text{Post Office Savings Bank Deposits}M2​=M1​+Post Office Savings Bank Deposits
  • M3M_3M3​ (Broad Money):
    M3=M1+Time Deposits with the Banking System (TD)\text{M}_3 = \text{M}_1 + \text{Time Deposits with the Banking System (TD)}M3​=M1​+Time Deposits with the Banking System (TD)
    Most widely monitored aggregate for macro policy and inflation forecasting.
  • M4M_4M4​:
    M4=M3+Total Post Office Deposits (excluding National Savings Certificates / NSC)\text{M}_4 = \text{M}_3 + \text{Total Post Office Deposits (excluding National Savings Certificates / NSC)}M4​=M3​+Total Post Office Deposits (excluding National Savings Certificates / NSC)

How It Works / Mechanism

New Monetary Aggregates (1998 Y.V. Reddy Report) & Velocity of Money

In 1998, the RBI Working Group (Chaired by Dr. Y.V. Reddy) restructured aggregates into New Monetary Aggregates (NM) and Liquidity Aggregates (L):

                     1998 RBI LIQUIDITY AGGREGATES
                                   │
    ┌──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┐
    ▼                              ▼                              ▼
   L1                             L2                             L3
NM3 + All Post Office       L1 + Term Deposits &        L2 + Public Deposits
Deposits (excl. NSC)         Borrowings of FIs (NABARD,   with NBFCs
                             EXIM, SIDBI, NHB) + CDs

New Monetary Aggregates (NM1,NM2,NM3NM_1, NM_2, NM_3NM1​,NM2​,NM3​)

  • NM1NM_1NM1​ = Currency with the Public + Demand Deposits with Banking System + "Other" Deposits with RBI.
  • NM2NM_2NM2​ = NM1NM_1NM1​ + Short-term Time Deposits of residents (contractual maturity up to 1 year).
  • NM3NM_3NM3​ = NM2NM_2NM2​ + Long-term Time Deposits of residents (maturity over 1 year) + Call/Term Funding from financial institutions.

Fisher's Velocity of Money (VVV)

The velocity of money represents the average frequency with which a unit of money is spent on final goods and services during a given period.
M⋅V=P⋅YM \cdot V = P \cdot YM⋅V=P⋅Y
Where:

  • MMM = Money Supply (M3M_3M3​ or M1M_1M1​)
  • VVV = Income Velocity of Money (V=P⋅YM=Nominal GDPMV = \frac{P \cdot Y}{M} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{M}V=MP⋅Y​=MNominal GDP​)
  • PPP = Price Level
  • YYY = Real Output (Real GDP)

Impact of Digital Payments (UPI/IMPS): Increases the transaction velocity of narrow money, allowing the same monetary base to intermediate a larger volume of transactions.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps, Formulas & Multi-Exam Question Patterns

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Currency in Circulation vs Currency with the Public:
    Currency in Circulation (CIC)=Currency with the Public (CWP)+Vault Cash held by Commercial Banks\text{Currency in Circulation (CIC)} = \text{Currency with the Public (CWP)} + \text{Vault Cash held by Commercial Banks}Currency in Circulation (CIC)=Currency with the Public (CWP)+Vault Cash held by Commercial Banks
    M0M_0M0​ uses Currency in Circulation, whereas M1M_1M1​ uses Currency with the Public (because vault cash is an asset of commercial banks and already backs demand deposits; adding it to M1M_1M1​ would cause double counting).
  • Trap 2: Post Office Deposits in M3M_3M3​: M3M_3M3​ does NOT include post office savings deposits. Post office savings deposits are included in M2M_2M2​ and M4M_4M4​ (and in L1L_1L1​).
  • Trap 3: Inter-Bank Deposits: Inter-bank deposits (deposits held by one commercial bank in another commercial bank) are excluded from all money supply measures (M1,M2,M3,M4M_1, M_2, M_3, M_4M1​,M2​,M3​,M4​) because they represent internal banking claims, not public purchasing power.
  • Trap 4: NSC in M4M_4M4​: National Savings Certificates (NSC) are explicitly excluded from M4M_4M4​ and L1L_1L1​.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & NABARD Grade A: Direct formula questions on M0,M1,M3M_0, M_1, M_3M0​,M1​,M3​, Y.V. Reddy aggregates (L1,L2,L3L_1, L_2, L_3L1​,L2​,L3​), and velocity calculations.
  • RPSC RAS & UPSC APFC: Concepts of Narrow vs Broad Money, high-powered money components, and liquidity ordering.
