Stressed Asset Resolution Architecture: SARFAESI Act, 2002, The Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code (IBC, 2016) & NARCL (Bad Bank)

The legal, institutional, and structural toolkit for resolving non-performing loans and stressed corporate assets in India. Covers the SARFAESI Act, 2002 (enforcement of security interest without court intervention under Section 13), the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC - Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process / CIRP timeline, Committee of Creditors / CoC, Section 14 moratorium, Section 29A disqualification, Section 53 liquidation waterfall), the RBI Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets (June 7, 2019 Circular / Inter-Creditor Agreement), and the National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited (NARCL / "Bad Bank" with sovereign Security Receipt guarantees).

Intuition & Context

Ending the Debtor's Paradise: The Shift from Recovery to Time-Bound Resolution

Prior to 2016, India was known as a "Debtor's Paradise". When a promoter defaulted on a ₹5,000 Crore bank loan, the bank spent 10 to 15 years filing cases in civil courts and Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs). While the court cases dragged on, the promoter continued living lavishly and controlling the company, while the factory assets rusted away and banks bled red ink.

The enactment of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC, 2016) revolutionized Indian credit culture. It introduced a Creditor-in-Control model: the moment a corporate borrower defaults on ₹1 Crore, the promoter loses management control, and a professional Resolution Professional takes over the company to sell it to the highest bidder within 180 days.

The IBC Liquidation Waterfall (Section 53)

If resolution fails and the company is liquidated, proceeds are distributed in a strict statutory hierarchy:

  1. Rank 1: Insolvency resolution process costs and liquidation costs (Paid in full first).
  2. Rank 2: Workmen's dues (past 24 months) + Debts owed to Secured Creditors (Pari-Passu).
  3. Rank 3: Wages and unpaid dues owed to employees (other than workmen) for past 12 months.
  4. Rank 4: Unsecured financial creditors.
  5. Rank 5: Central Government and State Government dues (taxes) + Unsecured debts.
  6. Rank 6: Preference shareholders.
  7. Rank 7: Equity shareholders (Residual owners at the very bottom).
How It Works / Mechanism

RBI June 7, 2019 Prudential Framework & The NARCL Dual Structure

1. RBI Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets (June 7, 2019)

  • Enacted after the Supreme Court struck down the February 12, 2018 circular (Dharani Sugars case).
  • 30-Day Review Period: The moment a borrower defaults (Day 1), lenders get a 30-day review period to design a resolution strategy.
  • Inter-Creditor Agreement (ICA): Lenders sign an ICA where decisions approved by 75%75%75% of lenders by value and 60%60%60% of lenders by number are legally binding on all lenders.
  • 180-Day Resolution Window: If a Resolution Plan (RP) is not implemented within 180 days, banks must make additional penal provisions (20%20%20% after 180 days, and an additional 15%15%15% after 365 days, totaling 35%35%35% extra provision).

2. The NARCL-IDRCL "Bad Bank" Architecture

  • NARCL (Public Sector Asset Reconstruction Company): Majority owned (51%51%51%) by Public Sector Banks. Acquires stressed loans from banks.
  • IDRCL (India Debt Resolution Company Limited): Majority owned (51%51%51%) by Private Sector Lenders. Acts as the specialized asset management agency to turn around and liquidate the operational assets.
  • Government Guarantee: The Union Government provides a sovereign backstop guarantee on the 85% Security Receipts for up to 5 years, covering any shortfall between realized value and face value.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Exemptions under SARFAESI: SARFAESI Act does NOT apply to Agricultural Land (Section 31(i)), nor does it apply to loans where the outstanding balance is less than ₹1 Lakh or less than 20%20%20% of the principal and interest.
  • Trap 2: CoC Voting Threshold under IBC: Passing a Resolution Plan or replacing a Resolution Professional requires 66% of voting share of Financial Creditors. Routine administrative decisions require 51%.
  • Trap 3: Section 29A Disqualification: Promoters who are willful defaulters or whose accounts have been NPAs for >= 1 year are strictly barred from bidding for their own company under Section 29A of IBC.
  • Trap 4: Government Dues in IBC Waterfall: Under Section 53 of IBC, Government taxes rank BELOW secured financial creditors and unsecured financial creditors (overriding the historic Crown Debt doctrine).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: IBC Section 7/9/14/29A/53, Section 13 SARFAESI notice, June 7 Circular ICA thresholds (75% value / 60% number), and NARCL 15:85 structure.
  • IIBF DBF, SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: 60-day SARFAESI notice, 180-day CIRP timeline, and Bad Bank concept.
