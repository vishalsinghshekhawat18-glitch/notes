11:T458,Prior to 1999, India operated under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act (FERA) 1973, a draconian law born in an era of acute foreign exchange scarcity where any unauthorized possession of foreign currency was a non-bailable criminal offense carrying prison terms. Following the 1991 economic liberalization, India shifted philosophy from criminal 'regulation and conservation' (FERA) to civil 'management and facilitation' (FEMA 1999). Under FEMA, foreign exchange transactions are split into two conceptual universes: (1) **Current Account Transactions** (import/export payments, foreign travel, medical treatment, overseas education)—these are **presumed open and free** unless expressly prohibited in government negative lists; and (2) **Capital Account Transactions** (buying overseas factories, investing in foreign real estate, taking external commercial borrowings)—these alter sovereign balance sheet assets/liabilities and are **presumed closed and restricted** unless explicitly permitted by RBI. All authorized transactions must flow through licensed banking gatekeepers: Authorised Dealers (ADs).12:Taa6,### 1. The Fundamental FEMA Legal Duality: Section 5 vs Section 6

```
FOREIGN EXCHANGE TRANSACTIONS UNDER FEMA 1999
├── 1. CURRENT ACCOUNT TRANSACTIONS (Section 5 / Section 2(j))
│   ├── Rule of Law: PERMITTED by default, unless explicitly prohibited / restricted
│   ├── Schedule I (Prohibited): Lottery winnings, football pools, banned magazines
│   ├── Schedule II (Govt Approval): Cultural tours, freight on exports, transhipment
│   └── Schedule III (RBI Approval beyond limits): LRS ($250k), business travel, medical
└── 2. CAPITAL ACCOUNT TRANSACTIONS (Section 6 / Section 2(e))
    ├── Rule of Law: PROHIBITED by default, unless specifically permitted by RBI rules
    ├── Permissible for Residents: Overseas Direct Investment (ODI), real estate abroad, LRS capital flows
    ├── Permissible for Non-Residents: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), FPI, NRE/FCNR deposits, ECBs
    └── Strictly Prohibited: Chit funds, Nidhi companies, agricultural/plantation real estate, TDRs
```

### 2. Classification Matrix of Authorised Persons (APs) under Section 10

| AP Category | Eligible Institutions | Scope of Permissible Forex Activities | Regulatory Oversight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Authorised Dealer Category-I (AD Cat-I)** | Scheduled Commercial Banks, selected State/Urban Co-op Banks | **Full spectrum:** All Current and Capital Account transactions (trade, remittances, loans, derivatives, FDI, ODI) | RBI Foreign Exchange Department |
| **Authorised Dealer Category-II (AD Cat-II)** | Upgraded FFMCs, non-bank entities, selected RRBs / Co-op Banks | **Specified Non-Trade Current Account** transactions: Private travel, medical treatment, overseas studies, gifts, business travel | RBI FED guidelines |
| **Authorised Dealer Category-III (AD Cat-III)** | Select financial institutions (e.g., EXIM Bank, SIDBI, IFCI) | **Trade-related transactions** and specialized foreign currency lines aligned with their core charters | Specialized RBI licensing |
| **Full-Fledged Money Changers (FFMC)** | Registered non-bank entities | **Purchase & sale of foreign currency notes / traveller's cheques** for private travel and business travel only | Periodic inspection by RBI |

### 3. Civil Penalties & Compounding (Sections 13 & 15)
FEMA is a **civil law** (not criminal). Contraventions attract monetary penalties under Section 13: up to **thrice the sum involved** if quantifiable, or up to **₹2 Lakhs** where not quantifiable, plus ₹5,000 per day for continuing contraventions. Section 15 provides for voluntary **Compounding of Contraventions** before RBI compounding officers, resolving procedural violations without court prosecution.13:T434,### High-Yield Distinctions & Examiner Traps

- **Trap 1: FERA vs FEMA Nature:** FERA 1973 was a criminal law (presumption of guilt, arrest powers, jail). FEMA 1999 is a **civil statute** (civil penalties, compounding). Imprisonment under FEMA occurs *only* for civil failure to pay adjudicated penalties (civil detention), NOT as a direct punishment for contravention.
- **Trap 2: Current Account vs Capital Account Default Rules:** Remember the opposing legal presumptions: **Current account is permitted unless prohibited**; **Capital account is prohibited unless permitted**.
- **Trap 3: Residential Status Formula (182-Day Rule):** FEMA looks at stay of **more than 182 days during the course of the PRECEDING financial year**, modified by the *intent and purpose* of going abroad (employment/business). This differs from the Income Tax Act Section 6 residential test.
- **Trap 4: AD Category Tiers:** AD Cat-I handles **all** forex transactions; AD Cat-II handles specified non-trade current transactions; FFMCs can only buy/sell foreign currency notes and travel cards.14:T42b,### FEMA 1999 Statutory Architecture

1. **Philosophical & Legal Transformation**
   - Shift from FERA 1973 (punitive/criminal) to FEMA 1999 (civil/regulatory facilitation).
   - Adjudication and compounding under Section 13 & 15.

2. **Transaction Duality**
   - **Section 5 (Current Account):** Full convertibility. Schedule I (Prohibited: lotteries, racing); Schedule II (Govt approval); Schedule III (RBI approval beyond limits / LRS).
   - **Section 6 (Capital Account):** Regulated convertibility. Inflows (FDI, FPI, ECB) and Outflows (ODI, foreign assets) governed by explicit RBI regulations.

3. **Authorised Persons Hierarchy (Section 10)**
   - **AD Category-I:** Commercial and Co-op banks (Universal forex operations).
   - **AD Category-II:** Specified non-trade current forex.
   - **AD Category-III:** Specialized institutions (EXIM Bank, SIDBI).
   - **FFMC:** Currency exchange and travel cards.

4. **Residency Determination (Section 2(v))**
