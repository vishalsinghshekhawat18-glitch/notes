Payment & Settlement Systems: PSSA 2007, NPCI Architecture, UPI Ecosystem & Wholesale Settlement (RTGS vs NEFT)

The legal, institutional, and technological architecture of payment and settlement systems in India under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (PSSA). Covers the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI: Section 8 company owned by banks), retail payment innovations (Unified Payments Interface / UPI, IMPS, AePS, NACH, BBPS, NETC FASTag), and wholesale gross/batch settlement rails: Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS: continuous individual gross settlement, minimum ₹2 Lakh, 24x7x365) vs National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT: half-hourly deferred net settlement batches, 24x7x365, no minimum or maximum limit).

Intuition & Context

The Digital Plumbing: How Money Moves Instantly Across India

When you scan a QR code at a street tea stall and pay ₹15 using UPI, what happens behind the scenes?

Within 1.5 seconds, money is debited from your private bank in Mumbai and credited to the tea vendor's cooperative bank in Jaipur. You never shared your bank account number or IFSC code, and the transaction cost zero fees.

India operates the most advanced digital payment infrastructure in the world. Built on open, interoperable public digital rails governed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) and regulated by the RBI, this payment ecosystem processes over 13 Billion instant UPI transactions every month.

UPI Innovations

  • UPI 123Pay: Feature phone payments via IVR, missed call, or sound-based tech (no internet required).
  • UPI Lite: On-device wallet for small offline transactions (up to ₹500 per transaction, wallet balance up to ₹2,000) without PIN.
  • Credit on UPI: Pre-sanctioned credit lines and RuPay credit cards linked directly to UPI.
How It Works / Mechanism

Wholesale Settlement Rails: RTGS vs NEFT Comparative Analysis

Large-value and interbank electronic transfers are processed through two distinct central bank settlement rails:

Feature Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT)
Settlement Type Gross Settlement (Each transaction settled individually without netting) Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) (Inflows & outflows netted across banks)
Processing Mode Real-Time / Continuous (Immediate irrevocable legal finality) Batch-based (Processed in 48 half-hourly batches per day)
Minimum Limit ₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh) ₹1 (No Minimum Limit)
Maximum Limit No Maximum Limit No Maximum Limit
Operating Hours 24x7x365 (Round the clock since Dec 2020) 24x7x365 (Round the clock since Dec 2019)
Ideal Use Case High-value corporate transactions, interbank settlements, real estate purchases Retail bill payments, salaries, routine remittances, small ticket transfers
Customer Charges Zero charges for online/digital transfers Zero charges for online/digital transfers
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Minimum Transaction Limits (RTGS vs NEFT):
    • RTGS has a statutory minimum transaction floor of ₹2 Lakh.
    • NEFT has NO minimum limit (starts at ₹1) and NO maximum limit.
  • Trap 2: Processing Mechanism (Gross vs Batch): RTGS is Gross Real-Time; NEFT is Deferred Net Settlement in 48 half-hourly batches.
  • Trap 3: NPCI Legal Entity: NPCI is a Not-for-Profit Section 8 company created by RBI and IBA under PSSA 2007 (it is NOT a statutory government corporation or commercial for-profit firm).
  • Trap 4: IMPS Transaction Limit: The maximum limit for IMPS transfers is ₹5 Lakh per transaction (raised from ₹2 Lakh in 2021).

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: PSSA 2007 provisions, RTGS vs NEFT mechanics (DNS vs Gross), and NPCI corporate structure.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: RTGS minimum limit (₹2 Lakh), NEFT 48 batches, IMPS limit (₹5 Lakh), and UPI features.
