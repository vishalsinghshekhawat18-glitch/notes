12:T545,Formulated by economist Irving Fisher (1930), the relationship connects three variables:

### 1. The Exact Fisher Equation
$$(1 + i) = (1 + r)(1 + \pi^e)$$
Expanding:
$$1 + i = 1 + r + \pi^e + r\pi^e$$
Subtracting 1 and dropping the cross-product term $r\pi^e$ (which is negligibly small for low rates):
$$i \approx r + \pi^e \implies r \approx i - \pi^e$$
Where:
- $i$ = Nominal interest rate (the stated rate on financial contracts)
- $r$ = Real interest rate (the real growth rate of purchasing power)
- $\pi^e$ = Expected inflation rate over the loan period

### 2. Ex-Ante vs Ex-Post Real Interest Rate
- **Ex-Ante Real Interest Rate ($r_{\text{ex-ante}} = i - \pi^e$)**: The real return expected at the time a loan contract is signed.
- **Ex-Post Real Interest Rate ($r_{\text{ex-post}} = i - \pi$)**: The actual real return realized after the actual inflation rate ($\pi$) is revealed at loan maturity.

### 3. The Fisher Effect (Long-Run Monetary Neutrality)
According to the Classical Dichotomy, the equilibrium real interest rate ($r$) is determined in the goods market by real forces (savings supply and investment productivity). Therefore, an increase in central bank money growth raises expected inflation $\pi^e$, causing a **one-for-one upward adjustment in nominal interest rates ($i$)**, leaving long-run equilibrium $r$ unaffected.13:T59e,When central bank policy rates or deposit rates fall below the prevailing inflation rate ($i < \pi$), the real interest rate turns **negative** ($r < 0$). This produces significant structural distortions in an emerging economy like India:

```
                  NEGATIVE REAL INTEREST RATE REGIME (i < π)
                                     │
        ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                            ▼                            ▼
SAVERS & DEPOSITS            BORROWERS & ASSETS           CURRENCY & FX
• Penalty on savers         • Subsidized debt cost       • Outflow of foreign capital
• Flight from bank deposits • Over-leveraging            • Depreciation pressure on ₹
• Shift to Gold & Real      • Speculative bubbles in     • Import inflation channel
  Estate (Physical Assets)    real estate & equities
```

### Key Channels
1. **Financial Disintermediation**: Households withdraw financial savings from banks and purchase physical inflation-hedges (Gold, Real Estate), starving the formal manufacturing sector of long-term investment capital.
2. **Financial Repression**: Governments historically maintained artificially low nominal interest rates below inflation to cheapen sovereign debt service at the expense of domestic savers.14:T490,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Exact vs Approximate Fisher Equation**: For low rates ($i = 6\%, \pi = 2\%$), the approximation $r \approx i - \pi = 4\%$ is fine. But for high rates ($i = 20\%, \pi = 10\%$), exact formula is $\frac{1.20}{1.10} - 1 = 9.09\%$, whereas approximation yields $10\%$. RBI Grade B Phase 2 often tests exact formula calculation.
- **Trap 2: Ex-Ante vs Ex-Post**: Ex-ante uses **expected inflation** $\pi^e$; Ex-post uses **actual realized inflation** $\pi$.
- **Trap 3: Real Rate and Monetary Policy Stance**: A policy repo rate of $6.50\%$ when CPI inflation is $4.00\%$ implies a positive real policy rate of $+2.50\%$ (restrictive/tight stance). If inflation surges to $7.00\%$, the real policy rate becomes $-0.50\%$ (accommodative/expansionary stance in real terms), even without any change in the nominal rate!
