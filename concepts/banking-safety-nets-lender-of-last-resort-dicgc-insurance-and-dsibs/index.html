Banking Safety Nets: Lender of Last Resort (LOLR), DICGC Deposit Insurance & Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs)

The three-layered safety net protecting depositors and preventing cascading panics in the Indian banking system: Central Bank Lender of Last Resort (LOLR under Sections 17 & 18 of RBI Act), Deposit Insurance under the DICGC Act, 1961 (statutory coverage of ₹5 Lakh per depositor per bank, mandatory 90-day interim payout under the 2021 Amendment Act), and the Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs / "Too Big to Fail") framework requiring Additional Tier 1 / CET1 capital surcharges for systemically critical lenders (SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank).

Intuition & Context

The Safety Cushion: Preventing the Domino Effect in Banking

Why does the collapse of a textile factory cause zero panic, while the collapse of a single mid-sized bank can freeze an entire economy?

Because banks are deeply interconnected through interbank deposits, payment clearing, and public psychology. If depositors see one bank shutter its doors, fear spreads like wildfire: "What if my bank is next?" Depositors rush to pull cash out of every bank in the city (contagion run).

To prevent this domino effect, modern economies construct a Three-Layer Safety Net:

  1. Lender of Last Resort (RBI): Provides instant emergency cash to solvent banks.
  2. Deposit Insurance (DICGC): Guarantees that small depositors will get up to ₹5 Lakh back even if their bank goes bankrupt.
  3. D-SIB Regulation: Forces "Too Big to Fail" banks to hold extra equity capital so they never fail in the first place.

How It Works / Mechanism

Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs): "Too Big to Fail"

Under RBI's D-SIB framework, banks with systemic footprints exceeding 2%2%2% of Indian GDP are assessed across four quantitative parameters:

  1. Size (40%40%40% weight) | 2. Interconnectedness (20%20%20% weight) | 3. Substitutability (20%20%20% weight) | 4. Complexity (20%20%20% weight).

Why Surcharges Matter: Because a government bailout of SBI or HDFC Bank would cost trillions in taxpayer money, the RBI forces these mega-banks to maintain higher CET1 capital buffers ($11.7% - 12.3%$ total CRAR) during normal times.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: DICGC Insurance Limit: The insurance limit was raised in Budget 2020 from ₹1 Lakh to ₹5 Lakh per depositor per bank.
  • Trap 2: Same Right & Capacity: If you have ₹5 Lakh in a Savings Account and ₹5 Lakh in a Fixed Deposit in the same bank in your individual name, you get only ₹5 Lakh total insurance. But if you have an individual account AND a joint account (with your spouse) in the same bank, both accounts are insured for ₹5 Lakh separately (different capacity).
  • Trap 3: Insured Institutions: DICGC covers ALL commercial banks AND cooperative banks. It does NOT cover NBFCs, Mutual Funds, or Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS).
  • Trap 4: Designated D-SIBs: Exactly 3 banks are classified as D-SIBs in India: SBI, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A: D-SIB bucketing criteria, additional CET1 surcharges, DICGC 90-day payout rule, and Bagehot's LOLR doctrine.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: DICGC limit (₹5 Lakh), 90-day timeline, 3 D-SIB names, and DICGC ownership (100% RBI subsidiary).
