```12:T42f,How does a government finance its Fiscal Deficit?

1. **Market Borrowings (Non-Monetized)**: Selling dated G-Secs and T-Bills to commercial banks, insurance companies, and mutual funds. (Does NOT expand the base money supply directly).
2. **Monetized Deficit (Deficit Financing / Printing Money)**:
   - That part of the fiscal deficit funded directly by the **Reserve Bank of India** through the purchase of government securities or direct overdrafts.
   - **Historical Evolution**:
     - *Pre-1997*: Automatic monetization via **Ad-hoc Treasury Bills (91-day 4.6% fixed rate)**. The government issued ad-hoc bills, RBI automatically printed money, sparking massive double-digit inflation!
     - *The 1997 Rangarajan-Manmohan Pact*: Abolished ad-hoc treasury bills on March 31, 1997, replacing them with **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** for temporary cash flow mismatches.
     - *FRBM Act 2003 (Section 5)*: Formally **prohibited RBI from subscribing to primary issuances of Central Government securities** from April 1, 2006 (except under emergency escape clauses).13:T468,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Fiscal Deficit Formula**: Fiscal Deficit is Total Expenditure minus **NON-DEBT Receipts** (Revenue Receipts + Non-Debt Capital Receipts). If you subtract Total Receipts including borrowings, the answer is always ZERO (accounting identity)!
- **Trap 2: Primary Deficit Zero Meaning**: If **Primary Deficit = 0**, it means **Fiscal Deficit = Interest Payments**. The government is borrowing money *solely and entirely* to service past accumulated debt interest!
- **Trap 3: Effective Revenue Deficit Concept**: ERD is always **smaller than Revenue Deficit** because it subtracts capital-creating grants.
- **Trap 4: Monetized Deficit Mechanism**: Under the FRBM Act 2003, the RBI is strictly barred from buying G-Secs directly in the primary auction; it can only conduct secondary market OMOs.

### Cross-Exam Focus
- **RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A**: Mathematical derivations of all 5 deficits, primary deficit signal, and ad-hoc treasury bill abolition history (1997).
