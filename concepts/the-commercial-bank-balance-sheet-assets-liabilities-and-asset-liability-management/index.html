The Commercial Bank Balance Sheet: Assets, Liabilities, Net Worth & Asset-Liability Management (ALM)

The first-principles financial accounting and risk structure of a commercial bank balance sheet under Form A (Third Schedule of Banking Regulation Act, 1949): the fundamental distinction between Bank Liabilities (Deposits, Borrowings, Capital & Reserves) and Bank Assets (Cash & RBI Balances, Money at Call, Investments in G-Secs/Bonds, Advances/Loans, Fixed Assets). Analyzes the mechanics of Asset-Liability Management (ALM), structural liquidity gaps, and interest rate sensitivity.

Intuition & Context

The Inverted Balance Sheet: Why What You Own Is What The Bank Owes

To understand bank accounting, you must flip your everyday perspective upside down.

When you check your banking app and see ₹50,000 in your savings account, that ₹50,000 is your asset. But to the bank, that ₹50,000 is a Liability—an obligation the bank owes to you and must repay upon your demand.

Conversely, if you take a ₹25,00,000 home loan, that loan is your liability (debt you owe), but it is the bank's primary Earning Asset—a legally enforceable contract entitling the bank to 20 years of monthly interest payments.

Bank Capital functions as the shock-absorbing equity cushion protecting depositors from loan default losses.

How It Works / Mechanism

Asset-Liability Management (ALM) & Structural Maturity Gaps

Because bank assets (long-term loans) and liabilities (short-term deposits) have different maturities and repricing dates, banks face two primary balance sheet risks:

1. Structural Liquidity Gap Analysis

Banks slot all cash inflows (maturing assets) and cash outflows (maturing liabilities) into standardized time buckets (e.g., Next Day, 2–7 Days, 8–14 Days, 15–30 Days, 1–3 Months, 3–6 Months, 6–12 Months, 1–3 Years, 3–5 Years, Over 5 Years):
Maturity Gapt=Maturing Assetst−Maturing Liabilitiest\text{Maturity Gap}_t = \text{Maturing Assets}_t - \text{Maturing Liabilities}_tMaturity Gapt​=Maturing Assetst​−Maturing Liabilitiest​

  • Negative Gap (Deficit): Maturing liabilities exceed maturing assets. Bank must scramble to borrow fresh short-term funds or refinance.
  • Regulatory Prudential Limits: RBI mandates strict tolerance limits on negative mismatches (e.g., maximum 20%20%20% cumulative mismatch in the 1–14 day bucket).

2. Interest Rate Risk: Net Interest Income (NII) Sensitivity

  • Asset-Sensitive (Positive Gap): Rate-sensitive assets (RSARSARSA) exceed rate-sensitive liabilities (RSLRSLRSL). A policy rate hike increases NIM.
  • Liability-Sensitive (Negative Gap): RSL>RSARSL > RSARSL>RSA. A policy rate hike squeezes NIM because deposit costs rise faster than loan yields.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Deposits as Assets vs Liabilities: The most common beginner trap is thinking bank deposits are assets. Deposits are strictly LIABILITIES of the bank; Advances/Loans are ASSETS.
  • Trap 2: Cash with RBI (CRR): Cash balances maintained with RBI to satisfy CRR are classified under Schedule 6 (Cash and Balances with RBI) as an asset of the commercial bank.
  • Trap 3: Schedule 12 (Contingent Liabilities): Letters of Credit (LCs) and Bank Guarantees (BGs) do not appear on the main balance sheet; they are listed separately under Schedule 12 (Contingent Liabilities) as off-balance sheet obligations.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • IIBF DBF & RBI Grade B: Mastery of Schedules 1 to 12, ALM time buckets, maturity mismatch limits, and NII interest rate sensitivity.
  • SBI/IBPS PO & RPSC RAS: Distinguishing bank assets from liabilities, role of bank capital as a loss buffer, and contingent liabilities.
