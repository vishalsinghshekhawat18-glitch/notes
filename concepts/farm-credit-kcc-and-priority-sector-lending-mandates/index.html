Farm Credit Architecture: Kisan Credit Card (KCC), Interest Subvention & Priority Sector Lending Mandates

The institutional framework of agricultural credit in India: Kisan Credit Card (R.V. Gupta Committee 1998, 5-year validity, 10% annual limit escalation), Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (7% nominal, 3% prompt repayment -> 4% effective interest), RBI Priority Sector Lending (18% agriculture, 10% Small & Marginal Farmers), and landholding classifications.

Intuition & Context

Freeing Farmers from the Moneylender's Usurious Grip

Historically, when an Indian farmer needed money to buy seeds or diesel before the monsoon, local village moneylenders charged 36% to 60% compound annual interest, trapping generations in hereditary bonded debt:

  1. The KCC Revolution: In 1998, RBI and NABARD created the Kisan Credit Card (KCC). Instead of begging for a new bank loan every season, the farmer receives a revolving 5-year credit line like a credit card.
  2. The 4% Interest Magic: If a farmer borrows ₹1,00,000 to plant crops and repays on time within 12 months, the Central Government gives a 3% prompt repayment discount on the 7% rate. The farmer pays only 4% interest—lower than consumer inflation!
  3. PSL Mandates: RBI legally forces commercial banks to lend at least 18% of all their loans to agriculture, ensuring banks do not divert all money to urban malls and real estate.

Agricultural Landholding Sizing Classes (Agriculture Census of India)

  • Marginal Farmer: Operational landholding of less than 1.0 hectare (< 2.47 acres). (Accounts for ~68.5% of total holdings).
  • Small Farmer: Operational landholding of 1.0 to 2.0 hectares (2.47 to 4.94 acres). (Accounts for ~17.7% of total holdings).
  • Small & Marginal Farmers (SMF Combined): Account for 86.2% of all operational farm holdings in India!
  • Semi-Medium: 2.0 to 4.0 hectares.
  • Medium: 4.0 to 10.0 hectares.
  • Large Farmer: Above 10.0 hectares (barely ~0.6% of holdings).
How It Works / Mechanism

RBI Priority Sector Lending (PSL) Agriculture Allocation Matrix

PSL Mandate Breakdown for Commercial Banks

  • Overall Priority Sector Target: 40%40%40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) or CEOBE.
  • Total Agriculture Lending Target: 18% of ANBC.
  • Small and Marginal Farmers (SMF) Sub-Target: 10% of ANBC (phased up to FY2024-26).
  • Non-Achievement Penalties (RIDF Allocation): If a commercial bank fails to meet its 18%18%18% agriculture or 10%10%10% SMF target, the shortfall is mandatorily deposited with NABARD in the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) at sub-market interest rates, penalizing the bank.

Competitive Examination Traps & Policy Reforms

Frequent Examiner Traps

  1. KCC Interest Calculation Trap:
    • Benchmark rate: 7%
    • Government subvention to banks: 1.5%
    • Prompt Repayment Incentive (PRI) to farmers: 3%
    • Effective net interest to farmer: 4% (Never confuse the 1.5% bank subvention with the 3% farmer discount!).
  2. KCC Validity & Escalation: Card validity is 5 years (not 3 years). Annual limit escalation is 10% (not 5% or 15%).
  3. KCC Animal Husbandry & Fisheries Cap: KCC limit for allied animal husbandry/fisheries activities is capped at ₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh) within the overall ₹3 Lakh ceiling for interest subvention.
  4. Small vs Marginal Landholding Boundaries: Marginal is < 1 ha; Small is 1 to 2 ha. Together, SMFs represent 86.2% of all farmers in India.
  5. Gupta Committee vs Nair Committee: KCC was designed by the R.V. Gupta Committee (1998). Priority Sector Lending was restructured by the M.V. Nair Committee (2012).

Exam Alignment

  • NABARD Grade A & RBI Grade B: Highest-frequency topic in banking and finance sections. Questions on PSL percentages, RIDF shortfalls, KCC interest rates.
  • UPSC APFC & CSE: GS Paper 3 / Economic Development. Rural credit institutional architecture and financial inclusion.
