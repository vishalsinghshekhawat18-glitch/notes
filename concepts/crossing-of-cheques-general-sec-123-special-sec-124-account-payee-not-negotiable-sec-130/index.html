Crossing of Cheques: General Crossing (Sec 123), Special Crossing (Sec 124), Account Payee & Not Negotiable Crossing (Sec 130)

Statutory framework and banking implications of crossing cheques under NI Act 1881: General Crossing (Sec 123), Special Crossing (Sec 124), non-statutory custom of 'Account Payee' Crossing, and 'Not Negotiable' Crossing (Sec 130), analyzing restrictions on paying bankers and impact on instrument transferability.

Intuition & Context

Why Crossing Is the Ultimate Safety Lock on Paper Cheques

If a person writes an open (uncrossed) bearer cheque and loses it on the street, any finder can walk into the bank branch and immediately receive cash over the counter. Once the cash is handed over, the money is untraceable.

Crossing acts as an unbreakable safety lock. By putting two simple transverse parallel lines across the face, the drawer commands the paying bank: 'Do not give cash across the counter to anyone. You may pay this money ONLY through a registered banking channel into a bank account'. This ensures an indelible audit trail. If the cheque is stolen, the thief cannot get cash and must deposit it into an identifiable bank account, making recovery and prosecution possible.

Core Principle

Statutory Types of Crossing & Comparative Effects

Classification of Cheque Crossings under NI Act 1881

Crossing Type Statutory Locator Visual / Textual Requirement Legal Mandate to Paying Banker Impact on Negotiability & Title
General Crossing Section 123 Two parallel transverse lines across face (words '& Co.' optional) Must pay only to a banker; cannot pay in cash across counter Fully negotiable; HDC can still acquire better title
Special Crossing Section 124 Name of a specific bank (e.g. 'State Bank of India'); parallel lines NOT mandatory Must pay only to the named bank (SBI) or its agent bank Fully negotiable; HDC can still acquire better title
Not Negotiable Crossing Section 130 General or Special crossing + words 'Not Negotiable' Must pay only to a banker (or specified banker) Destroys Title Cleansing: Instrument remains transferable, but transferee gets no better title than transferor (nemo dat quod non habet applies)
Account Payee Crossing Non-statutory custom recognized by Courts & RBI Circulars Two parallel lines + words 'Account Payee' / 'A/c Payee Only' Mandate to collecting banker: Credit proceeds strictly to the named payee's account Destroys transferability: Cannot be endorsed or transferred to any third party

Critical Statutory Rules on Crossing

  1. Parallel Lines Requirement: In General Crossing (Sec 123), two parallel transverse lines are mandatory. In Special Crossing (Sec 124), parallel transverse lines are NOT mandatory—the name of the banker alone written across the face constitutes a valid special crossing.
  2. Who May Cross a Cheque? (Section 125):
    • The Drawer may cross it generally or specially.
    • The Holder may cross an uncrossed cheque, convert general to special crossing, or add 'Not Negotiable'.
    • The Banker may cross it specially to another banker (its agent) for collection.
How It Works / Mechanism

Operational Mechanics: Paying and Collecting Banker Crossing Workflow

graph TD
    A[Cheque Presented for Clearing / Payment] --> B{Is the Cheque Crossed?}
    B -- Open / Uncrossed --> C[Drawee Bank Can Pay Cash Across Counter in Due Course]
    B -- Crossed Generally Sec 123 --> D[Drawee Bank Must Pay ONLY to a Bank Account Sec 126]
    B -- Crossed Specially Sec 124 --> E[Drawee Bank Must Pay ONLY to the Specified Bank Sec 126]
    B -- Crossed 'Account Payee' --> F[Collecting Bank Must Verify Payee Name Matches Account Exactly]
    F -- Payee Matches --> G[Proceeds Credited to Account]
    F -- Payee Mismatch / Third Party Endorsement --> H[Collecting Bank Refuses / Returns: Strict Negligence under Sec 131]
    D & E -- Paid Across Counter Negligently --> I[Paying Bank Loses Protection Sec 129: Liable to True Owner]
  • Trigger: Presentation of crossed cheque at counter or through clearing.
  • Process: Validation of crossing type against collecting channel.
  • Liability under Section 129: If a paying banker pays a crossed cheque otherwise than to a banker (or named banker in special crossing), the bank is liable to the true owner for any loss sustained.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Case Law & Tricky Scenarios

High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams

  1. Parallel Lines in Special Crossing Trap: A question often asks: 'Are two parallel transverse lines mandatory for a Special Crossing?' The answer is NO. Under Section 124, the name of a banker written across the face constitutes a special crossing even without transverse lines.
  2. 'Not Negotiable' vs 'Non-Transferable' Trap:
    • 'Not Negotiable' crossing (Sec 130) does NOT stop transferability; the cheque can still be transferred/endorsed from person to person. However, no transferee can get a better title than the transferor (title cleansing is destroyed).
    • 'Account Payee' crossing DOES stop transferability; the cheque cannot be transferred to anyone other than the named payee.
  3. Double Special Crossing Prohibition (Section 127): Where a cheque is crossed specially to more than one banker, the drawee banker shall refuse payment, EXCEPT when crossed to an agent banker for collection.
  4. Opening of Crossing Trap: Only the Drawer has the legal right to cancel or 'open' a crossing by writing 'Crossing Cancelled / Pay Cash' with full signature.
