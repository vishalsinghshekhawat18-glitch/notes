Galileo's Principle of Inertia & Newton's First Law of Motion

The foundational law of dynamics stating that a body remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless compelled to change by an unbalanced external force.

Intuition & Context

Why You Lurch Forward When a Bus Brakes

For two thousand years before Galileo, Aristotle taught that "things move only while you push them, and stop when you let go." Galileo discovered that stopping is NOT a natural desire of objects—it is caused by the hidden invisible force of friction. In a frictionless vacuum, a rolled ball rolls forever. When a speeding bus hits the brakes, the bus stops, but your body's upper half has inertia: it keeps moving forward at $60 , \text{km/h}$ until your seatbelt pulls on you.

Core Principle

The Three Types of Inertia & Everyday Manifestations

Classification of Inertia

Type of Inertia Physical Phenomenon Everyday Life Examples
Inertia of Rest Tendency of a stationary body to remain at rest. • Passengers fall backward when a stationary bus starts suddenly.
• Dust particles fall out when a carpet is beaten with a stick.
• Fruits detach and fall when a tree branch is vigorously shaken.
Inertia of Motion Tendency of a moving body to maintain its speed. • Passengers lurch forward when a moving bus brakes suddenly.
• An athlete takes a long running start before a long jump.
• A ball thrown upward in a moving train returns to the thrower's hands.
Inertia of Direction Tendency of a body to maintain its straight-line path. • Passengers lean outward when a car takes a sharp turn.
• Mud flies off tangentially from rotating bicycle tyres (hence mudguards).
• Sparks fly tangentially from a rotating grinding wheel.
How It Works / Mechanism

Mass as the Quantitative Measure of Inertia

1. The Mass-Inertia Relationship

Inertia is not a separate physical unit or dimension; it is an inherent property of matter directly proportional to Mass (mmm):
Inertia∝Mass (m)\text{Inertia} \propto \text{Mass } (m)Inertia∝Mass (m)

  • Kicking a football (m=0.45 kgm = 0.45 , \text{kg}m=0.45kg) sends it flying.
  • Kicking a stone of the exact same size (m=10 kgm = 10 , \text{kg}m=10kg) breaks your toe because its inertia is over 20 times greater.

2. Qualitative Definition of Force

Newton's First Law provides the Qualitative Definition of Force:

  • Force is that external agency (push or pull) which changes or tends to change the state of rest or uniform motion of a body.
