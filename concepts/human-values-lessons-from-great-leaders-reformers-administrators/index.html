Administrative Ethics & Moral Philosophy · Concept 2 of 11

Human Values & Lessons from Great Leaders, Reformers, and Administrators

The philosophical taxonomy of intrinsic and instrumental human values, universal ethical principles (truth, compassion, peace, righteousness, non-violence), and administrative lessons from Socrates, Ashoka, Gandhi, Ambedkar, and Sardar Patel.

Intuition & Context

From Moral Exemplars to Administrative Praxis

Why does an IAS or RAS officer study Gandhi, Patel, or Ambedkar instead of merely cramming administrative rules? Because rules govern routine procedures, but exemplary human values govern crises. When Sardar Patel addressed the first batch of Indian Administrative Service probationers at Metcalfe House in April 1947, he exhorted them to be the 'Steel Frame of India'—impartial, uninfluenced by political affiliations, and dedicated solely to national integration and public welfare.

How It Works / Mechanism

Gandhi's Talisman: The Universal Algorithmic Filter for Policy Decisions

Whenever a public officer is in doubt, or when self-interest begins to obscure official judgment, the Gandhian Talisman provides a precise cognitive test:

[SITUATION: Bureaucratic policy decision / discretionary fund allocation]
                     ↓
[STEP 1: RECALL THE VULNERABLE]
Recall the face of the poorest and the weakest person you have ever seen.
                     ↓
[STEP 2: TEST FOR DIRECT BENEFIT]
Ask: Will the step you contemplate be of any real use to them?
                     ↓
[STEP 3: EVALUATE EMPOWERMENT (Swaraj)]
Will they gain anything by it? Will it restore to them control over their life and destiny?
                     ↓
[STEP 4: DISSOLUTION OF EGO]
Then you will find your doubt and your self melting away.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps & Model Answer Blueprints

RPSC RAS Traps on Values:

  1. Generic Biographies vs. Administrative Application:
    • In RPSC questions like "Explain Sardar Patel's contribution to administrative values", students write his role in integrating princely states.
    • Requirement: Write specifically about his concept of civil servants as the Steel Frame, protecting civil servants who offer honest, fearless advice under Article 311, and insisting on administrative impartiality.
  2. Gandhi's Seven Sins Matrix:
    • High-frequency 2M/5M question. Memorize the exact 7 pairings (Wealth without Work, Pleasure without Conscience, Knowledge without Character, Commerce without Morality, Science without Humanity, Worship without Sacrifice, Politics without Principles).
