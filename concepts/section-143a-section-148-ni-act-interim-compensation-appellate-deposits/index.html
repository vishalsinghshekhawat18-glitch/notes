Section 143A & Section 148 NI Act: Interim Compensation & Mandatory Appellate Deposit Architecture

Procedural reforms introduced via the Negotiable Instruments (Amendment) Act 2018: Section 143A empowering trial courts to order interim compensation up to 20% of the cheque amount during trial, and Section 148 empowering appellate courts to mandate deposit of a minimum 20% of fine/compensation upon appeal against conviction.

Intuition & Context

Stopping the Delay Tactics: Why Parliament Added Teeth in 2018

Historically, dishonest drawers used a simple playbook to frustrate creditors: when sued under Section 138, they would plead 'not guilty', drag the trial out for 5 years using continuous adjournments, and if convicted, file an appeal in the Sessions Court, obtaining an automatic stay without paying a single rupee. The creditor was left holding a worthless paper for a decade.

To break this vicious cycle, Parliament enacted the Negotiable Instruments (Amendment) Act 2018, introducing Section 143A (ordering the accused to cough up up to 20% interim compensation right at the beginning of the trial) and Section 148 (ordering the convicted appellant to deposit at least 20% of the fine/award before an appeal is entertained). This completely rebalanced commercial power back to genuine payees.

How It Works / Mechanism

Operational Mechanics: Section 143A & Section 148 Execution Flow

    B --> C[Trial Court Exercises Discretion: Orders up to 20% of Cheque Amount]
    C --> D[Drawer Must Deposit Sum within 60 Days + 30 Days Extension]
    D --> E{Trial Conclusion}
    E -- Drawer Convicted --> F[Interim Compensation Adjusted against Final Fine / Award]
    E -- Drawer Acquitted --> G[Court Directs Complainant to Repay Sum + RBI Bank Rate Interest within 60+30 Days]
    F --> H[Drawer Appeals Conviction in Sessions Court]

    I --> J[Sum Released to Complainant Pending Appeal Outcome]
  • Trigger: Plea of not guilty (Sec 143A) or Filing of Appeal (Sec 148).
  • Timeline: 60 days standard + 30 days grace on sufficient cause = 90 days maximum.
  • Interest Rate on Refund: Bank rate published by the Reserve Bank of India prevailing at the beginning of the relevant financial year.
  • Enforcement: Attachment of property as fine under CrPC / BNSS.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Caps vs Floors & Supreme Court Precedents

High-Yield Traps for Banking Exams

  1. 'Maximum 20%' vs 'Minimum 20%' Trap:
    • Under Section 143A (Trial stage), the interim compensation is capped at a MAXIMUM of 20% of the cheque amount.
    • Under Section 148 (Appellate stage), the deposit is floored at a MINIMUM of 20% of the fine or compensation awarded.
  2. Prospective vs Retrospective Application (Landmark Rulings):
    • GJ Raja v. Tejraj Surana (2019): Section 143A is substantive and applies only prospectively to offences committed after the 2018 amendment (Sept 1, 2018).
    • Surinder Singh Deswal v. Virender Gandhi (2019): Section 148 is procedural and applies retrospectively even to pending appeals where complaint was filed before 2018.
  3. Interest on Refund Rule: If the drawer is acquitted, the complainant cannot just return the principal; they must pay interest at the RBI Bank Rate.
  4. Recovery Method Trap: If the drawer refuses to pay interim compensation under Sec 143A, the court cannot cancel bail immediately, but recovers it as a fine under Section 421 CrPC / Section 461 BNSS.
