Origin of Earth & The Solar System: Planetary Evolution, Lithosphere Differentiation & Earth Timeline

Cosmological, planetary, and geological genesis of the Solar System from the Nebular Hypothesis to the differentiation of the geospheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere) across the Geological Time Scale.

Intuition & Context

From Stardust to a Living Blue Marble

Every atom of iron in your blood was forged inside a dying star that exploded into space billions of years ago:

  1. The Nebula Whirlpool: 4.6 billion years ago, a spinning cloud of interstellar dust and hydrogen collapsed under its own gravity. The center ignited into our Sun; the leftover orbiting rings condensed into pebbles, rocks, and eventually eight planets.
  2. The Molten Separation (Differentiation): Early Earth was bombarded by asteroids and melted into a boiling molten sphere. Dense melted iron sank like lead fishing weights to the core, while foamy silicate scum floated to the top and cooled into Earth's rock crust.
  3. Planetary Outgassing: Deep volcanic eruptions burped out water vapor, which condensed into torrential rains lasting millions of years, filling the lowest hollows to form the primordial oceans.

The Hierarchy of Geological Time

  • Eon (Largest division): Hadean (4.6–4.0 Ga, molten earth), Archean (4.0–2.5 Ga, first unicellular life), Proterozoic (2.5–0.54 Ga, oxygenation, multicellular life), Phanerozoic (541 Ma–present, visible macroscopic life).
  • Phanerozoic Eras:
    1. Paleozoic Era (541–252 Ma): Cambrian explosion, fish, amphibians, coal forests (Carboniferous).
    2. Mesozoic Era (252–66 Ma): Age of Reptiles/Dinosaurs (Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous - Deccan traps eruption).
    3. Cenozoic Era (66 Ma–Present): Age of Mammals & Humans (Tertiary: Himalayan orogeny; Quaternary: Pleistocene ice ages & Holocene modern epoch).
How It Works / Mechanism

Atmospheric Evolution, Degassing & The Great Oxidation Event

The Three Stages of Atmospheric Evolution

  1. Stage 1 (Loss of Primordial Atmosphere): The primordial envelope of hydrogen and helium was stripped away from inner planets by violent early Solar Winds (T-Tauri phase of the young Sun).
  2. Stage 2 (Volcanic Degassing): As the interior of Earth cooled, intense volcanism expelled gases trapped within the mantle:
    Volcanic Emissions→H2O (Vapour)+CO2+N2+CH4+NH3\text{Volcanic Emissions} \to \text{H}_2\text{O (Vapour)} + \text{CO}_2 + \text{N}_2 + \text{CH}_4 + \text{NH}_3Volcanic Emissions→H2​O (Vapour)+CO2​+N2​+CH4​+NH3​
    Note: Free molecular oxygen (O2O_2O2​) was essentially ZERO in this reducing atmosphere.
  3. Stage 3 (Photosynthetic Modification & Ocean Sink):
    • As surface temperatures cooled below 100∘C100^\circ\text{C}100∘C, torrential rains washed atmospheric CO2CO_2CO2​ into oceans, forming limestone (CaCO3CaCO_3CaCO3​) sediment sinks.
    • Around 3.5–3.0 billion years ago, marine cyanobacteria evolved oxygenic photosynthesis (6CO2+6H2O→C6H12O6+6O26\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{C}6\text{H}{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_26CO2​+6H2​O→C6​H12​O6​+6O2​).
    • Oxygen first oxidized dissolved iron in oceans (Band Iron Formations - BIFs), and around 2.4–2.0 billion years ago saturated the oceans to escape into the atmosphere (The Great Oxidation Event or Oxygen Catastrophe), creating the protective stratospheric Ozone Layer (O3O_3O3​).
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Competitive Examination Traps & Paleontological Milestones

Frequent Examiner Traps

  1. Free Oxygen in Early Atmosphere Trap: Early volcanic degassing DID NOT produce free molecular oxygen (O2O_2O2​). Oxygen accumulated only hundreds of millions of years later through biological cyanobacterial photosynthesis in oceans.
  2. Density of Saturn: Saturn has an average density of $0.69\text{ g/cm}^3, which is LESS than the density of liquid water ($1.0\text{ g/cm}^3); if placed in a sufficiently giant bathtub, Saturn would float.
  3. Solar Wind Stripping: Terrestrial planets lack thick hydrogen envelopes because: (a) higher temperatures near the Sun prevented light gases from condensing, (b) their lower gravity could not retain light gases, and (c) intense early solar winds stripped them away.
  4. Age of Earth: Authoritative radiometric dating of meteorites and lunar samples establishes Earth's age at $4.54 \pm 0.05$ billion years (4.6 Ga).
