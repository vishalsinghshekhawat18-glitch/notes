12:T4bf,To understand administrative accountability, observe how an errant executive action is checked across all branches in Rajasthan.

Suppose the Department of Personnel (DOP) issues an arbitrary transfer order or cancels a public teacher recruitment exam due to question paper leaks:
1. **Legislative Control:** In the Rajasthan Vidhan Sabha, opposition MLAs grill the Education Minister during **Question Hour**, move a **Calling Attention Motion**, and the Public Accounts Committee (PAC) examines fiscal leakage.
2. **Executive Control:** The Chief Secretary issues administrative show-cause notices, suspends erring officers under Civil Services Conduct Rules, and orders an Anti-Corruption Bureau (ACB) inquiry.
3. **Judicial Control:** Candidates file a writ petition under Article 226 in the Rajasthan High Court at Jodhpur or Jaipur Bench. The High Court issues a **Writ of Mandamus** compelling the government to produce raw answer keys and quashes the arbitrary order as *ultra vires* and in violation of Natural Justice (*Audi Alteram Partem*).
4. **Ombudsman Control:** An aggrieved whistleblower files a complaint before the **Rajasthan Lokayukta**, probing disproportionate assets and corrupt misconduct.13:Tcbe,Administrative power without accountability degenerates into autocracy. Democratic governance creates a multi-layered matrix of internal and external controls.

### 1. The Classic Friedrich vs. Finer Debate (1940–1941)
- **Carl J. Friedrich (Internal / Subjective Responsibility):** Modern public administration is highly technical and scientific. Politicians cannot understand complex policies. Therefore, accountability must rely on the bureaucrat's **internalized moral conscience, professional standards, and responsiveness** to scientific knowledge.
- **Herman Finer (External / Objective Accountability):** Strongly denounced Friedrich's reliance on conscience. In a democracy, civil servants are servants, not masters. Conscience is subjective and easily corrupted. Finer insisted on **firm, external political control, legislative oversight, and legal disciplinary sanctions** for disobedience.
*Modern Synthesis:* Administrative accountability requires BOTH: Friedrich's internal ethical compass reinforced by Finer's external institutional handcuffs.

### 2. Comprehensive Matrix of Control Mechanisms

| Type of Control | Core Devices / Instruments | Strengths | Inherent Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legislative Control** | Question Hour, Zero Hour, Calling Attention, Adjournment Motion, No-Confidence Motion, Cut Motions, PAC, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU). | Establishes ultimate democratic sovereignty; exposes executive blunders publicly. | Post-mortem in nature; lack of technical expertise among legislators; party whips stifle independent inquiry. |
| **Executive Control** | Policy directives, budgetary ceilings, appointments & transfers, Civil Service Conduct Rules, disciplinary proceedings (Art. 311). | Continuous, proactive, direct, preventive, and immediate command. | Risk of political victimization, arbitrary transfers, and partisan misuse. |
| **Judicial Control** | Prerogative Writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto), Injunctions, Judicial Review (Arts. 32 & 226). | Impartial, protects Fundamental Rights, enforces Natural Justice and Rule of Law. | Slow, expensive, reactive, cannot intervene in policy merits (merely checks procedural legality). |
| **Citizen / Social Control** | RTI Act 2005, Citizen's Charters, Social Audits, Public Interest Litigation (PIL), Free Press. | Bottom-up, continuous, empowers victims, dismantles administrative secrecy. | Uneven awareness, risks of harassment or frivolous litigation. |

### 3. The Rajasthan Lokayukta: Institutional Architecture
Established under the **Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973** (First Lokayukta: Justice I.D. Dua, took office on August 28, 1973).
- **Jurisdiction:** Investigates allegations of corruption and misconduct against Ministers, Officers of IAS/RAS cadres, Heads of Departments, and Municipal Chairpersons.
- **STATUTORY EXCLUSIONS (High-Yield Exam Trap):**
  * **Chief Minister** (Excluded from Lokayukta's ambit in Rajasthan).
  * Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs).
  * High Court Judges and judicial officers.
  * Chairman and Members of the RPSC.
  * Village Sarpanch and Panchas.
  * Chief Election Officer and State Election Commissioners.14:T5d6,The High Court (Art. 226) and Supreme Court (Art. 32) review administrative actions along three classical grounds: Lack of Jurisdiction, Error of Law on the face of the record, and Violation of Principles of Natural Justice:

```
[Administrative Order / Executive Action Promulgated]
                         |
                         v
        --- GROUNDS FOR JUDICIAL INTERVENTION ---
  1. Substantive Ultra Vires (Beyond statutory powers)
  2. Procedural Ultra Vires (Mandatory statutory procedure violated)
  3. Mala Fide (Malice in fact or malice in law)
  4. Violation of Natural Justice (Nemo Judex In Causa Sua / Audi Alteram Partem)
                         |
                         v
              --- PREROGATIVE WRIT ISSUED ---
  - MANDAMUS: 'We Command' (Compelling an official to perform statutory duty)
  - CERTIORARI: 'To be Certified' (Quashing an illegal quasi-judicial order)
  - PROHIBITION: Halting ongoing proceedings in an inferior tribunal
  - QUO-WARRANTO: 'By what authority' (Challenging illegal usurpation of public office)
  - HABEAS CORPUS: 'Produce the body' (Challenging unlawful detention)
```

### The Public Accounts Committee (PAC) Pipeline:
The PAC acts as Parliament's financial watchdog. Armed with the audit reports of the **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)**—who acts as its *'friend, philosopher, and guide'*—the PAC summons departmental secretaries to account for every rupee spent, checking for fiscal regularity, waste, and embezzlement.15:T402,### RPSC RAS Mains Pitfalls & Scoring Insights:
- **Rajasthan Lokayukta Exclusions (2 Marks / 5 Marks):** This is one of RPSC's favorite traps. You MUST explicitly state who is EXCLUDED from the Rajasthan Lokayukta: **Chief Minister, MLAs, High Court Judges, RPSC Chairman/Members, and Sarpanchas**. Writing that the Chief Minister is covered will instantly result in zero marks.
- **Mandamus vs. Certiorari (2 Marks):**
  * **Mandamus:** Commands positive action ('Do your legal duty'). Issued to administrative or quasi-judicial bodies.
  * **Certiorari:** Quashes an illegal completed decision ('Decision is null and void'). Issued to judicial and quasi-judicial bodies.
- **Friedrich vs. Finer Core Thesis (5 Marks):** Frame the contrast neatly: Friedrich = **Internal / Moral / Professional Responsibility**; Finer = **External / Political / Disciplinary Accountability**.
- **PAC and CAG Relationship (2 Marks):** Remember the phrase: The CAG is the **'Friend, Philosopher, and Guide'** of the Public Accounts Committee.16:T4c4,### Architectural Breakdown: Accountability & Control Framework
- **Theoretical Foundation (The Friedrich-Finer Dialectic):**
  * Friedrich: Subjective internal responsibility; technical complexity demands professional ethics.
  * Finer: Objective external accountability; democracy demands strict subordination to elected representatives.
- **Typology of Administrative Control:**
  1. *Legislative:* Motions (Adjournment, No-Confidence), Question Hour, Financial Committees (PAC, Estimates, COPU).
  2. *Executive:* Hierarchical reporting, service rules, performance appraisal, Article 311 safeguards.
  3. *Judicial:* Judicial review under Articles 32 and 226 via Prerogative Writs:
     - Mandamus (compel public duty)
     - Certiorari (quash illegal decisions)
     - Prohibition (halt ultra vires trials)
     - Quo-Warranto (challenge illegal title to office)
     - Habeas Corpus (release unlawful detentions).
  4. *Ombudsman (Lokayukta):* Origin: Sweden (1809 Justitieombudsman).
- **Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayuktas Act, 1973:**
  * Key Inclusions: Ministers, IAS/RAS officers, local body chairpersons.
