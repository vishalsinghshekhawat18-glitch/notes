Fiscal Federalism Principles: Vertical & Horizontal Imbalances, Divisible Pool & Centrally Sponsored Schemes

The foundational principles, constitutional architecture, and inter-governmental transfer mechanisms of Indian Fiscal Federalism. Analyzes: (1) Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI: the structural gap arising because the Union Government controls the most buoyant tax bases while State Governments bear the primary constitutional responsibilities for social and economic services like health, education, and agriculture), (2) Horizontal Fiscal Imbalance (HFI: fiscal capacity disparities across rich vs poor States), (3) The Divisible Pool under Article 270 (gross central taxes minus cesses, surcharges, and collection costs), and (4) The scheme architecture of Central Sector Schemes (100% Union funded) vs Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS: cost-shared between Centre and States, categorized as Core of Core, Core, and Optional).

Intuition & Context

The Great Indian Family Budget: Who Earns vs Who Spends?

Imagine a joint family:

  • The Father (Central Government) has all the high-paying corporate jobs and investments (earning 60%60%60% of total family income via Income Tax, Corporate Tax, and Customs).
  • The Children (State Governments) have the duty of actually running daily life—buying groceries, paying school tuition, cooking food, taking sick members to the doctor, and policing the house (60%60%60% of total family expenses on Health, Education, Roads, and Police!).

If the Father doesn't share his salary every month, the children will go bankrupt in days! This mismatch is the Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI).

Furthermore, some brothers are rich and prosperous while other brothers are sick and struggling (Horizontal Fiscal Imbalance). The Constitution creates formal mechanisms to transfer money fairly to keep the entire union strong.

How It Works / Mechanism

Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS) vs Central Sector Schemes (CS)

How central funds flow to the States:

1. Central Sector Schemes (100% Central Funding)

  • Directly funded and executed by the Union Government on Union List subjects.
  • Examples: PM-KISAN (₹6,000/yr direct income support), Central Sector Crop Insurance, Space research, Urea fertilizer subsidies.

2. Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS - Cost-Shared)

  • Implemented by State Governments on State and Concurrent List subjects, with joint funding:
    • General States: 60% Centre : 40% State
    • NE & Himalayan States (Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, NE): 90% Centre : 10% State
    • UTs without legislature: 100% Centre
  • The 3-Tier CSS Classification (NITI Aayog 2015):
    1. Core of the Core Schemes (Mandatory, first charge on funds: MGNREGA, National Social Assistance Programme / NSAP).
    2. Core Schemes (Major national missions: Jal Jeevan Mission, PM Awas Yojana, Samagra Shiksha, Ayushman Bharat - PMJAY).
    3. Optional Schemes (States can choose to implement or substitute with own schemes).
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Exam Traps & Multi-Exam Crosswalks

Critical Examiner Traps

  • Trap 1: Cesses and Surcharges Exclusion: Under Article 271, Cesses (e.g., Health & Education Cess, Road & Infrastructure Cess) and Surcharges are NOT shared with States; they are retained 100% by the Centre. (A major source of Centre-State friction!).
  • Trap 2: CSS Funding Ratios: Normal CSS funding ratio is 60:40 for general states and 90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States.
  • Trap 3: Central Sector vs Centrally Sponsored: Central Sector Schemes are 100% funded by Centre (e.g. PM-KISAN). Centrally Sponsored Schemes are cost-shared with states (e.g. Samagra Shiksha, PMAY).
  • Trap 4: Article 270 vs Article 271: Article 270 creates the Divisible Pool; Article 271 allows the Centre to levy surcharges outside the divisible pool.

Cross-Exam Focus

  • RBI Grade B & UPSC APFC: Article 270 divisible pool arithmetic, cess/surcharge growth impact on effective devolution, and CSS rationalization.
  • RPSC RAS & NABARD Grade A: 2-mark definitions of VFI vs HFI, CSS sharing formulas (60:40 / 90:10), and Core of the Core schemes (MGNREGA/NSAP).
