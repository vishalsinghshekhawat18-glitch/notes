Physiographic Divisions of Rajasthan: Thar Desert, Aravalli Range, Eastern Plains & Hadoti Plateau

The structural geomorphology, climatic differentiation, and spatial distribution of Rajasthan's four cardinal physiographic divisions: Western Sandy Plain (61.11%), Aravalli Orogenic Range (9%), Eastern Alluvial Plain (23.03%), and South-Eastern Hadoti Basalt Plateau (6.86%).

Intuition & Context

The Great Geographical Diagonal of Rajasthan

Look at a map of Rajasthan:

  1. An ancient spine of weathered granite—the Aravalli Range—runs diagonally like a diagonal sword across the state from southwest to northeast.
  2. To its West lies Aridity: The Aravallis stand parallel to the incoming Southwest Monsoon winds from the Arabian Sea, allowing moisture-bearing clouds to slip past without rising or raining. The result is the Great Indian Thar Desert (61.11% of the state).
  3. To its East lies Fertility: The eastern side catches moisture from the Bay of Bengal branch, forming rich alluvial plains fed by the Banas and Chambal rivers.
  4. In the Far Southeast sits Hadoti: A rugged black-lava basalt plateau that drops into the dramatic sandstone gorges of the Chambal River.

How It Works / Mechanism

Geomorphology of Sand Dunes, Isohyet Gradients & Drainage Divides

1. The Isohyet Divide Geometry

  • 50 cm Isohyet: Runs directly along the western slope of the Aravalli Range, dividing Rajasthan into the arid/semi-arid western sector and the sub-humid/humid eastern sector.
  • 25 cm Isohyet: Bisects the Western Sandy Plain into the Hyper-Arid Marusthali (west of 25 cm, containing shifting dunes or Dhrians) and the Semi-Arid Rajasthan Bagar (east of 25 cm).

2. Dune Morphodynamics in the Thar Desert

  • Barchans: Crescent-shaped transverse dunes forming in areas of high wind speed and limited sand supply; horns point downwind; highly mobile and expand desertification (common in Churu, Bikaner, Osian).
  • Seifs / Longitudinal Dunes: Form parallel to prevailing southwest wind direction (common in Jaisalmer, Barmer).
  • Parabolic Dunes: U-shaped dunes anchored by desert scrub vegetation; horns point UPWIND (most widespread dune type in Rajasthan).

3. The Great Drainage Divide (Jal Vibhajak)

The Aravalli crest forms the great watershed of northwestern India:

  • Arabian Sea Drainage: Rivers originating on western slopes (Luni, West Banas, Sabarmati) drain southwest into the Rann of Kachchh and Gulf of Khambhat.
  • Bay of Bengal Drainage: Rivers originating on eastern slopes (Banas, Berach, Kothari) flow into the Chambal, which meets the Yamuna, eventually discharging into the Bay of Bengal.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Competitive Examination Traps & Rajasthan Geopolitical Boundaries

Frequent Examiner Traps

  1. Guru Shikhar Exact Height: Guru Shikhar on the Abu Massif is 1,722 meters high (often cited as 1,727 m when including the temple spire of Dattatreya at the summit). In standard RPSC exams, 1,722 m is the canonical topographic benchmark.
  2. Chhappan Plain vs Chhappan Hills:
    • Chhappan Plain (Chhappan ka Maidan): Alluvial plain formed by the Mahi River and its 56 village streams between Banswara and Pratapgarh.
    • Chhappan Hills (Chhappan ki Pahadiyaan): Dome-shaped granite hills located in Siwana, Barmer district (associated with Nakoda Parvat).
  3. Aravalli Length Distribution: Total length = 692 km (from Palanpur/Khedbrahma in Gujarat to Raisina Hill in Delhi). The portion inside Rajasthan is 550 km (approximately 80% of the entire range).
  4. Area vs Population Share Inversion:
    • Thar Desert: 61.11% area but only 40% population (low density).
    • Eastern Plain: 23.03% area but 39% population (highest population density in Rajasthan).

Exam Alignment

  • RPSC RAS (Prelims & Mains): Highest-frequency topic in Rajasthan Geography. Repeated 5 and 10-mark questions on Aravalli relief features, Thar desertification control, and Hadoti plateau geology.
