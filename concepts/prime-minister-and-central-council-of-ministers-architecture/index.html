The Union Executive: President, VP, PM & Council of Ministers · Concept 5 of 7

Prime Minister & Central Council of Ministers: Architecture & Collective Responsibility

The Prime Minister as the real executive (De Facto), Council of Ministers aiding and advising the President (Arts 74-75), 42nd/44th Amendments binding advice, 91st Amendment 15% size ceiling, and collective responsibility to Lok Sabha (Art 75(3)).

Intuition & Context

Intuitive Mental Model: Prime Minister & Central Council of Ministers

Think of Prime Minister & Central Council of Ministers as a fundamental structural safeguard in a constitutional democracy. Without this explicit mechanism, the balance between state power and individual freedom would collapse into arbitrary exercise of authority. In everyday governance, this provision ensures that public functionaries operate under defined legal boundaries, guaranteeing predictability, accountability, and the rule of law across the republic.

Core Principle

The Real Executive & Westminster Cabinet Governance

While the President is the formal Head of State, the Prime Minister is the Head of Government and the De Facto (real) executive in India. The Prime Minister is the Keystone of the Cabinet Arch (Ivor Jennings), coordinating national policy, commanding the majority in the Lok Sabha, and serving as the primary constitutional channel of communication between the Cabinet and the President (Article 78).

How It Works / Mechanism

Constitutional Architecture: Articles 74, 75 & 78

1. Aid and Advice Mechanism (Article 74):

• Article 74(1): There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President.
• 42nd Amendment (1976): Made the advice of the Council of Ministers strictly binding on the President.
• 44th Amendment (1978): Added a proviso allowing the President to send back the advice once for reconsideration; but if the Cabinet re-submits the same advice, the President MUST accept it.
• Article 74(2): The question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the President shall NOT be inquired into in any court.

2. Appointment, Size & 6-Month Rule (Article 75):

• PM appointed by President; other ministers appointed by President on PM’s advice.
• 91st Amendment (2003): Total ministers (incl. PM) in Union Council of Ministers cannot exceed 15% of Lok Sabha total strength (max approx. 81 ministers).
• Article 75(5): A Minister who is not a member of either House of Parliament for 6 consecutive months ceases to be a Minister.

3. Collective Responsibility vs Individual Responsibility:

• Collective Responsibility (Art 75(3)): Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. All ministers swim and sink together. If a No-Confidence Motion is passed, the whole ministry resigns.
• Cabinet Solidarity: A minister who disagrees with a Cabinet decision must either defend it publicly in Parliament or resign from the Cabinet (e.g. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar resigned in 1951 over the Hindu Code Bill).
• Individual Responsibility (Art 75(2)): Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President (exercised on the advice of the PM). The PM can dismiss any minister by advising the President to remove them.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Exception Clauses & High-Yield Pitfalls

Critical Traps & Multi-Statement Pitfalls for Aspirants:\n\n1. Article & Nomenclature Confusion: Examiners frequently confuse identical sounding powers across Union and State levels (e.g. Art 123 vs Art 213; Art 72 vs Art 161). Verify whether the authority belongs to the President or Governor.\n\n2. Absolute vs Qualified Language Trap: Beware of statements containing absolute words like "solely", "without exception", or "unconditional". Most constitutional powers are subject to ministerial aid and advice (Art 74/163) or judicial review (Basic Structure doctrine).\n\n3. Constitutional vs Statutory Distinction: Do not confuse bodies created by constitutional articles (e.g. UPSC, Finance Commission, ECI) with statutory watchdogs created by ordinary Acts of Parliament (e.g. NGT, CVC, Lokpal, NHRC).\n\n4. Appointment vs Removal Asymmetry: Remember high-yield asymmetries—such as State PSC members being appointed by the Governor but removable only by the President after a Supreme Court inquiry under Article 317.

DISTINCTION

Council of Ministers vs The Cabinet

Parameter Council of Ministers (COM) The Cabinet
Body Size Wider body (60–80 Ministers) Smaller core inner circle (15–20 Ministers)
Hierarchy Includes Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers Consists of Cabinet Ministers ONLY
Policy Role Implements policy decisions Shapes and dictates all national policy decisions
Constitutional Text Mentioned in original Constitution (Arts 74, 75) Not in original text; inserted in Article 352 by 44th CAA 1978
Meetings Rarely meets as a collective whole Meets regularly (weekly) to decide government business
