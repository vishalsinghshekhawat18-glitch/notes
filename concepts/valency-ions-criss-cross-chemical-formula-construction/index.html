Electronic Valency, Ions (Cations vs Anions) & Criss-Cross Formula Construction

The combining capacity of atoms governed by valence electrons, formation of electropositive cations and electronegative anions, and the criss-cross algorithm for writing balanced chemical formulae.

Intuition & Context

Why Table Salt is NaCl but Calcium Chloride is CaCl₂ (The Handshake Rule)

Think of valency as the number of "hands" an atom has for holding other atoms: Sodium (NaNaNa) has 1 hand (+1+1+1), and Chlorine (ClClCl) has 1 hand (−1-1−1). When they shake hands, one NaNaNa pairs perfectly with one ClClCl, making NaClNaClNaCl. But Calcium (CaCaCa) has 2 hands (+2+2+2). A single 1-handed Chlorine cannot satisfy Calcium—Calcium must shake hands with TWO separate Chlorine atoms simultaneously, making CaCl2CaCl_2CaCl2​!

The Criss-Cross Algorithm: Step-by-Step Construction

The 4-Step Criss-Cross Method:

  1. Write Symbols: Place the metal cation first, non-metal anion second.
  2. Write Charges/Valencies Below:
  3. Cross Over the Numbers:
  4. Simplify & Apply Brackets:
Example 1: Aluminium Oxide
Symbols:     Al      O
Valency:     3       2
Cross-over:  Al₂     O₃
Formula:     Al₂O₃

Example 2: Calcium Hydroxide
Symbols:     Ca      OH
Valency:     2       1
Cross-over:  Ca₁    (OH)₂
Formula:     Ca(OH)₂  (Brackets required for polyatomic OH!)

Example 3: Ammonium Sulphate
Symbols:     NH₄     SO₄
Valency:     1       2
Cross-over: (NH₄)₂  (SO₄)₁
Formula:    (NH₄)₂SO₄
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps: Bracket Omission & Variable Valency

Top Formula Traps in Competitive Exams:

  1. The Polyatomic Bracket Omission Trap:
    • Trap: Writing Calcium Hydroxide as CaOH2CaOH_2CaOH2​ (WRONG!).
    • Reason: CaOH2CaOH_2CaOH2​ means 1 Calcium, 1 Oxygen, and 2 Hydrogens.
    • Correct: Ca(OH)2Ca(OH)_2Ca(OH)2​ (Enclosing OHOHOH in brackets means 2 Hydroxide groups: 2 Oxygens and 2 Hydrogens).
  2. Variable Valency in Transition Metals:
    • Iron (Fe): Iron(II) / Ferrous =Fe2+= Fe^{2+}=Fe2+ (FeCl2FeCl_2FeCl2​); Iron(III) / Ferric =Fe3+= Fe^{3+}=Fe3+ (FeCl3FeCl_3FeCl3​).
    • Copper (Cu): Copper(I) / Cuprous =Cu+= Cu^+=Cu+ (Cu2OCu_2OCu2​O); Copper(II) / Cupric =Cu2+= Cu^{2+}=Cu2+ (CuOCuOCuO).
  3. Simplification Rule: For Magnesium Oxide (Mg2+Mg^{2+}Mg2+ and O2−O^{2-}O2−), crossing over gives Mg2O2Mg_2O_2Mg2​O2​, which must be simplified to the empirical ratio MgOMgOMgO.
