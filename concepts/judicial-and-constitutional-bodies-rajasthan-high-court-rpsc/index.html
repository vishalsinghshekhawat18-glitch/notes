Judicial & Constitutional Bodies of Rajasthan: High Court (Jodhpur/Jaipur) & RPSC (Ajmer)

The constitutional architecture and operational dynamics of apex state institutions: Rajasthan High Court under Article 214 (inaugurated 29 Aug 1949 at Jodhpur, Satyanarayan Rao Committee Jaipur Bench 1977, 50 judges) and Rajasthan Public Service Commission under Article 315 (constituted 20 Aug 1949, shifted to Ajmer 1956, 1 Chair + 7 Members, appointment by Governor vs removal by President).

Intuition & Context

Intuitive Mental Model: Judicial & Constitutional Bodies of Rajasthan

Think of Judicial & Constitutional Bodies of Rajasthan as a fundamental structural safeguard in a constitutional democracy. Without this explicit mechanism, the balance between state power and individual freedom would collapse into arbitrary exercise of authority. In everyday governance, this provision ensures that public functionaries operate under defined legal boundaries, guaranteeing predictability, accountability, and the rule of law across the republic.

Core Principle

Rajasthan High Court Setup & The Satyanarayan Rao Committee

  1. Inception & Seat:
    • Promulgated via Rajasthan High Court Ordinance, 1949.
    • Inaugurated on 29 August 1949 at Jodhpur by Rajpramukh Maharaja Sawai Man Singh.
    • First Chief Justice: Justice Kamala Kant Verma (sworn in with 11 judges from integrating princely states).

  2. Permanent Bench at Jaipur (1977):
    • The P. Satyanarayan Rao Committee (1957) was formed to allocate state capitals and apex institutions post-merger (recommended Capital at Jaipur, High Court Principal Seat at Jodhpur, RPSC and Board of Revenue at Ajmer, Mining Department at Udaipur, Agriculture at Bharatpur, Education at Bikaner).
    • A permanent bench at Jaipur was established on 31 January 1977 under Presidential order.
    • Sanctioned Judge Strength: 50 Judges (38 Permanent + 12 Additional).

How It Works / Mechanism

Procedural Mechanics & Statutory Workflow

The procedural execution of this provision follows a strict constitutional workflow:\n1. Initiation & Authority: Action originates strictly under the designated constitutional article or statutory section.\n2. Procedural Checks & Balances: Mandatory consultations, parliamentary majorities, or judicial safeguards must be satisfied prior to implementation.\n3. Legal Consequences & Remedies: Non-compliance renders the act ultra vires, opening avenues for judicial review under Articles 32 and 226.

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps, Exception Clauses & High-Yield Pitfalls

Critical Traps & Multi-Statement Pitfalls for Aspirants:\n\n1. Article & Nomenclature Confusion: Examiners frequently confuse identical sounding powers across Union and State levels (e.g. Art 123 vs Art 213; Art 72 vs Art 161). Verify whether the authority belongs to the President or Governor.\n\n2. Absolute vs Qualified Language Trap: Beware of statements containing absolute words like "solely", "without exception", or "unconditional". Most constitutional powers are subject to ministerial aid and advice (Art 74/163) or judicial review (Basic Structure doctrine).\n\n3. Constitutional vs Statutory Distinction: Do not confuse bodies created by constitutional articles (e.g. UPSC, Finance Commission, ECI) with statutory watchdogs created by ordinary Acts of Parliament (e.g. NGT, CVC, Lokpal, NHRC).\n\n4. Appointment vs Removal Asymmetry: Remember high-yield asymmetries—such as State PSC members being appointed by the Governor but removable only by the President after a Supreme Court inquiry under Article 317.

Legal & Constitutional Distinction

RPSC Architecture & The Critical Removal Asymmetry (Article 317)

  1. Constitutional Mandate (Articles 315–323):
    • Constituted: 20 August 1949 (effective 22 Dec 1949) at Jaipur; shifted to Ghooghra Ghati, Ajmer in 1956.
    • First Chairman: Sir S.K. Ghosh (Chief Justice of Rajasthan High Court held concurrent additional charge).
    • Composition: 1 Chairman + 7 Members (Total 8 members; at least 50% must have held government office for at least 10 years).
    • Tenure: 6 years or until attaining 62 years of age (whichever is earlier).

  2. Top Examination Trap: Appointment vs Removal Asymmetry:
    • Appointed by: The Governor of Rajasthan (Article 316).
    • Resignation addressed to: The Governor of Rajasthan.
    • Suspension power: The Governor may suspend the Chairman/Member pending inquiry.
    • REMOVED EXCLUSIVELY BY: The President of India (Article 317) on grounds of misbehavior after a mandatory inquiry report by the Supreme Court of India under Article 145!
