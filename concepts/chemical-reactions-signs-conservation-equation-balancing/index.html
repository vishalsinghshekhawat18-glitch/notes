Chemical Reactions, Signs of Chemical Change & Equation Balancing

The atomic rearrangement of reactants into products, experimental signs of chemical change (gas evolution, precipitate, color, temperature), and the hit-and-trial conservation balancing method.

Intuition & Context

Why You Cannot Just Add a "2" to Make Water into MgO

When magnesium burns in air, it reacts with oxygen gas (O2O_2O2​) to make white magnesium oxide (MgOMgOMgO). A beginner might be tempted to write Mg+O2⟶MgO2Mg + O_2 \longrightarrow MgO_2Mg+O2​⟶MgO2​. But MgO2MgO_2MgO2​ is magnesium peroxide—a totally different, unstable chemical! You can NEVER change the small subscript numbers inside a chemical formula, because that changes the identity of the molecule. You can ONLY change the big multiplying numbers (Stoichiometric Coefficients) in front: 2Mg+O2⟶2MgO\mathbf{2Mg + O_2 \longrightarrow 2MgO}2Mg+O2​⟶2MgO.

How It Works / Mechanism

Step-by-Step Balancing Walkthrough: Iron + Steam Reaction

Step-by-Step Hit-and-Trial Balancing:

Skeletal Equation: Fe+H2O⟶Fe3O4+H2\text{Skeletal Equation: } \text{Fe} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \text{H}_2Skeletal Equation: Fe+H2​O⟶Fe3​O4​+H2​

  1. Count Atoms on Both Sides:
    • Reactants: Fe=1,H=2,O=1\text{Fe} = 1, \text{H} = 2, \text{O} = 1Fe=1,H=2,O=1
    • Products: Fe=3,H=2,O=4\text{Fe} = 3, \text{H} = 2, \text{O} = 4Fe=3,H=2,O=4
  2. Step 1: Balance Oxygen Atoms (Maximum count = 4):
    • Put coefficient $4$ in front of H2O\text{H}_2\text{O}H2​O:

    Fe+4H2O⟶Fe3O4+H2\text{Fe} + \mathbf{4}\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \text{H}_2Fe+4H2​O⟶Fe3​O4​+H2​

  3. Step 2: Balance Hydrogen Atoms ($4 \times 2 = 8$ H on left):
    • Put coefficient $4$ in front of H2\text{H}_2H2​ on right:

    Fe+4H2O⟶Fe3O4+4H2\text{Fe} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \mathbf{4}\text{H}_2Fe+4H2​O⟶Fe3​O4​+4H2​

  4. Step 3: Balance Iron Atoms (3 Fe on right):
    • Put coefficient $3$ in front of Fe\text{Fe}Fe on left:

    3Fe (s)+4H2O (g)⟶Fe3O4 (s)+4H2 (g)\mathbf{3}\text{Fe (s)} + \mathbf{4}\text{H}_2\text{O (g)} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Fe_3O_4 \text{ (s)}} + \mathbf{4H_2 \text{ (g)}}3Fe (s)+4H2​O (g)⟶Fe3​O4​ (s)+4H2​ (g)

  5. Final Check: Fe: 3=3H: 8=8O: 4=4\text{Fe: } 3 = 3 \quad \text{H: } 8 = 8 \quad \text{O: } 4 = 4Fe: 3=3H: 8=8O: 4=4. (Perfect balance!)
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps: Gas Identification Tests & State Notations

Top Gas Identification Tests in Exam Questions:

  1. Hydrogen Gas (H2H_2H2​): Burns with a characteristic "POP" sound when tested with a burning splint.
  2. Oxygen Gas (O2O_2O2​): Rekindles a glowing wooden splint into a bright flame (supports combustion).
  3. Carbon Dioxide (CO2CO_2CO2​): Turns clear Lime Water [Ca(OH)2Ca(OH)_2Ca(OH)2​] milky due to insoluble CaCO3CaCO_3CaCO3​ precipitation; milkiness disappears upon excess CO2CO_2CO2​ passage (Ca(HCO3)2Ca(HCO_3)_2Ca(HCO3​)2​).
  4. Sulphur Dioxide (SO2SO_2SO2​): Suffocating smell of burning sulphur; turns orange Potassium Dichromate (K2Cr2O7K_2Cr_2O_7K2​Cr2​O7​) paper to Green.
