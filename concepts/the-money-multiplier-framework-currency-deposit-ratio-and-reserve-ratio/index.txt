12:T49a,Let:
- $M$ = Money Supply = Currency with Public ($C$) + Total Deposits ($D$)
- $M_0$ = Reserve Money = Currency with Public ($C$) + Total Reserves of Banks ($R$)

Divide numerator and denominator by Total Deposits ($D$):
$$m = \frac{M}{M_0} = \frac{C + D}{C + R} = \frac{\frac{C}{D} + 1}{\frac{C}{D} + \frac{R}{D}}$$

Defining:
- **Currency-Deposit Ratio ($c = \frac{C}{D}$)**: The proportion of money the public prefers to hold as cash relative to bank deposits.
- **Reserve-Deposit Ratio ($r = \frac{R}{D}$)**: The proportion of deposits banks hold as reserves ($r = \text{Required Reserves } r_r + \text{Excess Reserves } r_e$).

### The Master Multiplier Formula
$$m = \frac{1 + c}{c + r}$$

$$\text{Total Money Supply } M_3 = M_0 \times \left(\frac{1 + c}{c + r}\right)$$

### Comparative Statics (Direction of Changes)
- **$\frac{\partial m}{\partial r} < 0$**: Higher reserve requirement (CRR hike by RBI) $\implies$ lower money multiplier $\implies$ money supply contracts.
- **$\frac{\partial m}{\partial c} < 0$**: Higher cash holding by public (e.g., during election cycles or festive seasons) $\implies$ lower money multiplier $\implies$ banking liquidity tightens.13:T689,The money multiplier is not a mechanical constant; it is governed by behavioral decisions of the public, commercial banks, and the central bank:

```
                    DETERMINANTS OF MONEY MULTIPLIER (m)
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                          ▼                          ▼
  THE PUBLIC (c)            COMMERCIAL BANKS (r_e)      CENTRAL BANK (r_r)
• Income levels            • Risk perception           • CRR requirements
• Banking penetration      • Short-term interest rates • SLR stipulations
• Digital adoption (UPI)   • Liquidity buffers (SDF)   • Emergency windows (MSF)
• Informal economy share
```

### 1. Currency-Deposit Ratio ($c = C/D$)
- **Digital Payments (UPI/NEFT)**: Lowers $c$ because individuals need less physical cash for daily transactions. As cash moves into bank deposits, $m$ rises.
- **Interest Rates on Deposits**: Higher deposit interest rates incentivize public to deposit cash into banks, lowering $c$ and raising $m$.
- **Economic Uncertainty / Bank Runs**: Public rushes to withdraw deposits as cash, causing $c$ to spike and $m$ to collapse (the classic mechanism of monetary contraction in banking panics).

### 2. Reserve-Deposit Ratio ($r = R/D$)
- **Required Reserves ($r_r$)**: Directly set by RBI via CRR.
- **Excess Reserves ($r_e$)**: Kept by banks as insurance against liquidity shocks. When inter-bank call rates are low or economic risk is elevated, banks hold higher $r_e$, dampening $m$.14:T4d9,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Relationship between $c$ and $m$**: A frequent trap is assuming that because $c$ appears in both numerator and denominator, its effect is ambiguous. Since $r < 1$, the derivative $\frac{\partial m}{\partial c} = \frac{r - 1}{(c + r)^2} < 0$. Thus, **an increase in the currency-deposit ratio ALWAYS reduces the money multiplier**.
- **Trap 2: Demonetization Multiplier Shock**: During the November 2016 demonetization in India, the sudden mandatory deposit of high-denomination notes into banks caused $c$ to plummet temporarily, driving a sharp mechanical spike in the money multiplier, even as physical currency contracted.
- **Trap 3: Money Multiplier vs Velocity**: Money Multiplier ($m = M_3 / M_0$) is a **stock ratio** between two money aggregates. Velocity ($V = PY / M_3$) is a **flow-to-stock ratio** relating annual national output flow to money stock.

- **RBI Grade B & SEBI Grade A**: Advanced numerical questions calculating $m$, change in $M_3$ from open market operations (OMO) changing $M_0$, and sensitivity analysis.
