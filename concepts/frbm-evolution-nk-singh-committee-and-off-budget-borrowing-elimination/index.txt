11:T4af,### 1. Ending Extra-Budgetary Resources (EBR)
- **The Historic Problem**: Between 2016 and 2020, FCI accumulated over **₹3.3 Lakh Crore in loans from NSSF** to pay for food procurement subsidies, artificially suppressing the reported Central Fiscal Deficit by $1.0\% - 1.5\%$ of GDP.
- **The 2021 Cleanup**: Union Budget 2021-22 repaid the entire FCI-NSSF loan using direct budgetary provisions. Today, **$100\%$ of explicit subsidies are transparently on-budget**.

### 2. The Post-COVID Fiscal Consolidation Glidepath

```
     Central Fiscal Deficit (% of GDP)
         ▲
     10% │    9.2% (FY 2020-21: Peak COVID Stimulus)
         │     \
      8% │      \ 6.7% (FY 2021-22)
         │       \
      6% │        \ 6.4% (FY 2022-23) ──► 5.6% (FY 2023-24)
         │                                  \
      4% │                                   \ 4.9% (FY 2024-25 RE)
         │                                    \ ◄── TARGET: < 4.5% (FY 2025-26)
      2% │
         └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► Time (Years)
```12:T439,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: N.K. Singh Debt Targets**: General Government Debt target is **60% of GDP** (split as **40% for Centre** and **20% for States**). A classic exam trap swaps the Centre and State percentages!
- **Trap 2: Post-COVID Target**: The post-pandemic fiscal consolidation target announced in Budget 2021-22 is to bring the Central Fiscal Deficit to **BELOW 4.5% of GDP by FY 2025-26**.
- **Trap 3: Fiscal Council Status**: The Fiscal Council was **recommended** by the N.K. Singh Committee (and 13th/14th Finance Commissions), but has NOT yet been established as a statutory body in India.
- **Trap 4: Off-Budget Borrowings Accounting**: Off-budget borrowings were loans taken by PSUs (like FCI or NHAI) whose principal and interest were serviced by the Government of India.
