12:T4ac,Imagine three workers in an industrial hub outside Delhi:

1. **Worker A** earns ₹8,000 a month. This barely buys coarse rice, pays for a shared slum cot, and covers daily bus fares. If the factory owner says, *"My profits dropped, so I will now pay you ₹5,000,"* the law steps in and says: **NO**. Paying below the **Minimum Wage** is legally treated as forced labor (bonded labor under Article 23). An employer who cannot pay the minimum wage is legally forced to shut down.
2. **Worker B** earns ₹22,000 a month in a highly profitable steel rolling mill. The wage covers decent food, a 1-room apartment, school tuition for two children, and occasional doctor visits. This is a **Fair Wage**—it reflects the company's strong profits and prevailing industrial productivity.
3. **Worker C** earns ₹65,000 a month. It covers nutritious food, comprehensive health insurance, university tuition funds, recreation, and retirement savings. This is the **Living Wage**—the ultimate constitutional ideal envisioned by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar in Article 43 of the Constitution.

Wage policy is the legal and economic architecture that moves society upward from mere *survival* toward *flourishing*.13:T751,```

### The 15th ILC (1957) + Raptakos Brett (1992) Needs-Based Minimum Wage Formula

| Component | Physical Norm Specification | Legal / Committee Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Family Unit** | Standard working-class household = **3.0 Consumption Units** (Worker = 1.0, Wife = 0.8, Two Children = 0.6 each $\implies 1.0+0.8+1.2 = 3.0$). | 15th ILC (1957) |
| **2. Food Intake** | **2,700 Calories** per adult consumption unit per day (NIN / ICMR guidelines for moderate work). | 15th ILC (1957) |
| **3. Clothing** | **18 Yards per capita per annum** = **72 Yards** per standard family of 4 per year. | 15th ILC (1957) |
| **4. Housing Rent** | **10%** of the total minimum food and clothing expenditure. | 15th ILC (1957) |
| **5. Miscellaneous** | Fuel, lighting, and other miscellaneous items at **20%** of the total minimum wage. | 15th ILC (1957) |
| **6. Children & Health** | **Additional 25%** of total wage for children's education, medical requirements, recreation, and old age contingencies. | **Supreme Court (Raptakos Brett, 1992)** |14:T431,### 1. Neoclassical Value of Marginal Product of Labor ($VMP_L$)
In a competitive labor and product market, profit-maximizing firms hire labor until the nominal wage equals the Value of Marginal Product of Labor:
$$W = VMP_L = P \times MP_L$$
Where $P$ is output price and $MP_L = \frac{\partial Y}{\partial L}$ is marginal physical product of labor.
- *Exploitation in Monopsony*: If a single dominant employer (monopsonist) controls rural labor hiring, they face an upward-sloping labor supply curve and pay wages strictly below $VMP_L$ ($W < VMP_L$). A statutory minimum wage in a monopsony can **increase both wages AND total employment simultaneously** without causing layoffs.

### 2. Wage-Price Spiral and Phillips Curve
- **Mechanism**: A sharp rise in consumer price inflation (food/fuel) erodes real wages ($W_R = W_N / P$). Workers demand compensatory nominal wage hikes (Dearness Allowance - DA). Firms pass on the higher labor cost by raising output prices ($P \uparrow$), which triggers further wage demands—forming a self-reinforcing **Wage-Price Spiral**.15:T52b,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Capacity to Pay in Minimum Wage**: Under Indian constitutional law (Express Newspapers case), an employer's **financial inability to pay is NOT a valid defense** against paying the statutory Minimum Wage. Capacity to pay applies ONLY when negotiating Fair Wages or Living Wages.
- **Trap 2: Family Consumption Units in ILC Formula**: The 15th ILC formula defines a standard family as **3.0 Consumption Units** (representing 4 physical persons: husband, wife, and 2 children), NOT 4.0 units.
- **Trap 3: Minimum Wage vs National Floor Wage**: Under the Code on Wages 2019, the Central Government sets the **National Floor Wage**; State Governments cannot fix minimum wages *below* this Central floor.
