11:T459,Let us examine the central bank's balance sheet across both operations:

### 1. Unsterilized Forex Purchase (Liquidity Expands)
1. **Forex Market Action**: RBI buys US \USD 1 Billionillion ($+₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ Net Foreign Assets [NFA]).
2. **Domestic Payment**: RBI credits commercial bank balances with ₹8,400 Crore ($+₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ Bank Reserves).
3. **Balance Sheet Impact**: Reserve Money ($M_0 = NFA + NDA$) expands by ₹8,400 Crore $\\implies$ Broad Money ($M_3$) surges via money multiplier $\\implies$ **Inflationary Risk**.

### 2. Sterilized Forex Purchase (Liquidity Neutralized)
1. **Forex Market Action**: RBI buys US \USD 1 Billionillion ($+₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ NFA).
2. **Sterilization Action**: RBI simultaneously sells ₹8,400 Crore of Government Securities (G-Secs) to banks under Open Market Operations (OMOs) ($-₹8,400 \text{ Crore}$ Net Domestic Assets [NDA]).

$$\\Delta M_0 = \\Delta NFA (+8,400) + \\Delta NDA (-8,400) = 0$$
*Result*: Net Reserve Money ($M_0$) remains perfectly constant. Exchange rate appreciation is stopped, but domestic inflation is prevented!12:T566,```

### The Quasi-Fiscal Cost of Sterilization
Sterilization is not free. When the RBI holds US \USD 10 Billionillion of foreign exchange, it invests them in US Treasury Bills earning $\\sim 4\%$. But to sterilize the domestic liquidity, the government/RBI must issue domestic bonds paying $\\sim 7\%$. The **$3\%$ negative interest spread** ($7\% - 4\%$) represents the **Quasi-Fiscal Carrying Cost** borne by the public treasury to maintain exchange rate stability.13:T437,### Critical Examiner Traps
- **Trap 1: Can MSS Funds be Spent by Government?**: **NO.** Under the MoU between Ministry of Finance and RBI (2004), cash balances collected through MSS bond issuances are **strictly frozen/impounded in a separate account with the RBI**. The government CANNOT spend MSS money on budget projects.
- **Trap 2: Sterilization Formula**: Sterilization means $\\Delta NFA + \\Delta NDA = 0$. Buying foreign assets ($+NFA$) is sterilized by selling domestic assets ($-NDA$).
- **Trap 3: Defending Currency vs Building Reserves**: Central banks have **infinite capacity to buy dollars** (they can print unlimited domestic currency), but **finite capacity to defend depreciation** (they can only sell dollars until their foreign reserves hit zero).
