Public Administration & Management · Concept 10 of 12

New Public Management (NPM): The 3Es (Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness), Osborne & Gaebler's Entrepreneurial Government & Public Choice Theory

The market-oriented managerial transformation of the public sector, synthesizing Public Choice Theory and Neo-Taylorism to dismantle bureaucratic monopolies through contracting-out, customer-orientation, and steering rather than rowing.

Intuition & Context

Administrative Intuition: E-Mitra Kiosks and Highway Toll Concessions in Rajasthan

To witness New Public Management (NPM) in daily operation, look at how public services are delivered across Rajasthan today compared to the 1980s.

The Old Bureaucratic Monolith (Rowing):
In 1985, to pay an electricity bill, obtain a domicile certificate, or pay water charges, a citizen had to visit three separate government offices, stand in suffocating queues for six hours, and plead with indifferent government clerks. The state operated as both policy-maker and physical producer (rower).

The NPM Reinvention (Steering):
Today, the Government of Rajasthan operates E-Mitra—a network of over 85,000 digital kiosks operated entirely by private entrepreneurs (franchisees) under public regulatory oversight. The state no longer 'rows' by hiring 50,000 government cashiers. Instead, the state 'steers':

  1. It sets the software standards, service level agreements (SLAs), and fee ceilings.
  2. Private kiosk operators compete for citizen footfall.
  3. The citizen is treated as a 'Customer' who expects instant receipts and SMS alerts.

The same applies to Rajasthan State Highways: private consortia construct and maintain highways via Public-Private Partnerships (PPP / BOT concessions), while the state department focuses on regulatory auditing and policy direction.

Core Principle

The 3Es, Osborne & Gaebler's 10 Principles, and Bureaucracy vs. NPM Matrix

New Public Management represents the convergence of Managerialism (Neo-Taylorism) and Public Choice Theory, replacing bureaucratic inertia with market-driven performance.

1. The Core Performance Metrics: The 3Es

  • Economy (Input Criterion): Minimizing the cost of acquired resources (financial, human, material) without sacrificing quality ('Spending less').
  • Efficiency (Process Criterion): Maximizing the ratio of outputs achieved to inputs consumed ('Spending well' / Output per unit of Input).
  • Effectiveness (Outcome Criterion): Ensuring that outputs actually achieve the intended policy objectives and socio-economic outcomes ('Spending wisely').
    (Modern public governance often adds a 4th E: Equity—ensuring fair distributive access for the vulnerable).

2. Osborne & Gaebler's 10 Principles of Entrepreneurial Government

  1. Catalytic Government: Steering rather than rowing (Separating policy direction from service delivery).
  2. Community-Owned Government: Empowering citizens and communities to solve problems rather than fostering bureaucratic dependency.
  3. Competitive Government: Injecting competition into service delivery (public vs. private tendering).
  4. Mission-Driven Government: Driven by clear organizational mission rather than rigid statutory rules.
  5. Results-Oriented Government: Funding outcomes and impacts rather than inputs and budget line-items.
  6. Customer-Driven Government: Meeting the needs of the customer, not the bureaucracy.
  7. Enterprising Government: Earning money through user fees and investments rather than merely spending tax revenues.
  8. Anticipatory Government: Prevention rather than cure (proactive risk mitigation).
  9. Decentralized Government: From hierarchy to teamwork, participation, and frontline empowerment.
  10. Market-Oriented Government: Leveraging market mechanisms (vouchers, carbon credits, tax credits) rather than bureaucratic command-and-control.

3. Traditional Public Administration vs. New Public Management

Analytical Dimension Traditional Bureaucracy (Weber / Wilson) New Public Management (Hood / Osborne)
Governing Philosophy Weberian legal-rationalism, procedural compliance Entrepreneurial managerialism, market incentives
Role of Government Rowing and Steering simultaneously (State monopoly) Steering rather than Rowing (Regulator & Facilitator)
Citizen Perception Administrative Subjects or Voters Customers and Consumers

How It Works / Mechanism

The Public Choice Logic, Disaggregation Pipeline, and Denhardt's Critique

The institutional re-engineering under NPM dismantles bureaucratic monopoly via market forces:

[Monolithic Government Ministry (Rowing + Steering)]
                          |
                          v
             --- DISAGGREGATION PROCESS ---
  - Core Ministry retains Policy & Strategy (Steering)
  - Executive Agencies created for delivery (e.g., Next Steps Agencies in UK)
                          |
                          v
         --- INJECTION OF MARKET MECHANISMS ---
  - Competitive Tendering / Outsourcing / PPP Concessions
  - Citizen Charter Standards + Service Level Agreements (SLAs)
  - Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) & Citizen-Customer Choice
                          |
                          v
[Outcome: High Cost-Efficiency, Reduced Fiscal Overheads, Rapid Delivery]

The Democratic Deficit and The 'New Public Service' Critique:

Janet and Robert Denhardt (The New Public Service: Serving, Not Steering, 2000) leveled a devastating critique against NPM:

  1. Citizens are NOT mere Customers: Customers buy what they can afford; citizens possess constitutional rights, duties, and shared democratic sovereignty.
  2. Erosion of Public Interest: Reducing governance to market transactions destroys social cohesion, equity, and civic virtue. Government should 'Serve, not Steer'.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

RPSC RAS Mains Paper III Evaluation Pitfalls & Strategy

RPSC RAS Mains Pitfalls & Scoring Insights:

  • Coinage vs. Popularization (2 Marks): If asked 'Who coined New Public Management?', write Christopher Hood (1991). If asked 'Who gave the 10 principles of entrepreneurial government / Steering rather than Rowing?', write Osborne & Gaebler (1992).
  • The 3Es Exact Definitions (2 Marks / 5 Marks): In an RPSC RAS question on the 3Es, do not write generic sentences. Use the technical input-output formulation:
    • Economy: Minimizing the cost of inputs.
    • Efficiency: Maximizing the ratio of outputs to inputs.
    • Effectiveness: Maximizing the achievement of intended policy outcomes.
  • Steering vs. Rowing Metaphor (2 Marks): Explain precisely: 'Steering refers to policy formulation, regulation, and strategic guidance; Rowing refers to the actual operational production and physical delivery of public services.'
  • NPA vs. NPM Distinction (10 Marks): Ensure you contrast their ideological roots: NPA (1968) is anti-positivist, normative, and pro-social equity; NPM (1991) is pro-market, neo-liberal, and efficiency-driven.
