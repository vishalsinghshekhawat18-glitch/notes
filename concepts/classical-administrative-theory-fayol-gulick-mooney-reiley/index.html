Public Administration & Management · Concept 2 of 12

Classical Administrative Theory: Henri Fayol's 14 Principles, POSDCORB (Luther Gulick), and Mooney & Reiley

The structural-engineering approach to organizational design that posited universal management principles, top-down hierarchical coordination, departmentalization bases (4Ps), and formal scalar chains.

Intuition & Context

Administrative Intuition: File Routing in the Rajasthan Secretariat (Mantralaya)

To visualize Classical Theory in action, examine how a government file moves through the Government Secretariat (Shasan Sachivalaya) in Jaipur.

A policy proposal on agricultural solar pump subsidies follows an unyielding Scalar Chain: initiated by an Assistant Section Officer (ASO), scrutinized by the Section Officer (SO), forwarded to the Deputy Secretary (DS), cleared by the Special Secretary, reviewed by the Principal Secretary (Pr. Secy), and finally approved by the Cabinet Minister.

Every officer receives orders from exactly one immediate superior—the cardinal classical principle of Unity of Command.

However, what happens if there is an acute locust outbreak in Jaisalmer requiring instantaneous coordination between the Agriculture Department and the Disaster Management Department? If the file has to climb all the way to the Chief Secretary and descend back down, crops across Western Rajasthan will be annihilated. Here, Fayol's Gangplank (Passerelle) permits the Director of Agriculture to communicate laterally and directly with the Director of Relief, provided their respective superiors have granted prior authorization and are immediately briefed of decisions made.

Core Principle

Classical Structural Pillars: Fayol, Gulick, Urwick, and Mooney & Reiley

The Classical Theory (also termed Administrative Management Theory or Universalist School) viewed organization as a formal, rational structure governed by immutable, universal engineering laws.

1. Henri Fayol's 14 Principles of Management

  1. Division of Work: Specialization produces more and better work with the same effort.
  2. Authority and Responsibility: Authority is the right to give orders; responsibility is its natural corollary. The two must be commensurate.
  3. Discipline: Obedience, application, energy, and outward marks of respect in accordance with agreements.
  4. Unity of Command: An employee should receive orders from one and only one superior (prevents dual allegiance and conflict).
  5. Unity of Direction: One head and one plan for a group of activities having the same objective.
  6. Subordination of Individual Interest: Organizational goals must supersede personal inclinations.
  7. Remuneration of Personnel: Fair, equitable compensation satisfying both employee and employer.
  8. Centralization: Finding the optimum balance between central control and subordinate initiative based on organizational context.
  9. Scalar Chain (Line of Authority): The unbroken chain of superiors from highest authority to the lowest rank. Includes Gangplank for horizontal shortcuts.
  10. Order: Material order ('A place for everything and everything in its place') and Social order ('A place for everyone and everyone in their place').
  11. Equity: Combination of kindliness and justice toward employees.
  12. Stability of Tenure: Minimizing staff turnover to allow mastery of duties.
  13. Initiative: Encouraging employees to conceive and carry out plans.
  14. Esprit de Corps: Harmony and union among personnel (avoiding divide-and-rule).

2. Luther Gulick's 4Ps of Departmentalization

Gulick asserted that every departmental structure is organized according to one of four mutually competing bases:

  • Purpose (Function): Organized around the ultimate objective (e.g., Department of Education, Department of Health).
  • Process (Technique): Organized around specialized skills or equipment (e.g., Department of Law, Department of Public Works / Engineering).
  • Persons (Clientele): Organized around the target group served (e.g., Department of Social Justice & Empowerment, Tribal Area Development).
  • Place (Geography): Organized around physical territory (e.g., Border Area Development Authority, Western Railway).

How It Works / Mechanism

Scalar Hierarchy, the Gangplank Mechanism, and Simon's 'Proverbs' Critique

The scalar chain creates an unbending hierarchy, but Fayol recognized that dogmatic vertical transmission causes paralysis. He designed the Gangplank (Passerelle) mechanism:

          [A] (Chief Secretary)
         /   \
       [B]   [L] (Principal Secretaries)
       /       \
     [C]       [M] (Special Secretaries)
     /           \
   [D]           [N] (Deputy Secretaries)
   /               \
 [E] <===========> [O]  <--- GANGPLANK (Horizontal Bridge)
   (Direct lateral communication between E & O with prior clearance from D & N)

Herbert Simon's Devastating Critique: 'Proverbs of Administration'

In 1946–1947, Herbert Simon attacked the Classical school, demonstrating that its celebrated 'principles' are unscientific, mutually contradictory proverbs:

  1. Unity of Command vs. Specialization: Specialization dictates that a subordinate must take instructions from specialized experts (e.g., an accountant listens to a financial controller and an engineer listens to a chief architect). But Unity of Command dictates listening to only one superior. The two principles directly negate each other!
  2. Span of Control vs. Number of Tiers: If you narrow the span of control (fewer subordinates per boss) to ensure tight supervision, you inevitably multiply administrative tiers (creating an elongated, sluggish hierarchy). If you flatten the organization to speed up communication, you widen the span of control beyond human cognitive capacity.
Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

RPSC RAS Mains Paper III Evaluation Pitfalls & Strategy

High-Yield RPSC RAS Traps:

  • Unity of Command vs. Unity of Direction (2 Marks Trap): This is one of the most frequent 2-mark questions in RPSC RAS Paper III. Candidates routinely mix them up:
    • Unity of Command: Deals with personnel ('One employee should receive orders from one and only one boss'). Purpose: Prevents conflict and confusion.
    • Unity of Direction: Deals with activities and planning ('One head and one plan for a group of activities having the same objective'). Purpose: Ensures sound coordination of corporate plans.
  • Fayol's Original Book: Written in French in 1916 (Administration Industrielle et Générale), NOT English. Translated in 1949.
  • POCCC vs. POSDCORB: Do not attribute POSDCORB to Fayol! Fayol gave POCCC (Planning, Organizing, Commanding, Coordinating, Controlling). Gulick gave POSDCORB.
  • Gangplank Conditions (2 Marks): In an RPSC 2M question on Gangplank, simply writing 'horizontal communication' gets only 1 mark. You MUST state the two mandatory preconditions: (1) Prior permission of immediate superiors, and (2) Superiors must be kept informed immediately afterwards.
