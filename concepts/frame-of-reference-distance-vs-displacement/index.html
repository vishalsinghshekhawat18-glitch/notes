Frame of Reference, Position, Distance vs Displacement Mechanics

The foundational spatial kinematics describing motion relative to a reference frame and contrasting path length against vector displacement.

Intuition & Context

Why Distance Walked Does Not Mean Distance Traveled

If you run a complete $400 , \text{metre}$ lap on a circular running track and finish exactly where you started, your pedometer reads $400 , \text{m}$ (Distance), but your net physical relocation is exactly ZERO (Displacement). In physics, where you end up relative to where you started is the true vector change in your reality.

How It Works / Mechanism

Geometric Scenarios: Circular Tracks, Return Trips & Right Angles

High-Yield Classical Trajectories:

  1. Complete Circular Path of Radius rrr:

    • Initial position === Final position.
    • Distance=2πr\text{Distance} = 2\pi rDistance=2πr
    • Displacement=0\text{Displacement} = 0Displacement=0

Exam Trap & High-Yield Analysis

Examiner Traps & Ratio Heuristics

Core Examiner Traps:

  1. The Ratio Trap: The ratio of Magnitude of Displacement to Distance is always ≤1\le 1≤1 (∣Δx∣/s≤1|\Delta x| / s \le 1∣Δx∣/s≤1). The inverse ratio (Distance / Displacement) is always ≥1\ge 1≥1.
  2. Zero Displacement Fallacy: An object can travel thousands of kilometres (e.g. Earth orbiting the Sun in 1 year =940 million km= 940 \text{ million km}=940 million km) and still possess zero displacement after a complete orbital period.
  3. Straight-Line Reversal: If a car drives $100 , \text{m}$ forward and reverses $40 , \text{m}$, Distance=140 m\text{Distance} = 140 , \text{m}Distance=140m, while Displacement=+60 m\text{Displacement} = +60 , \text{m}Displacement=+60m.
